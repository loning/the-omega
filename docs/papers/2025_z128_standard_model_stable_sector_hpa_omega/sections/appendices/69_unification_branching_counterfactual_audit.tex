\subsection{Unification branching and counterfactual audits (scope and CAP contracts)}
\label{app:unification_branching_counterfactual_audit}

This appendix records a compact audit-oriented view of what ``unification'' can mean inside the paper's layer discipline, and how to treat grand-unification style statements as \emph{counterfactual candidates} rather than as theorem-level consequences of tick+CAP.
It is intentionally conservative: it registers a bounded set of alternative hypotheses and explains which parts of the unification narrative are matching-layer conventions versus interface dictionaries.

\subsubsection{Three notions of unification (separated by layer)}
\paragraph{(U1) Group unification (structure-level).}
\InterfaceTag This asks whether the gauge structure is a single connected compact group $G$ (with Standard Model factors embedded as subgroups) or a product of factors $G_1\times G_2\times G_3$ up to finite quotients.
In this paper the three-factor identification $U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ is an \emph{interface} closure conditional on bounded candidate families and explicit tie-breaks (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge} and Appendix~\ref{app:gauge3_holonomy_candidate_closure}).

\paragraph{(U2) Coupling unification (matching-level).}
\MatchTag Even for a fixed group structure, ``unification'' can mean relations among running couplings at some high scale, e.g.\ $g_1(\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}})=g_2(\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}})=g_3(\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}})$ in a chosen scheme and normalization.
Such statements are inherently scheme/threshold dependent and therefore live at the matching layer in this paper (Appendix~\ref{app:renormalization_dictionary_and_boundaries} and Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).

\paragraph{(U3) Representation/normalization conventions (matching-level).}
\MatchTag Hypercharge normalization and the definition of $g_1$ versus $g_Y$ are conventions that change the numerical form of ``unification benchmarks''.
The paper therefore separates $Z$-scale identities from unification-scale benchmarks (Remark~\ref{rem:gutsin2_benchmark}).

\subsubsection{Counterfactual candidate registry (bounded, explicit)}
\AuditTag To make look-elsewhere freedom explicit, we treat unification claims as a bounded registry of counterfactual candidates.
This registry is used only for audit-facing comparisons; it is not used as a premise in theorem-level folding arguments.
\AuditTag Cross-family comparisons over such registries are governed by the global MDL/prefix-code contract in Appendix~\ref{app:global_model_selection_mdl}:
enlarging the registry is an explicit enlargement of the audited search space and must be recorded as such.

\paragraph{Registry items.}
We consider the following counterfactual families (illustrative and bounded):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CF1 (three-factor baseline).} $G=U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ up to finite quotients, with factors selected by the holonomy-driven CAP rule (Appendix~\ref{app:gauge3_holonomy_candidate_closure}).
\item \textbf{CF2 (simple-group GUT benchmark).} A simple connected compact $G$ containing the Standard Model gauge algebra as a subalgebra, e.g.\ minimal $SU(5)$-type embeddings, treated only as a benchmark for matching-layer relations (Remark~\ref{rem:gutsin2_benchmark}).
\item \textbf{CF3 (intermediate unification).} Partial unification (e.g.\ an $SU(2)\times U(1)$ merge or other semisimple alternatives) treated as additional candidates, subject to explicit bounded complexity keys if used.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Audit contract (no tuning from global fits).}
\AuditTag If unification benchmarks are referenced, they enter only as externally stated comparison relations (e.g.\ tree-level $\sin^2\theta_W(\mu_{\mathrm{GUT}})=3/8$ in minimal $SU(5)$-type models; Remark~\ref{rem:gutsin2_benchmark}).
They are not used to tune protocol-layer primitives, holonomy diagnostics, or CAP tie-break rules.

\subsubsection{Why the present closure does not assert group unification (certificate-style summary)}
\paragraph{CAP-minimality is conditional and scoped.}
\AuditTag The paper's gauge-factor closure is not a claim that Nature \emph{must} realize a product group, nor that simple-group unification is impossible.
It is a claim of the following form:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Within the explicitly declared bounded candidate family and selection keys, the CAP-minimal factors compatible with the protocol holonomy diagnostics are $U(1)$ (rephasing), $SU(2)$ (minimal non-abelian rotation skeleton), and $SU(3)$ (minimal genuinely complex $3\times 3$ unitary support with CP-odd invariants).}
\end{quote}
This is precisely what Definition~\ref{def:holonomy_to_candidate_family_rule} and the bounded enumeration in Appendix~\ref{app:gauge3_holonomy_candidate_closure} implement.

\paragraph{Simple-group unification increases declared complexity.}
\AuditTag In the same bounded enumeration logic, a simple-group GUT candidate (e.g.\ $SU(5)$) has larger Lie-algebra dimension and larger minimal faithful representation dimension than the CAP-minimal factors selected to satisfy (H2)--(H3) in Appendix~\ref{app:gauge3_holonomy_candidate_closure}.
Therefore, within that declared complexity ordering, such candidates are \emph{not} CAP-minimal for the holonomy-driven interface requirements.

\subsubsection{Where unification can be pursued next (explicit boundary)}
\paragraph{What would count as progress inside this paper's discipline.}
\AuditTag A stronger unification statement would require at least one of the following, each with an explicit finite-domain audit:
\begin{itemize}
\item a bounded candidate-family route that produces a \emph{single} group $G$ from protocol diagnostics without importing unification-scale benchmarks as tuning targets;
\item a bounded matching-layer audit that makes coupling unification (or its failure) explicit in the $r$-coordinate RG dictionary, including threshold conventions and scheme dependence (Appendix~\ref{app:renormalization_dictionary_and_boundaries});
\item a falsifiable observational chain (e.g.\ proton-decay channels) that is explicitly registered as out-of-scope unless a protocol-to-observation bridge is supplied.
\end{itemize}
This appendix records the boundary so that future extensions can be added without mixing layers.
