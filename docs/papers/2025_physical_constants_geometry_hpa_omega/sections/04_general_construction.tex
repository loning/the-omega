\section{General construction: constants as invariants of readout geometry}
\label{sec:general_construction}

\subsection{Geometric objects and invariants}
We replace informal declarations of ``natural geometricization'' by a reusable template.

\begin{definition}[A geometricization template for constants]
\label{def:geom_template}
Given a family of geometric objects $\mathcal{G}(r)$ that can depend on a resolution/coarse-graining scale $r$, define an invariant or spectral quantity
\begin{equation}
I_r(\mathcal{G})\in\RR.
\end{equation}
Define a physical constant (or a vector of constants) by
\begin{equation}
C(r)=F\!\left(I_r(\mathcal{G})\right),
\end{equation}
where $F$ is the mapping into the observational convention. The map $F$ can contain metrological interfaces (defining constants) but must not introduce unit-dependent ambiguity into the dimensionless content.
\end{definition}

In the HPA--$\Omega$ context, typical sources for $\mathcal{G}(r)$ include:
\begin{itemize}
    \item topological channels induced by the readout cut (bulk/boundary/line);
    \item spectral data of defects in nonassociative sectors (e.g., octonionic or exceptional Jordan structures);
    \item symbolic dynamical systems generated by scan--projection and their orbit statistics;
    \item one-dimensional compilation/routing cost fields (the $\Omega$ part) and the induced lapse.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Impedance as a prototype invariant: from metric length to process cost}
In the HPA--$\Omega$ constant-geometry program, inverse couplings are identified with a \emph{geometric impedance}: a minimal process cost required to establish a stable interaction channel. Channel costs accumulate serially because readout constraints must be satisfied layer by layer, leading to an $\ell^1$-type accumulation rather than an $\ell^2$-type norm; this is formalized for the electromagnetic worked example by Axiom~\ref{ax:serial_impedance}.
