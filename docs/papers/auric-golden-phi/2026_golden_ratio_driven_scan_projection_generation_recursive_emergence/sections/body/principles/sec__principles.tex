\section{Starting Point and Principles: Anti-Resonance Baseline and Finite-Sample Certificates}\label{sec:principles}

\paragraph{Self-indicative ratio and renormalization fixed point}

We fix the golden ratio \(\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}\), and use the golden branch \(\alpha=\varphi^{-1}\in(0,1)\) as the normalization parameter in subsequent constructions (serving only as the scale-baseline notation).

\paragraph{Anti-resonance baseline}

For scan-parameter selection, this paper takes the Hurwitz/Markov worst rational-approximation scale as an externally auditable anti-resonance baseline (\cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}).

\begin{definition}[Markov constant (worst-approximation lower bound)]\label{def:markov-constant}
For \(\alpha\in\RR\setminus\QQ\), define
\[
c(\alpha):=\inf_{(p,q)\in\ZZ\times\NN} q^2\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|\in[0,\infty).
\]
If \(c(\alpha)>0\), then \(\alpha\) is called badly approximable (see \cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}).
\end{definition}

Hurwitz's theorem yields \(c(\alpha)\le 1/\sqrt5\), and this upper bound is attained precisely by the golden branch and its \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\ZZ)\)-equivalence class (\cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}); accordingly, we fix \(\alpha=\varphi^{-1}\) as the anti-resonant normalization baseline.
\par
At the protocol level, this choice can be read as a rigidity consequence of ``single generator -- binary window readout -- normalized closure.'' In this paper's scan-projection generator, ``time'' is defined as the iteration index of a single-generator action (Section \ref{sec:spg}), readout is fixed as finite-window binary records (same section), and certificates in the subsequent \(\mathbf{PW}\)-normalized closure are required to pass fixed interface checks. Under this constraint, scan-parameter selection is no longer empirical tuning, but externalized as a purely arithmetic worst-approximation/anti-resonance problem. The saturation property of the Hurwitz bound (with saturating class given only by the golden branch and its \(\mathrm{SL}_2(\ZZ)\)-equivalence class) therefore appears in this paper as the ``arithmetic rigidity of the golden branch'' (see also protocol-fixing assumptions in \cite{Internal2026POMGenerativeAddressThreeRegisterBridgeRH}).

\paragraph{Finite-sample stability certificate}

To connect this baseline with readout stability, we introduce in a one-dimensional Kronecker scan model the same quantitative indicator family used in Section \ref{sec:statistical-stability}.

\begin{definition}[Resonance-induced instability (auditable certificate for Kronecker scans)]\label{def:instability-envelope}
For irrational \(\alpha\in\RR\setminus\QQ\) and initial phase \(x_0\in[0,1)\), consider the Kronecker sequence
\[
x_t=\{x_0+t\alpha\}\in[0,1),\qquad t=0,1,\dots
\]
and define its star discrepancy by
\[
D_N^*(\alpha;x_0):=\sup_{0\le a\le 1}\left|\frac1N\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\ind_{[0,a)}(x_t)-a\right|.
\]
We interpret \(D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)\) as a certificate of ``resonance-induced instability'' at sample size \(N\) under interval-window binary readout (see \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974}).
\end{definition}

From Definition \ref{def:instability-envelope}, for any interval window \(W=[0,a)\) with indicator readout \(s_t=\ind_W(x_t)\), we have
\[
\left|\frac1N\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}s_t-a\right|\le D_N^*(\alpha;x_0).
\]
Moreover, if \(\alpha\) is badly approximable (equivalently, has bounded continued-fraction partial quotients), then there exists \(C_\alpha<\infty\) such that for all \(x_0\) and \(N\ge 2\),
\[
D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)\le C_\alpha\frac{\log N}{N}
\]
(the constant is controlled by the partial-quotient bound; see \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974}).

\begin{corollary}[Auditable star-discrepancy certificate for the golden branch]\label{cor:golden-branch-star-discrepancy-audit}
For \(\alpha=\varphi^{-1}=[0;1,1,1,\dots]\), continued-fraction partial quotients are uniformly bounded (by \(1\)); hence the above \(O((\log N)/N)\) discrepancy bound has a constant explicitly controlled by that bound (\cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974}). Furthermore, convergent denominators satisfy \(q_n=F_{n+1}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not optimized in this paper]\label{rem:what-is-optimized}
The term ``optimal'' in this paper is restricted to the Hurwitz/Markov baseline and its associated discrepancy-certificate chain. For stronger statistical functionals, such as global optimality or uniqueness of folded-type distributions, or distances relative to Parry baselines, this paper makes no such claim.
\end{remark}
