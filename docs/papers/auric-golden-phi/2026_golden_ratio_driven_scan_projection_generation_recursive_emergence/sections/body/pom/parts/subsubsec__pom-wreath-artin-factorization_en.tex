\subsubsection{Artin Factorization of the Wreath Lift: Block-Diagonal Channels for Label--Spectral Auditing}\label{subsubsec:pom-wreath-artin-factorization}
This paragraph aligns the wreath exchange of the Moment--Lift (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-mom-lift-wreath-exchange}) with the Artin factorization of covering determinants (Proposition \ref{prop:kernel-artin-factorization}),
thereby converting ``labeled higher-order collision statistics'' into a block-diagonal readout of finitely many irreducible channels.

\begin{corollary}[Artin-type factorization and twisted block spectrum of the wreath lift]\label{cor:pom-wreath-artin-factorization}
Take a finite group \(G\) and an integer \(q\ge 2\), and let \(H:=G\wr S_q=G^{\times q}\rtimes S_q\) be the wreath product (Definition \ref{def:pom-wreath-product}).
Let \(A_q\) denote a fixed realization of the \(q\)-th order collision moment-kernel (one may take the symmetric power quotient kernel \(\widetilde A_q\)), and let \(\mathrm{LIFT}_H\) denote the normal covering/permutation lift protocol compatible with the regular action of \(H\).
Write \(A_q^{(H)}\) for the resulting covering operator.

Then under the Peter--Weyl decomposition of the regular representation there exists a natural isomorphism
\[
\boxed{\;
A_q^{(H)}\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{\rho\in\widehat H}
\Bigl(\mathrm{Id}_{\dim\rho}\otimes A_{q,\rho}\Bigr)\; },
\]
where \(\widehat H\) is the family of irreducible complex representations of \(H\), and \(A_{q,\rho}\) is the corresponding \(\rho\)-twisted moment operator (channel block).
Consequently, for the covering \(\zeta\)-function (\(\zeta_{A}(z)=\det(I-zA)^{-1}\); see \S\ref{subsec:zeta-syntax-zeta}) we obtain the Artin-type factorization
\[
\boxed{\;
\zeta_{q}^{(H)}(z)
:=\det(I-zA_q^{(H)})^{-1}
\;=\;
\prod_{\rho\in\widehat H}\det(I-zA_{q,\rho})^{-\dim\rho}\; }.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition \ref{prop:kernel-artin-factorization}, for any normal covering/permutation lift by a finite group, the covering determinant admits an Artin factorization according to the irreducible decomposition of the regular representation;
applying this general result to the group \(H=G\wr S_q\) and the lifted operator \(A_q^{(H)}\) immediately yields the stated block decomposition and multiplicative factorization.
Proposition \ref{prop:pom-mom-lift-wreath-exchange} ensures that, under the moment semantics of ``unlabeled replication + permutation symmetry,'' the type-correct object for the lift axis is precisely \(G\wr S_q\), so the above decomposition provides a label-channelized fingerprint strictly consistent with the moment compilation. \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Rigid collapse of infinite torsion symmetry and coprime closure: symmetric factorial closure \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} rank-one spectral degeneration}
This paragraph elevates the extreme symmetry of the ``twisted point table'' into a rigidity constraint on the non-Perron spectrum: if the non-Perron part exhibits torsion symmetry simultaneously at infinitely many coprime orders, then no nontrivial spectral points other than zero are allowed.

\begin{theorem}[Rigid collapse of infinite coprime torsion symmetry: forcing non-Perron spectrum to vanish at infinitely many coprime symmetric orders]\label{thm:pom-infinite-coprime-torsion-symmetry-rankone}
Let \(F(t)=\prod_{k=2}^{d}(1-\lambda_k t)\) be the non-Perron characteristic polynomial of a primitive kernel (or its Perron-normalized channel block), where \(|\lambda_k|<1\).
Write
\[
-\log F(t)=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{u_n}{n}t^n,
\qquad
u_n:=\sum_{k=2}^{d}\lambda_k^n.
\]
For an integer \(q\ge 2\), we say \textbf{mod \(q\) torsion symmetry} holds if there exists an analytic function \(G_q\) such that
\[
\boxed{\;F(t)=G_q(t^q)\;}
\]
(equivalently: the power series of \(-\log F(t)\) contains only terms with powers divisible by \(q\), i.e., \(u_n=0\) whenever \(q\nmid n\)).
\par
If there exists an infinite set \(\mathcal Q\subseteq\{2,3,\dots\}\) whose elements are pairwise coprime, and for each \(q\in\mathcal Q\) the mod \(q\) torsion symmetry holds,
then we must have
\[
u_n=0\quad(\forall n\ge 1),
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
F(t)\equiv 1,\ \lambda_k=0\ (k\ge 2).
\]
Equivalently, the kernel degenerates spectrally to Perron line \(\oplus\) zero spectrum (rank-one spectral degeneration).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the coefficient characterization of ``mod \(q\) torsion symmetry,'' for each \(q\in\mathcal Q\) we have \(u_n=0\) whenever \(q\nmid n\).
For any fixed \(n\ge 1\), only finitely many \(q\) can divide \(n\); since \(\mathcal Q\) is infinite and pairwise coprime, we can always find some \(q\in\mathcal Q\) with \(q\nmid n\), forcing \(u_n=0\).
Thus \(u_n\equiv 0\), hence \(-\log F(t)\equiv 0\), i.e., \(F(t)\equiv 1\), from which all \(\lambda_k=0\). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Coprime multiplicative closure of finite torsion symmetry: two coprime symmetries simultaneously \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} product symmetry]\label{cor:pom-torsion-symmetry-coprime-closure}
Using the notation of Theorem \ref{thm:pom-infinite-coprime-torsion-symmetry-rankone}.
If both mod \(q_1\) and mod \(q_2\) torsion symmetries hold, and \(\gcd(q_1,q_2)=1\), then mod \(q_1q_2\) torsion symmetry also holds;
equivalently, there exists an analytic function \(H\) such that
\[
F(t)=H\!\left(t^{q_1q_2}\right).
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Mod \(q_i\) torsion symmetry implies \(u_n=0\) whenever \(q_i\nmid n\).
If both hold and \(\gcd(q_1,q_2)=1\), then \(u_n\) can only be nonzero when \(q_1q_2\mid n\).
Hence the power series of \(-\log F(t)\) contains only terms with powers divisible by \(q_1q_2\), so there exists an analytic \(H\) such that \(F(t)=H(t^{q_1q_2})\).
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Observable necessity condition on the character side: torsion symmetry \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} all nonprincipal character coefficients vanish]\label{rem:pom-torsion-symmetry-implies-no-nonprincipal-character}
If mod \(q\) torsion symmetry holds, i.e., \(F(t)=G_q(t^q)\), then for any \(a\in(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times\) we have
\(
F(\omega_q^{\,a})=G_q(1)
\)
as a constant.
Therefore, the twisted point logarithmic table \(g_q(a):=-\log F(\omega_q^{\,a})\) is constant on the unit group, and for any nontrivial Dirichlet character \(\chi\neq\mathbf 1\) we have
\[
\sum_{a\in(\ZZ/q\ZZ)^\times}\chi(a)\,g_q(a)=0.
\]
In other words, strong torsion symmetry necessarily causes the root-of-unity point table not to activate any nonprincipal channel in the character decomposition.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Computable shadow: character channelization and \texorpdfstring{$(\mathrm{RMOML})$}{RMOML}]\label{rem:pom-wreath-artin-computable-shadow}
The irreducible channel decomposition of Corollary \ref{cor:pom-wreath-artin-factorization} requires a complete representation classification for a general wreath product \(G\wr S_q\).
In the executable regime where \(G\) is a finite abelian group and only one-dimensional character channels are tracked,
the \((\mathrm{RMOML})\) rule given by Proposition \ref{prop:pom-rmoml-sound} and Remark \ref{rem:pom-wreath-shadow-rmoml} can be viewed as a computable shadow of this Artin decomposition:
it compresses the required twisted block readouts onto an enumerable character family, thereby forming an auditable ``label--spectral'' channel.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Schur--cycle index tomography: explicit recovery of nontrivial Schur channels from low-order collision moments}
Following the \(q\)-parallel moment semantics, the factor permutation group \(S_q\) naturally acts on the unquotiented \(q\)-parallel kernel; in the minimal realization the corresponding moment kernel commutes with this action,
so one can perform an equivariant Schur decomposition with respect to \(S_q\) and explicitly recover the spectral fingerprint of ``nontrivial permutation channels'' as character-weighted combinations of low-order collision moments.

\begin{theorem}[Character-weighted Schur tomography formula]\label{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography}
Fix an integer \(q\ge 2\).
Suppose that some unquotiented \(q\)-parallel moment-kernel realization (one may take a minimized realization of the tensorized moment-kernel \(A_q^{\mathrm{ten}}\)) carries a linear \(S_q\)-action that commutes with the moment kernel,
so there exists an \(S_q\)-equivariant decomposition
\[
A_q\ \cong\ \bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash q}\bigl(B_{q,\lambda}\otimes I_{f^\lambda}\bigr),
\qquad f^\lambda:=\dim S^\lambda,
\]
where \(\lambda\) runs over partitions of \(q\) and \(S^\lambda\) is the corresponding Specht module.
For any \(m\ge 1\) define the channel trace sequence
\[
T_{q,\lambda}(m):=\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(B_{q,\lambda}^{\,m}\right).
\]
On the other hand, for any permutation \(\sigma\in S_q\) write its cycle type as \(1^{c_1}2^{c_2}\cdots\) (where \(c_r\) is the number of cycles of length \(r\)), and define the cycle-type collision polynomial
\[
\mathcal{C}_\sigma(m):=\prod_{r\ge 1} S_r(m)^{\,c_r},
\qquad
S_r(m):=\sum_{x\in X_m} d_m(x)^r.
\]
Then for each partition \(\lambda\vdash q\) the explicit formula holds:
\[
\boxed{\;
T_{q,\lambda}(m)
=
\frac{f^\lambda}{q!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\chi^\lambda(\sigma)\,\mathcal{C}_\sigma(m)\; },
\]
where \(\chi^\lambda\) is the character of the Specht module \(S^\lambda\).
Moreover, the sum depends only on cycle type, so it can be compressed to a sum over conjugacy classes, i.e., partitions of \(q\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\rho_\lambda\) be the representation of \(S^\lambda\); then the central idempotent
\(
e_\lambda=\frac{f^\lambda}{q!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\chi^\lambda(\sigma)\,\sigma
\)
gives the \(\lambda\)-equivariant projection (by character orthogonality).
Since \(A_q\) commutes with \(S_q\), we have \(e_\lambda\) commuting with \(A_q\), and under the equivariant decomposition \(e_\lambda\) acts only on the Specht factor, so
\[
\mathrm{Tr}(e_\lambda A_q^{\,m})=\mathrm{Tr}(B_{q,\lambda}^{\,m})\,\mathrm{Tr}(\mathrm{Id}_{S^\lambda})
=f^\lambda\,T_{q,\lambda}(m).
\]
On the other hand,
\[
\mathrm{Tr}(e_\lambda A_q^{\,m})
=\frac{f^\lambda}{q!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_q}\chi^\lambda(\sigma)\,\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma A_q^{\,m}).
\]
Finally, for the unquotiented \(q\)-parallel kernel, \(\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma A_q^{\,m})\) depends only on the cycle type of \(\sigma\):
a cycle of length \(r\) constrains \(q\) parallel copies to \(r\) identified ones, contributing a power-sum moment \(S_r(m)\); cycles are mutually independent, yielding the product
\(
\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma A_q^{\,m})=\mathcal{C}_\sigma(m)
\).
Substituting back and canceling \(f^\lambda\) yields the formula. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Schur spectral auditing without explicit compilation of high-dimensional \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{MOM}_q$}{MOMq} kernels]\label{cor:pom-schur-spectrum-audit-from-low-moments}
For fixed \(q\ge 2\), all Schur channel trace sequences \(\{T_{q,\lambda}(m)\}_{m\ge 1,\ \lambda\vdash q}\)
are explicitly computed from the finite set \(\{S_r(m)\}_{1\le r\le q,\ m\ge 1}\) through one character-weighting pass (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography}).
Therefore, any auditing quantity that depends only on channel traces/characteristic polynomials (such as zero-pole structures of channel \(\zeta\)-functions, Schur envelope radii, and channel reduced determinant constants)
can be directly recovered from low-order collision moments without constructing the high-dimensional parallel automaton.
\end{corollary}

\begin{theorem}[Schur--Chebotarev: the prime-shadow deviation of nontrivial Schur channels is locked by character tomography]\label{thm:pom-schur-chebotarev-prime-deviation}
Fix an integer \(q\ge2\), and use the Schur decomposition of Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography}
\(
A_q\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash q}(B_{q,\lambda}\otimes I_{f^\lambda})
\).
For each channel, define the primitive orbit count
\[
p_{n,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{m\mid n}\mu(m)\,\Tr\!\bigl(B_{q,\lambda}^{\,n/m}\bigr),
\qquad
\Pi_\lambda(N):=\sum_{n\le N}p_{n,\lambda}.
\]
Let \(\rho_q:=\rho(B_{q,(q)})\) be the Perron root of the trivial channel, and let
\[
\Lambda_{q,\lambda}:=
\begin{cases}
\max\{|\mu|:\ \mu\in\mathrm{spec}(B_{q,(q)}),\ \mu\neq \rho_q\}, & \lambda=(q),\\[2pt]
\rho(B_{q,\lambda}), & \lambda\neq(q).
\end{cases}
\]
Then for each \(\lambda\vdash q\) there exists a constant \(C_{q,\lambda}>0\) such that for all \(N\ge2\)
\[
\boxed{\;
\Pi_\lambda(N)
=\mathbf 1_{\{\lambda=(q)\}}\sum_{n\le N}\frac{\rho_q^{\,n}}{n}
+O\!\left(\frac{C_{q,\lambda}\,\Lambda_{q,\lambda}^{\,N}}{N}\right)\; }.
\]
Moreover, the exponential rate \(\Lambda_{q,\lambda}\) in the error term can in principle be mutually recovered with the channel trace sequence \(\{T_{q,\lambda}(m)\}_{m\ge1}\) (and hence with \(\{S_r(m)\}_{1\le r\le q}\)) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-moment-genfunc-jordan} and Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography}).
Therefore, the prime-shadow count of a nontrivial Schur channel is essentially a ``pure error term,'' whose exponential rate is completely determined by \(\rho(B_{q,\lambda})\); in particular, the near-RH overcritical event
\(
\rho(B_{q,\lambda})>\sqrt{\rho_q}
\)
is equivalent to a nontrivial channel's prime-shadow deviation crossing the square-root threshold.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By the finite-dimensional explicit formula template (Remark \ref{prop:finite-dimensional-explicit-formula}), \(p_{n,\lambda}\) is a Möbius linear combination of \(\Tr(B_{q,\lambda}^n)\).
When \(\lambda=(q)\), \(B_{q,(q)}\) is a primitive nonnegative channel block, so
\(
\Tr(B_{q,(q)}^n)=\rho_q^{\,n}+O(\Lambda_{q,(q)}^{\,n})
\)
and from \(p_{n,(q)}=\rho_q^{\,n}/n+O(\Lambda_{q,(q)}^{\,n}/n)\) we obtain the cumulative form.
When \(\lambda\neq(q)\), the spectral radius of \(B_{q,\lambda}\) is \(\rho(B_{q,\lambda})=\Lambda_{q,\lambda}\), so \(|\Tr(B_{q,\lambda}^n)|\ll \Lambda_{q,\lambda}^{\,n}\) and yields \(|p_{n,\lambda}|\ll \Lambda_{q,\lambda}^{\,n}/n\) with the stated bound.
The final statement on ``recovering channel prime-shadow from low-order collision moment inputs'' follows from Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography} (character-weighted explicit formula for traces) and Proposition \ref{prop:pom-moment-genfunc-jordan} (finite-order recurrence/spectral envelope can be verified and recovered from finite data). \qed
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Verifiable separation of freezing entropy and Schur envelope}
The entropy rate \(h_\ast\) of max-fiber achievers (Definition \ref{def:pom-max-fiber-entropy-rate}) characterizes the zero-temperature intercept and the exponential complexity of the max-set (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-zero-temperature-two-term-expansion-maxfiber-entropy}),
while nontrivial Schur channels of \(S_q\) control the ``peripheral spectrum of permutation channels.''
To align both types of certificates within the same notation system, we use
\(\Lambda_q^{\mathrm{Schur}}\) (nontrivial Schur envelope radius) and \(\Delta_q\) (square-root defect) as introduced in Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-variance-growth-rate-spectral-identity}.

\begin{proposition}[Freezing certificate and near-RH certificate do not imply each other]\label{prop:pom-freezing-vs-schur-envelope-separation}
Under the above setting, the ``max-fiber entropy'' certificate \(h_\ast>0\) and the ``Schur envelope overcritical'' certificate \(\Delta_q>0\) belong to two essentially different auditing axes:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(h_\ast\) is controlled by the zero-temperature intercept as \(q\to\infty\) (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-zero-temperature-two-term-expansion-maxfiber-entropy}), and can be machine-verified by \(\rho(A^{\max})\) when the max-set is a finite-type subshift (Corollary \ref{cor:pom-max-fiber-subshift-spectral-entropy}).
  \item For each fixed \(q\), \(\Delta_q\) depends only on power-sum moment data of order \(\le q\): by Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography},
  all nontrivial channel trace sequences (and hence their spectral envelopes) can be explicitly recovered from \(\{S_r(m)\}_{1\le r\le q}\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore, in the absence of an additional coupling law ``max-set structure \(\Rightarrow\) nontrivial channel spectral gap,''
any attempt to deduce one class of certificate from the other must explicitly invoke extra constraints;
at the auditable protocol level, the two should be regarded as independent variables requiring separate verification.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Bidirectional reversible compilation across resolutions and moment orders}
Juxtaposing the Prony--Hankel reconstruction at fixed \(m\) (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-spectrum-prony-hankel-2r-reconstruction})
with the Schur tomography at fixed \(q\) (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography}), we obtain a bidirectional compilation bridge fully algebraized within a finite moment-order window.

\begin{theorem}[Bidirectional reversibility of fiber spectrum and Schur spectrum (finite moment-order window)]\label{thm:pom-fiber-schur-bidirectional-compilation}
Given an integer \(Q\ge 2\), and for all resolutions \(m\ge 1\) the collision moment data \(\{S_q(m)\}_{1\le q\le Q}\).
Let \(r_m:=|\mathcal{D}_m|\) be the number of distinct values in the fiber spectrum at layer \(m\) (see \S\ref{subsubsec:pom-moment-hankel-positivity-crossq}).
Under the following two auditable conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Fiber-side reversibility)} For each \(m\) we have \(Q\ge 2r_m-1\);
  \item \textbf{(Schur-side reversibility)} For each \(q\le Q\) the parallel kernel satisfies the \(S_q\)-equivariant realization assumption of Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography};
\end{enumerate}
the data \(\{S_q(m)\}_{1\le q\le Q,\ m\ge 1}\) simultaneously determines:
\[
\text{(Fiber side)}\ \{(a_i(m),n_i(m))\}_{i=1}^{r_m}\ \text{for each }m;\qquad
\text{(Schur side)}\ \{T_{q,\lambda}(m)\}_{m\ge 1,\ \lambda\vdash q}\ \text{for each }q\le Q.
\]
Thus, within a finite moment-order window, \(\{S_q(m)\}\) is both a ``fiber-spectrum tomographic scan'' and a ``Schur-spectrum tomographic scan,'' and there exists a fully reversible pure-algebraic compilation bridge between the two ends.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[``Information completeness'' of a single moment order: when \(q\) is large enough, Schur traces determine the complete fiber spectrum progressively in \(m\)]\label{thm:pom-single-q-schur-traces-complete-fiber-spectrum}
Fix a resolution \(m\), and write the value set of fiber multiplicity as
\[
\mathcal{D}_m=\{a_1<\cdots<a_{r_m}\}\subset\NN,
\qquad
n_i:=\#\{x\in X_m:\ d_m(x)=a_i\}.
\]
If given an integer \(q\ge 2r_m-1\), and the full channel trace vector at this \(q\)
\(
\mathbf T_q(m)=(T_{q,\lambda}(m))_{\lambda\vdash q}
\),
then one can uniquely recover \(\{(a_i,n_i)\}_{i=1}^{r_m}\), and hence uniquely recover the pushforward distribution
\(
w_m(x)=d_m(x)/2^m
\)
with its complete histogram structure (including multiplicities) on \(X_m\).
Moreover, this threshold is in general sharp: if only single-order Schur trace data at \(q\le 2r_m-2\) is available, uniqueness recovery cannot be guaranteed without additional structural assumptions (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-spectrum-prony-hankel-threshold-sharp}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Corollary \ref{cor:pom-schur-traces-determine-low-moments}, \(\mathbf T_q(m)\) (together with the known \(S_1(m)=2^m\)) can explicitly recover all power-sum moments \(\{S_k(m)\}_{1\le k\le q}\).
Also \(S_0(m)=|X_m|\) is uniquely determined by resolution \(m\) under the Fold protocol, so when \(q\ge 2r_m-1\) we obtain the moment sequence \(\{S_k(m)\}_{k=0}^{2r_m-1}\).
By the Prony--Hankel complete reconstruction theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-spectrum-prony-hankel-2r-reconstruction}, this finite moment sequence uniquely determines all atoms \(\{a_i\}\) and their multiplicities \(\{n_i\}\), yielding the stated fiber spectrum and pushforward histogram.
The sharpness of the threshold follows from Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-spectrum-prony-hankel-threshold-sharp}. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Twisted point--Schur dual microscope: single-layer twisted point table determines channel reduced constants]\label{thm:pom-torsion-schur-dual-microscope}
Fix an integer \(q\ge2\), and use the Schur decomposition
\(
A_q\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda\vdash q}(B_{q,\lambda}\otimes I_{f^\lambda})
\).
Write \(\rho_q:=\rho(B_{q,(q)})\) for the Perron root of the trivial channel, and assume \(\rho_q\) appears only in the trivial channel (i.e., for all \(\lambda\neq(q)\) we have \(\rho(B_{q,\lambda})<\rho_q\)).
For \(\lambda\vdash q\), define the channel reduced constant
\[
\mathcal{M}_{q,\lambda}
:=
\det\!\left(I-\frac{B_{q,\lambda}}{\rho_q}\right)^{-1}\qquad(\lambda\neq(q)),
\qquad
\mathcal{M}_{q,(q)}
:=
\det\nolimits'\!\left(I-\frac{B_{q,(q)}}{\rho_q}\right)^{-1}.
\]
Also define the single-layer twisted point value table (\(j=1,\dots,q-1\))
\[
\mathcal{T}_q(j)
:=
\zeta_{A_q}\!\left(\frac{\omega_q^j}{\rho_q}\right)
=
\det\!\Bigl(I-\frac{\omega_q^{\,j}}{\rho_q}A_q\Bigr)^{-1}.
\]
Then under the above assumptions, the data \(\{\mathcal{T}_q(j)\}_{j=1}^{q-1}\) together with \(\mathcal{M}_{q,(q)}\) jointly determine all nontrivial channel constants \(\{\mathcal{M}_{q,\lambda}\}_{\lambda\neq(q)}\):
there exists an explicit finite-algebraic elimination procedure (involving only finitely many character-weighting and finitely many determinant/coefficient operations), which can decompose the twisted point value table into factors of each Schur sector and recover \(\mathcal{M}_{q,\lambda}\) sector-by-sector.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Schur decomposition and multiplicativity of the determinant,
\[
\zeta_{A_q}(z)
=\prod_{\lambda\vdash q}\det(I-zB_{q,\lambda})^{-f^\lambda}.
\]
At \(z=\omega_q^j/\rho_q\ (j\neq0)\) each channel is analytic (by \(|\omega_q^j|=1\) and assumption \(\rho(B_{q,\lambda})<\rho_q\)), yielding a channel product decomposition of \(\mathcal{T}_q(j)\).
The simple pole of the trivial channel at \(z=1/\rho_q\) is reduced by \(\det'\) to a finite constant \(\mathcal{M}_{q,(q)}\), while the values of the remaining channels at that point are precisely \(\mathcal{M}_{q,\lambda}\).
Thus, once \(\{\mathcal{T}_q(j)\}_{j=1}^{q-1}\) is viewed as sampling channel factors at a set of root-of-unity points, combined with the channel decomposability provided by character-weighted Schur tomography (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-schur-cycle-index-tomography}), one can perform algebraic elimination on the channel factors in finitely many steps and recover \(\mathcal{M}_{q,\lambda}\).
\qed
\end{proof}
