\section{Closed field equations: from variation to Einstein gravity with information stress}
\label{sec:field_equations}

\subsection{Metric variation: information stress tensor and Einstein equation}
\label{subsec:metric_variation}

Varying the action \eqref{eq:omega_action} with respect to the metric yields the Einstein equation sourced by matter plus an \emph{information stress tensor}:
\begin{equation}
  G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}
  = 8\pi G\left(T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu} + T^{\mathrm{info}}_{\mu\nu}\right),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
  T^{\mathrm{info}}_{\mu\nu}
  = 2\lambda_{F}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla\chi)^{2}\right)
  - g_{\mu\nu} V(\chi^{2}),
  \qquad
  (\nabla\chi)^2 \equiv g^{\rho\sigma}\nabla_{\rho}\chi\,\nabla_{\sigma}\chi.
\end{equation}
Thus, once the Fisher-amplitude sector is accepted as the minimal covariant quadratic form encoding readout distinguishability, the tensorial structure of macroscopic gravity is fixed; mismatch and implementation costs enter through $T^{\mathrm{info}}_{\mu\nu}$ and the model-dependent $V(\chi^2)$.

\subsection{Information-field variation: amplitude equation and conserved flow}
\label{subsec:chi_variation}

Varying with respect to $\chi$ gives
\begin{equation}
  2\lambda_{F}\,\square \chi - \frac{\dd V}{\dd \chi} = 0,
  \qquad
  \frac{\dd V}{\dd \chi} = 2\chi\,\frac{\dd V}{\dd \varrho},
  \qquad
  \varrho=\chi^{2}.
\end{equation}
By diffeomorphism invariance, the total stress tensor is covariantly conserved. When the $\chi$ equation holds, the information sector can be treated as separately conserved within the effective theory.

More explicitly, the contracted Bianchi identity $\nabla^{\mu}G_{\mu\nu}=0$ implies
\begin{equation}
  \nabla^{\mu}\left(T^{(m)}_{\mu\nu}+T^{\mathrm{info}}_{\mu\nu}\right)=0.
\end{equation}
If the matter sector is conserved (or if matter exchanges flow with the $\chi$ sector through explicit couplings), then the $\chi$ equation ensures consistency of the split. This is the covariant statement that least-discrepancy closure must respect local conservation laws imposed by diffeomorphism invariance.

It is useful to define a natural ``information current'':
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mu} \equiv \chi \nabla_{\mu}\chi = \frac{1}{2}\nabla_{\mu}\varrho ,
\end{equation}
which captures the simplest covariant flow associated with the density gradient.

\subsection{Covariant closure and uniqueness: why the macroscopic equation must be Einstein}
\label{subsec:uniqueness}

CAP emphasizes that the macroscopic field equation should not be postulated by hand. If the effective continuum limit satisfies:
\begin{itemize}
  \item locality and diffeomorphism invariance,
  \item metric field equations of at most second differential order,
  \item and asymptotic flatness (or a controlled cosmological background),
\end{itemize}
then in four dimensions the only symmetric, divergence-free rank-2 tensor built from the metric and its derivatives up to second order is $G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, up to an overall coupling \cite{Lovelock1971}. (The next Lovelock density is the Gauss--Bonnet term, which is topological in $4$D and does not contribute to local metric field equations.) Therefore the gravitational skeleton is uniquely Einsteinian; CAP's micro-to-macro content lies in the structure of the effective stress tensor (mismatch and cost) and in the sector dictionary that links readout/implementation objects to covariant fields.

This is the central closure mechanism behind CAP: \emph{once the effective description is required to be local, covariant, and second-order, there is no freedom left in the left-hand side of the metric equation}. All microstructural choices---scan slope selection, coding stability, discrepancy penalties, compilation overhead---must appear on the right-hand side, as effective sources and potentials.
