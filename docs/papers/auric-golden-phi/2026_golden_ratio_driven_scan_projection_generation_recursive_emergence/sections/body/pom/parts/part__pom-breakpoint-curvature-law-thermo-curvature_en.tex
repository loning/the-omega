\begin{proposition}[Curvature Law of the Breakpoint Phase Diagram: Optimal Moment Order Jumps Completely Controlled by Discrete Curvature \texorpdfstring{$A_q$}{Aq}]\label{prop:pom-breakpoint-curvature-law-Aq}
For integer \(q\ge 2\), define the secant slope (certificate budget slope)
\[
\beta_q:=\frac{\log r_q-\log 2}{q-1},
\]
and define the Chernoff quantile certificate family (Remark \ref{rem:pom-qstar-phase-diagram-breakpoints})
\[
\gamma_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon):=\beta_q+\frac{1}{m(q-1)}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon},
\qquad
\gamma_m^{\mathrm{cert}}(\varepsilon):=\inf_{q\ge 2}\gamma_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon).
\]
For each \(q\ge 2\), define the discrete curvature increment
\[
\boxed{\;
A_q:=(q-1)\bigl(\log r_{q+1}-\log 2\bigr)-q\bigl(\log r_q-\log 2\bigr).\;}
\]
Then \(q\) and \(q+1\) are simultaneously optimal in the lower envelope (i.e., \(\gamma_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)=\gamma_m^{(q+1)}(\varepsilon)\)) if and only if
\[
\boxed{\;
m=m_{q\to q+1}(\varepsilon):=\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{A_q}.}
\]
Moreover, \(A_q\) admits the equivalent representation
\[
\boxed{\;
A_q=q(q-1)\bigl(\beta_{q+1}-\beta_q\bigr).}
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding \(\gamma_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)=\gamma_m^{(q+1)}(\varepsilon)\) yields
\[
\beta_q+\frac{1}{m(q-1)}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
=
\beta_{q+1}+\frac{1}{mq}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}.
\]
Rearranging gives
\(
\beta_{q+1}-\beta_q=\frac{1}{m\,q(q-1)}\log(1/\varepsilon)
\), hence
\(
m=\log(1/\varepsilon)/\bigl(q(q-1)(\beta_{q+1}-\beta_q)\bigr)
\).
On the other hand, direct simplification from \(\beta_q=\frac{1}{q-1}(\log r_q-\log 2)\) yields
\[
q(q-1)(\beta_{q+1}-\beta_q)
=(q-1)(\log r_{q+1}-\log 2)-q(\log r_q-\log 2)
=A_q,
\]
substituting back gives the breakpoint formula. QED.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Thermodynamic Realization of Breakpoint Curvature: \texorpdfstring{$A_q$}{Aq} as Discrete Curvature Sample of the Analytic Pressure Surface]\label{rem:pom-breakpoint-curvature-thermo-realization}
Under the two-dimensional pressure surface regime \(P(q,u)=\log r_q(u)-q\log|A_{\mathrm{out}}|\) (\S\ref{subsec:pom-pressure-surface-qu}), taking \(u=1\) yields the one-dimensional pressure \(\tau(q)=\log r_q\) with endpoint \(\tau(1)=\log 2\).
Under the real-parameter interpolation regime (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-real-q-pressure-analytic-interpolation}) and the spectral-gap/analytic-perturbation hypothesis, \(\tau\) is real-analytic and convex on open intervals;
moreover, its second derivative can be audited via the escort covariance (Corollary \ref{cor:pom-pressure-derivatives-from-escort}):
\[
\tau''(q)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\,\mathrm{Var}_{w_{m,q}}\!\bigl(\log d_m(X)\bigr)\ \ge\ 0.
\]
Accordingly, one can define in the continuous variable the secant function
\[
\beta(q):=\frac{\tau(q)-\tau(1)}{q-1}\qquad(q>1),
\]
and interpret \(A_q\) in Proposition \ref{prop:pom-breakpoint-curvature-law-Aq} as a discrete curvature sampling of this analytic convex curve:
\[
A_q=q(q-1)\bigl(\beta(q+1)-\beta(q)\bigr).
\]
Thus the breakpoint phase diagram (jump density of the optimal moment order \(q^\star\)) is isomorphic in computable sense to the "thermodynamic curvature" (read from \(\tau''\) or more generally the Hessian components of \(f(q,t)=\log\lambda(q,e^t)\)): both are controlled by the curvature intensity of the same convex surface in the \(q\)-direction, merely read out respectively via discrete differences and continuous covariances.
\end{remark}

\end{remark}
