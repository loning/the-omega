\section{Generalized first law: energy as computational flux}
\label{sec:first_law}

To elevate phase friction from a mismatch measure to a thermodynamic quantity, we need a dictionary relating entropy to energy, temperature, and work. In the scan--projection semantics, energy is not a primitive ``substance''; it is the operational \emph{computational flux} required to (i) perform finite-resolution readout, and (ii) maintain and repair structure against phase friction.

\subsection{Computational temperature as tick rate}

Let $\tau$ denote scan-time (intrinsic step count) and $t$ denote external/operational time. Define the local scan tick rate
\begin{equation}
\nu(x):=\frac{\dd \tau}{\dd t}.
\end{equation}
In the $\Omega$ implementation dictionary, $\nu$ is controlled by routing overhead through a lapse field $\lapse$ (Section~\ref{sec:lapse_thermo}). We define the \emph{computational temperature} by a proportionality
\begin{equation}
T_c(x):=\eta_T\,\nu(x),
\end{equation}
where $\eta_T$ sets units (one may take $\eta_T=1$ in natural units). The operational meaning is direct: faster local ticking forces more scan samples through the same finite-resolution readout channel per unit $t$, increasing mismatch generation rate.

\subsection{Structure information: Zeckendorf-effective bits}

In the golden branch, readout depth is canonically organized by Zeckendorf/Fibonacci coding \cite{Zeckendorf1972}. Let $I$ denote the number of \emph{effective} internal bits (or more generally, effective Ostrowski depth) that can be stably maintained under a given readout-and-control protocol. $I$ measures \emph{structure capacity}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item larger $I$ corresponds to finer internal models and more stable correlations;
  \item smaller $I$ corresponds to coarse macrostates near equilibrium.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{A first-law form for ASM}

\begin{proposition}[Generalized first law (computational flux form)]
\label{prop:first_law}
At fixed readout protocol class (fixed $\varepsilon$ and window family), the externally observed change of computational flux $E$ decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\dd E = T_c\, \dd S_{\mathrm{pf}} + \Xi\, \dd I + \delta W_{\mathrm{geom}}.
\end{equation}
Here $S_{\mathrm{pf}}$ is phase-friction entropy (Section~\ref{sec:asm}), $\Xi$ is the ``chemical potential'' conjugate to stable structure information $I$, and $\delta W_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is geometric work associated with changes in routing overhead / impedance.
\end{proposition}

\noindent Proposition~\ref{prop:first_law} can be read as a bookkeeping identity in the standard thermodynamic sense \cite{LandauLifshitz1980}: for a fixed protocol class, one may treat $E$ as an effective state function $E=E(S_{\mathrm{pf}},I,\text{geom})$ and \emph{define} the conjugate variables by partial derivatives,
\[
  T_c:=\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial S_{\mathrm{pf}}}\right)_{I,\text{geom}},
  \qquad
  \Xi:=\left(\frac{\partial E}{\partial I}\right)_{S_{\mathrm{pf}},\text{geom}},
\]
with $\delta W_{\mathrm{geom}}$ collecting protocol-dependent geometric work terms. The physical content is that finite-resolution readout forces an unavoidable tradeoff between dissipated mismatch and maintainable internal structure.

\subsection{Landauer cost and log-cost additivity}

The log structure of $S_{\mathrm{rd}}=k_B(-\log w)$ (Section~\ref{sec:asm}) matches the multiplicative-to-additive conversion intrinsic to readout. In particular, when independent constraints have success weights $w_1,w_2$, their joint weight is $w_1 w_2$ and the total log-cost is additive.

Landauer's principle provides a lower bound on the energetic cost of erasing one bit at temperature $T$ \cite{Landauer1961,Bennett1973}:
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{erase}} \ge k_B T \ln 2.
\end{equation}
In HPA--$\Omega$, the relevant temperature is the computational temperature $T_c$ and the total cost includes geometric impedance (Appendix~\ref{app:geometric_landauer}). This makes explicit how maintaining low-entropy structure is compatible with the second law: entropy is exported to the environment through an operationally irreversible readout-and-erase channel \cite{Szilard1929,Bennett1989}.
