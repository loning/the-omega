\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Summary}

We have analyzed a quartic spectral-closure model through three complementary lenses:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Algebraic:} the complete discriminant factorization (Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor}) determines the branch locus $\Branch$ exactly, with the nontrivial real branch point $\yLY$ identified as the unique real root of an explicit cubic (Proposition~\ref{prop:unique-real-root}).
At $\yLY$, two subdominant spectral roots collide and transition from real to complex conjugate (Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision}).
\item \textbf{Analytic:} the dominant spectral branch $\lambda_+(y)$ controls the free energy and yields closed-form fluctuation constants $\mu=5/18$, $\sigma^2=67/972$ via the quasi-powers theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:lln-clt}).
\item \textbf{Arithmetic:} the spectral curve is birationally equivalent to the conductor-$37$ elliptic curve (Theorem~\ref{thm:ellipticization}), and the branch locus is its ramification image under a degree-$4$ morphism (Theorem~\ref{thm:ramification-image}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope}

The generating function $Z(t,y)$ is taken as a starting point; a combinatorial or physical derivation is not provided here.
Identifying $Z_m(y)$ as the partition function of a specific lattice model or counting problem would provide additional physical context for $\yLY$ and is an important open question.

\subsection{Open directions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Equimodular curves.} Compute the complete topology of the equimodular locus $\{y\in\CC:\abs{\lambda_i(y)}=\abs{\lambda_j(y)}\}$ in the complex $y$-plane.
Determine the full set of endpoints and the role of the branch points $\{0,1,\yLY,\bar y_1,y_1\}$ (where $\bar y_1, y_1$ are the complex roots of $c(y)$) as termini of these curves.
\item \textbf{Edge singularity.} Determine whether $\yLY$ or another branch point serves as an edge singularity---in the sense of Fisher~\cite{Fisher1978}---for the zero-density measure, and compute the associated critical exponent if it exists.
\item \textbf{Generalization.} Extend the discriminant-factorization program to broader classes of spectral polynomials, aiming at a systematic framework for reduced rational families.
\item \textbf{Arithmetic significance.} Investigate whether the appearance of the conductor-$37$ curve and the discriminant relation $\Disc(c)\propto 37$ reflect a deeper structure connecting spectral-curve arithmetic to zero-distribution geometry.
\end{enumerate}
