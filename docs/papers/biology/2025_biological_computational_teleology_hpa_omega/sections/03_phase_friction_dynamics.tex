\section{From mismatch certificates to entropy production rates and computational thermodynamics}
\label{sec:phase_friction_dynamics}

Section~\ref{sec:readout_phase_friction} defined phase friction over a finite window via discrepancy and accumulated mismatch.
To connect that certificate to biological maintenance costs, we need rate-level quantities.

\subsection{Mismatch density and phase-friction entropy production}
\label{sec:mismatch_density}

Let $\tau$ denote intrinsic scan time (iteration count). In a spatially extended setting, let $x$ denote a location or subsystem label.
Fix a readout phase coordinate $u\in[0,1)$ (constructed from the experimental readout protocol) and a window length $N$.
At external time $t$, let $\{u_{t,1},\dots,u_{t,N}\}$ be the $N$ phase points produced by the protocol over a window ending at $t$, and define the windowed certificates $D_N^\ast(t)$, $E_N(t):=N D_N^\ast(t)$, and
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{pf}}^{(N)}(t) := \kB E_N(t).
  \label{eq:Spf_windowed}
\end{equation}
The phase-friction entropy flow is then defined by a finite-difference rate:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}}{\dd t}(t)
  := \lim_{\Delta t\to 0}\lim_{N\to\infty} \frac{S_{\mathrm{pf}}^{(N)}(t+\Delta t)-S_{\mathrm{pf}}^{(N)}(t)}{\Delta t},
  \label{eq:Spf_rate_def}
\end{equation}
with $N$ and $\Delta t$ chosen by the protocol and then smoothed at the analysis layer.
This expression should be read as an analysis-layer idealization, not as a requirement to take literal limits in empirical work.
Operationally, one fixes a readout uniformization map $y\mapsto u\in[0,1)$, a window length $N$, a step size $\Delta t$, and a smoothing operator, and then works with finite estimators extracted from $E_N(t)$.
All cross-system comparisons are meaningful only under matched protocol choices (same uniformization, $N$, $\Delta t$, and smoothing).
Section~\ref{sec:tests} records concrete data-analysis pipelines and decision criteria built from these finite estimators.
In intrinsic scan time, we define the \emph{mismatch density} (phase-friction entropy production density) as
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(x,\tau)
  := \frac{1}{\kB}\frac{\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}(x;\tau)}{\dd \tau},
  \label{eq:sigma_def}
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}}{\dd\tau} = \kB\,\sigma(x,\tau).
  \label{eq:Spf_rate_tau}
\end{equation}
Operationally, $\sigma$ is a rate extracted from the windowed mismatch certificate \eqref{eq:Spf_windowed} via \eqref{eq:Spf_rate_def}; it is not a phenomenological noise parameter.

\subsection{Computational lapse and externally observed entropy flow}
\label{sec:lapse_rescaling}

HPA--$\Omega$ introduces a \emph{computational lapse} $\lapse(x)$ determined by local routing overhead $\kappa(x)$ in the implementation dictionary \cite{Ma2025CAP,Ma2025FoP,Ma2025CT}.
Relative to a reference overhead $\kappa_0$, define
\begin{equation}
  \lapse(x) := \frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(x)}.
  \label{eq:lapse_def}
\end{equation}
Operationally, $\lapse$ is the protocol-level rescaling between external time $t$ and local scan time $\tau$:
\begin{equation}
  \dd \tau_{\mathrm{loc}}(x) = \lapse(x)\,\dd t.
  \label{eq:tau_lapse}
\end{equation}
Combining \eqref{eq:Spf_rate_tau} with \eqref{eq:tau_lapse} yields the externally observed entropy-flow law
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}}{\dd t}
  = \frac{\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}}{\dd\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}}\frac{\dd\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}}{\dd t}
  = \kB\,\sigma(x,\tau)\,\lapse(x).
  \label{eq:Spf_rate_t}
\end{equation}
It is convenient to introduce the external-time mismatch rate
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma(x,t) := \sigma(x,\tau)\,\lapse(x),
  \label{eq:Sigma_def}
\end{equation}
so that $\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}/\dd t = \kB\,\Sigma(x,t)$.
For biological systems, Eq.~\eqref{eq:Spf_rate_t} supplies a unifying interface: different tissues and environments can exhibit different effective computational lapse profiles (estimated from routing/transport/control overhead proxies), which rescale the entropy flow observed per unit external time.

\subsection{Computational temperature and the cost of control}
\label{sec:computational_temperature}

In information thermodynamics, temperature sets the energetic scale of information processing costs and feedback advantages \cite{Landauer1961,SagawaUeda2010}.
HPA--$\Omega$ introduces an effective \emph{computational temperature} $T_c$ associated to the readout/implementation interface \cite{Ma2025HPT}.
Operationally, $T_c$ is the coefficient that converts erased information into a minimal work/heat scale at the readout interface (Landauer scale): erasing $\Delta I$ nats costs at least $\kB T_c\,\Delta I$ (up to additional geometric impedance), and erasing one bit costs at least $\kB T_c\ln 2$.
This $T_c$ need not equal a physical bath temperature; rather, it is the operational temperature relevant for control and erasure at the interface.
In the next sections we use $T_c$ to formulate necessary inequalities for predictive control to offset phase-friction dissipation.
