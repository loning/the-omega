\section{Local Finite-Dimensional Hilbert Spaces}
\label{chap:local_hilbert}

\subsection{Decomposition of the Global State}
Can the global Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ be decomposed? In standard QFT, spacetime is a continuum, and the Hilbert space is a Fock space over continuous fields. This leads to the ultraviolet divergence problem. Omega Theory takes the opposite view: the continuum is an approximation.
We motivate a regulated localization structure in which the operational state space of a causal region admits an effective finite-dimensional description consistent with holographic entropy bounds (see Theorem \ref{thm:finite_information_dimension} in Appendix \ref{app:formal_proofs}). In the microscopic/QCA description this is realized as a local tensor-factor model together with a holographically constrained code subspace:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H} \cong \bigotimes_{x \in \Lambda} \mathcal{H}_x, \quad \dim \mathcal{H}_x = d < \infty.
\end{equation}
Physically, $x$ represents a "cell" or "atom of space," and $\mathcal{H}_x$ represents its quantum state capacity. The simplest non-trivial case is $d=2$ (qubits). To accommodate Standard-Model-like internal quantum numbers in the later model constructions, we will consider higher local dimensions; octonionic/$G_2$-structured organization provides one candidate mechanism for such an internal sector (Part~IV).

\subsection{The Problem of Isotropy on Lattices}
A regular lattice (e.g., hypercubic $\mathbb{Z}^3$) breaks rotational symmetry. At the Planck scale, this would manifest as anisotropic dispersion relations for high-energy particles. Since we observe Lorentz invariance to very high precision, any viable discrete substrate should be approximately/statistically isotropic in its long-wavelength behavior.
To remain compatible with these constraints, we impose statistical isotropy as a model assumption (Assumption~\ref{assump:M1}).
\begin{lemma}[Icosahedral directions form a spherical design (low-rank isotropy)]
\label{lemma:icosa_design}
The $12$ vertex directions of a regular icosahedron form a spherical $5$-design on $S^2$: for any polynomial $p$ on $\mathbb{R}^3$ of total degree $\le 5$, the average of $p$ over these $12$ unit vectors equals the average of $p$ over the unit sphere with respect to the normalized Haar measure.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
See \cite{DelsarteGoethalsSeidel1977}. As a consequence, all moment tensors up to rank $5$ obtained by averaging over these directions coincide with the corresponding $SO(3)$-invariant (spherical) averages.
\end{proof}
Equivalently, the associated discrete empirical measure has vanishing spherical multipoles for $1\le \ell\le 5$ (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:design_suppresses_lowell}). In particular, direction-averaged plane-wave factors $\frac{1}{12}\sum_{i=1}^{12}e^{i\,q\cdot \hat u_i}$ match the isotropic spherical average $\sin|q|/|q|$ through fifth order in $|q|$ (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:design_plane_wave_isotropy}), so anisotropy is pushed to higher order in long-wavelength expansions.
This provides a concrete sense in which icosahedral symmetry suppresses low-rank anisotropies: in a long-wavelength expansion, the leading anisotropic contributions are constrained to lie in higher spherical-harmonic sectors rather than appearing already at the lowest derivative orders.
This motivates the use of \textbf{Quasicrystals}. The Penrose tiling and its higher-dimensional analogs (i.e.\ cut-and-project sets obtained by projecting a higher-dimensional lattice) provide discrete point sets that possess long-range order and crystallographically forbidden rotational symmetries (e.g., 5-fold or icosahedral).
We define the \textbf{Omega Network} $\Lambda$ as a subset of points in $\mathbb{R}^3$ generated by the cut-and-project method from a higher-dimensional integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^N$ (where $N=6$ or $12$ for icosahedral symmetry). The projection is defined by a window $W$ in the orthogonal space $E_\perp$:
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda = \{ P_{||} \vec{n} \mid \vec{n} \in \mathbb{Z}^N, P_{\perp} \vec{n} \in W \}.
\end{equation}
This construction provides a natural candidate substrate whose large-scale statistics can approximate rotational invariance. The precise dynamical condition used in this manuscript is encoded in Assumption~\ref{assump:M1} at the level of the long-wavelength propagator/dispersion.

\subsection{Relation to Tensor Networks (MERA)}
The recursive construction of the Omega Network constitutes the \textbf{Golden MERA} realization (Assumption~\ref{assump:M3}) of the holographic encoding map $\Phi$ postulated in Axiom~O4 (see Theorem \ref{thm:golden_isometry} in Appendix \ref{app:formal_proofs} for an existence argument). This structure shares deep structural isomorphisms with Multi-scale Entanglement Renormalization Ansatz (MERA) networks used in efficient quantum simulations and AdS/CFT holography.

The links in our quasicrystal $\Lambda$ can be viewed as entanglement bonds. The Golden Ratio scaling ($\tau \to \phi \tau$) mimics the renormalization group flow in MERA, where coarse-graining the lattice preserves the long-range entanglement structure.
We propose that the effective bond dimension $\chi$ of the spacetime tensor network is related to the topological complexity of the $G_2$ fiber:
\begin{equation}
    S(\rho_A)\ \le\ |\gamma_{\min}(A)|\,\ln\chi,
\end{equation}
for boundary regions $A$, where $\gamma_{\min}(A)$ is a minimal cut separating $A$ from $A^c$ and $\chi$ is the bond dimension on internal edges (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}, Theorem~\ref{thm:mincut_entropy}). In holographic normalization one calibrates the discrete area element by matching $|\gamma_{\min}(A)|\ln\chi$ to $\mathrm{Area}(\gamma_A)/(4l_P^2)$ in the appropriate large-scale limit. This is consistent with Axiom~O4: a holographic encoding architecture enforces an area-law \emph{upper bound} on boundary entanglement and supports an error-correction interpretation of bulk locality (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}). In this tensor-network picture, curvature can be modeled as being encoded by the density/distribution of disentanglers in the network.

\section{Quantum Cellular Automata (QCA) as Fundamental Dynamics}
\label{chap:qca}

\subsection{Definition of QCA on a Quasicrystal}
A Quantum Cellular Automaton is a unitary evolution $\hat{U}$ on the composite Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = \bigotimes_{x \in \Lambda} \mathcal{H}_x$. Following the operational framework of D'Ariano, Perinotti, and Bisio \cite{Dariano2014}, we require:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unitarity}: $\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{U} = \hat{I}$ (Conservation of probability).
    \item \textbf{Causal Locality}: There exists a neighborhood scheme $\mathcal{N}$ such that the state of cell $x$ at step $t+1$ depends only on cells $y \in \mathcal{N}(x)$ at step $t$. Formally, if $\hat{A}_x$ is an observable at $x$, then $\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{A}_x \hat{U}$ is supported on $\mathcal{N}(x)$.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Isotropy (Assumption~\ref{assump:M1})}: While the lattice $\Lambda$ is discrete, we assume that mesoscopic neighbor-direction statistics are approximately rotationally invariant, so that long-wavelength propagators admit an approximately $SO(3)$-invariant expansion (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}).
\end{enumerate}

The global evolution is built from local "coin" and "shift" operators (or, equivalently, a finite-depth sequence of partitioned local updates). On the cut-and-project lattice $\Lambda$, we define the explicit update rule as a \textbf{Partitioned Quantum Cellular Automaton} (PQCA) in the standard circuit form: fix a constant depth $D\ge 1$, and for each layer $s=1,\dots,D$ choose a partition of the vertex set into disjoint neighborhoods
\begin{equation}
    V=\bigsqcup_k \mathcal{N}_k^{(s)},
\end{equation}
with uniformly bounded neighborhood size $\sup_{s,k}|\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}|<\infty$. Define the layer unitary
\begin{equation}
    \hat U^{(s)}\ \equiv\ \prod_k \hat U_{\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}}(\theta,\phi),
\end{equation}
where each $\hat U_{\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}}$ acts on the Hilbert space $\bigotimes_{x\in \mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}}\mathcal{H}_x$. Within a fixed layer the factors commute because their supports are disjoint, while different layers generally do not commute. One PQCA step is then the finite-depth circuit
\begin{equation}
    \hat U \ \equiv\ \hat U^{(D)}\cdots \hat U^{(1)}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Exact 1D Tape Realization and Routing Depth}
\label{sec:routeA_routing}
This construction provides an exact realization of a bulk-local PQCA step on an effectively one-dimensional nearest-neighbor computational substrate. This makes it possible to define a local \emph{routing overhead} as an intrinsic compilation cost.

Let $R \subset \Lambda$ be a finite patch with $|R|=N$. For each layer we define the restricted update by keeping only those neighborhood unitaries fully supported inside $R$ (dropping terms that cross the boundary), so that the restricted one-step update on $R$ is
\begin{equation}
    \hat U_R\ \equiv\ \hat U_R^{(D)}\cdots \hat U_R^{(1)},\qquad
    \hat U_R^{(s)}\equiv \prod_{k:\,\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)} \subset R} \hat U_{\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Tape ordering]
\label{def:tape_ordering}
For any finite patch $R \subset \Lambda$ with $|R|=N$, choose an ordering (a bijection)
\begin{equation}
    f_R: R \to \{1,\dots,N\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Tape encoding isometry]
\label{def:tape_encoding_isometry}
Define an isometric relabeling map
\begin{equation}
    E_R:\bigotimes_{x\in R}\mathcal H_x\to\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal H_i,
\end{equation}
by its action on computational basis product states:
\begin{equation}
    E_R\!\Big(\bigotimes_{x\in R}|s_x\rangle\Big)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\big|s_{f_R^{-1}(i)}\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

For each neighborhood block $\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)} \subset R$, define its tape span
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_k^{(s)} \equiv \max_{x\in \mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}} f_R(x)-\min_{x\in \mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}} f_R(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact 1D compilation]
\label{prop:exact_1d_compilation}
There exists a 1D nearest-neighbor circuit $\mathcal C_R$ such that
\begin{equation}
    \hat{U}_R = E_R^\dagger \, \mathcal C_R \, E_R,
\end{equation}
with compilation depth bounded by
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{depth}(\mathcal C_R)\le C\cdot D\cdot \max_{s,k}\big(\Delta_k^{(s)}+1\big),
\end{equation}
where the constant $C$ depends only on an upper bound on the neighborhood size $m\equiv \sup_{s,k}|\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}|$ (and the on-site dimension).
\end{proposition}
\textit{Proof sketch.}
Fix an ordering $f_R$ and let $m\equiv \sup_{s,k}|\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}|<\infty$. For each neighborhood block $\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}$, let $I_k^{(s)}\equiv[\min_{x\in\mathcal N_k^{(s)}}f_R(x),\,\max_{x\in\mathcal N_k^{(s)}}f_R(x)]$ be its tape interval, of length $\Delta_k^{(s)}+1$. Since $m$ is bounded, each local unitary $\hat{U}_{\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}}$ admits a fixed-depth nearest-neighbor implementation once its registers occupy a contiguous segment of the 1D tape (the required depth depends only on $m$ and the on-site dimension).\par
We implement each layer $\hat U_R^{(s)}=\prod_k \hat U_{\mathcal N_k^{(s)}}$ by a \emph{gather--execute--scatter} construction. (i) \textbf{Gather}: using a nearest-neighbor SWAP network on the tape, route the registers belonging to each $\mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}$ so that they become contiguous within $I_k^{(s)}$; this requires $\mathcal{O}(\Delta_k^{(s)}+1)$ SWAP layers because no register needs to move more than $\Delta_k^{(s)}$ sites. (ii) \textbf{Execute}: apply the local circuits for all $k$ in parallel (supports are disjoint within a layer). (iii) \textbf{Scatter}: undo the SWAP network. Scheduling this for $s=1,\dots,D$ yields the stated depth bound up to a constant factor, controlled by $\max_{s,k}(\Delta_k^{(s)}+1)$.

\begin{definition}[Local routing cost field]
\label{def:routing_cost_field}
For a fixed tape ordering $f_R$, define the ordering-dependent local routing cost
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_R(x;f_R)\equiv \max_{s,k:\,x\in \mathcal{N}_k^{(s)}}\big(\Delta_k^{(s)}+1\big).
\end{equation}
Since $f_R$ is not a physical datum but a relabeling choice for the 1D substrate, we define the physical routing cost by minimizing over orderings (capturing the best-case routing overhead within the tape-encoding viewpoint):
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_R(x)\equiv \inf_{f_R}\,\kappa_R(x;f_R),
\end{equation}
with $\inf$ replaced by $\min$ when the minimum is attained.
In the coarse-grained limit we write $\kappa(x)$ for the corresponding routing-cost field.
\end{definition}
We will later denote by $\kappa_0>0$ a reference minimal routing cost (e.g., the weak-field baseline), so that $\kappa(x)\ge \kappa_0$ in the regimes where the lapse identification is used.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Coin--shift form and the ``Golden'' texture.}
For the explicit models analyzed in this manuscript we use coined QCA/quantum-walk steps of the form
\begin{equation}
    U = S\,C,
\end{equation}
where $C=\bigoplus_x C_x$ is a strictly on-site unitary acting on a local spinor $\psi_x\in\mathbb{C}^2$ and $S$ is a conditional nearest-neighbor shift (a basis permutation) determined by the adjacency structure of the underlying graph.
A convenient local coin family is the spin rotation
\begin{equation}
    C_x(\theta(x),\hat{n})\equiv e^{-i\theta(x)\,\hat{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}},
\end{equation}
with $\vec{\sigma}$ the Pauli matrices. The defining ``golden'' ingredient of our quasicrystal models is that the coin texture $\theta(x)$ is determined by the cut-and-project internal coordinate $x_\perp$ (or, in 1D reductions, by the Sturmian/Fibonacci coding below). As an analytic toy model one may take a smooth modulation
\begin{equation}
    \theta(x)=\theta_0\left[1+\epsilon\cos\!\left(2\pi\,\vec{k}_\perp\cdot \vec{x}_\perp\right)\right],
\end{equation}
with $0<\epsilon\ll 1$, while the discrete (fully specified) Fibonacci-textured model is given in Section~\ref{sec:fib_dqca_spec}.

\subsubsection{Fibonacci/Sturmian coin textures}
The smooth phason modulation above is a convenient analytic toy model. A canonical discrete quasiperiodic texture is obtained by Sturmian (rotation) coding, which in the golden case yields the Fibonacci word \cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}. In a 1D reduction (or along a chosen lattice geodesic), fix an irrational slope $\alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ (golden case $\alpha=\phi^{-2}$) and an intercept $\rho\in[0,1)$, and define
\begin{equation}
    I_L=[0,1-\alpha),\qquad I_S=[1-\alpha,1),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \sigma(x)=
    \begin{cases}
    L,& \{x\alpha+\rho\}\in I_L,\\
    S,& \{x\alpha+\rho\}\in I_S,
    \end{cases}
\label{eq:sturmian_sigma}
\end{equation}
where $\{\cdot\}$ denotes the fractional part.\par
\noindent\textit{Remark (scan--projection / cut-and-project unification).} Eq.~(\ref{eq:sturmian_sigma}) is a direct instance of the scan--projection template of Axiom~O5: the scan is the rotation by $\alpha$, the readout is window projection onto $\{I_L,I_S\}$, and the intercept $\rho$ is a phason shift (or initial phase). The same window data also define the standard 1D cut-and-project (model set) construction of the Fibonacci chain; Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca} records the explicit embedding and phason interpretation.\par
We then define a piecewise-constant coin (mass/mixing) field by
\begin{equation}
    \theta(x)=
    \begin{cases}
    \theta_L,& \sigma(x)=L,\\
    \theta_S,& \sigma(x)=S.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This produces a Fibonacci-textured (quasiperiodic) Dirac walk that is compatible with the cut-and-project viewpoint: the Fibonacci chain is the standard 1D cut-and-project set with a compact window and hence bounded phason fluctuations.

\subsubsection{A fully specified 1D Fibonacci DQCA (golden texture)}
\label{sec:fib_dqca_spec}
To make the quasicrystal QCA proposal checkable, we now give an explicit 1D model on a Fibonacci (cut-and-project) substrate, including its local gates and the quantitative diagnostics used later for numerical verification.\par
\noindent\textbf{Fibonacci substrate (cut-and-project / Sturmian coding).}
Fix the golden slope $\alpha=\phi^{-2}$ and intercept $\rho\in[0,1)$. Define the tile-type word $\sigma(j)\in\{L,S\}$ by Eq.~(\ref{eq:sturmian_sigma}).
Let the physical coordinate of vertex $j\in\mathbb{Z}$ be
\begin{equation}
    r_{0}=0,\qquad r_{j+1}=r_{j}+\ell_{\sigma(j)},
\end{equation}
with $\ell_L\equiv \phi\,\varepsilon$ and $\ell_S\equiv \varepsilon$ for a microscopic length scale $\varepsilon>0$.
The resulting embedded vertex set $\{r_j\}\subset\mathbb{R}$ is the standard Fibonacci quasicrystal (a 1D cut-and-project set). The intercept $\rho$ implements a global \emph{phason shift}; allowing $\rho$ to vary slowly with $j$ defines a \emph{phason strain field} (Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca}).
\par
\noindent\textbf{Hilbert space and single-particle sector.}
For continuum-limit diagnostics we restrict to the single-excitation (single-particle) sector,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{1p} \equiv \ell^2(\mathbb{Z})\otimes \mathbb{C}^2,
\end{equation}
with basis $\{|j\rangle\otimes|R\rangle,\ |j\rangle\otimes|L\rangle\}$ interpreted as a 1D Dirac spinor at vertex $j$.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Local gates and one-step unitary.}
Define two on-site coin rotations (Long/Short) by
\begin{equation}
    C_L \equiv e^{-i\theta_L\sigma_y},\qquad C_S \equiv e^{-i\theta_S\sigma_y},
\end{equation}
and the position-dependent coin
\begin{equation}
    C \equiv \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} |j\rangle\langle j|\otimes C_{\sigma(j)}.
\end{equation}
Define the conditional shift on the 1D adjacency graph by
\begin{equation}
    S \equiv \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(|j+1\rangle\langle j|\otimes|R\rangle\langle R| + |j-1\rangle\langle j|\otimes|L\rangle\langle L|\Big).
\end{equation}
The one-step evolution is the coined quantum walk
\begin{equation}
    U \equiv S\,C,
\end{equation}
which is manifestly unitary and strictly local (range-1 on the chain). This model is fully specified by the parameters $(\varepsilon,\theta_L,\theta_S,\rho)$.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Why 1D is sufficient for verification.}
Although the Omega Network is higher-dimensional, the exact 1D tape compilation in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact_1d_compilation} shows that any finite patch update generated by bounded-range local gates admits an equivalent nearest-neighbor 1D realization (at the cost of routing depth). Therefore, a 1D Fibonacci-textured DQCA provides a computationally tractable testbed for the continuum-limit diagnostics (dispersion, group velocity, and norm-error scaling) while remaining faithful to the locality structure of the underlying PQCA.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Diagnostics for the Dirac limit.}
To extract dispersion on the aperiodic substrate we use periodic Fibonacci approximants: replace $\alpha$ by the rational approximant $\alpha_n\equiv F_{n-1}/F_n$ and impose period $N\equiv F_n$ on the coin texture. The resulting $N$-cell Floquet operator admits Bloch decomposition $U^{(n)}(k)$ with quasi-momentum $k\in[-\pi,\pi)$, yielding quasienergies $E^{(n)}_\pm(k)$ via eigenphases of $U^{(n)}(k)$.
We compare these to the target Dirac dispersion $E_D(k)=\sqrt{(c_{\mathrm{eff}}k)^2+m_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}$ in the long-wavelength regime, and we quantify errors by, e.g.,
\begin{equation}
    \delta_E^{(n)}(k_{\max})\equiv \max_{|k|\le k_{\max}} \big|E^{(n)}_+(k)-E_D(k)\big|,
\end{equation}
and wavepacket-propagation errors
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{prop}}(T)\equiv \left\|U^T|\psi_0\rangle-e^{-iH_D\,t}|\psi_0\rangle\right\|,\qquad t\equiv T\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
for band-limited initial states $|\psi_0\rangle$ (Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca} specifies the numerical protocol). These diagnostics allow a direct test of convergence and of dependence on phason strain.

\subsection{Emergence of the Dirac Equation}
\noindent\textbf{Standard 1D Dirac QCA.}
To make contact with the canonical 1D Dirac quantum cellular automaton, consider a translation-invariant 1D walk in momentum space with
\begin{equation}
    U(k)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    n\,e^{ik} & -i m\\
    i m & n\,e^{-ik}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    n^2+m^2=1,
\end{equation}
which yields a Dirac dispersion in the long-wavelength limit \cite{Dariano2014,Bisio2015}. In our parametrization one may identify $n=\cos\theta$ and $m=\sin\theta$ (up to conventions), so that the coin angle plays the role of an effective mass/mixing parameter.

It is a standard result in the QW/QCA literature that, in suitable translation-invariant settings, the long-wavelength continuum limit of discrete-time quantum walks yields the Dirac equation; see \cite{Arrighi2014,Dariano2014} (and related constructions in \cite{Bisio2015}).
For the translation-invariant 1D coined walk written above, diagonalization yields eigenphases $\pm E(k)$ satisfying the standard relation $\cos E(k)=n\cos k$ (with $n^2+m^2=1$). In the long-wavelength regime $k=\varepsilon p$ with a small-angle (small-mass) scaling $m=\varepsilon m_{\mathrm{phys}}$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
    E(k)^2=(\varepsilon p)^2+(\varepsilon m_{\mathrm{phys}})^2+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^4 p^4)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^4 p^2 m_{\mathrm{phys}}^2),
\end{equation}
so that the walk reproduces a Dirac dispersion at leading order. The corresponding \emph{operator-norm} convergence statement for band-limited initial states is recorded as Theorem~\ref{prop:dirac_limit} below, with a proof in Appendix~\ref{app:homogenization}.
Recent work on \textbf{quantum simulation of QED} \cite{Arrighi2019} provides explicit constructions in which U(1) gauge invariance is preserved exactly in QCA models by introducing link variables that transform under local unitaries; we use this as motivation for the gauge-field emergence discussion in Part~IV.

\subsubsection{Discrete vs. Emergent Lorentz Covariance}
How is Lorentz symmetry treated on the lattice? Two distinct frameworks exist in the QCA literature:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Discrete Lorentz Symmetry}: As pioneered by \textbf{Bisio, D'Ariano, and Perinotti} \cite{Bisio2015}, one can define a "discrete Lorentz transform" on regular Cayley graphs. This transformation maps the cellular automaton to an equivalent one with modified local rules, preserving causal cones exactly. In this framework, Lorentz covariance is an exact symmetry of the discrete structure ($SO(1,3)_{discrete}$).
    \item \textbf{Emergent Isotropic Symmetry}: The Omega Network approach differs. Since a quasicrystal lacks a global subgroup structure to support exact boosts, we rely on \textbf{emergent symmetry}. The lightcone speed $c=1$ is strict (causal locality), but the continuous Lorentz group $SO(1,3)$ is recovered only via the statistical isotropy of the Golden MERA.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Precision of the Approximation (long-wavelength sector)}: For translation-invariant coined walks, Theorem~\ref{prop:dirac_limit} provides an operator-norm Dirac approximation with error $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ on band-limited states (Appendix~\ref{app:homogenization}). On an aperiodic quasicrystal, exact Bloch diagonalization is unavailable; our working notion of ``approximate Lorentz symmetry'' is therefore that (i) mesoscopic edge-direction statistics are approximately isotropic (Assumption~\ref{assump:M1}), which suppresses low-order anisotropic derivative terms in long-wavelength expansions (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}, Propositions~\ref{prop:design_suppresses_lowell}--\ref{prop:design_plane_wave_isotropy}), and (ii) internal-coordinate (phason) fields vary slowly. We accordingly parametrize the leading deviation from an isotropic continuum Dirac/EFT description by a bookkeeping estimate
\begin{equation}
    \delta \mathcal{L} \sim \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon) + \mathcal{O}(\|\eta\|),
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon$ is the microscopic spacing and $\eta$ denotes a phason-strain measure (e.g.\ the discrete strain $\eta_j$ in Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca} or a bounded internal-displacement gradient in higher-dimensional cut-and-project settings). Thus, while we utilize the local unitary principles of the discrete framework, our global Lorentz symmetry is approximate and improves in the continuum/weak-strain regime.

\subsection{Theorem: The Continuum Dirac Limit (checkable 1D statement)}
While convergence is proven for regular grids, the quasicrystal program requires additional homogenization input. As a fully checkable subsystem statement, we record a rigorous 1D limit for the single-particle coined walk used throughout this manuscript.
\begin{theorem}[Dirac limit for a 1D coined walk (single-particle sector)]
\label{prop:dirac_limit}
Consider the single-particle coined walk on $\mathcal{H}_{1p}=\ell^2(\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{C}^2$ with one-step unitary $U_\varepsilon=S\,C_\varepsilon$, where $S$ is the conditional shift and $C_\varepsilon$ is an on-site coin of the form $C_\varepsilon=\exp(-i\varepsilon\,m\,\sigma_y)$ with fixed $m\in\mathbb{R}$. Let $|\psi_0\rangle$ be long-wavelength/band-limited in the sense that in lattice quasi-momentum variables $k\in[-\pi,\pi)$ its support obeys $|k|\le \varepsilon p_{\max}$ for some fixed $p_{\max}<\infty$ (equivalently, physical momenta $p=k/\varepsilon$ satisfy $|p|\le p_{\max}$).
Then for any fixed physical time $t\ge 0$ (with $T\equiv \lfloor t/\varepsilon\rfloor$ steps) one has
\begin{equation}
    \left\|U_\varepsilon^{T}|\psi_0\rangle-e^{-itH_D}|\psi_0\rangle\right\|\ \le\ C(t,p_{\max})\,\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
where $H_D\equiv -i\sigma_z\partial_x+m\,\sigma_y$ is the continuum $1{+}1$ Dirac Hamiltonian (in units with $c=\hbar=1$) acting on two-component spinors. A proof is given in Appendix~\ref{app:homogenization}.
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[Fibonacci-chain / Zeckendorf-textured DQCA]
For the fully specified Fibonacci/Sturmian-textured walk of Section~\ref{sec:fib_dqca_spec} with coin angles scaled as $\theta_{L/S}=m_{L/S}\varepsilon$, the same small-angle expansion yields a Dirac-type effective description on long wavelengths. The leading effect of the aperiodic texture enters through a quasiperiodic effective mass/mixing field and through controlled scattering between Floquet bands; the dependence on phason strain is quantified by the diagnostics and numerical protocol in Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca}.
\end{corollary}

The appendix collects the definitions and scaling heuristics used in this manuscript and points to the precise places where additional regularity assumptions enter.

\paragraph{Remark (Zeckendorf-clocked drive).}
One may also consider a time-dependent drive where the coin parameter alternates according to the intrinsic phase-orbit coding introduced in Part~II: define $\sigma_t\in\{L,S\}$ by an irrational circle rotation and set $\theta_t=\theta_L$ if $\sigma_t=L$ and $\theta_t=\theta_S$ if $\sigma_t=S$. In the golden case, the tick index $t$ admits a unique Zeckendorf digit structure, giving a sparse hierarchical schedule for when scale-dependent updates are activated \cite{Zeckendorf1972,AlloucheShallit2003}.

\subsubsection{Fermion doubling and a cut-and-project projection filter (with bounds)}
\label{sec:doubling_filter}
The \textbf{Nielsen--Ninomiya no-go theorem} \cite{Nielsen1981} shows that under locality, hermiticity, and exact translation invariance, lattice fermions generically exhibit species doubling. In the Omega construction translation invariance is broken at the microscopic level by the cut-and-project internal coordinate (or, in 1D reductions, by a Sturmian/Fibonacci texture), so the hypotheses of the theorem do not apply verbatim.
Rather than claiming an evasion, we formulate a concrete \emph{filtering mechanism} with explicit assumptions and an explicit mass-gap bound.\par

\noindent\textbf{Acceptance window and its Fourier decay.}
Let $W\subset E_\perp$ be the compact acceptance window defining the cut-and-project set, and let $\mathds{1}_W$ be its indicator function. Define the normalized window Fourier transform
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{W}(q)\ \equiv\ \frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(W)}\int_W e^{-i q\cdot y}\,dy,\qquad q\in E_\perp^*,
\end{equation}
so that $\widehat{W}(0)=1$ and $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le 1$.
For windows with piecewise $C^1$ boundary one has a quantitative decay bound $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le C_W/|q|$ for large $|q|$ (Riemann--Lebesgue with one integration by parts), and for the 1D Fibonacci window (an interval) the transform is explicitly a sinc function (Appendix~\ref{app:doubling_filter}).\par

\noindent\textbf{Projection-filter mass term.}
In the embedding picture, modes carry momenta $(k_{\parallel},k_\perp)$ and we introduce a momentum-dependent mass term controlled by the internal momentum $q\equiv k_\perp$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}(q)\ \equiv\ \mu\,\bigl(1-\widehat{W}(q)\bigr).
\label{eq:proj_filter_mass}
\end{equation}
This has the desired behavior: for physical long-wavelength modes one expects $q\approx 0$ and hence $\mathcal{M}(q)\approx 0$, while for modes pushed into the internal direction one expects $|q|$ of order the UV scale, for which $\widehat{W}(q)$ is small and $\mathcal{M}(q)\approx \mu$.\par

\noindent\textbf{Gap bound (statement).}
Assume that the cut slope is Diophantine (noble/golden case) so that the internal component of a would-be ``mirror'' momentum shift by $\pi/\varepsilon$ is bounded below by
\begin{equation}
    |q_{\mathrm{doubler}}|\ \ge\ \frac{c_0}{\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
for some $c_0>0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:doubling_filter} gives the 1D Fibonacci case explicitly).
Assume further that the window satisfies a decay bound $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le \delta_W(\varepsilon)$ for $|q|\ge c_0/\varepsilon$. Then
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{M}(q_{\mathrm{doubler}})| \ \ge\ \mu\bigl(1-\delta_W(\varepsilon)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Choosing the UV normalization $\mu=\mu_0/\varepsilon$ yields a physical doubler gap of order $1/\varepsilon$:
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{M}(q_{\mathrm{doubler}})| \ \ge\ \frac{\mu_0}{\varepsilon}\bigl(1-\delta_W(\varepsilon)\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, in the 1D Fibonacci interval-window case one has $\delta_W(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ from the explicit sinc bound, so the prefactor approaches $\mu_0$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$.\par

\noindent\textbf{Finite-volume check on periodic approximants.}
On the periodic Fibonacci approximants used for dispersion extraction, a doubler would manifest as an additional near-zero quasienergy branch near the Brillouin-edge momenta. Numerically (with fixed, reproducible parameters), the smallest quasienergy magnitude at $k=\pi$ remains bounded away from $0$ across increasing approximant size, consistent with a lattice-scale (hence physical $\sim 1/\varepsilon$) gap for the would-be doubler sector (Appendix~\ref{app:doubling_filter}).
