\opening{Dear Editor,}

I am pleased to submit my manuscript titled \textbf{``Omega Theory: Axiomatic Foundations of Holographic Spacetime and Interactive Evolution''} for consideration as an original research article (or Monograph) in \textit{Foundations of Physics}.

This paper presents a comprehensive attempt to axiomatize the foundations of cosmology and quantum mechanics from a purely information-theoretic perspective. Motivated by the tension between the unitarity of quantum evolution and the geometric nature of gravity, I propose a new framework---\textbf{Omega Theory}---which treats the entire universe as a single, static global state (represented by $|\Omega\rangle$ in a suitable Hilbert-space representation) on a quasi-local operator algebra of finite-dimensional degrees of freedom.

Key contributions of this work include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Omega Axiom}: An axiomatic ontology that identifies the universe with a single static state $|\Omega\rangle$, with perceived dynamics arising from spectral decompositions and basis-dependent phase structure.
    \item \textbf{Quasicrystal QCA and a Dirac-like continuum limit}: A concrete QCA ansatz on a quasicrystal substrate and a continuum-limit analysis that connects the long-wavelength dynamics to Dirac-like propagation with controlled lattice corrections.
    \item \textbf{The Omega Action}: An information-geometric action principle with Fisher-information terms, together with an explicit variational derivation of the associated field equations in a minimal setting.
    \item \textbf{Phenomenology and interpretation}: Parameterizations for observational signatures (varying constants, holographic-noise templates, and symmetry-suppressed LIV) and a formal ``Observer Operator'' proposal linking measurement-like selection to computability/incompleteness considerations.
\end{enumerate}

Given the journal's prestigious history of publishing foundational works that challenge and expand our understanding of physical reality (such as those by Bell, Bohm, and ‘t Hooft), I believe \textit{Foundations of Physics} is the ideal venue for this work. The paper adopts a rigorous deductive structure similar to a monograph, aiming to provide a self-consistent "Source Code" for the physical laws we observe.

I confirm that this manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by another journal. All authors (myself) have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I look forward to your response.
