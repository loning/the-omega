\title[Homological Visibility of Gluing Obstructions]{Homological Visibility of Gluing Obstructions in a State-Forcing Semantics}
\author{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
We develop a self-contained four-layer conservative extension of pointwise semantics and use it to study local objects and gluing obstructions in a state-forcing setting. Compatible local sectionability and its failure are shown to be genuinely new at the local-object enrichment level: they cannot be defined in the information-state forcing language without the local-object layer. For admitted references, compatible local existence is characterized by sheafification and typed readouts satisfy an address-before-value principle. To pass from local object functors to cohomology, we introduce realization prestacks whose stackification recovers visible value classes as global connected components. Each visible branch of the resulting realization stack determines a banded component gerbe; under a branch-constancy hypothesis these gerbes exhaust the stack. For finite abelian branch obstructions on finite Cech nerves, the universal coefficient theorem yields a homological evaluation map whose image is the intrinsic hidden subgroup and whose cokernel is the intrinsic visible quotient. This detects exactly the $H_2$-visible part of a branch obstruction; pure $\operatorname{Ext}$-type obstructions are character-blind. In the unique-branch case, the theory refines the standard no-global-section picture from sheaf-theoretic contextuality, with the character-blind $\operatorname{Ext}$-residual providing a precise mechanism for incomplete cohomological detection. We also obtain branch-sensitive and branch-uniform visible quotients, together with exact strictification budgets.
\end{abstract}
