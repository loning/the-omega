\section{Thermodynamic bounds for predictive AEC}
\label{app:aec_bounds}

This appendix summarizes the inequality chain used in Section~\ref{sec:bounds}.
We emphasize that these are \emph{necessary} conditions at the interface level, not sufficient conditions for biological viability.

\subsection{Predictive gain and mutual information rate}
\label{app:Fpred_bound}

In measurement--feedback thermodynamics, information obtained about a system can be converted into work or free-energy advantage, but the advantage is bounded by mutual information \cite{SagawaUeda2010,ParrondoHorowitzSagawa2015}.
In its simplest form, one obtains inequalities of the type
\begin{equation}
  \langle W\rangle \ \le\ \kB T\, I
\end{equation}
up to sign conventions and depending on whether $W$ denotes extracted work or required work.
At the HPA--$\Omega$ interface we use the computational temperature $T_c$ and consider a rate form:
\begin{equation}
  \dot F_{\mathrm{pred}}\ \le\ \kB T_c\,\dot I_{\mathrm{pred}}.
\end{equation}
The operational content is that predictive free-energy gain cannot exceed the energetic scale per nat times the predictive information rate (our convention $\log=\ln$). If $\dot I_{\mathrm{pred}}$ is expressed in bits per unit time, the corresponding bound acquires a factor $\ln 2$.

\subsection{Survival inequality and predictive efficiency}
\label{app:survival}

Let $\dot W_{\mathrm{diss}}$ be the dissipation rate required to resist phase friction and to maintain internal structure and model memory (including geometric impedance contributions).
A necessary condition for sustained AEC is a positive margin between predictive gain and dissipation:
\begin{equation}
  \dot F_{\mathrm{pred}}>\dot W_{\mathrm{diss}}.
\end{equation}
Combining the two inequalities yields the predictive information-rate threshold
\begin{equation}
  \dot I_{\mathrm{pred}}>\frac{\dot W_{\mathrm{diss}}}{\kB T_c}.
\end{equation}
If the dominant maintenance burden is exporting phase-friction entropy at computational temperature $T_c$, then the second law implies
\begin{equation}
  \dot W_{\mathrm{diss}} \ge T_c\,\frac{\dd S_{\mathrm{pf}}}{\dd t}
  = \kB T_c\,\Sigma(x,t),
\end{equation}
and hence $\dot I_{\mathrm{pred}}>\Sigma(x,t)$ (Eq.~\eqref{eq:Idot_threshold}).
This motivates the predictive efficiency
\begin{equation}
  \eta_{\mathrm{pred}} := \frac{\dot F_{\mathrm{pred}}}{\dot W_{\mathrm{diss}}}.
\end{equation}
