\subsection{Wave--particle duality, delayed choice, and Wheeler's ``Great Smoky Dragon'' (interface)}
\label{app:wave_particle_delayed_choice}

This appendix records a compact, audit-facing account of wave--particle duality and delayed-choice phenomena in a language compatible with the tick\,+\,CAP discipline.
The operational content is that the same underlying state admits distinct \emph{readout interfaces}:
coherent readout retains phase relations and therefore exhibits interference (cross terms in Born probabilities),
while event-record (which-way) readout produces an effectively decohered mixture and therefore obeys classical additivity.
The minimal algebraic statement of this dichotomy is already recorded in Lemma~\ref{lem:z128_interference_vs_mixture}.

\subsubsection{Duality as an interface statement: cross terms versus mixtures}
\label{subsec:dual_as_interface}

In the finite-observer interface package, readout is modeled by a POVM/instrument and probabilities are Born probabilities (Appendix~\ref{app:quantum_measurement_born}).
For a coherent superposition, Born probabilities contain cross terms, while for a mixture (or for a decohered state induced by an orthogonal record) those cross terms are absent; see Lemma~\ref{lem:z128_interference_vs_mixture}.
This is the operational content behind the slogan ``wave--particle duality'': the ``wave'' aspect is the presence of coherent cross terms, and the ``particle'' aspect is the event-record readout that yields discrete clicks and classical additivity.

\subsubsection{Complementarity and the \texorpdfstring{$V^2+D^2\le 1$}{V^2+D^2<=1} bound}
\label{subsec:engert_bound}

A quantitative form of complementarity relates
the interference visibility $V$ and the which-way distinguishability $D$.
In a two-path interference setting, one may define $V$ as the usual fringe visibility,
$
V=(I_{\max}-I_{\min})/(I_{\max}+I_{\min}),
$
and define $D$ as the optimal success bias for discriminating the two paths from an available which-way record.
Under standard assumptions (binary paths; optimal discrimination on the record),
one has the inequality
$$
V^2+D^2\le 1,
$$
which makes precise the statement that extracting which-way information reduces interference visibility \cite{Englert1996FringeVisibility}.
Historically, complementarity is the conceptual stance that one cannot simultaneously realize maximal wave and particle aspects in a single experimental arrangement; see, e.g., \cite{Bohr1928QuantumPostulate}.
The $V^2+D^2\le 1$ relation is consistent with Lemma~\ref{lem:z128_interference_vs_mixture}: increasing which-way recordability corresponds to suppressing the off-diagonal (coherence) terms that generate interference.

\subsubsection{Delayed-choice Mach--Zehnder: late choice of measurement basis}
\label{subsec:delayed_choice_mzi}

Wheeler's delayed-choice thought experiment asks whether one can decide \emph{after} the particle has entered an interferometer
whether to observe interference (wave-like statistics) or which-way information (particle-like statistics) \cite{Wheeler1978DelayedChoice}.
In a Mach--Zehnder interferometer with two beam splitters, the second beam splitter implements a basis change: it recombines amplitudes and makes interference observable.
Removing the second beam splitter changes the readout to a which-way measurement.

In an idealized two-path model, after the first beam splitter the (signal) state can be written as
$$
|\psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl(|0\rangle+\e^{\iu\phi}|1\rangle\Bigr),
$$
where $\phi$ is the controllable relative phase between the paths.
If the interferometer is \emph{closed} (second beam splitter present), the output detectors correspond to the $\{|+\rangle,|-\rangle\}$ basis with
$
|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}
$
and
$
|-\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt{2}.
$
Then the two output probabilities are
$$
P_+=\frac{1+\cos\phi}{2},\qquad P_-=\frac{1-\cos\phi}{2},
$$
which is interference.
If the interferometer is \emph{open} (second beam splitter absent), the output detectors correspond to the path basis $\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}$ and one finds
$$
P_0=P_1=\frac{1}{2},
$$
independent of $\phi$.

The delayed-choice aspect is that the decision to close/open the interferometer may be made at spacetime points that are timelike (or even spacelike, in some implementations) separated from the first beam splitter.
The consistent account is that the choice selects the \emph{readout interface} (measurement basis / POVM), not that it ``changes the past'':
in the absence of a which-way record, the statement ``which path occurred'' is not an interface-defined, auditable fact.
For a representative experimental realization of Wheeler's delayed-choice scenario, see, e.g., \cite{JacquesEtAl2007DelayedChoice}.

\subsubsection{Delayed-choice quantum eraser: conditional interference without signaling}
\label{subsec:delayed_choice_eraser}

The quantum eraser paradigm refines delayed choice by explicitly introducing a record system that can store which-way information \cite{ScullyDruhl1982QuantumEraser}.
A standard idealized model uses an entangled signal--idler state
$$
|\Psi\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\Bigl(|0\rangle_s|0\rangle_i+\e^{\iu\phi}|1\rangle_s|1\rangle_i\Bigr),
$$
where the idler $i$ can carry which-way information.
Tracing out the idler yields a maximally mixed reduced state on the signal,
$$
\rho_s=\Tr_i|\Psi\rangle\langle\Psi|=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(|0\rangle\langle 0|+|1\rangle\langle 1|\bigr),
$$
so any signal-only readout lacks interference (the off-diagonal terms vanish).
However, if one measures the idler in the rotated basis $|\pm\rangle_i=(|0\rangle_i\pm |1\rangle_i)/\sqrt{2}$ and conditions on the outcome,
the corresponding conditional signal states are
$$
|\psi_\pm\rangle_s\propto |0\rangle_s\pm \e^{\iu\phi}|1\rangle_s,
$$
which do exhibit interference, with opposite fringe phases for the two idler outcomes.
This is the operational sense in which ``erasing'' which-way information restores interference: interference reappears only in conditional (coincidence) statistics, not in the unconditional signal marginal.
Therefore the eraser does not enable superluminal signaling; it reorganizes correlations.
For an experimental delayed-choice implementation of the eraser logic, see, e.g., \cite{KimEtAl2000DelayedChoiceQuantumEraser}.

\subsubsection{Wheeler's ``Great Smoky Dragon'' and auditable facts}
\label{subsec:smoky_dragon}

Wheeler's ``Great Smoky Dragon'' metaphor emphasizes that between preparation and measurement one should not demand a classical narrative (a definite path or ``what really happened'') unless the protocol contains an auditable record that could witness that narrative.
In delayed-choice settings this becomes vivid: one may postpone the decision of which readout is implemented, yet the statistics remain consistent.
In the tick\,+\,CAP discipline this is not an interpretive add-on but a bookkeeping rule:
an ``event'' is the output of a declared readout (POVM/instrument), and claims about ``which alternative occurred'' are meaningful only insofar as the protocol includes a record subsystem that makes the alternatives distinguishable.
Lemma~\ref{lem:z128_interference_vs_mixture} is the concrete algebraic content of that stance.
