
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced Wish as \emph{protocol-stable period data}: a minimal operative target object defined by protocol invariance and auditable finite-resource realizability.
Within a controlled class of holographic scanning protocols, we recalled the scan--period interface via $\HSP$ and formulated deterministic finite-resource error certificates based on discrepancy and Hardy--Krause variation, together with explicit truncation/regularization bounds.

Our main new closed contribution was to define a certified discrepancy potential on protocol space and derive a dissipative variational dynamics:
a damped inertial gradient flow driven by the negative gradient of a computational potential built from certified discrepancy and explicit cost.
The associated energy is a Lyapunov function, yielding a precise ``minimal computational discrepancy'' principle under finite resources.

We provided reproducible pure-Python experiments that realize $\log 2$, $\pi$, $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ via Kronecker scans and truncated geometric kernels, demonstrate explicit error decompositions and certificates, and exhibit a sharp uniqueness gap in a bounded low-complexity constant search for $\alpha^{-1}(0)$.
Together, these results supply a concrete audit-compatible route for elevating ``constants'' and ``targets'' from interpretation-layer language to protocol-level objects with equations and falsifiable numerical tests.
