\subsection{Renormalization: dictionary, scheme dependence, and audit boundaries}
\label{app:renormalization_dictionary_and_boundaries}

This appendix records a compact renormalization dictionary in the layer discipline of the paper.
The goal is to make explicit which pieces are (i) matching-layer conventions, (ii) interface identifications that translate between protocol scales and standard QFT scales, and (iii) open problems beyond the present closure scope.

\subsubsection{Renormalized parameters and scheme dependence}
\paragraph{Running couplings as matching-layer objects.}
\MatchTag In standard QFT, renormalized couplings and masses depend on a renormalization prescription (scheme) and on a scale $\mu$.
In this manuscript, such dependence is treated as a matching-layer convention unless explicitly closed within a declared finite candidate family.

\paragraph{The $r$-coordinate dictionary.}
\InterfaceTag The paper uses the Fibonacci resolution coordinate $r$ as a scale proxy and records the RG chain rule in Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}.
This provides an explicit dictionary between logarithmic scale changes and additive shifts in $r$.

\subsubsection{Regularization and counterterms (scope boundary)}
\paragraph{Regularization path discipline.}
\AuditTag Whenever a continuum limit or a divergent expression appears, the paper follows an explicit regularization/finite-part discipline (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_primitives} and Appendix~\ref{app:abel_finite_part_notes}).
This discipline is a bookkeeping rule and is not, by itself, a constructive renormalization proof.

\paragraph{Open boundary.}
\AuditTag Constructive renormalization for interacting 4D QFTs (existence of the continuum limit, control of counterterms, and nonperturbative completeness) is not attempted here.
Any future attempt must declare the hypothesis space (field content, locality class, regulator family) and provide auditable error control.

\subsubsection{How renormalization enters this paper}
\paragraph{Where it is used.}
\MatchTag Renormalization conventions enter in two controlled ways:
\begin{itemize}
\item as reference dictionaries when comparing to PDG targets (scheme/threshold conventions already inventoried in Appendix~\ref{app:inference_ledger}),
\item as the standard running-coupling chain rule in the $r$ coordinate (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}) used to interpret resolution uplifts as threshold matching.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{What is not claimed.}
\AuditTag The paper does not claim a theorem-level derivation of the full Standard Model renormalization program.
Where beta-function coefficients are recorded, they are stated within explicitly declared interface assumptions and audited within the paper's bounded-complexity framework.

\subsubsection{Minimal failure points and falsifiability hooks (audit)}
\label{subsec:renorm_minimal_failure_points}
\AuditTag Renormalization enters as dictionary/matching structure in this manuscript.
Whenever a renormalization-dependent statement is invoked, at least the following minimal points must be declared and can fail explicitly:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(R1) Scheme dependence.} If the scheme is changed (e.g.\ $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ vs.\ on-shell), numerical values of couplings/masses shift; any comparison must be rephrased as a matching-layer shift (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).
\item \textbf{(R2) Threshold conventions.} If threshold matching is altered (decoupling choices, effective field content), the one-loop coefficients and running segments change discretely; the result is a different finite candidate family and must be audited as such (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).
\item \textbf{(R3) Nonperturbative boundary.} If a claim requires constructive/nonperturbative control (existence of interacting 4D models, mass gap, asymptotic completeness), it lies beyond the present scope and must be recorded as not closed.
\end{itemize}
In (R1)--(R2) the paper proceeds by declaring a bounded family of conventions and reporting sensitivity under that family; in (R3) it records the boundary and reverts to proxy-level or benchmark-only statements.
