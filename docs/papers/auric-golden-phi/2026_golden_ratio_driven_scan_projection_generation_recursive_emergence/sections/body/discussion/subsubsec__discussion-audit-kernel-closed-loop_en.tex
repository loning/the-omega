\subsubsection{Closed chain of unified audit protocol: resolution reflection—curvature—fiber spectrum—Hankel normal form—arithmetic rigidity}\label{subsubsec:discussion-audit-kernel-closed-loop}
On top of the ``certificate bundle'' perspective of the previous subsection, this paper also contains a set of structural nodes more oriented toward the operational layer and directly implementable as a unified audit protocol.
Together they form a closed chain from canonical slices to unforgeable integer fingerprints:
resolution reflection (normalization) \(\to\) gauge curvature (noncommutative obstruction) \(\to\) fiber spectrum and alias gates (visible phase) \(\to\) Hankel normal form (minimal realization) \(\to\) arithmetic rigidity and resonances (integral certificate).
This paragraph only consolidates these nodes into field-based certificates under the ``input–output–verification'' regime, without introducing new proof premises.

\paragraph{Resolution reflector: turning gauge choice into idempotent projection}
After fixing the top resolution \(M\), the reflector at resolution \(m\) consists of the pushforward \(P_{M\to m}\) and the fiber-uniform lift \(Q_{M\to m}\), defining
\[
\mu^{(m)}:=Q_{M\to m}P_{M\to m}\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_M)
\qquad\text{(Definition~\ref{def:pom-resolution-reflector})}.
\]
By \(P_{M\to m}\circ Q_{M\to m}=\mathrm{id}_{\Delta(X_m)}\) (same definition), idempotence follows:
\[
(\mu^{(m)})^{(m)}=\mu^{(m)},
\qquad
P_{M\to m}\mu^{(m)}=P_{M\to m}\mu,
\]
hence ``gauge fixing'' in this paper's regime is not a narrative convention but a reproducible operator \(Q_{M\to m}P_{M\to m}\).
In cross-resolution comparisons, reflecting data to the same canonical slice before discussing invariants avoids spurious laws induced by coordinate inconsistency (Remark~\ref{rem:pom-gauge-criterion}; see also the KL-tower residual additive identity: Theorem~\ref{thm:pom-kl-pythagoras-tower}).

\paragraph{Curvature and local noncommutativity: auditable trace of constant-level defect}
The noncommutativity between ``first truncate then fold'' and ``first fold then restrict'' across resolutions is given by the gauge curvature
\(\mathcal{K}_{m+1\to m}\) (Definition~\ref{def:pom-gauge-curvature});
for any bounded observable \(f:X_m\to\RR\), the curvature probability provides a strict error bar (Proposition~\ref{prop:pom-gauge-curvature-shadow-bound}).
Under the uniform microstate baseline, exact enumeration for \(m\le 20\) (Remark~\ref{rem:pom-gauge-curvature-audit}; Table~\ref{tab:fold_truncation_curvature_stats}) exhibits two traces directly relevant to audit protocols:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Constant trace of curvature probability:} \(\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{K}_{m+1\to m}=1]\) approaches \(1/3\) in the enumeration range, suggesting this obstruction is closer to a constant-level local defect rather than diffusing into macroscopic deviation as scale increases;
  \item \textbf{Constant trace of Hamming mismatch:} Table~\ref{tab:fold_truncation_curvature_stats} also reports the Hamming distance \(H_m\) of the two path outputs; its mean approaches \(1/2\) in the enumeration range, while \(\mathbb{E}[H_m]/m\) clearly tends to zero.
\end{itemize}
Since \(\mathcal{K}_{m+1\to m}=\mathbf 1[H_m>0]\), one may take
\[
\mathbb{E}[H_m\mid \mathcal{K}_{m+1\to m}=1]
=\frac{\mathbb{E}[H_m]}{\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{K}_{m+1\to m}=1]}
\]
as a purely numerical, falsifiable ``conditional mismatch digit count'' audit constant.
In the \(m\le 20\) enumeration range of Table~\ref{tab:fold_truncation_curvature_stats}, this ratio stably approaches \(3/2\), yielding a further verifiable local defect conjecture: the typical mismatch digit count when curvature occurs may converge to a constant (rather than grow with \(m\)).

\paragraph{Discrete phase compression of fiber spectrum: two-point limiting law and alias gate}
For bin-fold, the fiber scale degenerates in the exponential dominant order into a constant factor of the trailing two states (Theorem~\ref{thm:fold-bin-two-state-asymptotic}), and under uniform dyadic input further yields a two-point limiting law:
Let \(U_m\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{0,\dots,2^m-1\}\), \(W_m=\mathrm{Fold}^{\mathrm{bin}}_m(U_m)\); then the scaled variable
\(
Z_m=(\varphi/2)^m d^{\mathrm{bin}}_m(W_m)
\)
converges in distribution to a two-point distribution with support \(\{1,\varphi^{-1}\}\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:fold-bin-two-point-limit-law}).
This conclusion is equivalent to: under the uniform baseline, the ``typical fiber scale'' is almost reduced to a binary phase certificate, with exponentially negligible error on exponential scale.
\par
At the minimal anchor point window-\(6\), the alias structure can be further compressed into a gate-like discrete offset spectrum:
for the boundary sector \(X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}\), dyadic uplift exhibits canonical two-sheet covering, where each boundary word has exactly two preimages with strictly fixed difference \(34=F_9\) (Table~\ref{tab:fold6_boundary_sheet_pairs}; see also corresponding explanation in \S\ref{subsubsec:window6-terminal-propositions}).
Such ``fixed-offset two-sheet alias gates'' compress the non-uniformity of the fiber spectrum from continuous amplitude information into a reproducible discrete phase (sheet parity) certificate.
\par
The same mechanism also provides deterministic rescaling between dyadic cutoff depth and Fibonacci weight depth: by the definition of \(K(m)\) (Definition~\ref{def:K-of-m}) and its asymptotic estimate (e.g., \(K(m)=m\log_\varphi 2+O(1)\) given in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fold-bin-max-fiber-exponent}), there exists an unerasable linear scale mismatch between binary cutoff and Zeckendorf weight depth; this mismatch itself can be viewed as an independent resolution certificate coordinate for aligning ``bit-length budget'' and ``\(\varphi\)-resolution budget'' translation regime.

\paragraph{Hankel normal form: \texorpdfstring{$2d$}{2d} point locking of entire sequence for minimal realization equality protocol}
For fixed \(q\ge 2\), the Hankel rank \(d\) of the collision moment sequence \(a_n:=S_q(n+2)\) locks the minimal realization dimension, and the invertible Hankel block at minimal offset \(\ell_q\) constructs the Hankel-normal form
\(\mathrm{HANKELNF}_q\) (Definition~\ref{def:pom-hankel-normal-form}).
More strongly, if two realization paths produce \(S_q,S'_q\) with the same Hankel rank \(d\), then as long as they agree on a continuous window of length \(2d\), they share the same \(\mathrm{HANKELNF}_q\), hence completely agree for all \(m\ge 2\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:pom-moment-finite-sampling-decidable}; see also protocol formulation in Theorem~\ref{thm:dephys-hankel-finite-audit}).
Thus the computable equality of ``mechanism identity'' can be compressed into a hard interface of finite samples: once \(d\) and \(\ell_q\) are output as certificates, verification only needs to check \(2d\) integer samples.

\paragraph{Arithmetic rigidity and resonances: \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-adic jumps, NullMode, and integer division quotient factorization certificate}
Hankel witness block determinants not only provide rank lower bounds (Theorem~\ref{thm:pom-moment-hankel}) but also carry unforgeable arithmetic rigidity fingerprints.
For instance, the rigidity index at \(p=5\) exhibits a first high valuation jump at \(k=5\) (Remark~\ref{rem:pom-hankel-stiffness-p5}), giving a completely internal auditable arithmetic threshold.
\par
In the resonance window \(q=9,\dots,17\), Hankel rank drop triggers explicit modal constraints (Theorem~\ref{thm:pom-moment-resonance}), which can be offline output as integer NullMode certificates: for the corresponding Hankel block, an integer basis of \(\ker(H^{\mathsf T})\) is shown in Table~\ref{tab:fold_collision_resonance_nullmodes_q9_17}.
More strongly, these NullMode-induced annihilating polynomials \(A^{(j)}(\lambda)\) and the minimal recurrence characteristic polynomial \(P_q(\lambda)\) satisfy exact integral coefficient divisibility factorization
\(
A^{(j)}(\lambda)=Q^{(j)}(\lambda)P_q(\lambda)
\)
with zero remainder; the corresponding quotient polynomials \(Q^{(j)}\) are output and verified entry by entry as additional certificate fields (Table~\ref{tab:fold_collision_resonance_nullmodes_primitive_quotient_q9_17}).
Such ``resonance–algebraic quotient'' certificates simultaneously develop structural dimension reduction and arithmetic divisibility rigidity, forming the most forgery-resistant type of integral coefficient fingerprints in audit protocols.

\paragraph{Low-frequency compressible structural quantity: harmonic energy ratio as near-coboundary fingerprint}
In the three-dimensional Dirichlet–Mertens constant tensor \(C^{(3,3,5)}\) of the real-input 40-state kernel (third axis taking collision count \(N_2\bmod 5\)), the drift along the \(\ZZ/5\) axis for each \((a,b)\) exactly decomposes into ``mean \(+\) two harmonic complex matrices'' \(A^{(1)},A^{(2)}\) (see output in generated fragment~\ref{tab:real_input_40_arity_dirichlet_mertens_335_N2_summary}).

\mathcal{E}_1:=\frac{\|A^{(1)}\|_F^2}{\|A^{(1)}\|_F^2+\|A^{(2)}\|_F^2},
\]
and taking \(\mathcal{E}_1\) as a dimensionless fingerprint field for ``low-frequency compressibility'' and near-coboundary structure (the same generated fragment simultaneously outputs numerical certificates).
This field, together with small-angle quadratic law audit (\(\rho/\lambda\) consistency at finite angle points), jointly constitutes a reproducible ``low-frequency structure–diffusive slow mode'' splicing diagnostic interface.

\paragraph{Audit kernel interface: unified output from finite samples to field-based certificates}
Organizing the above nodes by a unified interface yields a reproducible audit kernel protocol template:
input is finite samples produced by any implementation path (distribution pushforward, finite-segment moments, or finite-window enumeration statistics), output is a set of field-based certificate objects; any field with stable mismatch can serve as a refutation interface for mechanism equivalence.
In this paper's established object and script system, the output can include at least:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Resolution reflector slice \(\mu^{(m)}\) and its KL-tower residual ledger (Definition~\ref{def:pom-resolution-reflector}; Theorem~\ref{thm:pom-kl-pythagoras-tower});
  \item Curvature probability and error bar (Definition~\ref{def:pom-gauge-curvature}; Proposition~\ref{prop:pom-gauge-curvature-shadow-bound}; Table~\ref{tab:fold_truncation_curvature_stats});
  \item Bin-fold two-point phase and window-\(6\) gate alias offset spectrum (Corollary~\ref{cor:fold-bin-two-point-limit-law}; Table~\ref{tab:fold6_boundary_sheet_pairs});
  \item Hankel-normal form \(\mathrm{HANKELNF}_q\) and \(2d\)-point finite equality window (Definition~\ref{def:pom-hankel-normal-form}; Proposition~\ref{prop:pom-moment-finite-sampling-decidable});
  \item \(p\)-adic rigidity index and resonance NullMode/division quotient factorization (Remark~\ref{rem:pom-hankel-stiffness-p5}; Tables~\ref{tab:fold_collision_resonance_nullmodes_q9_17}--\ref{tab:fold_collision_resonance_nullmodes_primitive_quotient_q9_17});
  \item Low-frequency structural quantity \(\mathcal{E}_1\) and small-angle quadratic law type diagnostic fields (fragment~\ref{tab:real_input_40_arity_dirichlet_mertens_335_N2_summary}).
\end{itemize}
Thus, this paper's ``audit first, interpret later'' can be realized as a completely implementation-neutral verification protocol: under fixed readout and baseline regime, any alternative implementation claiming the same mechanism must reproduce the same set of certificate fields; otherwise it can be rejected offline, machine-verifiably, without entering internal mechanism details.
