
\section{Constructive spacetime: coarse-grained geometry from folded scan dynamics}
\label{sec:constructive_spacetime}

\subsection{Locality as a compilation/addressing effect}
\label{sec:locality_as_address}

At resolution $n$, the physical adjacency graph is the screen lattice $(\Sigma_n,\sim)$.
The scan order provides an \emph{addressing} of this graph, but it does not in general preserve all local interactions for free.
Instead, the cost of implementing a screen-local gate using scan-nearest-neighbor primitives depends on the local routing overhead $\kappa$.
This yields a natural operational meaning of ``curvature'' or ``gravitational potential'' in terms of inhomogeneous compilation cost.

In particular, regions in which $\kappa$ is larger are regions in which local operations take more global ticks to realize.
This is the structural origin of the lapse field \eqref{eq:lapse_def} and of gravitational redshift in this framework (Section~\ref{sec:gravity}).

\subsection{Why a non-differentiable address still yields smooth effective geometry}
\label{sec:coarse_graining_geometry}

The continuous Hilbert curve is nowhere differentiable, and even the discrete address path is highly tortuous at fine scales.
However, physical measurement is always performed with finite resolution.
Let $w^{(\varepsilon)}$ be a readout kernel at scale $\varepsilon$ (or, equivalently, a POVM element family $E^{(\varepsilon)}$).
The effective observable is a coarse-grained field $\Phi_\varepsilon$ obtained by smoothing over many lattice sites.

In this regime, discrete differential operators on $\Sigma_n$ (graph Laplacian, discrete gradients) can approximate continuum operators provided $\varepsilon$ is large compared with the lattice spacing $2^{-n}$ and the readout kernel is sufficiently regular.
Thus, classical smooth geometry is an \emph{effective} description of coarse-grained fields, not a microscopic property of the address curve itself.

\subsection{Scale as resolution: Hilbert self-similarity and canonical time coding}
\label{sec:scale_renormalization}

Hilbert addressing is strictly hierarchical: $H_{n+1}$ is built recursively from $H_n$ by subdividing the lattice into $2^d$ subcubes and connecting appropriately rotated/reflected subpaths.
This induces a natural notion of ``coarse graining'':
the map $n\mapsto n-1$ groups $2^d$ sites into a block and projects $\Sigma_n$ to $\Sigma_{n-1}$.

On the time side, canonical integer codings (Ostrowski/Zeckendorf for the golden branch) provide a multi-scale decomposition of scan time.
Together, these two hierarchical structures support a renormalization viewpoint in which:
\begin{itemize}
\item resolution level $n$ acts as a discrete spatial scale;
\item Abel weight $r$ acts as an analytic radial coordinate (regularizing long tails before taking the boundary limit);
\item coarse-grained dynamics is defined by pushing forward the scan/readout protocol through these scale maps.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Admissible address maps and robustness under coarse graining}
\label{sec:address_robustness}

Hilbert folding (Axiom~\ref{ax:H1}) commits to a concrete finite-resolution addressing primitive, but the broader scan--fold paradigm admits alternative address families.
This matters because the addressing enters the compilation problem and therefore the overhead field $\kappa$ (Section~\ref{sec:kappa_bounds}).
At the same time, physical predictions are formulated at finite readout resolution, so only coarse-grained features of protocol fields are observable.
This subsection records a minimal robustness statement in the closed layer.

\begin{definition}[Admissible address family (coarse-grained locality class)]
\label{def:admissible_address_family}
An address family $\{A_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ with $A_n:\{0,1,\dots,2^{dn}-1\}\to\Sigma_n$ is called admissible if each $A_n$ is a bijection and if there exist constants $C_d>0$ and $c_d>0$ such that, after rescaling $\Sigma_n$ to the unit cube by $\tilde x:=2^{-n}x$, the induced discrete path obeys a uniform H\"older-type locality bound
\[
\norm{2^{-n}A_n(t)-2^{-n}A_n(s)}_\infty\ \le\ C_d\,\left|\frac{t-s}{2^{dn}}\right|^{1/d},
\qquad s,t\in\{0,\dots,2^{dn}-1\},
\]
and a uniform non-degeneracy bound in the reverse direction
\[
\left|\frac{t-s}{2^{dn}}\right|\ \le\ c_d\,\norm{2^{-n}A_n(t)-2^{-n}A_n(s)}_\infty^{d}.
\]
Continuous Hilbert/Peano-type constructions satisfy such bi-H\"older bounds in their classical forms; see \cite{Sagan1994}.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Hilbert vs.\ Z-order (Morton) locality]
\label{rem:hilbert_vs_zorder}
Different admissible families can have substantially different clustering properties at finite resolution, and therefore different compilation overhead landscapes.
In spatial indexing, Hilbert orders are known to provide stronger clustering/locality than Z-order/Morton-type orders for a wide range of window queries and locality metrics; see \cite{MoonJagadishFaloutsosSaltz2001}.
In the present framework, this translates into a principled sensitivity channel: the map choice changes the distribution of address separations and therefore changes $\kappa$ and $\lapse$ in substrate models where compilation is dominated by scan-order routing.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Neighbor index separation and a scan-chain overhead proxy]
\label{def:neighbor_index_separation}
Fix an admissible address map $A_n:\{0,1,\dots,2^{dn}-1\}\to\Sigma_n$ and write its inverse as $A_n^{-1}:\Sigma_n\to\{0,1,\dots,2^{dn}-1\}$.
For a screen-neighbor edge $\{x,y\}\in E_{\mathrm{screen}}$ define the \emph{scan-order separation}
\[
\Delta_{A_n}(x,y):=\abs{A_n^{-1}(x)-A_n^{-1}(y)}.
\]
Define the associated local scan-chain overhead proxy by
\[
\widetilde\kappa_{A_n}(x):=\max_{y\sim x}\Delta_{A_n}(x,y),
\]
interpreted as the largest scan-order separation among screen neighbors of $x$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Scan-chain lower bound from address separations]
\label{prop:scan_chain_separation_lower_bound}
Assume the hardware graph is the unit-weight path on vertices $\{0,1,\dots,2^{dn}-1\}$ and the placement is $\pi_n(x)=A_n^{-1}(x)$.
Then implementing any primitive interaction between screen neighbors $\{x,y\}\in E_{\mathrm{screen}}$ using adjacent primitives on the chain requires at least $\Delta_{A_n}(x,y)$ global ticks.
Consequently, for any local update task family $\mathcal{G}_x$ that requires at least one interaction between $x$ and each of its neighbors, the compilation overhead satisfies
\[
\kappa(x)\ \ge\ \widetilde\kappa_{A_n}(x),
\]
up to fixed constant factors determined by the primitive gate set and scheduling convention.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On a path graph with unit weights, information cannot propagate faster than one edge per tick.
Thus any interaction between sites placed at chain positions $A_n^{-1}(x)$ and $A_n^{-1}(y)$ requires at least their graph distance, which equals $\abs{A_n^{-1}(x)-A_n^{-1}(y)}$.
The local-update bound follows by taking the maximum over the required neighbor interactions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Address-family dependence of \texorpdfstring{$N(x)$}{N(x)} on a scan chain]
\label{rem:address_dependence_lapse}
In the GR dictionary of Section~\ref{sec:gravity}, the lapse $N$ is identified with the operational field $\lapse=\kappa_0/\kappa$, hence also with the potential $\Phi=-\log N=\log(\kappa/\kappa_0)$.
In the scan-chain specialization $\pi_n(x)=A_n^{-1}(x)$, Proposition~\ref{prop:scan_chain_separation_lower_bound} shows that address-order neighbor separations control the overhead landscape and therefore directly control the lapse landscape.
This makes the dependence of $N(x)$ on the choice of address family (Hilbert vs.\ Morton/Z-order, etc.) an experimentally testable effect: Tables~\ref{tab:address_kappa_proxy} and~\ref{tab:kappa_redshift_toy} report the induced proxy statistics and the corresponding redshift ratios for fixed substrate model, with only the address family varied.
Since all physical comparisons are formulated either as lapse ratios or as potentials (which are invariant under global tick reparameterizations; Remark~\ref{rem:lapse_calibration_reparam}), the sensitivity to address choice is a genuine spatial inhomogeneity rather than a coordinate artifact.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Address choice as a protocol gauge (diffeomorphism analogy)]
\label{rem:address_as_gauge}
In continuum gravity, diffeomorphism invariance expresses the fact that physical predictions do not depend on the choice of coordinates.
In the present constructive setting, an address family plays a role closer to a \emph{protocol gauge choice}: it is a concrete discrete chart that fixes how scan time is folded into screen locality and therefore fixes a concrete compilation problem on a given substrate.
Accordingly, at fixed microscopic substrate the lapse landscape can depend on the address choice (Remark~\ref{rem:address_dependence_lapse}).
Operational invariance is recovered only after specifying the readout resolution: Definition~\ref{def:eps_universality_class} formalizes when two protocol realizations are indistinguishable at scale $\varepsilon$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson_stability} then bounds the induced changes in coarse-grained potentials and lapse ratios.
In this sense, diffeomorphism-style ``gauge freedom'' is replaced here by an explicit, testable universality statement at finite resolution.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{A stability estimate for coarse-grained potentials}
\label{sec:potential_stability}

Let $w^{(\varepsilon)}$ be a nonnegative readout kernel at scale $\varepsilon$ with $\int w^{(\varepsilon)}=1$.
For any field $F$ define its coarse-graining by convolution $F_\varepsilon:=w^{(\varepsilon)}\ast F$.
If $\Phi$ is defined as the Poisson potential of a protocol-defined source density $\sigma$ (Section~\ref{sec:poisson_from_action}), then small changes in $\sigma$ imply small changes in $\Phi$ in standard Sobolev norms.

\begin{proposition}[Stability of Poisson potentials under source perturbations]
\label{prop:poisson_stability}
Let $\Omega\subset\RR^3$ be a domain with fixed boundary conditions (periodic, Dirichlet, or suitable decay at infinity).
Let $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ be two source densities and let $\Phi_1,\Phi_2$ solve
\[
-\Delta\Phi_i=4\pi G\,\sigma_i
\]
with the same boundary convention.
Then
\[
\norm{\nabla(\Phi_1-\Phi_2)}_{L^2(\Omega)}\ \le\ 4\pi G\,\norm{\sigma_1-\sigma_2}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Set $\delta\Phi=\Phi_1-\Phi_2$ and $\delta\sigma=\sigma_1-\sigma_2$.
Then $-\Delta\delta\Phi=4\pi G\,\delta\sigma$.
Testing against $\delta\Phi$ and integrating by parts yields
\[
\norm{\nabla\delta\Phi}_{L^2(\Omega)}^2
=4\pi G\,\langle \delta\sigma,\delta\Phi\rangle
\le 4\pi G\,\norm{\delta\sigma}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\,\norm{\nabla\delta\Phi}_{L^2(\Omega)},
\]
hence the claimed estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Resolution-\texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon$}{epsilon} universality class (protocol equivalence)]
\label{def:eps_universality_class}
Fix a boundary convention on $\Omega$ and a readout kernel $w^{(\varepsilon)}$ of width $\varepsilon$ with $\int w^{(\varepsilon)}=1$.
Consider two protocol realizations (address family, routing scheme, hardware graph $G_{\mathrm{phys}}$, placement $\pi_n$, horizon and regularization budgets, etc.) that induce source densities $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:defect_charge_sigma}.
Define the coarse-grained sources $\sigma_{i,\varepsilon}:=w^{(\varepsilon)}\ast\sigma_i$.
For a tolerance $\delta>0$, we say the two realizations are \emph{$(\varepsilon,\delta)$-equivalent} if
\[
\norm{\sigma_{1,\varepsilon}-\sigma_{2,\varepsilon}}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\ \le\ \delta.
\]
The collection of realizations mutually $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-equivalent forms an operational universality class at readout resolution $\varepsilon$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coarse-grained invariance of potentials and lapse ratios in a universality class]
\label{prop:eps_invariance_lapse}
Fix a boundary convention on $\Omega$ such that a Poincar\'e-type inequality holds in the chosen gauge (e.g.\ periodic with zero-mean gauge, or Dirichlet with $\Phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0$), and let $C_P$ be the corresponding constant:
\[
\norm{f}_{L^2(\Omega)}\le C_P\,\norm{\nabla f}_{L^2(\Omega)}.
\]
Let two protocol realizations be $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-equivalent in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:eps_universality_class}.
Let $\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}$ denote the Poisson potentials of the coarse-grained sources:
\[
-\Delta\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}=4\pi G\,\sigma_{i,\varepsilon}
\]
with the same boundary convention and gauge choice, and define the corresponding coarse-grained lapse fields $N_{i,\varepsilon}:=\e^{-\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}}$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Energy-norm invariance.}
\[
\norm{\nabla(\Phi_{1,\varepsilon}-\Phi_{2,\varepsilon})}_{L^2(\Omega)}\ \le\ 4\pi G\,\delta.
\]
\item \textbf{Pointwise invariance after readout smoothing.}
For any nonnegative normalized kernel $\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}$ used to read out the potential at scale $\varepsilon$, define $\widetilde\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}:=\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}\ast\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}$ and $\widetilde N_{i,\varepsilon}:=\e^{-\widetilde\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}}$.
Then
\[
\norm{\widetilde\Phi_{1,\varepsilon}-\widetilde\Phi_{2,\varepsilon}}_{L^\infty(\Omega)}
\ \le\
4\pi G\,C_P\,\norm{\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}}_{L^2(\Omega)}\,\delta,
\]
and therefore for all $x\in\Omega$ one has the multiplicative lapse bound
\[
\exp\!\bigl(-\Delta_\varepsilon\bigr)\ \le\ \frac{\widetilde N_{1,\varepsilon}(x)}{\widetilde N_{2,\varepsilon}(x)}\ \le\ \exp\!\bigl(\Delta_\varepsilon\bigr),
\qquad
\Delta_\varepsilon:=4\pi G\,C_P\,\norm{\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}}_{L^2(\Omega)}\,\delta.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:eps_universality_class}, one has the coarse-grained source bound
\[
\norm{\sigma_{1,\varepsilon}-\sigma_{2,\varepsilon}}_{H^{-1}(\Omega)}\le \delta.
\]
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson_stability} to the pair $(\sigma_{1,\varepsilon},\sigma_{2,\varepsilon})$ yields the energy-norm bound.
For the pointwise statement, set $\delta\Phi_\varepsilon:=\Phi_{1,\varepsilon}-\Phi_{2,\varepsilon}$ and note
\[
\widetilde\Phi_{1,\varepsilon}-\widetilde\Phi_{2,\varepsilon}
=\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}\ast\delta\Phi_\varepsilon.
\]
Young's inequality gives $\norm{\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}\ast\delta\Phi_\varepsilon}_{L^\infty}\le \norm{\widetilde w^{(\varepsilon)}}_{L^2}\,\norm{\delta\Phi_\varepsilon}_{L^2}$.
The Poincar\'e inequality bounds $\norm{\delta\Phi_\varepsilon}_{L^2}\le C_P \norm{\nabla\delta\Phi_\varepsilon}_{L^2}$.
Combining with the energy estimate yields the stated $L^\infty$ bound.
The multiplicative lapse bound follows from $\widetilde N_{i,\varepsilon}=\exp(-\widetilde\Phi_{i,\varepsilon})$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Practical robustness criterion at resolution \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon$}{epsilon}]
\label{rem:robustness_eps}
If a change of address map (or other protocol parameters) modifies the source density only at scales below the readout resolution, then the coarse-grained sources $\sigma_{1,\varepsilon}$ and $\sigma_{2,\varepsilon}$ are close, and Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson_stability} implies that the coarse-grained potentials $\Phi_{1,\varepsilon}$ and $\Phi_{2,\varepsilon}$ (hence $\lapse_{i,\varepsilon}=\e^{-\Phi_{i,\varepsilon}}$) are close as well.
Thus, sensitivity of physical conclusions to the address map can be made quantitative: it is controlled by the norm of the induced source perturbation after coarse graining.
Equivalently, within the universality class of Definition~\ref{def:eps_universality_class}, the coarse-grained lapse field $N_\varepsilon=\lapse_\varepsilon$ is insensitive to microscopic address/graph details beyond their impact on the coarse-grained source.
\end{remark}
