\section{Origin of matter: topologically locked phase defects and inertial budget}
\label{sec:matter_defects}

\subsection{Topological quantization: a phase Gauss law}
\label{subsec:phase_gauss}

CAP models an ``isolated defect'' as a source of the phase potential $\Phi$ (Section~\ref{subsec:phase_potential}). To stabilize such sources against continuous deformation, one introduces a topological quantization input analogous in structure to Dirac quantization: the flux of phase pressure through a closed surface is quantized,
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\mathrm{top}}
  := \int_{S} \mathbf{P}_{\Phi}\cdot \dd\mathbf{S}
  \in q_{0}\,\ZZ,
  \qquad
  q_{0}=4\pi \ \ (\text{convention}).
\end{equation}
This is the same structural move as magnetic-charge quantization in gauge theory \cite{Dirac1931,WuYang1975}: global consistency of phase (holonomy) turns flux into a topological invariant.
This implies that ``mass'' or ``defect charge'' is not an arbitrary continuously tunable parameter but a label of allowed topological sectors in the readout--phase structure.

\subsection{Mass as sustained budget: internal winding and geometric impedance}
\label{subsec:mass_budget}

Omega Dynamics further proposes an organizing hypothesis: stable matter corresponds to phase errors that cannot be removed by local gauge redefinitions (closed but not exact), i.e.\ \emph{topologically locked defects}. Maintaining a nontrivial topological sector requires persistent implementation budget, which manifests macroscopically as inertial mass and as resistance to propagation (geometric impedance).

In HPA language, such defects can be modeled as localized impedance centers in the scan--phase dynamics: routing around a local algebraic obstruction requires extra scan cycles and accumulates additional geometric phase. From the readout perspective, this appears as propagation delay and curvature, consistent with the computational-lapse dictionary of Section~\ref{subsec:two_times}.

At the current stage, this serves as a phenomenological template. Completing the spectrum from microscopic algebraic/topological data (including stability, interactions, and possible internal winding classification) remains an open task within the broader $\Omega$ program \cite{Ma2025FoP}.
