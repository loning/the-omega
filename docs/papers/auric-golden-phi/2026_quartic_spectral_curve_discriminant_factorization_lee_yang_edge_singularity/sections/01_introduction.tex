\section{Introduction}

The geometry of partition-function zeros provides a unifying description of critical phenomena in statistical physics and analytic combinatorics.
Classical approaches often rely on large-state numerical approximations, whereas finite-dimensional \emph{spectral-closure} models allow one to turn the localization of a ``critical boundary'' into exact algebraic and spectral computations.

In this note we study a family of partition functions \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) controlled by a quartic spectral curve.
The main features are, in the required minimality-first order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the sequence is fully determined by a \emph{minimal} reduced rational generating function (Definition~\ref{def:minimal-spectral-closure}), hence satisfies a minimal linear recurrence of fixed order and has fixed Hankel rank;
  \item the algebraic structure of the branch locus is given by a complete factorization of the discriminant of a quartic spectral polynomial; the only nontrivial real branch point \(\yLY<0\) marks a collision and real-to-complex transition of one spectral pair (Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision}), while the negative-axis dominance pattern is kept as computational evidence only (Section~\ref{sec:zeros}).
  \item an explicit field-trace representation (with denominator compression to \(\{0,1,-1\}\cup\{P_{\mathrm{LY}}=0\}\)) makes the exact algebraic dependency on \(y\) explicit and keeps the analyticity set auditable (Theorem~\ref{thm:trace-representation}, Corollary~\ref{cor:denominator-compression});
  \item a limiting log-moment generating function induced by the dominant spectral branch yields closed-form fluctuation constants, producing auditable limit laws by Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff-nonneg};
  \item all key quantities can be computed (and certified) by finitely many algebraic operations together with an order-\(4\) recurrence, giving a parameter-size-independent verification pipeline;
  \item the spectral curve \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) is isomorphic to the elliptic curve \(E:\xi^2+\xi=\lambda^3-\lambda\) of conductor~\(37\) (Cremona \(37\mathrm{a}1\)); the parameter \(y=\lambda^2+\xi\) defines a degree-\(4\) morphism \(\varphi:E\to\mathbb{P}^1\) whose ramification locus maps onto the branch cubic \(c(y)\) via a resultant identity, and the discriminant of~\(\Pi\) equals the norm of the different of the function field extension \(\QQ(E)/\QQ(y)\); the generic Galois group is~\(S_4\), and the Puiseux amplitudes at nontrivial branch points are governed by a cubic sharing the arithmetic invariant \(3^9\cdot 37\) with the branch cubic discriminant (Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic}).
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model and the associated quartic spectral polynomial.
Section~\ref{sec:hankel} establishes minimality of the order-\(4\) spectral closure and the fixed Hankel rank.
Section~\ref{sec:disc} computes and factors the discriminant, characterizing the branch locus and the spectral-root collision at~\(\yLY\).
Section~\ref{sec:free} derives the free energy and the limiting log-moment generating function, and, by Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff-nonneg}, gives a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem with explicit constants.
Sections~\ref{sec:zeros}--\ref{sec:algo} discuss zero accumulation, and give an auditable computational workflow.
Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic} develops the elliptic normalization and arithmetic structure underlying the spectral curve: ellipticization, ramification and Galois theory, the Abel--Jacobi differential closure, modularity at level~\(37\), the cubic number field of the branch locus, and the Latt\`es dynamics at the bad prime.
