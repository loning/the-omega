\section{Newtonian limit: Poisson equation from Einstein closure and the $\Omega$ action}
\label{app:newtonian_limit}

This appendix makes explicit the weak-field chain used in Section~\ref{subsec:phase_potential}: why a Poisson equation is the correct macroscopic operator in the static, slow-variation limit, and how the $\Omega$ action sources it through an effective energy density.

\subsection{Poisson equation from the weak-field Einstein equation}
\label{app:poisson_from_gr}

In the Newtonian regime (static sources, nonrelativistic velocities, weak fields), write the metric in the standard scalar-potential form
\begin{equation}
  \dd s^{2} = -(1+2\phi)\,\dd t^{2} + (1-2\phi)\,\dd\mathbf{x}^{2},
  \qquad |\phi|\ll 1 .
\end{equation}
To leading order, the $00$ component of the Einstein equation yields the Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \phi = 4\pi G\,\rho,
\end{equation}
where $\rho=T_{00}$ is the energy density in the nonrelativistic limit; see e.g.\ \cite{Wald1984,Carroll2004,MisnerThorneWheeler1973}.
In particular, for isolated sources, $\rho=0$ outside the support of matter and therefore $\Delta\phi=0$ in the exterior region, forcing $\phi(r)=-GM/r$ under asymptotic flatness.

\subsection{Effective energy density from the Fisher-amplitude sector}
\label{app:rho_info}

For the minimal $\Omega$ action \eqref{eq:omega_action}, the information stress tensor is
\begin{equation}
  T^{\mathrm{info}}_{\mu\nu}
  = 2\lambda_{F}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\chi\,\nabla_{\nu}\chi - \frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}(\nabla\chi)^{2}\right)
  - g_{\mu\nu} V(\chi^{2}).
\end{equation}
In a static weak-field configuration with negligible time derivatives of $\chi$, one may read off an effective energy density
\begin{equation}
  \rho_{\mathrm{info}}
  \equiv T^{\mathrm{info}}_{00}
  \approx \lambda_{F}\,(\nabla\chi)^2 + V(\chi^{2}),
\end{equation}
up to higher-order corrections in $\phi$ and in time derivatives.
Therefore, the Newtonian potential sourced by the $\chi$ sector satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\phi \approx 4\pi G\left(\rho_{m}+\rho_{\mathrm{info}}\right),
\end{equation}
which is the precise sense in which mismatch penalties (encoded in $V$) and implementation-gradient energy (encoded in $(\nabla\chi)^2$ through the cost--density map) backreact on macroscopic geometry.

\subsection{Phase potential as a rescaled Newtonian potential}
\label{app:phi_vs_Phi}

Section~\ref{subsec:phase_potential} introduces a phase potential $\Phi$ with $\Delta\Phi=4\pi\rho_{\Phi}$ and phase pressure $\mathbf{P}_{\Phi}=-\nabla\Phi$.
In the weak-field regime, this is consistent with GR provided one identifies $\Phi$ with a rescaled Newtonian potential:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi = \gamma\,\phi,
  \qquad
  \rho_{\Phi} = \gamma\,G\left(\rho_{m}+\rho_{\mathrm{info}}\right),
\end{equation}
for some calibration constant $\gamma$ determined by the chosen normalization of $\Phi$ and of the mismatch density $\sigma$.
With this identification, the ``phase pressure'' acceleration coincides with gravitational acceleration up to the same calibration:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{a} = -\nabla\phi = -\frac{1}{\gamma}\nabla\Phi = \frac{1}{\gamma}\mathbf{P}_{\Phi}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Quantitative fit targets for the toy Poisson experiment}
\label{app:poisson_fit_targets}

For an isolated defect, the exterior prediction is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(r) \approx C_0 - \frac{M}{r},
\end{equation}
with $C_0$ a gauge constant (zero-mean convention in the periodic FFT solver) and $M$ the effective defect strength.
In a periodic box, the far field deviates due to image charges; therefore fits should be performed over an intermediate ``near-field'' radius range where periodic effects are small.
The reference script in Appendix~\ref{app:repro_code} reports least-squares estimates of $(C_0,M)$ and RMS residuals for a chosen fit window.
