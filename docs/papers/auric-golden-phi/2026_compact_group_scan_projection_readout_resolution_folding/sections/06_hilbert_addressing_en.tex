\section{Hilbert Addressing: Locality Interface from Scanning Ticks to Screen Grid Points}

\subsection{Addressing Axioms and Locality Preservation}

Finite observers typically record readouts on discrete screens/registers. Let $\Sigma_n=\{0,\dots,2^n-1\}^d$ be $d$-dimensional screen grid points. Define the \textbf{Hilbert addressing map}
\[
H_n:\{0,\dots,2^{dn}-1\}\to\Sigma_n
\]
as a bijection satisfying first-order locality: adjacent ticks maintain proximity on the screen (e.g., $\ell^1$ distance is 1). This map translates ``temporal ordinal'' to ``spatial position,'' and makes explicit the geometric structure of accessible subalgebras. Different layout groups (e.g., $D_4$) correspond to different readout basis choices, belonging to protocol choice degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Accessible Subalgebras and Observer Sectors}

In the full observable algebra $\mathfrak{A}$, a finite observer can only access the subalgebra $\mathfrak{A}_{\mathrm{obs}}(n,m,\varepsilon)$ generated by finite registers, finite-depth scans, and finite windows. Hilbert addressing provides a way to correspond this subalgebra to screen-local blocks, thus converting ``accessibility'' to geometric locality and compilation path length constraints. We can thereby define ``observer sectors'' as families of subalgebras that remain closed under given resource constraints.
