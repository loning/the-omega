\section{Gravity as entropic response: phase potential and phase pressure}
\label{sec:entropy_force_gravity}

This section summarizes the thermodynamic interpretation of the ``phase pressure'' mechanism developed in the HPA--$\Omega$ literature \cite{Ma2025HPD,Ma2025CAP}. The guiding idea is that coarse-grained readout mismatch acts as a source for an effective potential whose gradient generates an acceleration. In contrast to purely statistical entropic-gravity proposals \cite{Verlinde2011}, the source is \emph{deterministic mismatch} induced by scan--projection incompatibility.

\subsection{Mismatch density and phase potential}

Let $\sigma$ denote a coarse-grained mismatch density (Section~\ref{sec:lapse_thermo}). On macroscopic scales we define the phase potential $\Phi$ as the stationary point of a quadratic functional $S_\Phi[\Phi;\sigma]$ (Appendix~\ref{app:phase_potential}); equivalently, $\Phi$ satisfies the Poisson equation \cite{Jackson1998}:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \Phi = 4\pi\,\rho_\Phi,\qquad \rho_\Phi := \kappa_\Phi\,\sigma,
\label{eq:poisson_phi}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_\Phi$ is a coupling constant. Its normalization can be fixed by matching the exterior monopole term to the Newtonian potential, $\kappa_\Phi Q_\sigma = G M_g$ (Appendix~\ref{app:phase_potential}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:kappa_phi_calibration}). The associated phase-pressure field is the conservative vector field
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_\Phi := -\nabla\Phi.
\end{equation}
For isolated localized sources, Eq.~\eqref{eq:poisson_phi} yields a $1/r$ potential in the weak-field regime, recovering the Newtonian template at leading order (Appendix~\ref{app:phase_potential}).

\subsection{Entropic-force form}

Define a computational free energy functional
\begin{equation}
F_{\mathrm{free}} := E - T_c S_{\mathrm{pf}},
\end{equation}
where $E$ is computational flux and $T_c$ is computational temperature (Section~\ref{sec:first_law}). In a quasi-static approximation, effective dynamics tends to decrease $F_{\mathrm{free}}$. For a degree of freedom with coordinate $\mathbf{x}$, the corresponding force is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{entropic}} := -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} F_{\mathrm{free}}
\;=\; -\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}E + S_{\mathrm{pf}}\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}T_c + T_c\,\nabla_{\mathbf{x}} S_{\mathrm{pf}}.
\label{eq:entropic_force}
\end{equation}
\noindent In the isothermal and weak-backreaction regime ($\nabla T_c\approx 0$ and $|\nabla E|$ subleading for the effective degree of freedom), Eq.~\eqref{eq:entropic_force} reduces to the standard entropic-force form $\mathbf{F}\approx T_c\nabla S$ \cite{LandauLifshitz1980}. When $S_{\mathrm{pf}}$ is controlled by the mismatch density $\sigma$ and $\sigma$ sources $\Phi$, the phase-pressure field $\mathbf{P}_\Phi$ provides a geometric representative of this entropic response. In words: matter flows toward configurations that reduce mismatch production and routing impedance.

\subsection{Black holes as computational blackbodies}

Black-hole thermodynamics suggests that horizons act like thermal boundaries \cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975,Unruh1976}. In the present semantics, the horizon limit corresponds to a strong-overhead region where $\lapse\to 0$ (Section~\ref{sec:lapse_thermo}). External observers then see effective freezing of internal scan dynamics; mismatch that cannot be actively repaired by the slowed local computation is exported through the boundary readout channel and appears approximately thermal at the level of marginal statistics, while correlations can carry information \cite{Ma2025HPD}.

This coexistence of thermal \emph{marginals} with global unitarity is consistent with the modern understanding of the information paradox. In unitary evaporation, the fine-grained entropy of Hawking radiation follows a Page curve \cite{Page1993}. In semiclassical gravity, the island formula (and related replica-wormhole methods) reproduces the Page curve and resolves the paradox within controlled settings \cite{Almheiri2019,Penington2020}. In HPT language: outcome-discarding readout channels naturally generate approximately thermal coarse-grained statistics, while the ontic unitary scan retains the correlations required for information recovery.
