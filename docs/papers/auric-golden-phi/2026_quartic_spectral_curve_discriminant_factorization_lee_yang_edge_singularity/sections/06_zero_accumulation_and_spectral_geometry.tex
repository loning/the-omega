\section{Zero accumulation and the spectral geometry of the boundary}\label{sec:zeros}

\subsection{Background: the Beraha--Kahane--Weiss theorem}

For fixed \(y\), write the characteristic roots of the recurrence as
\[
\lambda_1(y),\dots,\lambda_4(y),
\]
and let \(a_i(y)\) be the spectral coefficients so that
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{i=1}^4 a_i(y)\lambda_i(y)^m,\qquad (y\in\CC\setminus\Branch)
\]
for each \(m\ge0\).

The accumulation behavior of zeros of recursively defined polynomial families is governed by the classical theorem of Beraha, Kahane, and Weiss~\cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1980}.
In a specialized form:

\begin{theorem}[BKW-type zero accumulation]\label{thm:bkw}
Let \(y_0\in\CC\setminus\Branch\) be such that the roots \(\lambda_i(y_0)\) of \(\Pi(\cdot,y_0)\) are simple.
If there exist distinct indices \(i,j\) with
\[
\left|\lambda_i(y_0)\right|=\left|\lambda_j(y_0)\right|=\max_{1\le k\le4}\left|\lambda_k(y_0)\right|,
\]
and \(a_i(y_0)a_j(y_0)\neq0\), then \(y_0\) belongs to the limiting zero accumulation set of \(Z_m\), i.e.
every neighborhood of \(y_0\) contains zeros of \(Z_m\) for arbitrarily large \(m\).

Conversely, if \(y_0\notin\Branch\) is an accumulation point of zeros, then for every neighborhood of \(y_0\) there are \(m\)-indices where the dominant roots are tied in modulus and nonvanishing.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Non-bifurcating equimodular accumulation]\label{cor:equimodular-accum}
If \(y_0\in\RR\setminus\Branch\) is a non-bifurcating equimodular point in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:equimodular-negative} and all tied branches at \(y_0\) have nonzero coefficients, then \(y_0\) belongs to the limiting zero-accumulation set of \(\{Z_m\}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{lemma}[Nonvanishing BKW coefficients off \(\Branch\)]\label{lem:coeff-nonzero}
Assume \(y_0\notin\Branch\cup\{-1\}\), and let \(t_i(y_0)=1/\lambda_i(y_0)\) be the four simple roots of \(D(t,y_0)\).
Then for every \(m\ge0\),
\[
Z_m(y_0)=\sum_{i=1}^4 a_i(y_0)\lambda_i(y_0)^m,
\qquad
a_i(y_0)=-\lambda_i(y_0)\frac{N(t_i(y_0),y_0)}{D_t(t_i(y_0),y_0)}.
\]
Each \(D_t(t_i(y_0),y_0)\neq0\) by simplicity, and \(N(t_i(y_0),y_0)\neq0\) because \(N\) and \(D\) are coprime for \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-order}, Appendix~\ref{app:degeneracies}).
Hence every \(a_i(y_0)\neq0\), in particular \(a_i(y_0)a_j(y_0)\neq0\) whenever \(i\neq j\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bkw} is the standard BKW localization principle specialized to order~4 recurrences and polynomial initial data, as in~\cite{Sokal2001}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Application to our model}

For our family \(\{Z_m\}\), Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor} shows that the branch locus is
\[
\Branch=\{0,1\}\cup\{c(y)=0\}.
\]
Hence any non-branch parameter lies in an open interval of the real axis or a real-analytic region of \(\CC\) where the root moduli are locally constant up to orderings.

\begin{proposition}[Exact real branch geometry]\label{prop:real-branch-endpoints}
The nontrivial real branch endpoint is the unique real root \(\yLY\in(-1.13446,-1.13445)\) of \(c(y)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:unique-real-root}).
By Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision}, one spectral pair collides at \(\yLY\), and this collision is the only nontrivial real branch-point event for \(y\in\RR\).
By Theorem~\ref{thm:yeq}, the negative real axis also has a non-bifurcating equimodular point \(\yEQ\notin\Branch\), where a real branch and a conjugate pair compete in modulus.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:coeff-nonzero}, all competing coefficients are nonzero at \(\yEQ\), so by Theorem~\ref{thm:bkw} this \(\yEQ\) lies in the limiting zero-accumulation set of \((Z_m)\).
\(\yLY\) is therefore a branch endpoint, while \(\yEQ\) is the first algebraic negative-axis point where modulus competition occurs without any branch multiplicity.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor}, \(\Disc_\lambda\Pi=0\) exactly at \(y=0,1\) or \(c(y)=0\).
The cubic discriminant computation in Proposition~\ref{prop:unique-real-root} gives \(c(y)\) one real root \(\yLY\) and two nonreal roots.
Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision} identifies the local collision type at \(y=\yLY\).
No other real branch value exists.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rmk:equimodular-numeric}
\textbf{Numerical observation (non-rigorous):} full global competition geometry and higher-order transition loci are investigated numerically in Section~\ref{sec:algo} and Appendix~\ref{app:pseudocode}. 
This evidence is used only as empirical context and is not part of the current closed-form theorems.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Complex-plane geometry}

\noindent
In the full complex plane, zero accumulation curves are expected to form equimodular competition sets from Theorem~\ref{thm:bkw}; their endpoints and junctions are constrained by \(\Branch\) and by the branch-type collision at~\(\yLY\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision}).
A complete analytic characterization of all such curves is beyond the scope of this paper.
The computational workflow in Section~\ref{sec:algo} and Appendix~\ref{app:pseudocode} compares both coefficient-propagation and contour-counting descriptions against this constrained boundary.
