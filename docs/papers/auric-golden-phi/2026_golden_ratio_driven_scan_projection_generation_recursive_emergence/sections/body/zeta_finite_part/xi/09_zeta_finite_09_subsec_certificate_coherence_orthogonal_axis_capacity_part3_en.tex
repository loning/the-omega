\begin{corollary}[Invisibility threshold under fixed resolution budget (bit/ledger calibration)]\label{cor:xi-offcritical-visibility-threshold-bit-budget}
Under the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression},
if one wishes to \emph{force} a positive defect contribution from this off-critical-line zero at some radius audit point \(\varrho\in(0,1)\) (i.e., require \(\varrho>|w_\rho|\), hence \(D_\zeta(\varrho)\ge \log(\varrho/|w_\rho|)>0\)),
then the endpoint resolution threshold must be satisfied
\[
1-\varrho\ \lesssim\ \frac{\delta}{\gamma^2}
\]
(the high-height scaling law of Corollary \ref{cor:app-horocycle-visible-resolution}).
In particular, if one parametrizes the resolution budget by dyadic bit depth \(b\) such that
\(
1-\varrho\asymp 2^{-b}
\),
then the necessary condition can be equivalently written as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-offcritical-bit-budget}
\boxed{\;
b\log 2\ \gtrsim\ 2\log|\gamma|+\log\frac{1}{\delta}+O(1)\; }.
\end{equation}
In other words: under a fixed budget \(b\), there exists a height threshold such that when \(|\gamma|\) is sufficiently large, the second-order radial signal of off-critical-line deviation must fall into the non-distinguishable scale;
in the pure phase regime (unitary slice), its semantic consequence stably manifests as "non-addressable" (\(\mathsf{NULL}\), Corollary \ref{cor:xi-null-second-order-radial-channel}).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary \ref{cor:app-horocycle-visible-resolution}, the necessary condition is \(1-\varrho\lesssim \delta/\gamma^2\).
Substituting \(1-\varrho\asymp 2^{-b}\) and taking logarithms yields
\(
b\log 2\gtrsim 2\log|\gamma|+\log(1/\delta)+O(1)
\).
The last statement follows from Corollary \ref{cor:xi-null-second-order-radial-channel}'s "second-order radial channel is suppressed by the pure phase regime." This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The leading term \(2\log|\gamma|\) in equation \eqref{eq:xi-offcritical-bit-budget} reflects the irreducible demand on endpoint resolution from height growth;
this scaling is consistent with the "second-order radial channel" of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}.
Within a finite height band \(|\gamma|\le T\), the same compression law also allows one to rewrite the existence of "off-slice" zeros as single-radius defect inequality certificates, thereby obtaining direct band-exclusion conclusions.

\begin{theorem}[Single-radius Jensen defect certificate: verifiable criterion for finite-height band exclusion]\label{thm:xi-jensen-single-radius-band-exclusion}
Let \(T\ge 1\), \(\Delta\in(0,\tfrac12]\), and define
\[
  R(T,\Delta)^2:=\frac{T^2+(\Delta-1)^2}{T^2+(\Delta+1)^2}\in(0,1).
\]
Then for any off-critical-line zero \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) satisfying \(|\gamma|\le T\), \(\delta=\tfrac12-\beta\ge\Delta\), its disk image \(w_\rho\) satisfies
\[
  |w_\rho|\le R(T,\Delta).
\]
Furthermore, for any audit radius \(\varrho\in(R(T,\Delta),1)\), there is a single-zero Jensen lower bound
\[
  D_\zeta(\varrho)\ \ge\ \log\frac{\varrho}{|w_\rho|}\ \ge\ \log\frac{\varrho}{R(T,\Delta)}
\]
(Proposition \ref{prop:app-jensen-single-zero-lower-bound}).
Therefore, if one verifies at a selected radius \(\varrho>R(T,\Delta)\) that
\[
  D_\zeta(\varrho)\ <\ \log\frac{\varrho}{R(T,\Delta)},
\]
then this serves as an off-line verifiable \emph{band-exclusion certificate}: under this protocol calibration, there exists no off-critical-line zero satisfying \(|\gamma|\le T\) and \(\tfrac12-\beta\ge\Delta\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the closed form of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression},
\[
|w_\rho|^2
:=1-\frac{4\delta}{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}
:=\frac{\gamma^2+(1-\delta)^2}{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}.
\]
Note that the right-hand side is monotonically increasing in \(\gamma^2\) and monotonically decreasing in \(\delta\) (within the band \(\delta\in(0,1)\)).
Therefore, under the constraints \(|\gamma|\le T\), \(\delta\ge\Delta\),
\[
|w_\rho|^2
\le \frac{T^2+(1-\Delta)^2}{T^2+(1+\Delta)^2}
:=R(T,\Delta)^2,
\]
from which \(|w_\rho|\le R(T,\Delta)\).
When \(\varrho>|w_\rho|\), Proposition \ref{prop:app-jensen-single-zero-lower-bound} gives
\(
D_\zeta(\varrho)\ge \log(\varrho/|w_\rho|)
\);
using \(|w_\rho|\le R(T,\Delta)\) yields the stated lower bound and certificate form. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit single-point certificate taking \texorpdfstring{$\varrho=\tfrac12(1+R(T,\Delta))$}{varrho=(1+R)/2}]\label{cor:xi-jensen-single-radius-band-exclusion-midradius}
Using the notation of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-jensen-single-radius-band-exclusion}, and let
\(
\varrho:=\tfrac12\bigl(1+R(T,\Delta)\bigr)
\).
If one verifies that
\[
  D_\zeta(\varrho)\ <\ \log\frac{1+R(T,\Delta)}{2R(T,\Delta)},
\]
then it follows that there exists no off-critical-line zero in the band region with \(|\gamma|\le T\) and \(\tfrac12-\beta\ge\Delta\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Substituting \(\varrho=\tfrac12(1+R(T,\Delta))\) into the certificate inequality of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-jensen-single-radius-band-exclusion} yields the result. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Falsifiable restatement of "constructing an off-line zero": the fourth register or failure witness}
In the closure semantics of this paper, "constructing a zero not on \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\)" is not equivalent to giving a numerical point;
it is equivalent to giving a \emph{verifiable address object} that can pass the closure checker (Definition \ref{def:multi_axis_address}), or giving a failure witness that the address cannot possibly exist within the protocol.

\begin{proposition}[The unique falsifiable interface for off-line zero assertions: extension of the mode axis or output of \texorpdfstring{$\mathsf{NULL}$}{NULL} failure witness]\label{prop:xi-offcritical-falsifiable-restatement}
Under the unitary slice visible-domain constraint and \(\mathbf{PW}\) type closure, any assertion claiming "there exists an off-line zero (\(|u|\neq 1\), equivalently \(|w|<1\))" can only manifest within the system in one of the following two ways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Mode axis extension (explicit radial recording)}: Extension readout/certificate type, such that the mode axis \(a_{\mathrm{mode}}\) in the three-axis address prefix explicitly carries radial data (the \(\operatorname{proj}^{\mathrm e}\) calibration of Remark \ref{rem:xi-soundness-vs-completeness}); and at high heights, this extension must pay the endpoint capacity lower bound given by Corollary \ref{cor:xi-offcritical-visibility-threshold-bit-budget}.
  \item \textbf{Failure witness (protocol lockdown)}: Output an off-line verifiable failure witness proving that the assertion cannot be typed under the given visible-domain/closure protocol, so that its readout must degrade to \(\mathsf{NULL}\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offset-null-type-safety}; and see Proposition \ref{prop:xi-null-three-way} for the three-way decomposition of \(\mathsf{NULL}\)).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover: if one allows externalized side information to achieve injectivization with the goal of "reducing \(\mathsf{NULL}\)," then the externalized axis must structurally be a residue/inverse-limit axis (prime-register) rather than an additional continuous coordinate register decoupled from phase (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-pw-no-continuous-hair}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If the mode axis is not extended, then \(|u|\neq 1\) (or \(|w|<1\)) falls outside the visible domain and is directly read out as \(\mathsf{NULL}\) by the filtering of Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null}, and receives type-safe rejection by Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offset-null-type-safety}.
If the mode axis is extended, then by the extension-preservation construction of Remark \ref{rem:xi-soundness-vs-completeness}, one obtains a non-degenerate extension readout; the necessary budget at high heights is given by Corollary \ref{cor:xi-offcritical-visibility-threshold-bit-budget}.
Finally, "externalization must be residue/inverse-limit axis rather than additional continuous register" is item (2) of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-pw-no-continuous-hair}. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dichotomy of off-slice zero events: acceptance or \texorpdfstring{$\mathsf{NULL}$}{NULL}\,+\,off-line witness]\label{cor:xi-offcritical-dichotomy-acceptable-or-null}
Under the visible-domain constraint locked to the unitary slice and \(\mathbf{PW}\) closure, any attempt to objectify an "off-slice zero event" as a verifiable readout can only result in:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Acceptance)} The event passes the typing verification interface of the unitary slice and is accepted, thus falling into the protocol-allowed visible type domain; in this sense, its zero parameter satisfies \(|u_L(s_0)|=1\);
  \item \textbf{(Falsifiable rejection)} The event has no address in the closure and reads out as \(\mathsf{NULL}\); and by Theorem \ref{thm:xi-null-complete-trichotomy-offline}, this \(\mathsf{NULL}\) must be accompanied by an off-line verifiable failure witness, whose support must be non-trivial in one of the three orthogonal directions "out-of-domain rejection / gluing twist / hard resource insufficiency."
\end{enumerate}
Therefore there exists no third path "introducing an additional continuous coordinate register decoupled from phase to circumvent \(\mathsf{NULL}\)": any continuous extension that is to reduce \(\mathsf{NULL}\) must, in the factor sense, be equivalent to externalizing the unique radial register \(\varrho(u)=\log|u|\) (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-unique-continuous-transverse-register}); otherwise it will still be compiled by the closure type system into \(\mathsf{NULL}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The dichotomy comes from Proposition \ref{prop:xi-offcritical-falsifiable-restatement}: off-line zero assertions can only appear in the system as "mode axis extension" or "\(\mathsf{NULL}\) failure witness."
When the assertion is acceptable within the pure phase regime, it must pass the visible-domain filtering of the unitary slice (Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null}), thereby satisfying \(|u_L(s_0)|=1\); when the assertion is not typable, its readout is \(\mathsf{NULL}\), and by Theorem \ref{thm:xi-null-complete-trichotomy-offline} one obtains the off-line witness classification.
The last statement follows from the "no additional continuous hair" principle (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-pw-no-continuous-hair}) and the uniqueness of continuous transverse axes (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-unique-continuous-transverse-register}). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Logarithmic mean potential inversion law under self-dual symmetry]\label{prop:xi-selfdual-mean-potential-inversion}
Let \(A=\{u\in\CC:\ r_0<|u|<r_0^{-1}\}\) and let \(G\) be analytic on \(A\) with \(G\not\equiv 0\).
Suppose there exists an integer \(m\) such that for all \(u\in A\),
\[
G(u)=u^{m}\,\overline{G(1/\overline{u})}.
\]
For \(r\in(r_0,r_0^{-1})\), define the logarithmic mean potential
\[
\mathcal{M}_G(r):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\bigl|G(re^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\bigr|\,\dd\theta.
\]
Then for all \(r\in(r_0,r_0^{-1})\), the strict identity holds
\[
\boxed{\ \mathcal{M}_G(r)=m\log r+\mathcal{M}_G(1/r)\ }.
\]
Equivalently, letting \(r=e^t\), the map \(t\mapsto \mathcal{M}_G(e^t)-\frac{m}{2}t\) is an even function, whose unique possible equilibrium slice is \(t=0\), i.e., \(|u|=1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take \(u=re^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\), then \(1/\overline{u}=r^{-1}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\).
By hypothesis,
\(
|G(re^{\mathrm{i}\theta})|
:=|u|^{m}\,|G(r^{-1}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})|
:=r^{m}|G(r^{-1}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})|
\).
Taking logarithms on both sides and averaging over \(\theta\) yields the stated identity; substituting \(r=e^t\) and rearranging yields the even-parity and equilibrium-slice conclusions. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

The above inversion law gives a strict version of "\(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) is the axis of symmetry": the symmetry is not the interchange of coordinate axes, but the inner and outer domains being mirror images under the self-dual involution; its fixed set \(|u|=1\) thus becomes the unique equilibrium surface of the logarithmic mean potential (after normalization).

\paragraph{Endpoint absorption coefficient: single-point flux compression of the in-disk zero set}
The above endpoint phase current density \(j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)\) (in the finite Blaschke case, this is the endpoint Poisson weight summation) not only gives a "non-cancellable positive lower bound," but also provides a single-point readable radial decay-rate certificate: the in-disk zero set can be compressed into the absorption coefficient at endpoint \(-1\), and read out from the first-order decay rate of \(w=-r\uparrow-1\).

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint absorption coefficient and radial absorption law]\label{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product with zero set \(\{a_k\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{D}\) (counted with multiplicity).
Define the absorption coefficient at endpoint \(-1\)
\[
\Phi_{-1}(B):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{a_k}(-1)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1-|a_k|^2}{|1+a_k|^2}\ \in[0,\infty).
\]
Then as \(r\uparrow 1\), along the endpoint radial \(w=-r\) one has the asymptotic law
\[
\log|B(-r)|=-(1-r)\,\Phi_{-1}(B)+o(1-r).
\]
In particular, \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\) if and only if \(B\equiv 1\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
See Appendix Proposition \ref{prop:app-endpoint-blaschke-radial-absorption}. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint Julia--Carath\'eodory indicator equals absorption coefficient: angular-derivative form of \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_{-1}$}{Phi-(-1)}]\label{thm:xi-endpoint-julia-indicator-equals-absorption}
Under the setting of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}, define the Julia--Carath\'eodory indicator at endpoint \(-1\)
\[
\mathcal{J}_{-1}(B):=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\frac{1-|B(-r)|^2}{1-r^2}.
\]
Then this limit exists and satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;\mathcal{J}_{-1}(B)=\Phi_{-1}(B)\;}
\]
and (the angular derivative exists and is finite)
\[
\boxed{\;\Phi_{-1}(B)=\Re\!\left(-\frac{B'(-1)}{B(-1)}\right).\;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the radial absorption law of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient},
\(
\log|B(-r)|=-(1-r)\Phi_{-1}(B)+o(1-r)
\).
Hence
\[
|B(-r)|^2=\exp\!\bigl(2\log|B(-r)|\bigr)
=\exp\!\bigl(-2(1-r)\Phi_{-1}(B)+o(1-r)\bigr)
=1-2(1-r)\Phi_{-1}(B)+o(1-r),
\]
thus
\(
1-|B(-r)|^2=2(1-r)\Phi_{-1}(B)+o(1-r)
\).
Note that \(1-r^2=2(1-r)+o(1-r)\), so \(\mathcal{J}_{-1}(B)=\Phi_{-1}(B)\).
\par
On the other hand, a finite Blaschke product is analytic in a neighborhood of the closed disk with \(|B(-1)|=1\), hence
\(
B(-r)=B(-1)+(1-r)B'(-1)+o(1-r)
\);
substituting and expanding gives
\(
1-|B(-r)|^2=-2(1-r)\Re\!\bigl(B'(-1)/B(-1)\bigr)+o(1-r)
\),
dividing by \(1-r^2\) and letting \(r\uparrow 1\) yields
\(
\mathcal{J}_{-1}(B)=\Re\!\bigl(-B'(-1)/B(-1)\bigr)
\).
Combining with the first step gives the conclusion. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Additive conservation and single-point tomography of endpoint absorption coefficient]\label{cor:xi-endpoint-absorption-additive-tomography}
Under the setting of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}, the following identities and verifiable readouts hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Additive conservation of endpoint Poisson--Busemann mass)}
  \[
  \Phi_{-1}(B)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathcal{B}_{-1}(a_k)
  \qquad(\text{counted with multiplicity}),
  \]
  where \(\mathcal{B}_{-1}(w)=\frac{1-|w|^2}{|1+w|^2}\) is the exponentialized Busemann coordinate at endpoint \(-1\) (Proposition \ref{prop:app-busemann-poisson-minus1}).
  \item \textbf{(Single-point tomography: reading out total offset along one radial)}
  \[
  \Phi_{-1}(B)=\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\frac{-\log|B(-r)|}{1-r}.
  \]
  \item \textbf{(Certificatization of off-slice offset total)}
  If one further assumes \(a_k=w_{\rho_k}\) is given by the disk image of off-slice zeros \(\rho_k\),
  and let \(\delta_k:=\tfrac12-\Re(\rho_k)=\mathcal{B}_{-1}(w_{\rho_k})\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}),
  then
  \[
  \Phi_{-1}(B)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\delta_k.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Item (1) follows directly from \(P_{a}(-1)=\frac{1-|a|^2}{|1+a|^2}=\mathcal{B}_{-1}(a)\) (Proposition \ref{prop:app-busemann-poisson-minus1}) and the definition \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=\sum_k P_{a_k}(-1)\).
Item (2) follows from dividing both sides of the asymptotic formula of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient} by \(1-r\) and letting \(r\uparrow 1\).
Item (3) follows by combining item (1) with \(\delta_k=\mathcal{B}_{-1}(w_{\rho_k})\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Height compression and endpoint decoupling: visible-scale splitting of the two certificate channels}
Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression} shows: for the disk image \(w_\rho\) of an off-slice zero \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\), the radial gap satisfies
\(
1-|w_\rho|^2=\frac{4\delta}{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}
\)
and undergoes quadratic compression with \(|\gamma|\);
therefore if one attempts to read out the contribution of this zero at a fixed radius using only the radial-gap channel (Jensen circular mean), then one must enter the endpoint boundary-layer resolution \(1-\varrho\lesssim \delta/\gamma^2\), thus triggering an unavoidable resolution/bit-budget threshold (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-offcritical-visibility-threshold-bit-budget}; and see Theorem \ref{thm:xi-jensen-quadratic-resolution-barrier} for the barrier form).
\par
In contrast, the offset coordinate \(\delta=\mathcal{B}_{-1}(w_\rho)\) of the same zero is the horocycle layer label at endpoint \(-1\) (Proposition \ref{prop:app-busemann-poisson-minus1} and Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}), which does not decay with \(|\gamma|\);
under the calibration "offset is carried only by Blaschke data," it directly enters the additive conserved quantity \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=\sum_k \delta_k\) of the endpoint absorption coefficient and can be read out from the single-point radial decay rate (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-endpoint-absorption-additive-tomography}).
Therefore, the verifiable information of "off-slice offset" naturally splits into two orthogonal certificate channels in the closed-loop interface: one radial-gap channel undergoing quadratic radial compression with height, and another endpoint channel decoupled from height and carried by endpoint Poisson--Busemann mass and single-point flux.

\begin{definition}[Two endpoint absorption coefficients]\label{def:xi-endpoint-two-absorption-coefficients}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product with zero set \(\{a_k\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{D}\) (counted with multiplicity).
In addition to the endpoint \(-1\) absorption coefficient \(\Phi_{-1}(B)\) of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}, define the absorption coefficient at endpoint \(+1\)
\[
\Phi_{+1}(B):=\sum_{k=1}^{N}P_{a_k}(+1)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1-|a_k|^2}{|1-a_k|^2}\ \in[0,\infty).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint absorption parity law and linear amplification under Adams scale gates]\label{thm:xi-endpoint-absorption-adams-rescaling}
Let \(B\) be a finite Blaschke product, and let the Adams scale gate be \(\pi_m(w):=w^m\) (\(m\in\NN\)).
Denote the power pullback \(B^{(m)}:=B\circ\pi_m\), i.e., \(B^{(m)}(w)=B(w^m)\).
Then for any \(m\ge 1\),
\[
\boxed{\;
\Phi_{-1}\bigl(B^{(m)}\bigr)=
\begin{cases}
m\,\Phi_{-1}(B),& m\ \text{is odd},\\[2pt]
m\,\Phi_{+1}(B),& m\ \text{is even},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\Phi_{+1}\bigl(B^{(m)}\bigr)=m\,\Phi_{+1}(B).\;}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
First deduce the radial absorption law for endpoint \(+1\) from Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}: let \(\widetilde B(w):=B(-w)\), then \(\widetilde B\) is still a finite Blaschke product with \(\widetilde B(-r)=B(r)\).
Moreover,
\[
\Phi_{-1}(\widetilde B)
:=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1-|(-a_k)|^2}{|1+(-a_k)|^2}
=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1-|a_k|^2}{|1-a_k|^2}
=\Phi_{+1}(B),
\]
so by Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient},
\[
\log|B(r)|
=\log|\widetilde B(-r)|
=-(1-r)\,\Phi_{-1}(\widetilde B)+o(1-r)
=-(1-r)\,\Phi_{+1}(B)+o(1-r)
\qquad(r\uparrow 1).
\]
\par
For \(\Phi_{-1}(B^{(m)})\), take \(w=-r\uparrow-1\), then
\(
B^{(m)}(-r)=B((-r)^m)
\).
If \(m\) is odd, then \((-r)^m=-r^m\uparrow-1\), and by Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient} (applied to \(r^m\uparrow 1\)),
\[
\log|B^{(m)}(-r)|=\log|B(-r^m)|=-(1-r^m)\,\Phi_{-1}(B)+o(1-r^m).
\]
If \(m\) is even, then \((-r)^m=r^m\uparrow+1\), and by the \(+1\) radial absorption law of the previous step,
\[
\log|B^{(m)}(-r)|=\log|B(r^m)|=-(1-r^m)\,\Phi_{+1}(B)+o(1-r^m).
\]
Since \(1-r^m=m(1-r)+o(1-r)\) (\(r\uparrow 1\)), and \(B^{(m)}\) is still a finite Blaschke product to which Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient} applies, we have
\(
\log|B^{(m)}(-r)|=-(1-r)\,\Phi_{-1}(B^{(m)})+o(1-r)
\);
comparing the first-order coefficients yields the stated parity law.
\par
For \(\Phi_{+1}(B^{(m)})\), take \(w=r\uparrow+1\), then \(B^{(m)}(r)=B(r^m)\) and \(r^m\uparrow 1\).
Applying the \(+1\) radial absorption law of the previous step to both \(B\) and \(B^{(m)}\), we get
\[
\log|B^{(m)}(r)|
=\log|B(r^m)|
=-(1-r^m)\,\Phi_{+1}(B)+o(1-r^m)
=-(1-r)\,m\,\Phi_{+1}(B)+o(1-r)
\]
and
\(
\log|B^{(m)}(r)|=-(1-r)\,\Phi_{+1}(B^{(m)})+o(1-r)
\).
Comparing the first-order coefficients gives \(\Phi_{+1}(B^{(m)})=m\Phi_{+1}(B)\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Parity and degeneration/merging of endpoint channels]\label{rem:xi-endpoint-absorption-adams-parity}
The power gate \(\pi_m(w)=w^m\) satisfies \(\pi_m(+1)=+1\) and \(\pi_m(-1)=(-1)^m\) at the endpoints:
for odd \(m\), the endpoint \(-1\) remains invariant, so the \(\Phi_{-1}\) channel is linearly amplified without changing the base point;
for even \(m\), the endpoint \(-1\) is sent to \(+1\), so \(\Phi_{-1}(B^{(m)})\) reads the absorption coefficient \(\Phi_{+1}(B)\) of \(B\) at \(+1\).
In particular, for even \(m\), the dual endpoint channels merge into a single indicator on \(B^{(m)}\):
\[
\Phi_{-1}(B^{(m)})=\Phi_{+1}(B^{(m)})=m\,\Phi_{+1}(B),
\]
so endpoint auditing degenerates to one dimension at this scale.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Auditable terminal discriminant form: one-dimensional endpoint index and multi-scale audit chain]\label{rem:xi-endpoint-absorption-terminal-index}
Under the Hardy/Smirnov factorization and "offset carried only by Blaschke data" calibration, off-slice offset can be compressed into the one-dimensional endpoint index \(\Phi_{-1}(B)\) (with companion endpoint \(+1\) index \(\Phi_{+1}(B)\), Definition \ref{def:xi-endpoint-two-absorption-coefficients}):
on one hand it equals the limit of the single-point radial decay rate \(\lim_{r\uparrow 1}\frac{-\log|B(-r)|}{1-r}\) (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-endpoint-absorption-additive-tomography}), and on the other hand satisfies \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\iff B\equiv 1\) (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}), thus giving an endpoint discriminant interface for "no off-slice offset" independent of radial-gap resolution.
Furthermore, the Adams scale gate \(\pi_m\) gives deterministic linear rescaling of the endpoint index (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-endpoint-absorption-adams-rescaling}):
odd \(m\) preserves the endpoint \(-1\) channel, so the family \(\{\Phi_{-1}(B^{(m)})\}_{m\ \mathrm{odd}}\) forms a lossless multi-scale audit chain; it deterministically amplifies the "total offset" as an additive conserved quantity with integer-coefficient scale parameters, without introducing new continuous degrees of freedom.
Even \(m\) triggers degeneration and merging of endpoint channels (Remark \ref{rem:xi-endpoint-absorption-adams-parity}), causing the chain to lose the distinction between \(-1\) and \(+1\) at that scale.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Multi-scale consistency \texorpdfstring{$\Longrightarrow$}{=>} fixed-slice support: sufficient condition for uniform upper bound of odd Adams gate endpoint budget]\label{thm:xi-multiscale-consistency-implies-rh}
Under the Hardy/Smirnov factorization calibration, let the inner factor of the disk completion object \(F_\zeta\) be a pure Blaschke object \(B\) (no singular inner factor).
If there exists a constant \(M<\infty\) such that for all odd \(m\ge 1\) Adams scale gates \(\pi_m(w)=w^m\), there is a uniform upper bound
\[
\Phi_{-1}\bigl(B\circ \pi_m\bigr)\le M,
\]
then \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\), hence \(B\equiv 1\).
Therefore \(F_\zeta\) has no zeros in the open unit disk \(\mathbb{D}\), and RH holds (Corollary \ref{cor:app-endpoint-phase-certificate} and \S\ref{subsec:unit-disk-inner-outer}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:xi-endpoint-absorption-adams-rescaling}, for odd \(m\),
\(
\Phi_{-1}(B\circ\pi_m)=m\,\Phi_{-1}(B)
\).
If this is uniformly bounded for all odd \(m\), then letting \(m\to\infty\) gives \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\).
By the last sentence of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}, \(B\equiv 1\).
Under the no-singular-inner-factor hypothesis, \(B\equiv 1\) is equivalent to the absence of point-like zero data in the disk, so \(F_\zeta\) has no zeros in \(\mathbb{D}\); by Corollary \ref{cor:app-endpoint-phase-certificate}, this returns to RH. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Horizon renormalization: strict scaling law of endpoint flux}
The endpoint absorption coefficient \(\Phi_{-1}(B)\) is the single-point compression of in-disk point defects; the accompanying scale semantics is given by the "dilation semigroup."
Appendix \eqref{eq:scale-psi-m} introduces the Möbius semigroup \(\{\psi_m\}_{m>0}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{D})\) preserving the fixed points \(\{\pm 1\}\), whose endpoint multiplier satisfies \(\psi_m'(-1)=m^{-1}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:scale-linearization-identity-multipliers}).
The following theorem shows: the endpoint Poisson weight (i.e., the single-atom contribution to flux) is strictly amplified by the factor \(m\) under this scale chain.

\begin{theorem}[Scale covariance of endpoint Poisson weight]\label{thm:xi-endpoint-poisson-weight-scaling}
Let \(m>1\) and \(\psi_m\) as in \eqref{eq:scale-psi-m}.
For the endpoint Poisson weight
\(
P_a(-1):=\frac{1-|a|^2}{|1+a|^2}
\)
at any \(a\in\mathbb{D}\), there is a strict identity
\[
\boxed{\;P_{\psi_m(a)}(-1)=m\,P_a(-1)\;}
\]
Therefore if \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product with zero set \(\{a_k\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathbb{D}\) (counted with multiplicity), and let
\[
a_k^{(n)}:=\psi_{m^n}(a_k),
\]
and further let \(B^{(n)}\) be the Blaschke product with zero set \(\{a_k^{(n)}\}\) (multiplicity preserved), then the endpoint hair flux satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;\Phi_{-1}(B^{(n)})=m^n\,\Phi_{-1}(B)\;}
\]
where \(\Phi_{-1}\) is defined by Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By direct calculation from \eqref{eq:scale-psi-m} (see also the proof of Proposition \ref{prop:scale-linearization-identity-multipliers}), one obtains the identity
\[
\frac{1-\psi_m(a)}{1+\psi_m(a)}=m\,\frac{1-a}{1+a}.
\]
On the other hand, for any \(z\in\mathbb{D}\),
\[
\Re\!\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z}\right)=\frac{1-|z|^2}{|1+z|^2}=P_z(-1),
\]
so taking the real part immediately gives \(P_{\psi_m(a)}(-1)=m\,P_a(-1)\).
The last formula follows from term-by-term summation and the semigroup property \(\psi_{m^n}=\psi_m^{\circ n}\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Cross-scale closure \texorpdfstring{$\Longrightarrow$}{=>} no-hair fixed point]\label{cor:xi-nohair-fixedpoint-flux-closure}
Let \(m>1\) and take the scale pushforward family \(\{B^{(n)}\}_{n\ge 0}\) of the above theorem.
If there exists a constant \(M<\infty\) such that
\[
\sup_{n\ge 0}\Phi_{-1}(B^{(n)})\le M,
\]
then \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\), hence \(B\equiv 1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:xi-endpoint-poisson-weight-scaling}, \(\Phi_{-1}(B^{(n)})=m^n\Phi_{-1}(B)\).
When \(m>1\), if the left-hand side is uniformly bounded in \(n\), then necessarily \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\).
By the last sentence of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}, \(B\equiv 1\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Extreme resonance criterion of endpoint flux (no singular inner factor calibration)]\label{cor:xi-extreme-resonance-criterion}
Under the Hardy/Smirnov condition calibration, write the disk-based completion object as the factorization \(F_{\zeta}=B\,S\,O\) (Appendix \S\ref{subsec:unit-disk-inner-outer}).
Further assume no singular inner factor \(S\equiv 1\), and \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product (zero set counted with multiplicity as \(\{a_k\}\)).
Then the following propositions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(B\equiv 1\) (no point-like zero data in the disk);
  \item \(\Phi_{-1}(B)=0\);
  \item Endpoint phase current density \(j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)=0\), where \(j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi):=\sum_k P_{a_k}(-1)\) (Theorem \ref{thm:app-inner-phase-current-poisson}).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, if there exists an auditable upper bound \(j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)\le M<\infty\), then the total off-critical-line offset satisfies the linear constraint
\[
\sum_k\delta_k\le M.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
When \(S\equiv 1\) and \(B\) is a finite Blaschke product, by Theorem \ref{thm:app-inner-phase-current-poisson},
\(
j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)=\sum_k P_{a_k}(-1)=\Phi_{-1}(B)
\).
Each term \(P_{a_k}(-1)\) is strictly positive, so
\(
j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)=0
\iff
\Phi_{-1}(B)=0
\iff
\{a_k\}=\varnothing
\iff
B\equiv 1
\),
establishing the equivalence.
The last formula follows directly from Corollary \ref{cor:app-endpoint-phase-current-offcritical} (or the upper-bound paragraph of Corollary \ref{cor:app-endpoint-phase-certificate}): \(j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)\ge \sum_k\delta_k\), so if \(j_{\mathrm{in}}(\pi)\le M\), then \(\sum_k\delta_k\le M\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Mathematical formulation of threshold-carrying (extreme resonance)]\label{rem:xi-threshold-carrying-extreme-resonance}
In the disk-based fixed-slice semantics of this paper, the existence of finite Blaschke data \(B\) is equivalent to the point-spectrum residue axis of the minimal realization: its minimal residue dimension is \(\deg B\) (Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-point-spectrum-correspondence}).
Therefore, once \(B\equiv 1\) (i.e., endpoint flux is zero), in-disk discrete point-spectrum resonance is excluded;
if singularities are still allowed, then they can only be carried in a boundary manner on the fixed slice \(|w|=1\) (for example via boundary singular inner factor \(S\) and boundary measure, see Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-defect-zero-fixed-slice-support} and Appendix \S\ref{subsec:unit-disk-inner-outer}).
\par
In this sense, "extreme resonance" can be rigorously understood as: singularities no longer appear in in-disk discrete modes, but are only allowed to appear as threshold boundary support;
this formulation is compatible with the closure semantics of the unitary slice (rejecting additional orthogonal residue axes) and the zero-anomaly calibration of strict Beck--Chevalley/strictification (see the reduction chain of Theorem \ref{thm:dephys-offline-hair-curvature-reduction}).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Resonance quality factor and detection threshold of Toeplitz--PSD]\label{prop:xi-extreme-resonance-toeplitz-threshold}
Using the horizon Carath\'eodory logarithmic derivative \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) and the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}).
If there exists an off-critical-line zero \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) (\(\beta<\tfrac12\), \(\delta:=\tfrac12-\beta>0\)), denote its disk image \(w_\rho=\mathcal{C}(\gamma+\mathrm{i}\delta)\in\mathbb{D}\), and define the resonance quality factor
\[
Q_\rho:=\frac{1}{1-|w_\rho|^2}=\frac{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}{4\delta}
\qquad\text{(Corollary \ref{cor:app-offcritical-horocycle-busemann})}.
\]
Then the in-disk pole leaves an explicit leading-term imprint in the multipole moments \(\{c_n\}\) of \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:app-horizon-pole-imprint}), so under the regime "remainder has auditable upper bound," finite-level verification of Toeplitz--PSD has an intrinsic detection threshold: if one wishes to exclude this pole with a finite-level PSD certificate (i.e., force the appearance of a finite-dimensional failure witness), then the required moment order \(N\) must satisfy
\[
N\ \gtrsim\ Q_\rho\,\log\!\bigl(1+\delta Q_\rho\bigr)
\asymp \frac{\gamma^2}{\delta}\,\log(1+\gamma^2)
\]
(Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-detection-threshold}; and see Remark \ref{rem:app-horizon-toeplitz-witness-endpoint-focus} for the explanation of endpoint focusing).
\end{proposition}

Finally it must be emphasized: "zero leakage" should be understood as a verifiable regime rather than a single-point accident.
On the disk-based completion \(F_\zeta\), RH is equivalent to defect identically zero \(D_{\zeta}(\varrho)\equiv 0\) (Corollary \ref{cor:app-rh-iff-jensen-defect-zero}), and also equivalent to the countable constraint \(D_{\zeta}(\varrho_n)=0\) for any approach sequence \(\varrho_n\uparrow 1\) (Theorem \ref{thm:app-jensen-countable-criterion}).
Under this "zero leakage regime," the Jensen--Blaschke mechanism excludes all in-disk point-like zeros; combined with the fixed slice \(|u|=1\iff \Re(s)=\tfrac12\) of the completion self-dual parameter \(u=u(s)\), one obtains: point-like zeros can only be carried on the fixed slice, hence can only be carried on \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
As a complement, the role of Ramanujan-type symmetry in this paper's framework is not "interchange of axes," but rather forcing phase-based closure through local spectral bounds, thereby channel-by-channel eliminating amplitude/radial leakage (\S\ref{subsubsec:ramanujan-phase-lift-symmetry}; Theorem \ref{thm:ramanujan-phase-lift-principle}).
\par
Therefore in this paper's system, the unitary slice is the minimal section of continuous visible parameters: radial information deviating from this section enters at the endpoint layer with the quadratic compression law (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}), its visibilization requires irreducible endpoint resolution/bit budget (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-endpoint-resolution-lower-bound}), and can be certificatized by single-radius Jensen defect inequalities on finite-height bands (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-jensen-single-radius-band-exclusion}).
Comoving localization can develop offset at linear scale, but the cost is introducing explicitly-given addressable prefixes (Remark \ref{rem:xi-comoving-horizon-localization-address-null}); under the fixed visible interface, if one rejects externalizing this prefix, then off-slice offset is still forced to enter through the endpoint quadratic radial channel and trigger the corresponding capacity threshold.
The above budget axes are mutually orthogonal and non-substitutable in the protocol semantics (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-null-complete-trichotomy-offline}): raising the budget of one axis cannot offset the threshold violation of another axis; when rejecting mode-axis externalization, "off-slice assertions" cannot form verifiable addresses at the type layer and stably degrade to \(\mathsf{NULL}\).
