\section{Introduction}

The tension between discrete observation and continuous structure permeates multiple levels: physical measurement, computational accessibility, and arithmetic/geometric encoding. If we restrict the observer to a finite-resource entity, then ``accessible information'' is not given directly by the intrinsic state's continuity, but rather mediated through a protocol: choosing readable pointers, setting resolution, executing scans, performing projections, and forming discrete records in finite memory. This process simultaneously generates probability and an irreversible effective arrow of time, as well as stable grammar and compression structures, so that observational sequences can exhibit ``thermalization'' characteristics in marginal distributions while retaining nontrivial information in long-range correlations.

The goal of this paper is not to give an extrinsic interpretation of some pre-given theory, but rather to provide a unified formal framework that is precise, computable, and auditable, placing the following elements in the same formal chain:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Definability of conceptual existence:} A concept first exists as an addressed object at the semantic/protocol level; the undefined is formally ``null'' rather than ``false''. Readout projects it into a binary event.
  \item \textbf{Compact group scan and binary projection readout:} Scanning is realized by rotations on compact groups (or more generally, group actions); readout is a finite-resolution POVM of pointer phase (or its discrete approximation), thus forming binary/finite-alphabet sequences.
  \item \textbf{Extremal stability of golden branches:} Golden ratio-related slopes possess extremal ``phase-locking resistance'' properties in the sense of rational approximation, inducing the slowest pseudo-periodic appearance, thereby providing low-complexity and stable scanning reference for finite observers.
  \item \textbf{Resolution folding and stable sectors:} Within a finite viewing window of length $m$, the primitive $2^m$ words can be compressed via a folding map to the stable language of ``no adjacent $1$'s'', whose size is the Fibonacci number $F_{m+2}$, producing explicitly characterizable fiber degenerate structures.
  \item \textbf{Hilbert addressing and compute---geometry dictionary:} Map scanning tick ordinals through Hilbert addressing to screen grid points to preserve locality and form accessible subalgebras; compilation routing overhead fields further induce effective lapse, forming correspondence from computational complexity to geometric redshift.
  \item \textbf{Arithmetic consistency verification via prime skeleton:} Process scanning data through cusp evaluation, $q$-expansion, and Hecke verification to obtain recursive constraints generated by primes, thus implementing rigid consistency checks on finite data.
\end{enumerate}

To avoid unverifiable narrative, this paper adopts ``hierarchical discipline'': at the ``construction layer'' we use only operational definitions and provable propositions; at the ``interpretation layer'' we discuss possible correspondences to quantum measurement, thermodynamics, gravity-like effects, and arithmetic rigidity, explicitly marking their verifiable/falsifiable interfaces.
