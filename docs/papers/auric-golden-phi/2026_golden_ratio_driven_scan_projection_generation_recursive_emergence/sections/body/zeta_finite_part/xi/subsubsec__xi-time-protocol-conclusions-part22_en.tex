\paragraph{Arithmetic addressing, Smith invariants, and computable governance of connectivity hierarchy}
Under the discrete residual calibration of Conclusions \ref{con:xi-tree-potential-cycle-basis-residual-decomposition}--\ref{con:xi-connectivity-hierarchy-finite-critical-budgets}, zero-residual equivalence classes and their merger chains with budget can not only be described graph-theoretically, but can be further compressed to quotient structures of finite abelian groups and frozen predicates of Smith invariants.
The following conclusions completely reduce "connectivity growth" to a verifiable arithmetic quotient chain, and provide an algorithmic governance framework outputtable in a single scan.

\begin{conclusion}[Integer lattice coset characterization of zero-residual reachability and existence-uniqueness of arithmetic addressing]\label{con:xi-arithmetic-address-existence-uniqueness}
Let \(G=(V,E)\) be a finite graph.
Each directed edge \(e:u\to v\) is assigned a residual label \(r(e)\in\ZZ^d\), with the convention that the reverse edge satisfies \(r(e^{-1})=-r(e)\);
it is also assigned a budget threshold \(c(e)\in\NN\).
For budget \(B\), define the subgraph \(G_{\le B}\) (edges satisfying \(c(e)\le B\)).
If \(u,v\) lie in the same connected component of \(G_{\le B}\), let
\[
R_B(u,v)\subseteq\ZZ^d
\]
denote the set of all path residual sums from \(u\) to \(v\).
Then there exists a sublattice \(H_B\subseteq\ZZ^d\) of rank at most \(d\) (generated by all cycle residuals in that component, independent of spanning tree choice), such that for any \(u,v\) in that component,
\[
R_B(u,v)=\alpha_B(v)-\alpha_B(u)+H_B,
\]
where \(\alpha_B:V\to\ZZ^d\) can be taken as any spanning tree potential function (the \(\Phi_B\) of Conclusion \ref{con:xi-tree-potential-cycle-basis-residual-decomposition}).
This defines the arithmetic address
\[
a_B:V\to Q_B:=\ZZ^d/H_B,
\qquad
a_B(v):=\alpha_B(v)\bmod H_B,
\]
then this \(a_B\) is unique up to constant translation, and
\[
0\in R_B(u,v)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad a_B(u)=a_B(v).
\]
Thus, zero-residual equivalence classes at budget \(B\) are strictly equivalent to the fiber decomposition of the arithmetic address.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Functoriality of arithmetic addressing under budget filtration: connectivity hierarchy as a finite commutative quotient chain]\label{con:xi-arithmetic-address-functorial-quotient-chain}
Using the notation of Conclusion \ref{con:xi-arithmetic-address-existence-uniqueness}, sublattice monotonicity \(H_B\subseteq H_{B'}\) holds with budget growth (\(B\le B'\)), inducing a surjective homomorphism
\[
\pi_{B}^{B'}:Q_B\to Q_{B'}
\]
satisfying \(a_{B'}=\pi_{B}^{B'}\circ a_B\).
In particular, the zero-residual equivalence relation family \((\sim_B)\) is completely controlled within each connected component by a finite abelian group quotient chain: any class merger from \(B\le B'\) corresponds exactly to further quotienting \(Q_B\) by the subgroup generated by newly added cycle residuals.
Thus, all observable effects of connectivity growth can be reduced to the monotonic quotient structure of \(\{Q_B\}\), independent of redundant path details.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Single cycle residual merger factor law: merger amplitude equals its order in the old quotient group]\label{con:xi-single-cycle-merge-factor-order}
Fix budget \(B\), consider adding a single edge \(e\) (with threshold \(B^+\)) within a connected component, such that in \(G_{\le B^+}\) a new independent cycle residual vector \(h\in\ZZ^d\) is formed for the first time, satisfying
\[
h\notin H_B,
\qquad
H_{B^+}=H_B+\ZZ h.
\]
Denote the order of the element \([h]\) in the old quotient group \(Q_B=\ZZ^d/H_B\) by \(m\) (i.e., the minimal positive integer \(m\) such that \(mh\in H_B\)).
Then the new quotient group satisfies
\[
Q_{B^+}\cong Q_B/\langle [h]\rangle,
\]
so that the maximum possible merger factor of zero-residual equivalence classes in that component precisely equals \(m\):
any two points \(u,v\) whose address difference falls within \(\langle[h]\rangle\) at budget \(B\) must be merged at \(B^+\); and except for mergers forced by \(\langle[h]\rangle\), no additional mergers occur.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Prime decomposition independence: connectivity hierarchy separable by \(p\)-primary components for each prime]\label{con:xi-prime-decomposition-independence}
Still under the setting of Conclusions \ref{con:xi-arithmetic-address-existence-uniqueness}--\ref{con:xi-single-cycle-merge-factor-order}, suppose that within a certain budget range, \(H_B\) reaches full rank \(d\), so that \(Q_B\) is a finite abelian group.
For any prime \(p\), denote by \(Q_B^{(p)}\) its \(p\)-primary subgroup.
Then the arithmetic address induces a decomposition \(a_B=(a_B^{(p)})_p\), and
\[
u\sim_B v\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall p,\ a_B^{(p)}(u)=a_B^{(p)}(v).
\]
Moreover, class mergers caused by budget lifting are mutually independent across \(p\)-primary components: if the merger factor of a newly added cycle residual \([h]\in Q_B\) is \(m=\mathrm{ord}([h])\), then its prime factorization
\(
m=\prod_p p^{\nu_p(m)}
\)
precisely corresponds to the merger depth \(\nu_p(m)\) at each \(Q_B^{(p)}\).
Thus, connectivity growth can be decoupled in a number-theoretic sense prime-by-prime, and controlled by a set of mutually independent \(p\)-adic towers.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Arithmetic closed form for optimal fingerprint bit-length: determined by quotient group order, not node count]\label{con:xi-optimal-fingerprint-bitlength-arithmetic-closed-form}
In the full-rank case, suppose the arithmetic address image generates all of \(Q_B\) (e.g., there exist \(d\) vertices whose address differences generate \(\ZZ^d/H_B\)).
Then any collision-free deterministic encoding of zero-residual equivalence classes at budget \(B\) requires a fingerprint length \(k_B\) satisfying
\[
k_B\ge \left\lceil\log_2|Q_B|\right\rceil,
\]
and there exist constructions achieving this lower bound.
Furthermore, if a pairwise collision probability upper bound of \(\delta_B\) is allowed, then the minimal achievable bit-length satisfies
\[
k_B=\left\lceil\log_2|Q_B|+\log_2\frac1{\delta_B}\right\rceil.
\]
Thus, in this model, the optimal fingerprint complexity is determined by the closed-form quantity
\(
|Q_B|=[\ZZ^d:H_B]
\),
independent of redundant edges, path counts, and \(|V|\) growth (consistent with the information-theoretic lower bound of Conclusion \ref{con:xi-multiscale-fingerprint-optimal-bitlength}, and providing an arithmetically attainable closed form here).
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Stabilization criterion: connectivity ceases to grow if and only if Smith invariants of the cycle lattice freeze]\label{con:xi-smith-invariants-freeze-stabilization}
Within the same component, let \(I_B:=[\ZZ^d:H_B]\) (finite index in the full-rank case).
Then \(I_B\) is monotonically non-increasing with budget growth, and there exists a budget \(B_\ast\) such that the equivalence relation no longer merges for all \(B\ge B_\ast\), if and only if there exists \(B_\ast\) such that \(I_B=I_{B_\ast}\) for all \(B\ge B_\ast\);
equivalently, the Smith invariant sequence \((s_1(B)\mid\cdots\mid s_d(B))\) of the sublattice \(H_B\) becomes constant for \(B\ge B_\ast\).
Thus, whether connectivity has saturated can be determined by the freezing of finite abelian group invariants, without tracking the entire redundant evolution of the graph.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Single-pass governance algorithm: simultaneously outputting critical merger chain and verifiable generators for arithmetic address basis]\label{con:xi-one-pass-governance-algorithm}
Sort all edges by threshold \(c(e)\) in non-decreasing order and scan one-by-one, maintaining within each connected component:
a union-find structure (tracking connected components of \(G_{\le B}\)), and an incremental Smith form of the cycle residual generator matrix (tracking invariants and index \(I_B\) of the sublattice \(H_B\subseteq\ZZ^d\)).
Then there exists a single-pass algorithm with time complexity
\(
O(|E|\cdot \mathrm{poly}(d)\cdot \log R)
\)
(where \(R\) is the upper bound on residual coordinate magnitude) that simultaneously outputs:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All critical budget points and their corresponding merger factors (order \(m\) computed via Conclusion \ref{con:xi-single-cycle-merge-factor-order});
  \item The minimal verifiable cycle generator set at each budget level (providing a Smith basis for \(H_B\));
  \item The arithmetic address \(a_B(v)\) of each vertex (represented in invariant component coordinates), enabling \(O(d)\)-time determination of whether any pair of vertices is zero-residual equivalent.
\end{enumerate}
This conclusion provides a computable and verifiable governance theorem: connectivity hierarchy and audit structure can be simultaneously extracted and output with certificated generators in a single scan.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Merger depth lower bound: \(p\)-adic precision of cycle residuals limits connectivity convergence rate]\label{con:xi-merge-depth-lower-bound-padic}
In the full-rank case, fix a prime \(p\).
Suppose that during the budget lifting process, each newly introduced independent cycle residual \([h]\in Q_B\) has \(p\)-order at most \(p^{t}\), i.e., for all critical steps,
\(
\nu_p(\mathrm{ord}([h]))\le t
\).
Denote the index of the \(p\)-primary component at some initial budget \(B_0\) by \(|Q_{B_0}^{(p)}|=p^{E}\).
Then to completely collapse the \(p\)-primary component to trivial (thereby eliminating all class splitting caused by \(p\)-adic differences in that component), the number of critical merger steps is at least
\[
\left\lceil \frac{E}{t}\right\rceil.
\]
Thus, when the \(p\)-adic order of auditable cycle certificates is bounded, connectivity growth possesses an insurmountable convergence rate lower bound; this bound is precisely characterized by the purely arithmetic quantities \(E\) and the \(p\)-adic information amount \(t\) providable per step.
\end{conclusion}
