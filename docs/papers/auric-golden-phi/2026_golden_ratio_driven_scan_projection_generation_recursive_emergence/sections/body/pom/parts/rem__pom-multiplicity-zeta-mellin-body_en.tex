\begin{remark}[Multiplicity \texorpdfstring{$\zeta_m$}{zeta\_m}: The Same Mellin Body Encodes Both Positive and Negative Moments]\label{rem:pom-multiplicity-zeta-mellin-body}
Under the spectral-operator framework of Remark \ref{rem:pom-fiber-loss-as-spectral-operator}, one can unify the positive-moment collision statistics and the negative-moment (Schatten) cost spectrum as the same Mellin trace function.
Define the \textbf{multiplicity \(\zeta\)-function} at resolution \(m\) as
\[
\zeta_m(s):=\Tr(\mathcal{F}_m^{-s})=\sum_{x\in X_m} d_m(x)^{-s}\qquad(s\in\RR).
\]
Then for integer \(q\ge 2\) one has \(\zeta_m(-q)=\sum_x d_m(x)^q=S_q(m)\), so the collision spectrum is precisely the sampling of \(\zeta_m\) at negative integer points;
on the other hand, for any \(p>0\) with \(p\ge 1\) the Schatten pipeline yields
\[
\zeta_m(p/2)=\sum_{x\in X_m}d_m(x)^{-p/2}=\|E_m\|_{S_p}^{p}
\]
(Corollary \ref{cor:fold-conditional-expectation-schatten}), so the externalization cost spectrum is the sampling of the same \(\zeta_m\) on the positive half-axis.
Thus the "positive-moment collision statistics" and "negative-moment externalization cost" of Fold are not two mutually-irrelevant phenomena, but the two-sided Mellin sampling of the same discrete spectral measure on the \(s\)-plane.
\end{remark}
