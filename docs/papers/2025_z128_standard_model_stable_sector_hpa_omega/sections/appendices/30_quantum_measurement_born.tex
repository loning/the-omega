\subsection{Quantum readout and Born probabilities (self-contained interface closure)}
\label{app:quantum_measurement_born}

This appendix records a minimal quantum interface package compatible with the tick\,+\,CAP discipline:
finite observers have an effective Hilbert space; readout is finite resolution; probabilities are Born probabilities for that readout.
We then record two complementary closure routes for the Born rule:
(i) a protocol-counting template aligned with projection-fiber semantics, and
(ii) a mature uniqueness theorem (Gleason--Busch) that characterizes quantum probabilities from noncontextual additivity.

\subsubsection{Finite-resolution readout as POVMs and instruments}
\label{subsec:z128_povm_instruments}

\paragraph{POVM readout.}
Let $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ be an effective observer sector and let $\rho$ be a density operator on $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}$.
Finite-resolution readout is modeled by a POVM $\{E_k\}_k$ with $E_k\succeq 0$ and $\sum_k E_k=I$, giving Born probabilities
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:z128_born_povm}
P_k=\Tr(\rho\,E_k).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Instruments and state update.}
An associated instrument can be written in Kraus form $\rho\mapsto M_k\rho M_k^\dagger/P_k$ with $E_k=M_k^\dagger M_k$.
Any POVM can be realized by a dilation (system--ancilla unitary followed by a projective measurement on the ancilla), by Naimark/Stinespring theorems; see, e.g., \cite{Naimark1940,Stinespring1955,Kraus1983,NielsenChuang2000}.

\subsubsection{Born weights from projection-induced counting (protocol template)}
\label{subsec:z128_born_counting_template}

\paragraph{Counting semantics.}
\InterfaceTag Finite observability already induces equivalence classes: many microdescriptions map to the same coarse outcome.
If a coarse outcome label $k$ corresponds to a degeneracy class of $n_k$ admissible micro-realizations within a fine-grained protocol ensemble, and if the induced micro-measure is uniform over admissible micro-realizations (a sharp-readout symmetry hypothesis), then coarse probabilities are counting ratios $P_k=n_k/N$.

\begin{theorem}[Born weights as counting under a uniform micro-measure (template)]
\label{thm:z128_born_counting_template}
Let $|\psi\rangle=\sum_k c_k|k\rangle$ be a normalized state.
Assume a fine-grained realization in which each coarse branch $k$ corresponds to $n_k$ admissible micro-realizations in a finite ensemble of size $N_{\mathrm{micro}}$, and assume the induced micro-measure is uniform on admissible micro-realizations.
If $|c_k|^2\approx n_k/N_{\mathrm{micro}}$ in the fine-graining limit, then the induced coarse outcome probabilities satisfy
\[
P_k=\frac{n_k}{N_{\mathrm{micro}}}\approx |c_k|^2,
\]
and in the limit one obtains $P_k=|c_k|^2$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Relation to z128 fibers.}
\InterfaceTag In the z128 finite folding language, stable readout labels already carry fibers $P(w)=\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)$.
The counting template above is the quantum-probability analogue: a coarse outcome corresponds to a multiplicity class in a fine-grained ensemble, and probabilities arise as normalized multiplicities.

\subsubsection{Born rule as the unique noncontextual probability rule (mature theorem)}
\label{subsec:z128_gleason_busch}

\begin{theorem}[Gleason--Busch characterization of quantum probabilities]
\label{thm:z128_gleason_busch}
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a complex Hilbert space.
Suppose a probability assignment $\mu$ maps each orthogonal projector $P$ to $\mu(P)\in[0,1]$ and is finitely additive on orthogonal families: for any pairwise orthogonal projectors $\{P_i\}$ with $\sum_i P_i = I$, one has $\sum_i \mu(P_i)=1$.
If $\dim\mathcal{H}\ge 3$, then there exists a unique density matrix $\rho$ such that
\[
\mu(P)=\Tr(\rho\,P)\qquad\text{for all projectors }P.
\]
For $\dim\mathcal{H}=2$, an analogous conclusion holds if one assumes the same additivity on the full set of POVM effects (Busch's extension).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
See Gleason's theorem \cite{Gleason1957} and Busch's POVM extension \cite{Busch2003}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation in the tick+CAP discipline.}
\InterfaceTag The theorem can be read as a rigidity statement: once one commits to noncontextual additivity for readout probabilities on an effective Hilbert space (a natural semantic stance for finite observers), the Born form is forced.
In that sense, the Born rule is not an extra dynamical axiom but the unique probability rule compatible with the declared readout semantics.
