\subsubsection{From Self-Dual Compression to One-Variable \texorpdfstring{$\Xi$}{Xi}: Strict Functional Equation and Critical Line Real-Valuedness}\label{subsec:xi-from-self-dual}
This section compresses the appendix's ``self-dual + completion + unit-circle real-valuedness'' triple into a one-variable \(\Xi\)-object, and interprets the functional equation as the naturality constraint of the automorphism of the completion projective system on sections; correspondingly, the ``critical line'' is no longer regarded as a symmetry axis in the \(s\)-plane but is interpreted as the unitary slice (\(|u|=1\)) on the parameter side---the protocol-visible slice.

\paragraph{Self-dual condition}
Let \(\Delta(z,u)\) be a two-variable polynomial/analytic function (in the kernel caliber of this paper, typically
\(\Delta(z,u)=\det(I-zB(u))\), see \S\ref{subsec:zeta-online-kernel}), satisfying the two-variable self-dual identity
\[
\boxed{\ \Delta(z,u)=\Delta(uz,1/u)\ }.
\]
For the synchronization kernel weighted case, this identity is given in Appendix \ref{app:kernel-self-dual}; and from it the completion parity and coefficient palindrome constraints are derived (e.g., Corollary \ref{cor:delta-coeff-palindrome}).

\paragraph{New one-variable object: arbitrary base \texorpdfstring{$L>1$}{L>1}}
Take any constant base \(L>1\) and define
\[
z_L(s):=L^{-s},\qquad u_L(s):=L^{2s-1},
\]
and define the one-variable function
\[
\boxed{\ \Xi_{\Delta,L}(s):=\Delta\!\bigl(z_L(s),u_L(s)\bigr)=\Delta\!\bigl(L^{-s},L^{2s-1}\bigr)\ }.
\]
When taking \(L=\lambda=\rho(B(1))\), this coincides with the section \(\Xi(s)\) of Appendix \ref{app:kernel-functional-equation} (Proposition \ref{prop:kernel-xi-functional-equation}).

\begin{proposition}[Strict functional equation: sectional naturality of the projective system automorphism]\label{prop:xi-delta-L-functional-equation}
Under the above self-dual condition, for any \(L>1\) the strict identity
\[
\boxed{\ \Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=\Xi_{\Delta,L}(1-s)\ }
\]
holds.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(\tau:(z,u)\mapsto(uz,u^{-1})\). The self-dual identity \(\Delta(z,u)=\Delta(uz,1/u)\) is equivalent to \(\Delta=\Delta\circ\tau\).
For any \(L>1\), direct computation gives
\[
u_L(1-s)=u_L(s)^{-1},\qquad z_L(1-s)=u_L(s)\,z_L(s),
\]
whence \((z_L(1-s),u_L(1-s))=\tau(z_L(s),u_L(s))\).
Therefore
\[
\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)
=\Delta\!\bigl(z_L(s),u_L(s)\bigr)
=\Delta\!\Bigl(\tau\bigl(z_L(s),u_L(s)\bigr)\Bigr)
=\Delta\!\bigl(z_L(1-s),u_L(1-s)\bigr)
=\Xi_{\Delta,L}(1-s).
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Projective system automorphism: the object of the functional equation]\label{rem:xi-projection-system-automorphism}
The self-dual identity \(\Delta(z,u)=\Delta(uz,1/u)\) can be viewed as an involution on the two-variable interface:
define
\[
\tau:\ (z,u)\longmapsto (uz,u^{-1}),
\qquad \tau^2=\mathrm{id}.
\]
Then the self-dual condition is equivalent to \(\Delta=\Delta\circ\tau\), i.e., \(\Delta\) is invariant under the automorphism \(\tau\) of the completion projective system.
\par
For any \(L>1\), the completion section map \(s\mapsto (z_L(s),u_L(s))\) and \(s\mapsto 1-s\) are strictly aligned on this interface:
\[
(z_L(1-s),u_L(1-s))
=(L^{s-1},L^{1-2s})
\;=\;
\bigl(u_L(s)\,z_L(s),\,u_L(s)^{-1}\bigr)
=\tau\bigl(z_L(s),u_L(s)\bigr).
\]
Therefore, the functional equation of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-delta-L-functional-equation} is not ``symmetry about some geometric axis,'' but rather the pullback of \(\tau\)-invariance to the completion section. This caliber also explains why the unitary-slice verification protocol naturally locks onto the visible domain \(|u|=1\): this is a parameter slice compatible with \(\tau\) that supports Hardy-type real-valuedness and spectral crossing audits.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Completion and ``critical line real-valuedness''}
Write
\[
\Delta(z,u)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_j(u)\,z^j.
\]
If the coefficients satisfy the completion palindrome structure \(c_j(u)=u^j c_j(1/u)\) (Corollary \ref{cor:delta-coeff-palindrome}), then one can define the completion coefficients
\[
d_j(u):=u^{-j/2}c_j(u).
\]
Appendix Corollary \ref{cor:completion-real-line} shows: when \(|u|=1\), \(d_j(u)\in\RR\) (after completion, ``coefficients on the unit circle are real'').

\begin{proposition}[Critical line real-valuedness: completion \(\Rightarrow\) Hardy-type real-valuedness]\label{prop:xi-delta-L-critical-line-real}
Under the self-dual condition and the conditions of Corollary \ref{cor:completion-real-line}, for any \(L>1\) and any real number \(t\),
\[
\boxed{\ \Xi_{\Delta,L}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\in\RR\ }.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take \(s=\tfrac12+it\), then \(u_L(s)=L^{2it}\) satisfies \(|u_L(s)|=1\), and
\[
\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)
=\Delta(L^{-s},u_L(s))
=\sum_{j=0}^{N}c_j(u_L(s))\,L^{-js}
=\sum_{j=0}^{N}d_j(u_L(s))\,\bigl(L^{-1/2}\bigr)^{j}\,e^{-ijt\log L}.
\]
Since \(|u_L(s)|=1\Rightarrow d_j(u_L(s))\in\RR\), the above expression is the evaluation at \(t\) of a ``real-coefficient'' trigonometric polynomial in the real variable \(e^{-it\log L}\), and hence its value is real. Equivalently, one may replace \(\log\lambda\) by \(\log L\) in the proof of Appendix Proposition \ref{prop:kernel-hardy-real-even} for the Hardy real-valuedness \(Z(t)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Critical line \texorpdfstring{$\Leftrightarrow$}{<->} unit circle slice]\label{rem:xi-critical-line-unit-circle}
From \(u_L(s)=L^{2s-1}\) it is clear: the line \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) corresponds exactly to \(|u_L(s)|=1\).
Therefore, in the verifiable semantics of this paper, the ``critical line'' is not an additional geometric axiom, still less a symmetry axis in the \(s\)-plane; it is the unitary slice of the completion projective system on the parameter side, and at the protocol level is fixed as the unique continuous slice of the visible domain \(X_{\mathrm{vis}}=\mathsf{Phase}\) (Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null}).
Combined with Remark \ref{rem:xi-projection-system-automorphism}, the structural object of the functional equation is the projective system automorphism \(\tau\), and the unitary slice is the audit slice that remains stable under projection of this automorphism to the visible domain: on this slice the completion coefficients can be taken to be real, thereby supporting Hardy-type real-valuedness and the spectral crossing interface.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Determinant rewriting on the unitary slice and spectral crossing criterion]\label{prop:xi-unitary-slice-spectral-crossing}
Under the self-dual condition, further assume
\(
\Delta(z,u)=\det(I-zB(u))
\)
comes from a finite-dimensional (auditable) matrix family \(B(u)\) (e.g., the synchronization kernel weighted case, see \S\ref{subsec:zeta-online-kernel} and Appendix \ref{app:kernel-self-dual}).
Fix \(L>1\) and let \(\theta:=2t\log L\).
Then for any \(t\in\RR\), one has the strict identity
\[
\boxed{\;
\Xi_{\Delta,L}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)
=
\det\!\Bigl(I-U_L(\theta)\Bigr),\qquad
U_L(\theta):=L^{-1/2}e^{-i\theta/2}\,B(e^{i\theta})\; }.
\]
Therefore the critical line zeros satisfy the equivalent criterion
\[
\boxed{\;
\Xi_{\Delta,L}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)=0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
1\in\mathrm{Spec}\bigl(U_L(\theta)\bigr),\ \ \theta=2t\log L\; }.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Take \(s=\tfrac12+it\). Then
\(
u_L(s)=L^{2it}=e^{i\theta}
\)
where \(\theta=2t\log L\), and
\(
z_L(s)=L^{-s}=L^{-1/2}e^{-it\log L}=L^{-1/2}e^{-i\theta/2}
\).
From \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=\Delta(z_L(s),u_L(s))=\det(I-z_L(s)B(u_L(s)))\), the stated rewriting follows.
The zero criterion follows from the fact that the determinant vanishes if and only if \(1\) is an eigenvalue of \(U_L(\theta)\). \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Completion constant on the self-dual section: \texorpdfstring{$w$}{w} fixed, \texorpdfstring{$\Xi$}{Xi} degenerates to a cosine polynomial}
To avoid confusion with the complex variable \(s\), this paragraph denotes the completion trace coordinate as \(S\).
If the self-dual \(\Delta(z,u)\) can also be written as a completion determinant
\(\widehat\Delta(w,S)\) in the form
\[
\Delta(z,u)=\widehat\Delta\!\left(z\sqrt{u},\ \sqrt{u}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}\right)
\]
where \(\widehat\Delta\) is forced to exist by the invariant subring on the self-dual kernel; e.g., for the synchronization kernel weighted case see Proposition \ref{prop:sync-kernel-weighted-hat-delta}.
Then along the self-dual Dirichlet section \(z=L^{-s},\ u=L^{2s-1}\),
\[
w:=z\sqrt{u}=L^{-s}\cdot L^{s-1/2}=L^{-1/2}\quad(\text{constant}),
\qquad
S_L(s):=\sqrt{u}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}=L^{s-1/2}+L^{1/2-s},
\]
yielding the one-dimensional compression identity
\[
\boxed{\ \Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=\widehat\Delta\!\bigl(L^{-1/2},\,S_L(s)\bigr)\ }.
\]
In particular, on the critical line \(s=\tfrac12+it\),
\[
S_L\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)=L^{it}+L^{-it}=2\cos(t\log L),
\]
so the Hardy-type section \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}(\tfrac12+it)\) is a ``real polynomial in the cosine.''

\begin{proposition}[Pure algebraic criterion for the critical line version of zeros: \texorpdfstring{$\Xi$}{Xi}-RH \(\Longleftrightarrow\) root interval]\label{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}
Under the above completion representation, let
\[
P_L(S):=\widehat\Delta\!\bigl(L^{-1/2},S\bigr)\in\RR[S].
\]
Then the following two statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All zeros of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) satisfy \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\);
  \item All roots of \(P_L(S)\) lie in the real interval \([-2,2]\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(y:=L^{s-1/2}\in\CC^\times\). Then \(S_L(s)=y+y^{-1}\), whence
\[
\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=\widehat\Delta\!\bigl(L^{-1/2},\,y+y^{-1}\bigr)=P_L(y+y^{-1}).
\]
Therefore \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=0\) if and only if \(S_0:=y+y^{-1}\) is some root of \(P_L\).
\par
On the other hand,
\[
|y|=|L^{s-1/2}|=L^{\Re(s)-1/2},
\]
so \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) if and only if \(|y|=1\).
And \(|y|=1\) if and only if \(y=e^{i\theta}\) (for some \(\theta\in\RR\)), in which case
\(
y+y^{-1}=2\cos\theta\in[-2,2]
\) is real.
Conversely, if \(S_0\in[-2,2]\) is real, the solutions of \(y+y^{-1}=S_0\) satisfy \(|y|=1\).
Therefore ``all zeros satisfy \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\)'' is equivalent to ``all roots of \(P_L\) lie in \([-2,2]\).''
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unit circle root criterion: \texorpdfstring{$\Xi$}{Xi}-RH \(\Longleftrightarrow\) unit circle root distribution of a self-reciprocal polynomial]\label{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}
In the notation of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}, let \(d:=\deg P_L\) and define
\[
\Phi_L(y):=y^{d}P_L(y+y^{-1})\in\RR[y].
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\Phi_L\) is a self-reciprocal (reciprocal/palindromic) polynomial, i.e.,
  \[
  \boxed{\ y^{2d}\Phi_L(1/y)=\Phi_L(y)\ }.
  \]
  \item The following statements are equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] All zeros of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) satisfy \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\);
    \item[(ii)] All complex roots of \(\Phi_L(y)\) satisfy \(|y|=1\);
    \item[(iii)] All roots of \(P_L(S)\) lie in the real interval \([-2,2]\).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By definition \(\Phi_L(y)=y^{d}P_L(y+y^{-1})\); substituting \(y\mapsto 1/y\) gives
\(
\Phi_L(1/y)=y^{-d}P_L(y+y^{-1})
\),
hence \(y^{2d}\Phi_L(1/y)=y^{d}P_L(y+y^{-1})=\Phi_L(y)\), so \(\Phi_L\) is self-reciprocal.
\par
On the other hand, \(\Phi_L(y)=0\) if and only if \(P_L(S_0)=0\) and \(S_0=y+y^{-1}\).
If \(S_0\in[-2,2]\), then \(y\) can necessarily be written as \(y=e^{i\theta}\) (\(\theta\in\RR\)), whence \(|y|=1\); conversely if \(|y|=1\), then \(S_0=y+y^{-1}\in[-2,2]\).
Therefore ``all roots of \(P_L\) lie in \([-2,2]\)'' is equivalent to ``all roots of \(\Phi_L\) lie on the unit circle,'' and combining with Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion} yields (i)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(ii)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cayley hyperbolization: real-axis equivalent of the unit circle root criterion]\label{prop:xi-rh-cayley-hyperbolicity}
In the notation of Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}, let \(D:=\deg\Phi_L=2d\).
Define the Cayley transform
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-cayley-y-from-x}
\boxed{\;
y=\mathcal{C}(x):=\frac{x-\mathrm{i}}{x+\mathrm{i}},
\qquad
x=\mathcal{C}^{-1}(y)=\mathrm{i}\,\frac{1+y}{1-y}\;}
\end{equation}
(which bijects \(\overline{\RR}=\RR\cup\{\infty\}\) to the unit circle \(\TT\), and satisfies \(\mathcal{C}(\infty)=1\)).
Define the univariate polynomial
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-cayley-psi}
\boxed{\;
\Psi_L(x):=(x+\mathrm{i})^{D}\,\Phi_L\!\left(\frac{x-\mathrm{i}}{x+\mathrm{i}}\right)\; }.
\end{equation}
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All complex roots of \(\Phi_L(y)\) satisfy \(|y|=1\);
  \item All complex roots of \(\Psi_L(x)\) lie in \(\RR\cup\{\infty\}\) (counting roots at \(x=\infty\) in the projective sense).
\end{enumerate}
When \(\Phi_L\in\RR[y]\) is a self-reciprocal polynomial, the coefficients of \(\Psi_L\) can be taken to be real after normalization, so the above condition can be stated as the univariate real \textbf{hyperbolicity} of \(\Psi_L\) (all roots are real).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:xi-cayley-y-from-x} one directly verifies: for \(x\in\RR\), \(|\mathcal{C}(x)|=1\); conversely for \(|y|=1\) and \(y\neq 1\), \(x=\mathrm{i}(1+y)/(1-y)\in\RR\), and \(y=1\) corresponds to \(x=\infty\).
Therefore the Cayley transform gives a bijection at the level of root sets:
\(
\mathrm{Roots}(\Phi_L)\subseteq\TT
\Longleftrightarrow
\mathrm{Roots}(\Psi_L)\subseteq\overline{\RR}
\):
if \(\Phi_L(y_0)=0\) and \(y_0=\mathcal{C}(x_0)\) (\(x_0\in\RR\) or \(x_0=\infty\)), then clearing denominators gives \(\Psi_L(x_0)=0\) (counting \(x_0=\infty\) as a projective root); conversely if \(\Psi_L(x_0)=0\) then \(y_0=\mathcal{C}(x_0)\) is a root of \(\Phi_L\) with \(|y_0|=1\).
\par
If \(\Phi_L\) is self-reciprocal and has real coefficients, write \(\Phi_L(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{D}a_k y^k\) with \(a_k=a_{D-k}\in\RR\).
Expanding via \eqref{eq:xi-cayley-psi},
\[
\Psi_L(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{D} a_k\,(x+\mathrm{i})^{D-k}(x-\mathrm{i})^{k}.
\]
Using the symmetry \(a_k=a_{D-k}\), one can pair the \(k\) and \(D-k\) terms to write the right-hand side as \(\sum_k 2a_k\,\Re\bigl((x+\mathrm{i})^{D-k}(x-\mathrm{i})^{k}\bigr)\), whence \(\Psi_L\) (after overall constant normalization) has real coefficients. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Cohn-type derivative disk-interiority criterion: equivalent certificate for unit circle roots]\label{prop:xi-rh-cohn-derivative}
In the notation of Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}, \(\Phi_L\in\RR[y]\) is a self-reciprocal polynomial with \(\Phi_L(0)=\mathrm{lc}(P_L)\neq 0\).
Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All complex roots of \(\Phi_L\) satisfy \(|y|=1\);
  \item \(\Phi_L\) is self-inversive, and all complex roots of its derivative \(\Phi_L'(y)\) satisfy \(|y|\le 1\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore the \(\Xi\)-RH condition (any equivalent form of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion} / Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}) can be given via the \textbf{derivative disk-interiority} of \(|y|\le 1\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Self-reciprocity and real coefficients imply
\(
\Phi_L(y)=y^{D}\overline{\Phi_L(1/\overline{y})}
\)
(where \(D=\deg\Phi_L\)), i.e., \(\Phi_L\) is self-inversive.
By the Cohn theorem: a polynomial has all its zeros on the unit circle if and only if it is self-inversive and all zeros of its derivative lie in the closed unit disk; see \cite{LalinSmyth2012UnimodularitySelfInversive} (which reviews and generalizes Cohn's original criterion).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Schur--Cohn positive semidefinite certificate interface: rewriting ``unit circle roots/interval roots'' as auditable LMI]\label{rem:xi-schur-cohn-sdp-interface}
Let \(p(y)=a_0+a_1y+\cdots+a_n y^n\in\CC[y]\) (\(a_0\neq 0\)).
Define the lower triangular Toeplitz matrices
\[
X(p):=\begin{pmatrix}
a_0 & 0 & \cdots & 0\\
a_1 & a_0 & \ddots & \vdots\\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0\\
a_{n-1} & \cdots & a_1 & a_0
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
Y(p):=\begin{pmatrix}
\overline{a_n} & 0 & \cdots & 0\\
\overline{a_{n-1}} & \overline{a_n} & \ddots & \vdots\\
\vdots & \ddots & \ddots & 0\\
\overline{a_1} & \cdots & \overline{a_{n-1}} & \overline{a_n}
\end{pmatrix},
\]
and let the Schur--Cohn Hermitian form matrix be
\[
H(p):=X(p)X(p)^\ast-Y(p)Y(p)^\ast\in\Mat_n(\CC)_{\mathrm{Herm}}.
\]
Schur--Cohn theory gives: if \(p\) has no zeros on the unit circle, then all zeros of \(p\) lie in the open unit disk \(|y|<1\) if and only if \(H(p)\succ 0\); moreover the inertia index of \(H(p)\) gives the count of zeros inside/outside the unit circle (for the scalar case survey see \cite{DymYoung1990SchurCohnMatrixPolynomials}).
\par
Combining with Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-cohn-derivative}, in the typical non-degenerate case (e.g., all unit-circle roots of \(\Phi_L\) are simple), the \(\Xi\)-RH is equivalent to \(H(\Phi_L')\succ 0\);
in the boundary case allowing multiple roots, the closed-disk condition \(|y|\le 1\) can be replaced by the strict version \(|y|<1\), or \(H(\Phi_L')\succ 0\) can be relaxed to \(H(\Phi_L')\succeq 0\) supplemented with a finite audit of boundary roots.
\par
When the coefficients of \(\Phi_L\) (or more generally the channel polynomial \(\Phi_{L,\rho}\)) lie in the invariant subring \(\QQ[u+u^{-1}]\), along the unit circle \(u=e^{it}\) one can view the coefficients as polynomials in \(x=2\cos t\in[-2,2]\); then \(H(\Phi_{L,\rho}')\) becomes a \textbf{univariate polynomial matrix} \(H(x)\), so the ``all-angle'' kernel version (G)RH can be compressed to the interval positive semidefinite constraint
\[
H(x)\succeq 0\qquad(x\in[-2,2]).
\]
This interval positive semidefiniteness can further be given auditable certificates via SOS/semidefinite programming.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Horizon Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz certificate: unified positivity framework for the \texorpdfstring{$\Xi$}{Xi}-slice interval root criterion]\label{thm:xi-slice-horizon-carath-toeplitz}
Following the notation of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion} and Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}:
given \(L>1\), let
\(
P_L(S)=\widehat\Delta(L^{-1/2},S)\in\RR[S]
\),
let \(d=\deg P_L\), and let
\(
\Phi_L(y)=y^{d}P_L(y+y^{-1})\in\RR[y]
\)
(so that \(D:=\deg\Phi_L=2d\) and \(\Phi_L(0)\neq 0\)).
Define the \textbf{horizon Carath\'eodory function} (with disk variable \(\omega\in\mathbb{D}\))
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-slice-carath-def}
\boxed{\;
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}(\omega)
:=
1-\frac{2}{D}\,\omega\,\frac{\Phi_L'(\omega)}{\Phi_L(\omega)},
\qquad \omega\in\mathbb{D}.
\;}
\end{equation}
Expand \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) in a normalized power series at \(\omega=0\):
\[
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}(\omega)=c_0+2\sum_{n\ge 1}c_n\omega^n,
\qquad c_{-n}:=\overline{c_n},
\]
and for \(N\ge 0\) define the Toeplitz principal submatrix
\[
\mathbf{T}_N:=\bigl(c_{j-k}\bigr)_{0\le j,k\le N}.
\]
Then the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All zeros of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) satisfy \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) (equivalently: all roots of \(P_L\) lie in \([-2,2]\));
  \item All complex roots of \(\Phi_L\) satisfy \(|y|=1\);
  \item \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) is analytic on \(\mathbb{D}\) with \(\Re \mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}(\omega)\ge 0\) (i.e., \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) belongs to the Carath\'eodory class);
  \item For all \(N\ge 0\), the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy \(\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0\) holds.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\((1)\Longleftrightarrow(2)\) has been given in Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}.
\par
\((2)\Rightarrow(3)\) Suppose the roots of \(\Phi_L\) (counted with multiplicity) are \(\{y_j\}_{j=1}^{D}\subset\TT\), and let \(\alpha_j:=1/y_j=\overline{y_j}\in\TT\).
Then \(\Phi_L(\omega)=\Phi_L(0)\prod_{j=1}^{D}(1-\alpha_j\omega)\).
From \eqref{eq:xi-slice-carath-def}, direct computation gives
\[
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}(\omega)
=1+\frac{2}{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\frac{\alpha_j\omega}{1-\alpha_j\omega}
=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\frac{1+\alpha_j\omega}{1-\alpha_j\omega}.
\]
For each \(\alpha\in\TT\), the function \(\omega\mapsto\frac{1+\alpha\omega}{1-\alpha\omega}\) is analytic on \(\mathbb{D}\) with positive real part;
hence its average is also analytic on \(\mathbb{D}\) and satisfies \(\Re\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\ge 0\), i.e., \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) belongs to the Carath\'eodory class.
\par
\((3)\Rightarrow(2)\) If \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) is analytic on \(\mathbb{D}\), then \(\Phi_L\) has no zeros in \(\mathbb{D}\) (otherwise \(\Phi_L'/\Phi_L\) would have a pole inside the disk).
Since \(\Phi_L\) is a self-reciprocal polynomial (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}), its zeros correspond in pairs under \(y\leftrightarrow 1/\overline y\): if there were a zero with \(|y_0|>1\), then \(1/\overline y_0\in\mathbb{D}\) would also be a zero, a contradiction.
Therefore \(\Phi_L\) also has no zeros with \(|y|>1\), so all zeros lie on \(\TT\).
\par
\((3)\Longleftrightarrow(4)\) Following Appendix \ref{subsubsec:app-horizon-weyl-carath-toeplitz} on the Carath\'eodory/Herglotz representation and truncated moment completability (Carath\'eodory--Fej\'er),
\(\Re f\ge 0\) (\(f\) analytic on \(\mathbb{D}\)) if and only if the family of Toeplitz principal submatrices generated by its power series moments is positive semidefinite;
applying this general result to \(f=\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) gives the conclusion. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Horizon Weyl/Herglotz version: upper half-plane positivity criterion for the \texorpdfstring{\(\Xi\)}{Xi}-slice]\label{cor:xi-slice-weyl-herglotz}
Following the notation of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-slice-horizon-carath-toeplitz}, define the completion critical section
\[
E_{\Delta,L}(z):=\Xi_{\Delta,L}\!\left(\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}z\right),
\qquad z\in\CC,
\]
and its \textbf{horizon Weyl/Carath\'eodory logarithmic derivative}
\[
m_{\Delta,L}(z):=-\frac{E'_{\Delta,L}(z)}{E_{\Delta,L}(z)},
\qquad z\in\mathbb{H}.
\]
Then conditions (1)--(4) of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-slice-horizon-carath-toeplitz} are also equivalent to:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(5)] \(m_{\Delta,L}\) is analytic on \(\mathbb{H}\) and satisfies \(\Im m_{\Delta,L}(z)\ge 0\) (i.e., \(m_{\Delta,L}\) is a Herglotz/Nevanlinna function).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Condition (1) of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-slice-horizon-carath-toeplitz} is equivalent to all zeros of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) satisfying \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\), i.e., all zeros of \(E_{\Delta,L}\) being real (since \(E_{\Delta,L}(z)=0\iff \Xi_{\Delta,L}(\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}z)=0\)).
By Proposition \ref{prop:xi-delta-L-critical-line-real}, \(E_{\Delta,L}(t)\in\RR\) for all \(t\in\RR\), hence \(E_{\Delta,L}(\overline z)=\overline{E_{\Delta,L}(z)}\) and zeros are conjugate-symmetric about the real axis.
Therefore, ``all zeros are real'' if and only if \(E_{\Delta,L}\) has no zeros in \(\mathbb{H}\), equivalent to \(m_{\Delta,L}\) being analytic on \(\mathbb{H}\).
\par
Under this analyticity premise, the equivalence between the Herglotz positivity of \(m_{\Delta,L}=-E'_{\Delta,L}/E_{\Delta,L}\) and ``all zeros are real'' follows from the same-type argument of Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-weyl-herglotz} (replacing \(E_{\zeta}\) with \(E_{\Delta,L}\));
combining gives (1)\(\Longleftrightarrow\)(5), and merging with (1)\(\Longleftrightarrow\)(2)\(\Longleftrightarrow\)(3)\(\Longleftrightarrow\)(4) of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-slice-horizon-carath-toeplitz} yields the conclusion. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Same-type interface of Ramanujan--Herglotz for finite kernels (role of the completion functional equation)]\label{rem:xi-finite-kernel-ramanujan-herglotz-interface}
For the Dirichlet-ized \(\zeta\)-function of a finite-dimensional kernel \(Z_M(s)=\det(I-\lambda^{-s}M)^{-1}\), the Ramanujan radius bound (kernel RH) is equivalent to the absence of nontrivial poles in the right half-open strip \(\tfrac12<\Re(s)<1\) (Corollary \ref{cor:finite-rh-halfstrip-nopoles}).
If one further satisfies the spectral reciprocal closure \(\mu\mapsto \lambda/\mu\) (Proposition \ref{prop:reciprocal-spectrum-functional-equation}), then the completion functional equation forces the pole geometry into a symmetric structure about \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\);
the ``half-strip pole-free'' condition is then promoted to critical line localization of the critical-strip toy-RH, and can be equivalently rewritten as the upper half-plane Weyl/Herglotz positivity criterion, thereby being same-type in certificate language with the horizon Weyl criterion for \(\Xi_\zeta\) (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-weyl-herglotz}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Horizon spectral measure and reflection positivity in the finite-dimensional case]\label{rem:xi-slice-horizon-measure-reflection-positivity}
When the equivalent conditions of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-slice-horizon-carath-toeplitz} hold,
\(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}\) is a rational Carath\'eodory function, so its Herglotz representation can be taken as a finite atomic measure (see the rational case of the representation formula \eqref{eq:app-horizon-herglotz-disk} in Appendix \ref{subsubsec:app-horizon-weyl-carath-toeplitz}, or see \cite{Simon2005OPUC1}):
if \(\mathrm{Roots}(\Phi_L)=\{y_j\}_{j=1}^{D}\subset\TT\), then there exists a probability measure
\(
\mu_{\Phi_L}:=\frac{1}{D}\sum_{j=1}^{D}\delta_{y_j}
\)
such that
\[
\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_L}(\omega)=\int_{\TT}\frac{\xi+\omega}{\xi-\omega}\,d\mu_{\Phi_L}(\xi).
\]
Its Fourier moments \(c_n=\int_{\TT}\xi^{-n}\,d\mu_{\Phi_L}(\xi)\) are the ``horizon multipole moments/hair coefficients'';
and the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy \(\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0\) gives the \emph{reflection positivity} certificate that this moment sequence arises from a positive measure (aligned with the semantics of Appendix Remark \ref{rem:app-horizon-multipole-positivity-hair}).
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Channelized version: horizon Toeplitz--PSD certificate for kernel version (G)RH]\label{cor:xi-channel-horizon-toeplitz-certificate}
Under the channelized setting of Remark \ref{rem:xi-artin-channel-pipeline}, if for each channel \(\rho\) the completion section \(\Xi_{\rho,L}\) defines the corresponding self-reciprocal polynomial \(\Phi_{L,\rho}\) (the per-channel version of Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}), and \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{L,\rho}}\) is defined as in \eqref{eq:xi-slice-carath-def},
then ``kernel version (G)RH holds at base \(L\)'' (per-channel critical line constraint) is equivalent to the horizon positivity/certificate hierarchy simultaneously holding for all channels \(\rho\):
\[
\Re\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{L,\rho}}(\omega)\ge 0\quad(\omega\in\mathbb{D})
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\mathbf{T}^{(\rho)}_N\succeq 0\quad(\forall N\ge 0),
\]
where \(\mathbf{T}^{(\rho)}_N\) is the Toeplitz principal submatrix of \(\mathscr{C}_{\Phi_{L,\rho}}\).
Therefore, the ``interval root/unit circle root/Schur--Cohn LMI''-type spectral certificates of the main text and the horizon Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz PSD hierarchy of Appendix \ref{subsubsec:app-horizon-weyl-carath-toeplitz} are merged into a unified certificate language under the same horizon positivity framework.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Joukowski compression and alignment with the finite-dimensional toy-RH dictionary]\label{rem:xi-joukowski-toy-rh-alignment}
The variable substitution of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion},
\(
y=L^{s-1/2}
\)
and
\(
S=y+y^{-1}
\),
can be viewed as the realization of the Joukowski map in Dirichlet coordinates: it compresses the unit circle \(|y|=1\) to the real interval \([-2,2]\), and the endpoints
\(
S=\pm 2
\)
correspond to
\(
y=\pm 1
\).
Therefore, ``whether zeros fall on the critical line \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\)'' is equivalent in \(y\)-coordinates to ``whether the corresponding roots fall on the unit circle,'' and in \(S\)-coordinates to ``whether the corresponding roots fall in \([-2,2]\).''
\par
This geometric compression is fully same-type with the finite-dimensional \(\zeta\) dictionary of Appendix \ref{app:real-input-40-finite-rh}: for a finite-dimensional kernel with spectral radius \(\lambda=\rho(M)\),
writing the Dirichlet variable as \(z=\lambda^{-s}\), the critical strip pole positions are controlled by the normalized spectral radius
\(
|\mu|/\sqrt{\lambda}
\),
and toy-RH (or the Ramanujan radius bound) is equivalent to all nontrivial spectral points satisfying \(|\mu|\le \sqrt{\lambda}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:finite-rh-triangle-dict} and Corollary \ref{cor:finite-rh-halfstrip-nopoles}).
In the ``saturating'' case \(|\mu|=\sqrt{\lambda}\), letting
\(
y:=\mu/\sqrt{\lambda}
\)
gives \(|y|=1\) and \(S=y+y^{-1}\in[-2,2]\), consistent with the \((y,S)\) compression coordinates of this section.
In particular, the unique saturating eigenvalue of the real-input \(40\)-state kernel is \(-\sqrt{\lambda}\) (Remark \ref{rem:finite-rh-40-ramanujan}), corresponding to \(y=-1\) and \(S=-2\), so ``single-frequency critical oscillation'' can be understood as the saturation of this Joukowski compression at the endpoint (see also Remark \ref{rem:finite-rh-40-hidden-minusA0} for further decomposition of its structural origin).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Half-angle lift endpoint decomposition paradigm (same-type as Joukowski compression)]\label{rem:xi-half-angle-endpoint-paradigm}
Besides the \((y,S)=(L^{s-1/2},\,y+y^{-1})\) Joukowski compression, the present paper also has a fully same-type ``half-angle completion'' compression in the unitary twist variable \(u=e^{i\theta}\): taking the half-angle variable \(\mathfrak{r}=e^{i\theta/2}\) so that \(u=\mathfrak{r}^2\), and letting \(s=\mathfrak{r}+\mathfrak{r}^{-1}\in[-2,2]\).
Then the power semigroup \(u\mapsto u^m\) strictly corresponds in the completion coordinates to \(s\mapsto C_m(s)\) (Chebyshev--Adams), and at the endpoint \(u=-1\) (\(s=0\)) there is an auditable odd/even channel jet splitting: odd-\(m\) channels control the linear principal scale, while even-\(m\) channels only enter the higher-order jets and finite part; simultaneously, primitive polynomials of odd-length sectors strictly vanish at \(u=-1\), so the endpoint principal singularity is monopolized by the even-length sector.
The unified formulation of this endpoint decomposition is given in Appendix \ref{app:unit-circle-phase-gate}, Proposition \ref{prop:endpoint-decomposition-paradigm}.
\end{remark}
