\subsection{Euler product closure: Dirichlet sum versus prime Euler product}
\label{app:quant_fits_euler_product}

For the discriminant eigenform $\Delta$ (weight $12$), the associated $L$-series admits the Euler product
\begin{equation}
L(\Delta,s)=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\tau(n)}{n^s}
\;=\;
\prod_{p}\left(1-\tau(p)p^{-s}+p^{11-2s}\right)^{-1},
\end{equation}
which converges absolutely for $\Re(s)>13/2$ (e.g.\ \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}). \begin{equation}

\end{equation}
where the theoretical even moments are
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}[x^{2n}]=\frac{C_n}{4^n},\qquad C_n=\frac{1}{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.

\subsection{Gauss--Kuzmin relaxation rate: a toy exponential fit}
\label{app:quant_fits_gauss_kuzmin}

Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss_kuzmin_convergence} states exponential relaxation of $G^n$ toward the Gauss invariant measure for a broad class of initial distributions. As a simple, fully reproducible sanity check, we sample $\xi_0\sim \mathrm{Unif}(0,1)$, iterate $\xi_{n+1}=G(\xi_n)$, and measure the Kolmogorov--Smirnov distance between the empirical CDF of $\xi_n$ and the Gauss CDF $F(x)=\log_2(1+x)$. A least-squares fit of $\log(\mathrm{KS}_n)$ against $n$ over $n=0,\ldots,4$ yields an effective decay factor $\lambda_{\mathrm{fit}}\approx 0.3815$ with $R^2\approx 0.995$ (toy metric; not an attempt to estimate the optimal Wirsing constant). The table is reproducible via Experiment~13 in Appendix~\ref{app:repro_code}.
\par
For a fixed sample size $N$, the empirical CDF itself has an intrinsic sampling noise floor. The Dvoretzky--Kiefer--Wolfowitz inequality (with sharp constant due to Massart) implies
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{P}\!\left(\sup_x |F_N(x)-F(x)|>\varepsilon\right)\le 2\e^{-2N\varepsilon^2},
\end{equation}
see \cite{DvoretzkyKieferWolfowitz1956,Massart1990}. For $N=200{,}000$, the $95\%$ bound is $\varepsilon_{0.95}\approx 3.04\times 10^{-3}$, so once $\mathrm{KS}_n$ reaches the $10^{-3}$ scale, non-monotone fluctuations are consistent with finite sampling rather than genuine loss of mixing.

\subsection{Khinchin and L\'evy constants: metrical scaling of continued fractions}
\label{app:quant_fits_khinchin_levy}

Beyond digit frequencies, the metrical theory of continued fractions provides sharp constants controlling typical scaling. For $\xi=[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]$ with convergents $p_n/q_n$, one has for Lebesgue-a.e.\ $\xi$ the L\'evy limit
\begin{equation}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log q_n = \frac{\pi^2}{12\log 2},
\end{equation}
and Khinchin's constant for the geometric mean of digits:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\prod_{j=1}^n a_j\right)^{1/n}=K_0\approx 2.6854520010,
\end{equation}
see, e.g., \cite{IosifescuKraaikamp2002}. The following table reports a reproducible Monte Carlo sanity check using samples from the Gauss measure (equivalent to Lebesgue) and finite digit lengths.
