\section{Golden Gating and Rigorous Treatment of ``Golden Superposition States''}

\subsection{Golden Branches: Definition and Extremal Properties}

Take the golden ratio $\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2$. A golden branch can take slope $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ (or transformations equivalent under $\mathrm{SL}(2,\ZZ)$). Its continued fraction expansion has the simplest all-$1$ structure, and is therefore the extremal object in the Hurwitz/Markov sense that is ``hardest to approximate by rationals'': for any rational $p/q$, a uniform lower bound exists on the approximation obstacle, making short pseudo-periods appear latest. Operationally, this means stronger phase-locking resistance ability at fixed tolerance, thus maximizing ``non-periodic openness'' in finite-resolution scanning.

\subsection{Golden Gating: Marginal Distribution Induced by Window Length}

Take window $W=[1-\alpha,1)$ and let $s_n=\ind_W(x_n)$. Under unique ergodicity,
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac1N\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}s_n=\mu(W)=\alpha.
\]
When $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$, we obtain
\[
\mathbb{P}(s=1)=\varphi^{-1},\qquad \mathbb{P}(s=0)=1-\varphi^{-1}=\varphi^{-2}.
\]
Thus we can rigorously define ``golden gating'' as: under golden-slope scanning and window readout, the limiting marginal distribution of binary events is in the ratio $\varphi^{-1}:\varphi^{-2}$. Finite-sample deviations are given deterministic bounds by $D_N^*$ (Section 3).

\subsection{``Golden Superposition States'' and Physical Superposition: Distinction and Connectable Structures}

This paper restricts ``golden superposition states'' to one of the following two types, and distinguishes their logical position from physical quantum superposition:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Marginal mixed golden state (statistical type).} Treat readout results as classical random variables whose limiting distribution is $(\varphi^{-2},\varphi^{-1})$. This uncertainty arises from finite resolution and scanning complementarity, not from coherent linear superposition in Hilbert space. The mathematical object is a probability distribution or diagonal density matrix.
  \item \textbf{Projected stable subspace golden state (grammatical type).} At fixed window length $m$, introduce the stable language $X_m$ (no adjacent $1$'s) and define projection $P_\varphi$ to $\ell^2(X_m)$. Here ``golden'' manifests in the growth rate of allowed words and subspace dimension with $m$ following Fibonacci scaling, not Born phase interference in a single measurement.
\end{enumerate}

By contrast, \textbf{physical quantum superposition states} are coherent linear combinations of Hilbert space vectors, containing phase information not simulable by classical mixing, and produce observable differences in appropriate interference experiments. The connectable structure between the two lies in: any classical mixture embeds as a diagonal density matrix; and grammatical stable projections can be viewed as a class of quantum channels/projective measurements whose fixed points or principal eigenvectors may have golden ratio-related spectral radius. However, unless explicit off-diagonal coherence terms and interfering observables are given, ``golden superposition states'' should not be equated with physical superposition states.
