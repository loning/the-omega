\begin{conclusion}[Strict DLR--regenerative decomposition of golden mean finite volume: any \(k\)-point function is completely independent under ``forced-zero'' partition]\label{con:xi-terminal-golden-mean-dlr-regeneration-factorization}
On the Fibonacci cube graph \(\Gamma_n\), take uniform counting and denote the legal word set \(X_n:=V(\Gamma_n)\) (Definition \ref{def:pom-fibonacci-cube}).
For any index set \(S=\{i_1<\cdots<i_k\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\), if there exists \(j\) such that \(i_{j+1}-i_j=1\), then the constraint is infeasible and
\[
\#\{v\in X_n:\ v_{i_j}=1\ \forall j\}=0.
\]
If the spacing condition \(i_{j+1}-i_j\ge 2\) is satisfied, then there exists a strict product factorization
\[
\#\{v\in X_n:\ v_{i_j}=1\ \forall j\}
=F_{i_1}\Bigl(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}F_{i_{j+1}-i_j-1}\Bigr)F_{n-i_k+1},
\qquad |X_n|=F_{n+2}.
\]
Therefore, under the finite-volume Gibbs/DLR caliber, the conditional events \(\{v_{i_j}=1\}\) force the adjacent positions to \(0\) on both sides of each \(i_j\), and split the system into \(k+1\) mutually disjoint free intervals;
under this condition, the configurations on each free interval are strictly independent, and each segment is uniformly distributed over the corresponding length golden mean SFT.
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
See Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fibcube-kpoint-count}; where ``adjacent positions are forced to \(0\)'' and free segment splitting are precisely the structural source of the product factorization, so conditional independence also follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Finite volume \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} Parry bulk phase at exponential rate: error locked solely by the second eigenvalue \texorpdfstring{$-\varphi^{-2}$}{-phi^{-2}}]\label{con:xi-terminal-unif-xn-to-parry-exp-rate}
Let \(\nu_{\mathrm{Parry}}\) be the Parry maximum entropy equilibrium state of the golden mean SFT (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-parry-limit-chain-explicit}), whose two-state Markov transition matrix has unique nontrivial eigenvalue \(\lambda_2=-\varphi^{-2}\).
For the uniform legal word \(U_n:=\mathrm{Unif}(X_n)\), take any fixed block length \(k\ge 1\).
Then there exists a constant \(C_k\) such that for any position \(t\) and
\(
d:=\min\{t-1,\ n-t-k+1\}
\),
the exponential-type bulk phase convergence bound holds:
\[
\sup_{a_1\cdots a_k\in X_k}\Bigl|
U_n(X_t\cdots X_{t+k-1}=a_1\cdots a_k)-\nu_{\mathrm{Parry}}([a_1\cdots a_k])
\Bigr|
\le C_k\,\varphi^{-2d}.
\]
Therefore, the correlation length and boundary influence decay rate from finite volume to bulk phase are both completely locked by the same algebraic constant \(\varphi^{-2}=|\lambda_2|\).
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
Local statistics convergence to the Parry measure is given by Proposition \ref{prop:pom-unif-xn-local-limit-parry}.
Writing the cylinder set count as a product of power matrix elements of the golden mean adjacency matrix \(A\), and applying Perron--Frobenius decomposition to the left and right completion segments respectively, we can compress the error term precisely to the contribution of the sub-mode;
since the second eigenvalue of \(A\) is \(-\varphi^{-1}\), the normalized error decays as \((|\varphi^{-1}|/\varphi)^d=\varphi^{-2d}\) in the sense of distance \(d\) from the boundary.
Incorporating the constant for finitely many legal blocks into \(C_k\) yields the indicated upper bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Closed forms for edges and average degree of fiber cube geometry: average degree linear coefficient exactly \texorpdfstring{$\frac{2}{5}(3-\varphi)$}{(2/5)(3-phi)}]\label{con:xi-terminal-fibcube-edge-avg-degree-closed}
The number of edges and vertices of the Fibonacci cube \(\Gamma_n\) satisfy the closed forms
\[
|E(\Gamma_n)|=\frac{nF_{n+1}+2(n+1)F_n}{5},
\qquad
|V(\Gamma_n)|=F_{n+2},
\]
so the average degree is
\[
\overline{\deg}(\Gamma_n)
=\frac{2|E(\Gamma_n)|}{|V(\Gamma_n)|}
=\frac{2}{5}\left(
n\frac{F_{n+1}}{F_{n+2}}+2(n+1)\frac{F_n}{F_{n+2}}
\right)
\sim \frac{2}{5}(3-\varphi)\,n.
\]
Furthermore, if the fiber reconstruction graph \(\mathcal F_x\) admits a Cartesian factorization
\(
\mathcal F_x\cong\prod_{i=1}^r\Gamma_{\ell_i}
\)
(Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-reconstruction-cartesian-product}), then we have the complete closed forms for edges and average degree:
\[
|E(\mathcal F_x)|
=\sum_{i=1}^r |E(\Gamma_{\ell_i})|\prod_{j\ne i}|V(\Gamma_{\ell_j})|,
\qquad
\overline{\deg}(\mathcal F_x)=\sum_{i=1}^r \overline{\deg}(\Gamma_{\ell_i}).
\]
Therefore, the ``local switchability'' scale of fibers strictly linearly scales in the bulk phase limit, anchored by the same coefficient \(\frac{2}{5}(3-\varphi)\) (with additive contribution from long components).
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
The edge count closed form and average degree formula for \(\Gamma_n\) are given by Corollary \ref{cor:pom-fibcube-edge-closed-form}.
The Cartesian decomposition of fibers is given by Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-reconstruction-cartesian-product}.
For Cartesian product graphs, the edge set decomposes by ``selecting one edge in one factor and fixing vertices in the remaining factors,'' so we obtain the edge count formula; the average degree additivity follows from
\(
2|E|/|V|
\)
and the multiplicativity of vertex counts.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Strong version of discrete Weyl law: counting function error strictly bounded by \(1\) over the entire interval, hence Kolmogorov distance is \(O(1/k)\)]\label{con:xi-terminal-discrete-weyl-strong-remainder}
For spectral parameter \(\mu\in(0,4)\), let \(\theta(\mu)=\arccos(1-\mu/2)\in(0,\pi)\), and define the counting function
\(
N_k(\mu)=\#\{p\le k:\mu_p\le\mu\}
\).
Then there exists a completely explicit inverse-function formula
\[
N_k(\mu)=\Bigl\lfloor \frac{2k+1}{2\pi}\theta(\mu)+\frac12\Bigr\rfloor,
\qquad
\Bigl|N_k(\mu)-\frac{2k+1}{2\pi}\theta(\mu)\Bigr|\le 1
\quad(\forall\mu\in(0,4)).
\]
Therefore, the empirical distribution function \(F_k(\mu):=N_k(\mu)/k\) uniformly approximates the limit distribution function \(F(\mu)=\theta(\mu)/\pi\) over the entire interval with \(O(1/k)\) precision.
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
See Corollary \ref{cor:pom-Lk-discrete-weyl}.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Boundary spectral law is a strict quadratic tilt of the bulk spectral law: \texorpdfstring{$\dd\rho(\mu)=\frac{\mu(4-\mu)}{2}\,\dd\nu(\mu)$}{d rho=(mu(4-mu)/2)d nu}]\label{con:xi-terminal-boundary-bulk-spectral-quadratic-tilt}
Let \(\nu\) be the bulk phase limit measure and \(\rho\) be the boundary limit measure.
Then they satisfy the exact Radon--Nikodym relation
\[
\dd\rho(\mu)=\frac{\mu(4-\mu)}{2}\,\dd\nu(\mu),
\qquad
\int f(\mu)\,\dd\rho(\mu)=\frac12\int \mu(4-\mu)\,f(\mu)\,\dd\nu(\mu).
\]
Therefore, all integrable functionals of boundary spectral statistics can be exactly computed under the bulk measure with a single weighting/change of measure.
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
See Corollary \ref{cor:pom-Lk-boundary-bulk-radon-nikodym}.
\end{proof}
\begin{conclusion}[Numerical certificate robustness of Toeplitz--PSD: spectral margin and coefficient error yield completely explicit feasible region]\label{con:xi-terminal-toeplitz-psd-numerical-robustness}
Let \(\mathbf{T}_N=(c_{j-k})_{0\le j,k\le N}\) be the Toeplitz truncation matrix, and \(\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_N=(\widehat c_{j-k})\) be any Hermitian approximation.
If there exists a verifiable spectral margin \(\lambda_{\min}(\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_N)\ge\eta>0\) and the truncation error satisfies
\(
\|\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_N-\mathbf{T}_N\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le \eta/2
\),
then we must have \(\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0\).
Moreover, if the coefficient-wise error satisfies \(\max_{0\le n\le N}|\widehat c_n-c_n|\le\varepsilon\), then
\[
\|\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_N-\mathbf{T}_N\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le\sum_{k=-N}^{N}|\widehat c_k-c_k|
\le (2N+1)\varepsilon.
\]
Therefore, finite-order Toeplitz--PSD verification can be reduced to a numerical--algebraic hybrid certificate chain of ``compute the minimum eigenvalue of \(\widehat{\mathbf{T}}_N\) and give a verifiable error bound.''
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
See Theorem \ref{thm:xi-spectral-margin-finite-psd-certificate}.
\end{proof}
\begin{conclusion}[Refutation threshold for single-atom negative defect is \texorpdfstring{$1/(N+1)$}{1/(N+1)} order and sharp: minimum information budget for horizon order is precisely locked]\label{con:xi-terminal-toeplitz-negative-atom-threshold-sharp}
Under the horizon Toeplitz certificate caliber, consider the confrontation of a background positive measure with a single-atom negative defect \(\varepsilon\,\delta_{\xi_0}\).
There exist density lower bound \(m_0\) and upper bound \(M_0\) such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(\varepsilon\le m_0/(N+1)\), then for all \(x\in\CC^{N+1}\) we have \(x^\ast \mathbf{T}_N x\ge 0\), so \(\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0\) (negative defect is completely absorbed by the background, horizon irrefutable).
  \item If \(\varepsilon> M_0/(N+1)\), then there exists an explicit direction \(u_k=\xi_0^{-k}\) such that \(u^\ast\mathbf{T}_N u<0\), so \(\mathbf{T}_N\not\succeq 0\) (negative defect must be detected at the \(N\)-th level).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore, the refutability threshold for single-atom defects is strictly of order \(1/(N+1)\) and is sharp: to forcibly distinguish an \(\varepsilon\)-level defect, one must have horizon order budget \(N\gtrsim \varepsilon^{-1}\).
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
See Proposition \ref{prop:discussion-toeplitz-negative-atom-threshold}.
\end{proof}
