Please consider the enclosed manuscript,

"Homological Visibility of Gluing Obstructions in a State-Forcing Semantics,"

for publication in the Annals of Pure and Applied Logic.

The manuscript develops a self-contained four-layer conservative extension of pointwise semantics and uses it to study the homological visibility of finite abelian gluing obstructions. Its starting point is a forcing-necessity result: compatible local sectionability and its failure are shown to be genuinely new predicates at the local-object enrichment level, not definable in the information-state forcing language without the local-object layer.

The first structural contribution is categorical. The paper introduces realization prestacks whose stackification identifies visible value classes with the global connected components of a realization stack, correcting the naive single-gerbe picture. Each visible branch determines a banded component gerbe, and under a branch-constancy hypothesis these gerbes exhaust the stack. For finite abelian branch obstructions on finite Cech nerves, the universal coefficient theorem yields a homological evaluation map whose cokernel is the intrinsic visible quotient. This quotient detects exactly the H_2-visible part of a branch obstruction; pure Ext-type obstructions are character-blind. In the unique-branch case, the theory refines the standard no-global-section picture from sheaf-theoretic contextuality, with the character-blind Ext-residual providing a precise mechanism for the incomplete cohomological detection identified by Caru.

The paper also obtains branch-sensitive and branch-uniform total visible quotients, exact multi-branch strictification budgets, and refinement-monotonicity results for branchwise visibility. Two worked examples exhibit the visible and blind extremes. Complexity upper bounds for the associated finite-state support problems are recorded in an appendix.

We believe the manuscript fits the journal's scope in mathematical logic and its applications to mathematics and theoretical computer science. The paper combines methods from formal semantics, sheaf theory, and homological algebra in a self-contained presentation that does not presuppose any external framework.
