\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What is explained and what is not}
\label{sec:what_explained}

The $m=6$ folding template is a \emph{minimal} arithmetic layer.
It provides:
\begin{itemize}
\item a fixed $64\to 21$ compression space $X_6$ with a $18\oplus 3$ cyclic/boundary split;
\item a finite encoding search space ($24$) for the unknown two-bit nucleotide dictionary;
\item a rigid start--stop symmetry at the unique encoding selected by the control-boundary objective.
\end{itemize}

It does not claim to derive the full biochemical translation apparatus, tRNA recognition, or evolutionary pathways.
Rather, it posits that if a finite-resolution readout constraint exists at a low layer, then many higher-level degrees of freedom are pre-constrained by arithmetic topology.

\subsection{Why the control-boundary objective is a minimal prior}
\label{sec:why_control_objective}

The reverse-compilation step requires a criterion to select one encoding among $24$ discrete candidates.
Our objective uses only translation-control semantics and the folding-defined boundary sector.
Two structural facts make it a particularly weak form of supervision:
\begin{itemize}
\item the boundary sector is extremely sparse at $m=6$ (only six codons, independent of encoding);
\item the control set $\mathcal{K}=\{\mathrm{AUG,UAA,UAG,UGA}\}$ is the smallest standard set tied directly to start/stop control, without invoking amino-acid physicochemical properties, codon-usage statistics, or evolutionary assumptions.
\end{itemize}

Maximizing the boundary-hit count $S(\mu)$ therefore asks only whether the known control symbols preferentially occupy the rare topological endpoints of the $64\to 21$ folding space.
Importantly, the objective does not enforce the $14/48$ start--stop symmetry; the observed coincidence of $\mathrm{AUG}$ and $\mathrm{UAA}$ on the same boundary word arises as a consequence of the folding preimage structure and the finite search.
Alternative weak objectives (e.g.\ combining boundary hits with mutual information or with sequence-level context statistics) can be layered on later, but are not required for the closed $m=6$ identifiability result.
In particular, we do not use codon-usage frequencies or codon-bias objectives as supervision at this stage \cite{SharpLi1987,HershbergPetrov2008}.

\subsection{Why three stop codons do not all lie in the boundary sector}
\label{sec:stops_not_all_boundary}

The boundary sector contains exactly six microstate indices (two per boundary word), independent of the nucleotide encoding (Appendix~\ref{app:fold6_preimages}).
In particular, under the standard stop set $\{\mathrm{UAA,UAG,UGA}\}$ it is impossible to place all three stop codons inside the boundary sector, and in fact at most one stop can be boundary for any bijective encoding (Proposition~\ref{prop:stop_hits_at_most_one}).
\medskip

\noindent A complementary check across nonstandard translation tables is also consistent with this rigidity.
Using the NCBI translation-table collection (\path{gc.prt}), we find that under $\mu^\ast$ at window length $6$ the stop-set boundary-hit count is at most one across all listed codes:

Thus, in the minimal $m=6$ template, the stop set is forced into a rigid arithmetic stratification (Definition~\ref{def:stop_classes}): $\mathrm{UAA}$ is the unique boundary stop, $\mathrm{UAG}$ is a non-boundary stop with $\Delta=34$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$ is a non-boundary stop with maximal uplift $\Delta=55$.
Any biological mechanism that differentiates the three stop codons must therefore act through context-sensitive factors beyond boundary occupancy at $m=6$.

\subsection{Recoding and stop-context sensitivity as an uplift interface}
\label{sec:recoding_uplift_interface}

At the arithmetic level, the three standard stop codons have distinct $(V,\Delta)$ signatures under $\mu^\ast$ (Section~\ref{sec:stop_fine_structure}):
$\mathrm{UAA}$ is boundary-aligned with $\Delta=34$, $\mathrm{UAG}$ is non-boundary with the same $\Delta=34$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$ has maximal uplift $\Delta=55$.
This yields a quantitative interface prediction: context-sensitive termination and recoding should correlate with the uplift class $\Delta$ and with local stop-context uplift windows.
Empirically, translation termination is known to depend on the nucleotide context following the stop codon \cite{McCaughanBrownDalphinBerryTate1995}, and recoding phenomena exist in which $\mathrm{UGA}$ can specify selenocysteine and $\mathrm{UAG}$ can specify pyrrolysine in specialized contexts \cite{HatfieldGladyshev2002,SrinivasanJamesKrzycki2002}.

The framework here does not attempt to derive these biochemical mechanisms.
Instead it provides an explicit quantitative signal family $(Z,U)$ that can be extracted from sequences and compared across stop classes and contexts using the protocols in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_tests}.

\paragraph{Baseline dependence and scale.}
In this dataset, the most stable separation is an upstream uplift shift: recoding sites exhibit substantially larger $\overline{U}_{\mathrm{before}}$ than CDS-deduplicated terminal stops across multiple radii $k$, while the downstream contrast is more $k$-dependent (Appendix~\ref{app:recoding_sites}).
This motivates multi-$k$ reporting and caution against treating a single-$k$ after-window contrast as an invariant signature.

\paragraph{Multi-resolution sensitivity.}
Because uplift $\Delta$ is defined relative to a finite Zeckendorf window, its empirical separability can change with resolution.
Accordingly, we also report rank-based discrimination summaries (AUC) across Fold$_m$ for $m\in\{6,7,8,9\}$ in Appendix~\ref{app:recoding_sites}.
At the codon scale ($N\in\{0,\dots,63\}$), $m=9$ is a saturating window and yields $\Delta_m\equiv 0$ (see Section~\ref{sec:resolution_upgrade}); the corresponding AUC entries therefore collapse to $0.5$ as a sanity check rather than a biological signal.

\paragraph{Window-size stability.}
To test whether stop-class differences persist across window sizes, we computed pairwise Mann--Whitney tests for $k\in\{3,5,10,20\}$:
\input{sections/generated/multi_k_stability_table}
The after-window effects (particularly UAA--UGA) are strong at small $k$ but attenuate at $k=20$, indicating that the signal is \emph{local} to the stop codon rather than distributed uniformly across the downstream region.
Before-window differences are consistently non-significant, aligning with the dicodon-null finding that ORF-level codon-pair structure explains upstream effects.

\paragraph{Boundary-sector check.}
A boundary-hit enrichment would be a distinct and stronger mechanism than an uplift shift.
Instead, boundary-hit rates in the $k=10$ windows around Sec sites are significantly \emph{depleted} relative to the coding background in the same dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:boundary_enrichment_results}), indicating that the recoding-context signal is not reducible to boundary-sector proximity at $m=6$.

\paragraph{CDS-level terminal-stop baselines and stratification.}
Because a single CDS can contain multiple \texttt{transl\_except} sites, terminal-stop contexts are treated at CDS resolution: each CDS contributes at most one terminal-stop window to the reference distribution.
We also report codon- and domain-stratified tests to make explicit which subsets drive the aggregate signal; in the present dataset, recoding is dominated by $\mathrm{UGA}$/Sec events and non-eukaryotic strata are small.

\paragraph{Within-CDS controls.}
In coding regions, the stop codons $\mathrm{UGA}$ and $\mathrm{UAG}$ are normally forbidden as internal codons; thus an internal same-codon control (Control-B) is typically unavailable once \texttt{transl\_except} sites are excluded.
Instead we compare each recoding site to random internal non-stop codon positions from the same CDS (Control-C), which provides a within-gene baseline for uplift-context windows.
The resulting signal persists under this within-CDS random-internal baseline, indicating that the aggregate contrast is not explained solely by between-gene variation.
However, composition-matched within-CDS controls can invert the direction of the comparison, showing that $U$-window statistics are strongly coupled to local GC and dinucleotide structure.
Accordingly, we treat composition-adjusted analyses against terminal-stop baselines as the more interpretable summary for the recoding dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:composition_controls}).
In the same dataset, the Control-C AUC sign reversals observed at $m=8$ are driven by the dominant Sec/UGA, eukaryotic stratum rather than by sparse Pyl events (Appendix~\ref{app:recoding_sites}).
Within this dominant stratum, the paired Control-C differences $dU_{\mathrm{before}}$ and $dU_{\Delta}$ shift markedly across $m$, and the correlation between $dU_{\mathrm{before}}$ and the paired GC contrast $dGC_{\mathrm{before}}$ changes sign at $m=8$ (Appendix~\ref{app:recoding_sites}), consistent with composition-coupled resolution sensitivity.

\subsection{Resolution upgrade and context}
\label{sec:resolution_upgrade}

Two limitations point to clear extensions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Higher resolution.} The window length $m=6$ is the minimal case matching $20+\mathrm{Stop}$ by cardinality.
Higher windows $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ introduce new degrees of freedom, richer boundary structure, and potentially a closer match to amino-acid degeneracy patterns \cite{Ma2025RF}.
\item \textbf{Sequence context.} Translation is context-sensitive (wobble pairing, recoding signals, local RNA structure).
In the folding language, context dependence naturally corresponds to conditioning the spectrum traces on neighborhoods and on reading-frame-aligned patterns.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Stability of the control objective across $m$.}
Because $m$ is a resolution parameter, it is natural to ask whether the same control-boundary alignment objective that identifies $\mu^\ast$ at $m=6$ remains stable when the Zeckendorf window is widened.
We performed an exhaustive scan for $m\in\{6,7,8,9\}$ using the same control set $\{\mathrm{AUG,UAA,UAG,UGA}\}$ and the boundary-hit score $S_m$ (Definition~\ref{def:control_boundary_score} with $\mathrm{Fold}_6$ replaced by $\mathrm{Fold}_m$).
At $m=6$, the optimum is unique and equals $\mu^\ast$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:unique_encoding}); at $m=7$, the maximal score remains $2$ but $\mu^\ast$ attains $0$, because the shared folded word of $\mathrm{AUG}$ and $\mathrm{UAA}$ is not in the boundary sector.
For $m\in\{8,9\}$, the maximal score drops to $1$ and multiple encodings tie for the maximum.
These facts, summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}, indicate that the $m=6$ identifiability is a minimal-resolution phenomenon tied to the special boundary preimage structure at window length $6$.
Multi-resolution objectives (e.g.\ maximizing $\sum_{m\in M} S_m$ over $M=\{6,7,8\}$) can be considered, but are not used as premises in the present work.

\paragraph{Saturating resolution at the codon scale.}
In this paper the microstate index is codon-scale: $N\in\{0,\dots,63\}$ under a two-bit encoding.
The Zeckendorf expansion of any $N\le 63$ uses at most nine Fibonacci weights ($1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34,55$), hence the length-$9$ window contains the full admissible digit string.
Therefore $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ is lossless for $m\ge 9$ at this scale: $V_m(N)=N$ and the uplift vanishes identically, $\Delta_m(N)=N-V_m(N)\equiv 0$.
Consequently, uplift-based statistics are nontrivial only for $m\le 8$ in the codon-scale model; including $m=9$ is useful as an internal consistency check but cannot carry discriminative signal in $\Delta_m$.
At the same time, the cyclic boundary condition $w_1=w_m=1$ becomes increasingly restrictive as $m$ grows on a fixed $N\le 63$ range, so the set of boundary-mapping codons is not monotone in $m$; Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables} records both the boundary-codon counts and the stop-codon signatures across $m\in\{6,7,8,9\}$ under $\mu^\ast$.
On the index layer, this behavior is explained by the explicit boundary preimage sets $B_m(\le 63)=\{N\le 63:\mathrm{Fold}_m(N)\in X_m^{\mathrm{bdry}}\}$, which change sharply with $m$ (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}): for example $|B_6(\le 63)|=6$ with $\{14,17,19,48,51,53\}$, whereas $B_7(\le 63)$ consists of five indices $\le 33$ and $B_9(\le 63)$ consists of three indices $\ge 55$.
This implies that at $m=7$ any boundary hit necessarily has $\Delta_7=0$ (since all boundary indices lie below the missing weight $34$), and that at $m=9$ the only boundary hits are among the three indices that already carry the $55$-weight digit and the leading $1$ digit.
Under $\mu^\ast$ this makes $\mathrm{UGA}$ boundary at $m=9$ but not $\mathrm{UAA}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}), while at $m=7$ the shared word $w_7(\mathrm{AUG})=w_7(\mathrm{UAA})=\texttt{1000010}$ fails the boundary condition because its last digit is $0$.

\paragraph{What is $m=6$-specific and what is not.}
The identification theorem and the start--stop $14/48$ symmetry are statements about the specific minimal-resolution interface $(\Omega_6,\mathrm{Fold}_6,X_6)$: changing $m$ changes both the target space and the boundary condition, and the same control set need not identify the same encoding.
By contrast, the \emph{sequence-level} observables (context windows of $\Delta_m$ and derived rank/discrimination statistics) can be computed for any $m$ and are naturally treated as a robustness axis: effects that persist across $m\in\{6,7,8\}$ are stronger candidates for biological relevance than effects visible at only one resolution.

\subsection{Negative results and transparent reporting}
\label{sec:negative_results}

Scientific integrity requires reporting results that do not support the main hypothesis alongside those that do.
We document several negative or null findings from the computational reinforcement program:

\paragraph{Incremental predictive power: mixed evidence.}
We tested two versions of the nested-model analysis for incremental uplift signal.
In the original M0/M1/M2 hierarchy (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}), adding uplift to composition features yielded $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{M2-M1}=-0.0015$ with $p=0.57$, suggesting redundancy.
However, an extended model hierarchy (M0--M5) incorporating explicit structure proxies and translation-rate features reveals a different picture:
\input{sections/generated/nested_models_extended_table}
In this extended analysis, uplift provides \emph{significant} incremental AUC ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{M2-M1}=+0.0017$, $p=0.01$; $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{M4-M3}=+0.0039$, $p=0.01$) after controlling for GC, dinucleotide, and structure features.
Translation-rate proxies (tAI) also contribute additional signal ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{M5-M4}=+0.0014$, $p=0.01$).
The discrepancy between analyses may reflect differences in model specification or sampling; we report both for transparency.

\paragraph{Generative null models: dicodon vs dinucleotide.}
We tested two complementary generative nulls to assess whether stop-class differences survive sequence shuffling.
Under the \emph{ORF-level dicodon null} (Eulerian-path permutation preserving the full CDS+stop codon-pair multiset), the observed stop-class differences in $\overline{U}_{\mathrm{before}}$ are no longer significant ($p>0.29$ for all pairwise contrasts; Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}).
However, under the \emph{per-window dinucleotide null} (Eulerian-path permutation of the $k$-codon window sequence preserving dinucleotide counts), the before-window contrasts remain highly significant:
\input{sections/generated/stop_context_window_dinuc_null}
This discrepancy suggests that the codon-pair (dicodon) structure at the ORF level is a stronger confounder than the dinucleotide structure at the window level, and that some residual stop-class signal in the before-window persists after dinucleotide shuffling.

\paragraph{Uplift--structure correlation is fully explained by composition.}
To test whether uplift encodes RNA secondary structure information beyond simple composition, we computed Spearman correlations between $\overline{U}(k)$ and a GC-based structure proxy (higher GC $\to$ more stable folding) for $k=10$ windows around terminal stops.
\input{sections/generated/stop_context_window_structure_corr}
The raw correlation is significant ($\rho\approx -0.16$, $p<0.001$), but after partialling out GC and dinucleotide composition, the correlation vanishes ($\rho\approx 0$, $p>0.9$).
This confirms that uplift does not capture structural information beyond what is already present in composition features, consistent with the lack of incremental predictive power in the nested-model analysis.

\paragraph{Cross-task validation yields mixed rankings.}
Under a uniform prior over all $24$ encodings, $\mu^\ast$ does not dominate the independent validation tasks:
it ranks $8/24$ on the RefSeq stop-class AUC task, $5/24$ on the recoding AUC task, and $3/24$ on the nonstandard-table Fisher score (Appendix~\ref{app:cross_task_validation}).
The combined Fisher $p$-value is $0.0833$, which is suggestive but not strongly significant at conventional thresholds.
In particular, the RefSeq stop-class task alone yields $p_A=0.33$, indicating that $\mu^\ast$ is not exceptional for that specific metric.

\paragraph{Out-of-sample RefSeq bucket is weak.}
In the three-bucket out-of-sample design, the RefSeq bucket (B1) shows $\mu^\ast$ at rank $14/24$ ($p=0.58$), while the recoding and nonstandard buckets are more favorable ($3/24$ and $2/24$ respectively; Appendix~\ref{app:out_of_sample_validation}).
This asymmetry suggests that the biological signal, if present, is domain- or task-dependent.

\paragraph{Start--stop corpus-level symmetry is not detected.}
The $14/48$ start--stop boundary coincidence is an arithmetic fact under $\mu^\ast$.
However, when we test whether this structure manifests as a corpus-level symmetry statistic $T_k$, $\mu^\ast$ ranks $20/24$ ($p=0.83$) under the encoding null (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}).
Thus, the ``arithmetic symmetry'' does not translate into a detectable corpus-level pattern in the current RefSeq sample.

\paragraph{Control-set sensitivity.}
The uniqueness of $\mu^\ast$ as the control-boundary optimum depends on the choice of control set $\mathcal{K}$.
When $\mathcal{K}$ is extended to include alternative start codons or reduced to stops only, $\mu^\ast$ is no longer uniquely optimal (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}).
This underscores that the identification result is contingent on the minimal prior $\mathcal{K}=\{\mathrm{AUG,UAA,UAG,UGA}\}$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
These negative results do not invalidate the arithmetic framework, but they constrain its empirical scope.
The $m=6$ identification theorem and the $14/48$ symmetry remain mathematically exact; however, the downstream sequence-level predictions are either weak, task-dependent, or confounded by composition.
Future work should (i) seek independent datasets where uplift provides incremental signal, (ii) design controlled experiments (Appendix~\ref{app:bio_validation}) that isolate arithmetic contributions from compositional correlates, and (iii) explore whether higher-resolution templates ($m>6$) reveal stronger or more stable biological signatures.

\subsection{A research program}
\label{sec:program}

The scripts provided here are designed to support a sustained experimental program:
\begin{itemize}
\item scan known nonstandard genetic codes to test the boundary-migration prediction (P3);
\item compute $(Z,U)$ statistics around known recoding sites (selenocysteine, pyrrolysine) to test the uplift hypothesis (P2) \cite{HatfieldGladyshev2002,SrinivasanJamesKrzycki2002};
\item compare codon-usage biases with the folding-derived spectra to probe whether organisms preferentially select codons with certain $(Z,U)$ profiles under ecological or translational constraints.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Mechanistic bridge: translation rate proxies.}
A critical question is whether the arithmetic uplift $\Delta$ captures any mechanistic aspect of translation beyond sequence composition.
We tested the correlation between uplift and tRNA Adaptation Index (tAI), a standard proxy for translation rate:

At the codon level, uplift and tAI are uncorrelated ($\rho=0.005$, $p=0.97$), indicating that the arithmetic structure does not directly encode translational efficiency.
At the window level, the raw correlation is negative (high uplift $\to$ low tAI), but this reverses after controlling for GC content, suggesting that GC is a strong confounder in the uplift--translation-rate relationship.

\paragraph{RNA secondary structure: actual MFE analysis.}
Using ViennaRNA, we computed actual minimum free energy (MFE) for stop-context windows and tested whether ``arithmetic friction'' manifests as thermodynamic instability:

\textbf{Counterintuitively}, high Uplift correlates with \emph{lower} MFE (more stable structure, $\rho\approx -0.2$), contrary to the hypothesis that arithmetic friction corresponds to energetic instability.
However, after controlling for GC content, the correlation effectively vanishes ($|\rho|<0.08$), indicating that this relationship is entirely mediated by composition: high-GC sequences simultaneously have low Uplift and stable structures.

To test whether uplift patterns distinguish recoding sites qualitatively, we performed a case study on well-characterized selenocysteine (Sec) insertion sites:

\paragraph{Ribosome profiling: future work.}
Direct mapping of Ribo-seq data onto the RefSeq corpus to test whether high-$\overline{U}_{\mathrm{after}}$ windows exhibit elevated ribosome pause scores remains as future work.
This would provide the most direct mechanistic link between arithmetic uplift and translation dynamics, connecting ``arithmetic friction'' to observable ribosome behavior.
