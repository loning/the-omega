\section{\texorpdfstring{$\varph$--$\pi$--$e$}{phi-pi-e} Three-Channel Stability Decomposition}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\varph$}{phi} channel: Syntactic legality and Fibonacci growth}

Define the $\varph$ channel as the syntactic constraint with local prohibition law $11$, and the stable type space is $X_m$. Its count satisfies a Fibonacci recurrence. Setting $F_1=F_2=1$, we have
\[
\card{X_m}=F_{m+2}.
\]
This identity transforms ``golden'' from rhetoric into strict growth law: the number of stable types grows at rate $\varph^m$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi} channel: Periodic closure and cycle/boundary splitting}

Periodic orbit counting requires that the connection of endpoints also satisfies the syntactic constraint. Define the cyclic admissible set
\[
X_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}:=\{w\in X_m:\ w_m w_1\neq 11\},
\quad
X_m^{\mathrm{bdry}}:=X_m\setminus X_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}.
\]
Then there exists an explicit decomposition
\[
\card{X_m^{\mathrm{bdry}}}=F_{m-2},\qquad
\card{X_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}}=F_{m+2}-F_{m-2}=F_{m-1}+F_{m+1}.
\]
The latter is the Lucas number $L_m$, which can also be written as the trace of the Fibonacci companion matrix
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}
\]
as
\[
\card{X_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}}=\Tr(A^m)=L_m.
\]
The justification for the $\pi$ channel is that periodic closure and orbit counting (e.g., Artin--Mazur type $\zeta$ functions) are sensitive to endpoint admissibility; thus ``closure'' is a stability condition consistent with periodic dynamics. $\pi$ appears here as the symbol constant for ``closed phase geometry'': its role is intimately tied to the periodic structure of the unit circle group $U(1)$ and its metric normalization.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$e$}{e} channel: Analytic stability domains and logarithmic readout cost}

At the readout layer, the concatenation of probability weights is multiplicative, while additive cost is obtained through logarithmic readout. Define the cost of weight $w\in(0,1]$ as
\[
C:=-\log w.
\]
Then the concatenated weight $w_{\mathrm{tot}}=\prod_j w_j$ corresponds to total cost $C_{\mathrm{tot}}=\sum_j C_j$. Under weak regularity conditions such as continuity and measurability, the logarithm is the unique (up to a proportionality constant) homomorphism mapping multiplicative composition to additive composition, so $e$ as the base of the natural logarithm is uniquely determined by structural necessity.
Furthermore, when stability is expressed as analyticity of certain generating functions or $\zeta$ functions (such as the convergence radius determined by the spectral radius of a transfer operator), the $e$ channel can be understood as the encoding constant for ``analytic stability domains'': its essence is the relationship between exponential weights $e^{-C}$ and pole barriers.

\subsection{Channel merging at minimal window and separation at high resolution}

In the minimal local model, the ``prohibition of adjacent events'' in the $e$ channel can coincide exactly with the $\varph$ channel's syntactic legality (the same local prohibition law); in this case the three-channel decomposition degenerates into a two-type structure: syntactic legality and periodic closure remain distinguishable, while analytic stability coincides with syntactic legality. High-resolution cases (longer windows, weighted transfer operators, or countable-state syntax) can lead to separation of analytic stability domains from local syntactic constraints, making the $e$ channel an independently verifiable object.
