\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

For a mixing subshift of finite type $(X_A^+,\sigma)$ with primitive
adjacency matrix $A$, the dynamical $\zeta$-function
\[
  \zeta_A(z)
  =\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\Tr(A^n)}{n}z^n\right)
  =\det(I-zA)^{-1}
\]
is rational, with poles at the reciprocals of the non-zero eigenvalues
of \(A\). The primitive orbit counts
\[
  p_n(A)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\Tr(A^{n/d})
\]
are recovered from the trace sequence by M\"obius inversion. These
facts are classical. The present paper is concerned with finer
information at the Perron pole \(z=\lambda^{-1}\), where
\(\lambda=\rho(A)\) is the Perron root.

After removing the Perron factor, one is left with two closely related
objects: the Abel finite-part constant \(\log\MM(A)\) and the reduced
determinant
\[
  C(A)=\det\nolimits'(I-A/\lambda)^{-1}.
\]
Our starting point is that these quantities interact naturally with the
cyclic lifts \(A^{\langle q\rangle}=A\otimes P_q\) and with the values
of the reduced spectral polynomial
\[
  F_A(t)=\frac{\det(I-tA/\lambda)}{1-t}
\]
at roots of unity. This leads to explicit formulas for the finite part,
to reconstruction of the non-Perron spectrum from cyclic-lift data, and
to arithmetic consequences for congruence classes and finite-group
extensions.

The paper has two main themes. The first is that the sequence of
reduced determinants of cyclic lifts already contains the full reduced
spectral polynomial. The second is that, for finite-group extensions,
Peter--Weyl decomposition gives a twisted orbit-counting theory parallel
to the root-of-unity decompositions in the abelian setting. The point
is not the rationality of \(\zeta_A\), which is standard, but the
systematic use of the finite part at the Perron pole as a spectral
invariant.

\subsection{Main results}

Our first theorem summarises the finite-part and cyclic-lift results.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-finite-part-fingerprint}
Let \(M\) be a primitive non-negative matrix with Perron root
\(\lambda=\rho(M)>1\), non-Perron eigenvalues
\(\mu_2,\dots,\mu_d\), and primitive orbit counts \(p_n(M)\). Set
\[
  F_M(t):=\frac{\det(I-tM/\lambda)}{1-t}
  =\prod_{j=2}^d\left(1-\frac{\mu_j}{\lambda}t\right),
  \qquad
  C(M):=\det\nolimits'(I-M/\lambda)^{-1}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item The Abel finite-part constant satisfies
  \[
    \log\MM(M)
    =\log C(M)
     +\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n(M)\bigl(\lambda^{-n}+\log(1-\lambda^{-n})\bigr).
  \]
  In particular, \(\log C(M)-\log\MM(M)\) is strictly positive and
  admits explicit truncation bounds.

  \item For the cyclic lifts \(M^{\langle q\rangle}=M\otimes P_q\),
  \[
    C(M^{\langle q\rangle})
    =\frac{1}{q}\prod_{j=2}^d
      \left(1-\left(\frac{\mu_j}{\lambda}\right)^q\right)^{-1},
    \qquad q\ge 1.
  \]
  Equivalently,
  \[
    \Psi_q(M):=\log q+\log C(M^{\langle q\rangle})
    =\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{u_{qm}(M)}{m},
    \qquad
    u_n(M):=\sum_{j=2}^d\left(\frac{\mu_j}{\lambda}\right)^n.
  \]

  \item The sequence \(\{C(M^{\langle q\rangle})\}_{q\ge 1}\)
  determines the reduced spectral polynomial \(F_M\) and hence the
  non-Perron spectrum of \(M\) with algebraic multiplicity. Moreover,
  \[
    \limsup_{q\to\infty}\abs{\Psi_q(M)}^{1/q}
    =\max_{2\le j\le d}\abs{\mu_j}/\lambda.
  \]
  If \(q\ge d\), then the values
  \(\{F_M(\omega_q^j)\}_{0\le j\le q-1}\) already determine \(F_M\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

Several results in \Cref{sec:finite-part} refine this statement. For
prime moduli,
\Cref{thm:finite-part-dirichlet-character-inversion-prime} recovers the
residue-class decomposition of the reduced power sums from one
character table, together with \(C(M)\) and \(C(M^{\langle p\rangle})\).
\Cref{cor:finite-part-near-rh-q-axis-envelope} and
\Cref{thm:finite-part-single-layer-torsion-near-rh} give equivalent
forms of the square-root bound for the reduced spectrum, one in terms
of the growth of the cyclic-lift constants and one in terms of one
root-of-unity layer. \Cref{thm:finite-part-boundary-multiplicity-qaxis-energy}
recovers the multiplicity pattern on the boundary of the reduced
spectrum from Cesaro averages, and
\Cref{thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-polylog-dirichlet-xi} associates to
the discrepancy \(\Psi_q(M)-\log C(M)\) a Dirichlet series with residue
\(-\log C(M)\) at \(s=1\). Finally,
\Cref{cor:finite-part-torsion-table-galois-certificate} gives a
rationality test for one root-of-unity layer, and
\Cref{thm:finite-part-residue-drift-trichotomy} shows that, for
exponentially growing families with subexponential state complexity,
linear growth of \(\log C\) is incompatible with the square-root bound.

Our second theorem concerns finite-group extensions. Let \(G\) be a
finite group, let \(\tau:E_A\to G\) be an edge cocycle, and let
\(\widetilde A\) be the adjacency matrix of the skew-product
extension. For \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\) write \(A_\varrho\) for the
twisted transition matrix and \(\chi_\varrho\) for its character. For
a class function \(\phi:G\to\CC\), define
\[
  T_{\phi,n}:=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^n)}\phi(\tau_n(x)),
  \qquad
  \mathcal L_\phi(z):=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{T_{\phi,n}}{n}z^n,
\]
and
\[
  \Pi_\phi(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\phi(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|}.
\]
For a conjugacy class \(C\subset G\), set
\[
  \Delta_C(A):=\mathsf M_C(A)-\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathsf M(A).
\]
For \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\), write
\[
  \Pi_\varrho(z):=\sum_{\gamma\in\cP}\chi_\varrho(\tau(\gamma))z^{|\gamma|},
\]
and decompose the Adams transforms of the irreducible characters as
\[
  \psi^m\chi_\varrho=\sum_{\sigma\in\Irr(G)}a_{\varrho,\sigma}^{(m)}\chi_\sigma.
\]

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:intro-peter-weyl}
In the above setting:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
  \item The extension determinant factorises as
  \[
    \det(I-z\widetilde A)
    =\prod_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{\dim\varrho}.
  \]

  \item For every class function \(\phi\) and every \(|z|<\lambda^{-1}\),
  \[
    \mathcal L_\phi(z)
    =\sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
      \widehat\phi(\varrho)\,
      \log\det(I-zA_\varrho)^{-1},
  \]
  and
  \[
    \Pi_\phi(z)
    =\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,
      \mathcal L_{\psi^m\phi}(z^m).
  \]
  Consequently, for every \(\varrho\in\Irr(G)\),
  \[
    \Pi_\varrho(z)
    =\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}
      \sum_{\sigma\in\Irr(G)}
        a_{\varrho,\sigma}^{(m)}
        \log\det(I-z^mA_\sigma)^{-1},
  \]

  \item If \(p_{n,C}\) denotes the number of primitive orbits of length
  \(n\) whose \(G\)-label lies in the conjugacy class \(C\subset G\),
  then
  \[
    \sum_{n\ge 1}p_{n,C}z^n
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\varrho\in\Irr(G)}
      \chi_\varrho(C^{-1})\,\Pi_\varrho(z).
  \]
  If
  \[
    \eta:=\max_{\varrho\neq\mathbf 1}\rho(A_\varrho)<\lambda,
    \qquad
    \Lambda_*:=\max\{\Lambda(A),\eta,\lambda^{1/2}\},
  \]
  then
  \[
    p_{n,C}
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}\frac{\lambda^n}{n}
     +O\!\left(\frac{\Lambda_*^n}{n}\right),
  \]
  \[
    \sum_{n\le N}\frac{p_{n,C}}{\lambda^n}
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}\log N+\mathsf M_C(A)+O(N^{-1}),
  \]
  with
  \[
    \mathsf M_C(A)
    =\frac{|C|}{|G|}\mathsf M(A)
     +\frac{|C|}{|G|}\sum_{\varrho\neq \mathbf 1}
      \chi_\varrho(C^{-1})\,\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1}).
  \]
  Moreover,
  \[
    \Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})
    =\sum_{C\subset G}\Delta_C(A)\chi_\varrho(C),
    \qquad \varrho\neq \mathbf 1,
  \]
  and
  \[
    \sum_{C\subset G}\frac{|G|}{|C|}\abs{\Delta_C(A)}^2
    =\sum_{\varrho\neq \mathbf 1}
      \abs{\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})}^2.
  \]

  \item The primitive class profile is functorial under passage to
  quotient groups: if \(q:G\twoheadrightarrow Q\) and
  \(\bar\tau=q\circ\tau\), then pullback of class functions intertwines
  both \(\mathcal L_\phi\) and \(\Pi_\phi\). Consequently the
  abelianisation \(G_{\mathrm{ab}}\) gives the exact one-dimensional
  shadow of the finite-group extension, while the higher-dimensional
  values \(\Pi_\varrho(\lambda^{-1})\) measure the genuine non-abelian
  defect. An explicit \(S_3\)-extension shows that a channel may have
  vanishing periodic traces and still carry non-zero primitive bias.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The point of \Cref{sec:chebotarev} is that finite-group extensions are
not merely a Peter--Weyl restatement of orbit counting. The
class-function logarithms, the Adams operations, and the M\"obius
inversion on primitive orbits combine to produce a non-abelian
determinant calculus parallel to the root-of-unity reconstruction
theory of \Cref{sec:finite-part}.

\Cref{sec:shadows} closes this parallel into a theorem. It proves that
the primitive class profile is functorial under passage to quotient
groups, identifies the abelian shadow as the exact image under
abelianisation, and isolates the higher-dimensional Peter--Weyl
channels as the genuine non-abelian defect. The explicit \(S_3\)-model
then shows that vanishing periodic traces do not imply vanishing
primitive bias.

\subsection{Relation with previous work}

For shifts of finite type, the rationality of dynamical
\(\zeta\)-functions goes back to Bowen--Lanford and Manning
\cite{BowenLanford1970Zeta,Manning1971Axiom}. The operator-theoretic
framework was developed by Ruelle and by Parry--Pollicott
\cite{Ruelle1976ZetaExpanding,Ruelle1978Thermodynamic,ParryPollicott1990Zeta};
see also \cite{LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998} for general symbolic
background. Twisted transfer operators and their role in orbit-counting
problems are standard in thermodynamic formalism, and on the
equidistribution side one should compare our orbit-splitting results
with the Chebotarev-type theorems of Pollicott and Sharp
\cite{PollicottSharp2007Chebotarev}. For graph coverings, the
determinantal identities should be compared with Ihara--Bass theory and
its weighted variants
\cite{Bass1992Ihara,ChoeKwakParkSato2007Bartholdi,Sato2008WeightedBartholdiCoverings}.

What seems to be new here is the systematic use of the finite part at
the Perron pole as an invariant from which one can recover reduced
spectral data. The cyclic-lift constants provide a natural arithmetic
family attached to the reduced determinant, and the root-of-unity
formulas show that one sufficiently large layer already determines the
reduced spectral polynomial. On the non-abelian side, previous work
studied orbit splitting, Chebotarev-type equidistribution, and the
structural theory of finite-group extensions
\cite{ParryPollicott1986Chebotarev,NooraniParry1992ChebotarevShifts,BoyleSchmieding2017FiniteGroupExtensions,Parry1999LivsicNonAbelian}.
The new point here is different: primitive Peter--Weyl series are
written directly in terms of twisted determinants by Adams--M\"obius
inversion, and the conjugacy-class Mertens constants become exact
non-abelian Fourier coordinates of the twisted primitive invariants.
Accordingly, the last section is not another Chebotarev theorem in the
equidistribution sense; its novelty lies in the primitive-orbit
determinant calculus and in the explicit reconstruction of the twisted
primitive data from class constants.

\subsection{Organisation}

\Cref{sec:preliminaries} fixes notation for primitive orbit counts,
reduced determinants, cyclic lifts, and finite-group extensions. In
\Cref{sec:finite-part} we prove the finite-part formula, the cyclic-lift
identities, the reconstruction results for the reduced spectrum, and
the Dirichlet-series consequences. \Cref{sec:chebotarev}
develops the class-function and Adams--M\"obius theory for finite-group
extensions, including explicit conjugacy-class Mertens constants and
their non-abelian Fourier reconstruction. Finally,
\Cref{sec:shadows} proves quotient functoriality, identifies the
abelian shadow and the genuine non-abelian defect, and works out an
explicit \(S_3\)-example.
