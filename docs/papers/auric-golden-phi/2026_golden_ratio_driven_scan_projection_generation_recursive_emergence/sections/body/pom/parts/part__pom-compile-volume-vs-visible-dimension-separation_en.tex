\begin{remark}[Separation of Compilation Volume and Visible Dimension: Polynomial Volume Upper Bound of Symmetric Power Quotient Kernel vs.\ Strict Compression of Minimal Realization]\label{rem:pom-compile-volume-vs-visible-dimension}
For \(q\)-fold collision, directly taking \(q\) copies of synchronous direct product over the base kernel produces exponential state space \(|Q|^q\) (where \(|Q|\) is the base state count).
In the case with leaf permutation symmetry (Remark \ref{rem:pom-star-sympress}), taking the symmetric group quotient compresses the exponential state space to the occupation-number space \(N_q\), whose cardinality satisfies the polynomial upper bound
\[
|N_q|\ \le\ \binom{q+|Q|-1}{|Q|-1}.
\]
Correspondingly, the symmetric power quotient kernel \(\widetilde A_q\) yields an executable "compilation backend volume" metric: \(\log|N_q|\) records the combinatorial state-space size, not the true degrees of freedom in spectral semantics.
\par
On the other hand, Hankel minimization on the sequence side (Remarks \ref{rem:pom-minh-functor}; \ref{rem:pom-sympower-then-minh}) compresses the same collision moment sequence \(S_q(m)\) to its minimal integer-coefficient recurrence order \(d_q\), equivalent to the minimal realization dimension (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-moment-minrecurrence-hankel}).
In the verified range, \(d_q\) is often significantly smaller than the above compilation volume (e.g., \(d_4=5\) see Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_moment_spectrum_k2_8}; at higher orders resonance gaps appear \(\Delta(q)=(2\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1)-d_q>0\), see Remark \ref{rem:pom-sympower-then-minh}).
\par
Thus in this manuscript's framework one must distinguish two types of "volume":
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Compilation volume \(\log|N_q|\)}: Determined by symmetric power quotient kernel/occupation-number space, belonging to the combinatorial volume of the implementation backend;
  \item \textbf{Visible dimension \(d_q\)}: Determined by Hankel rank/minimal realization, which is the truly auditable dimension of collision sequence semantics at the boundary.
\end{itemize}
The difference between the two is the structural redundancy induced by symmetry and algebraic constraints; it is not an "optimization trick", but a verifiable regime-level compression phenomenon (e.g., yielded by Hankel null certificates).
\end{remark}
