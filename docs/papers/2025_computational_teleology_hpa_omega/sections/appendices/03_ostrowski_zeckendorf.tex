\section{Ostrowski/Zeckendorf numeration and readout space complexity}
\label{app:ostrowski_zeckendorf}

We briefly recall the numeration objects used to quantify readout space.

\paragraph{Mechanical words and prefix sums.}
For irrational rotation by $\alpha$ and window offset $\beta$, the Sturmian prefix sum identity
\begin{equation}
S_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}s_k=\lfloor N\alpha+\beta\rfloor-\lfloor\beta\rfloor
\end{equation}
connects symbolic readout to a rigid ``staircase vs line'' geometry. This is the basic tool for translating prefix length into approximation/resolution.

\paragraph{Ostrowski numeration.}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ have continued fraction expansion $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\dots]$ and convergents $p_n/q_n$.
Ostrowski numeration represents integers as constrained digit sums over the denominators $q_n$.
The digit constraints are determined by the partial quotients $a_n$; see standard references in Diophantine approximation \cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}.

\paragraph{Golden specialization.}
For $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ one has $a_n\equiv 1$ and $q_n=F_{n+1}$ (Fibonacci numbers). The digit constraints reduce to ``no adjacent $1$'' in a Fibonacci basis, yielding Zeckendorf uniqueness \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.

\paragraph{Space complexity meaning.}
In the scan--readout semantics, the Ostrowski/Zeckendorf digit length is a discrete measure of how much orthogonal-cut grammar is needed to specify the readout coordinate at a given resolution.
