\section{From ``parameter-input chemistry'' to ``geometric-inevitability chemistry''}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{The origin problem of chemistry}
Chemistry is empirically robust: atoms and molecules exist stably across wide ranges of temperature and pressure, exhibit reproducible valence patterns, and possess sharp spectroscopic fingerprints. In the standard nonrelativistic account, chemical bonding emerges from the competition between electronic kinetic energy and Coulomb attraction, together with the Born--Oppenheimer (BO) separation that makes molecular geometry meaningful as a slowly varying nuclear scaffold \cite{BornOppenheimer1927,SzaboOstlund1996}.

However, two dimensionless quantities dominate the very possibility of this structure:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Electromagnetic coupling strength} $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$, which fixes atomic-unit scales $a_0$ and $E_h$ and hence the absolute energy/length scale of chemistry.
\item \textbf{Nuclear--electronic mass hierarchy} $\mu=m_p/m_e$, which fixes the small parameter $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BO}}\sim \mu^{-1/2}$ and hence the separation between electronic, vibrational, and rotational degrees of freedom.
\end{itemize}
In the conventional narrative, $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ and $\mu$ are empirical inputs; chemical stability is then described as the fact that these happen to land in a ``habitable window.'' This viewpoint makes the existence of chemistry appear as contingent.

\subsection{HPA--\texorpdfstring{$\Omega$}{Omega}: constants as protocol invariants}
The HPA--$\Omega$ program proposes a different starting point: observable dimensionless ``constants'' are not adjustable parameters but invariants of a scan--projection readout protocol. In this viewpoint, ``probability,'' ``noise,'' and ``thermodynamics'' are not external assumptions but emerge from finite-resolution readout. The framework therefore enforces a methodological discipline: identify a closed set of axioms and geometric definitions, derive invariants, then connect to operational constants through a minimal interface map \cite{Ma2025HPOT,Ma2025FoP,Ma2025PCG}.

Within this discipline, the constants-geometry manuscript \cite{Ma2025PCG} proposes and audits a concrete geometricization of $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ and $\mu$:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi,\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}=6\pi^5.
\end{equation}
The present paper asks a chemistry-facing question:
\begin{quote}
Can chemical bonding and molecular stability be rephrased as necessary consequences of the same protocol-geometric invariants $(\alpha_{\mathrm{em}},\mu)$?
\end{quote}

\subsection{Contributions and structure}
Our contributions are twofold.
\begin{enumerate}
\item We provide a chemistry-oriented bridge from the geometric invariants $(\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}},\mu_{\mathrm{geo}})$ to atomic units and BO scale separation, isolating how they rigidly fix (i) the eV scale of chemistry and (ii) the stability of molecular geometry and isotope effects.
\item We anchor the above scale claims with rigorous stability-of-matter results (placing $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ inside known stability regimes) and with the textbook $\mathrm{H}_2^+$ Born--Oppenheimer curve as the minimal molecule-level reference.
\end{enumerate}
