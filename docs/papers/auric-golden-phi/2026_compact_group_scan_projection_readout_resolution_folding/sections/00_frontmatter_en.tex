\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes a unified formal framework centered on ``scan---projection readout---resolution folding'' to characterize how discrete scanning of a finite observer on a compact group induces binary-valued readouts, stable grammar, and auditable statistical certificates, further connected to addressable locality, computational compilation overhead, and arithmetic rigidity. The framework takes three-valued existential logic as its starting point: a concept's ``existence'' is first formalized as definability (whether an address provides it), subsequently projected by a readout operator into a binary event. Compact group scanning is generated by a pair of incompatible Weyl unitary operators, ensuring inherent complementarity between scanning time and pointer phase, thereby producing ineliminable mismatch and irreducible statistical uncertainty at finite resolution. The paper derives extremal properties of golden branches (golden ratio-related slopes) in the sense of phase-locking resistance, constructs a measurable readout model for ``golden gating,'' and introduces at the finite window level a resolution folding map that compresses $2^m$ primitive readout words into $F_{m+2}$ golden grammatically-stable words (no adjacent $1$'s), obtaining explicit multi-to-one fiber structures and degenerate spectra. Further, the paper treats Hilbert curve-style addressing as a ``time---space interface,'' interpreting the geometrization of subalgebras accessible to finite observers, and proposes a ``compute---geometry dictionary'': compilation routing overhead fields induce effective lapse, thereby reproducing gravity-like redshift structures in a pure information-theoretic/computational semantic. Finally, the paper incorporates $q$-expansions of modular forms at cusps and Hecke skeleta generated by primes into a single chain, proposes an end-to-end reproducible protocol from scanning data to arithmetic consistency verification, and provides verifiable predictions and control experiment designs.
\end{abstract}
