\section{Discussion: Validity Boundaries, Identification Tasks, and Theoretical Completeness}

\subsection{Validity Boundaries}

The rigorous content of this framework lies in: cut-and-project and scan readout can map static high-dimensional discrete structure to three-dimensional quasi-periodic geometry and temporal sequence; finite-resolution readout induces equivalence classes and statistics; stable sectors provide a protocol definition for ``reified'' structure. Going further to identify stable types as specific particle spectra, and defect channels as specific interaction fields, is identification-layer work that must proceed through explicit data fitting and falsifiable intermediate propositions.

\subsection{Identification Tasks}

Core identification tasks required for theoretical completion include:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Construct reversible ``readout--type'' re-encoding pipelines on concrete experimental data, verify degeneracy fingerprints and quasi-periodic spectra;
  \item Provide cross-scale resolution increase rules ($m\to m+1$) and verify their stratified consistency with observational statistics;
  \item Provide computable connection structures for defect channels, making them correspond to observable interaction compensation terms or constraint consistency conditions.
\end{enumerate}
