
\section{Programmatic layer: ground state, energy, and discrepancy (not used in proofs)}
\label{sec:programmatic}

This section offers an interpretation-layer reading of the closed-layer rigidity.
Nothing here is used as an input to Theorem~\ref{thm:polar_rigidity}.

\subsection{RH as ``no exponential gain/loss''}
\label{sec:programmatic_no_gain}

The mode decomposition
\[
\e^{(\rho-\tfrac{1}{2})t}=\e^{(\beta-\tfrac{1}{2})t}\,\e^{\iu\gamma t}
\]
suggests a physical analogy: $\beta-\tfrac{1}{2}$ is a gain/loss rate and $\gamma$ is a frequency.
The critical line $\beta=\tfrac{1}{2}$ is precisely the locus of purely oscillatory modes, compatible with unitary scan evolution and bounded readout.
From this point of view, Theorem~\ref{thm:polar_rigidity} says that \emph{protocol admissibility forces the zeta spectrum to be ``ground-state stable''} in the sense of excluding exponential amplification under canonical Abel regularization.

\subsection{Discrepancy as an auditable proxy for finite-resource ``energy''}
\label{sec:programmatic_discrepancy_energy}

In HPA-style finite-resource analysis, deterministic error certificates often take the form
\[
\left|\avgN{f}-\int f\right|\ \le\ \HKVar(f)\,\DNstar(P_N),
\]
a Koksma--Hlawka type inequality controlling sampling error by the product of a kernel regularity measure and the star discrepancy of the scan orbit \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974, DrmotaTichy1997}.
The golden branch provides explicit logarithmic bounds on $\DNstar(P_N)$ (Section~\ref{sec:scan_discrepancy}), suggesting a low-complexity ``low-energy'' scan family.

In this programmatic reading, discrepancy controls a provable resource cost for approximating regulated traces.
Exponential modes induced by $\beta\neq\tfrac{1}{2}$ are precisely the kind of behavior that defeats such controlled, compact readout: they trigger internal Abel thresholds (Lemma~\ref{lem:abel_obstruction}) and hence force blow-ups in any reasonable finite-resource proxy.
The toy numerics in Section~\ref{sec:experiments} illustrate this contrast.
