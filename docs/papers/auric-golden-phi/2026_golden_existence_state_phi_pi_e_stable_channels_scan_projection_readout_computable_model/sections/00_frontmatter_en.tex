\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes and formalizes a ``scan--projection readout'' framework to describe how the binary proposition of ``existence/non-existence'' emerges as a readout projection of a single ontological state under finite-resolution observation, and achieves computable stable structures on the golden branch. The core construction is as follows: taking a normalized global state as the ontological carrier, we introduce a non-periodic unitary scan operator to generate access sequences; we employ finite-window projections to read out continuous phase trajectories as binary mechanistic words; under syntactic constraints (no adjacent ``11''), we obtain golden mean shift stable subspaces whose dimension grows according to Fibonacci law with window length, inducing a class of ``golden existence projections'' and ``golden existence states''. We further propose a three-channel stability decomposition: the $\varph$ channel corresponds to syntactic legality and self-similar growth; the $\pi$ channel corresponds to periodic closure and orbit counting structure; the $e$ channel corresponds to analytic stability domains and logarithmic readout cost. This allows the proposition ``only existence states can be observed'' to be rigorously formulated as a statistical statement conditioned on stable projection conditions. As a computable-layer result amenable to verification, we provide a folding map $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ based on Zeckendorf representation and window truncation, compressing $2^m$ microscopic states into $\card{X_m}$ stable types, and achieving a rigid $64\to 21$ compression with boundary/cycle $3\oplus 18$ splitting in the minimal case $m=6$. Finally, at the arithmetic skeleton level, we discuss primes as multiplicative generators, $\pi$ as a closed-phase geometric invariant, and $e$ as the unique continuous homomorphism from multiplicative weight to additive cost, and provide reproducible algorithmic interfaces and falsifiable hierarchical prediction lists.
\end{abstract}
