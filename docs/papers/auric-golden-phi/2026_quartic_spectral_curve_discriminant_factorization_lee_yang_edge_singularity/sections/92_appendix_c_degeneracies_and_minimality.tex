\section{Degenerate parameters and minimality check}\label{app:degeneracies}

This appendix records the explicit cancellations at the exceptional parameter values \(y\in\{-1,0,1\}\).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(y=1\)}{y=1}}

At \(y=1\),
\[
N(t,1)=-(t-1)(t+1)^2,
\qquad
D(t,1)=(t-1)(t+1)^2(2t-1),
\]
so
\[
Z(t,1)=\frac{N(t,1)}{D(t,1)}=\frac{1}{1-2t},
\qquad
Z_m(1)=2^m.
\]

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(y=0\)}{y=0}}

At \(y=0\),
\[
N(t,0)=-(t-1)(t+1),
\qquad
D(t,0)=(t-1)^2(t+1),
\]
so after cancellation the reduced denominator degree drops from \(4\) to \(2\).

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\(y=-1\)}{y=-1}}

At \(y=-1\), one has \(N(t,-1)=D(t,-1)\), hence \(Z(t,-1)\equiv 1\).

\subsection{Minimality for generic \texorpdfstring{\(y\)}{y}}

For $y\notin\{-1,0,1\}$, none of the above common factors occur; equivalently, $N(t,y)$ and $D(t,y)$ are coprime.
Therefore the reduced denominator has degree $4$ and the order-$4$ recurrence \eqref{eq:recurrence} is minimal, as used in Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-order}.

To verify coprimality rigorously, one computes $\gcd_t(N(t,y),D(t,y))$ via the Euclidean algorithm in $\QQ(y)[t]$.
The remainder sequence terminates at a nonzero element of $\QQ(y)$ (specifically, a rational function in~$y$ whose numerator vanishes only at $y\in\{-1,0,1\}$), confirming coprimality for all other~$y$.

\subsection{Nonnegativity of coefficients of $Z_m(y)$}

We verify the claim of Remark~\ref{rmk:nonneg-coeffs} that $Z_m(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor m/2\rfloor} a_{m,k}y^k$ with $a_{m,k}\ge 0$ for all $m,k$.
This is immediate for $m\le 3$ from the initial values $Z_0=1$, $Z_k=(y+1)^k$ for $k=1,2,3$.

For $m\ge 4$, the recurrence \eqref{eq:recurrence} gives a coefficient-level relation:
\[
a_{m,k}=a_{m-1,k}+2a_{m-2,k-1}+a_{m-2,k}-a_{m-3,k}-a_{m-4,k-1}-a_{m-4,k-2}.
\]
The presence of subtractive terms prevents a straightforward inductive argument.
We verify nonnegativity computationally: propagating the recurrence in the polynomial ring $\ZZ[y]$ and inspecting all coefficients of $Z_m(y)$ for $m\le 300$, we confirm that every coefficient is strictly positive (with the minimum coefficient at each~$m$ being at least~$1$).
A combinatorial proof---which would follow immediately from a counting interpretation of $Z_m(y)$---would establish nonnegativity for all~$m$ and is an interesting open question connected to the physical interpretation problem discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
