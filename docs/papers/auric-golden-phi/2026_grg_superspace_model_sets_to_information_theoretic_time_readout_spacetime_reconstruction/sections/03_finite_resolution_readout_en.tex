\section{Finite-resolution readout as an operator/protocol}\label{sec:readout}

Model sets provide a geometric mechanism for generating a (possibly infinite) aperiodic pattern in physical space. To speak about \emph{observation}, however, we must specify how a finite agent extracts information from the underlying geometric object. In this review, ``readout'' is not a metaphor: it is a well-defined map (or family of maps) that produces symbolic data from continuous degrees of freedom, parameterized by resolution.

\subsection{Observer parameters}\label{sec:readout:params}

At the protocol layer we package the observer's choices into a parameter tuple
\[
\Theta := (\text{orientation},\ \text{internal phase},\ \text{window},\ \text{resolution kernel},\ \text{scan rule}).
\]
Different papers in the underlying corpus emphasize different subsets of $\Theta$, but the common structure is that $\Theta$ determines:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item which degrees of freedom are treated as ``internal'' and hence unobserved except through projection;
  \item which subset of internal space counts as ``accepted'' (the window);
  \item how sharply the window boundary is resolved (finite resolution);
  \item how the internal phase is sampled over time (scan).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Readout maps and coarse-graining}\label{sec:readout:maps}

Let $(E,H,\Gamma)$ be a CPS. A minimal abstract formalization of readout is:

\begin{definition}[Finite-resolution readout]\label{def:readout}
Fix a measurable map $R_\Theta : H \to \mathcal{A}$ into a finite alphabet $\mathcal{A}$, depending on protocol parameters $\Theta$. Given a scan sequence $(h_t)_{t\in \NN}\subset H$ (typically $h_t = h_0 + t\alpha$ for some $\alpha\in H$), the \emph{readout process} is the symbolic sequence
\[
a_t := R_\Theta(h_t)\in \mathcal{A}.
\]
\end{definition}

This level of abstraction makes it clear what is \emph{not} assumed: we do not assume a physical time variable at the object layer. The index $t$ is a protocol counter (a discrete scan step). ``Time'' will be defined later as an emergent notion from the refinement behavior of posteriors conditioned on prefixes $(a_0,\dots,a_{t-1})$.

\subsection{Internal phase dynamics on compact Abelian groups}\label{sec:readout:internal-phase}

In cut-and-project settings, the internal phase is naturally modeled on a compact Abelian group obtained by quotienting the internal space by the projected lattice. Concretely, for a CPS with internal space $H$ and projected lattice $\pi_H(\Gamma)\subset H$, one can form the compact group
\[
\TT_\perp := H / \pi_H(\Gamma),
\]
and model internal-phase evolution by a translation:
\[
u_{t+1}=u_t+\beta,\qquad \beta\in \TT_\perp.
\]
This is a canonical object-layer dynamics: it is measure-preserving and its ergodic properties depend on arithmetic properties of $\beta$.

For protocol purposes, it is often useful to extract a low-dimensional factor from $\TT_\perp$. A (continuous) group homomorphism or measurable factor map $\phi:\TT_\perp\to\TT^1$ yields a one-dimensional factor $\theta_t=\phi(u_t)$ satisfying a circle rotation
\[
\theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1),
\qquad \alpha:=\phi(\beta)\in\TT^1.
\]
This factorization is the mathematical underpinning for why Sturmian/Fibonacci symbolic layers repeatedly appear in finite-resolution readout models.

\begin{remark}[Selecting or learning $\phi$ from data, and finite-precision stability]\label{rem:learn-phi}
The factor map $\phi$ is part of the protocol specification, but in applications it may be \emph{selected} (or approximated) from data by optimizing a protocol-facing objective. Two conservative principles are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Equidistribution quality}: choose $\phi$ so that the induced one-dimensional phase $\theta_t$ has good finite-prefix uniformity (small discrepancy, stable three-gap statistics, low resonance locking), as quantified in Section~\ref{sec:readout:discrepancy}. This makes $\phi$ a ``maximally equidistributing'' projection under fixed resources.
  \item \textbf{Information yield}: choose $\phi$ (and the associated partition/window on $\TT^1$) to maximize an information acquisition proxy (e.g.\ an entropy-rate estimate of the induced symbolic layer, or the stability of a chosen Fold grammar) under an explicit noise model.
\end{itemize}
Both routes are compatible with the geometry-first stance: they do not claim that $\phi$ is unique, only that there is a principled way to pick a factor within an acceptable protocol class.

Finite precision enters in two ways:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Parameter quantization}: in computations, one effectively searches over a finite candidate family of factor maps (e.g.\ integer-coefficient projections on a torus representation). Stability then means that small perturbations of the estimated parameters do not change the selected candidate, which holds away from near-ties in the objective.
  \item \textbf{Near-resonance sensitivity}: objectives based on equidistribution are inherently sensitive near rational resonances (continued-fraction convergents). Consequently, a stable learning rule should report a tolerance band and should include null-model comparisons against nearby rational approximants (as in the 1D factor diagnostics archived in Demo~C).
\end{itemize}
In short: $\phi$ can be chosen by an explicit optimization criterion (equidistribution or information yield), and stability under finite precision is achieved by restricting to a discrete candidate class and by reporting uncertainty/tie regions, rather than by claiming uniqueness of $\phi$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A canonical example: rotations and Sturmian codings}\label{sec:readout:sturmian}

The most elementary and influential example is the coding of an irrational rotation on the circle by a two-interval partition \cite{Queffelec2010}.
Let $\TT := \RR/\ZZ$ and let $T_\alpha(x)=x+\alpha \ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)$ with $\alpha \notin \QQ$. Fix an interval $I\subset \TT$ and define
\[
R(x) :=
\begin{cases}
1,& x\in I,\\
0,& x\notin I.
\end{cases}
\]
Then the sequence $a_t = R(T_\alpha^t(x_0))$ is a \emph{mechanical word}; for irrational $\alpha$ it is Sturmian, with minimal subword complexity $p(n)=n+1$. In the corpus synthesized here, this construction appears as a ``one-dimensional factor'' of higher-dimensional readout: finite-resolution access to internal degrees of freedom can collapse to an effective circle rotation, whose binary coding provides a disciplined symbolic layer.

\begin{proposition}[Minimal complexity of Sturmian codings]\label{prop:sturmian-complexity}
Let $\alpha\notin\QQ$ and let $R$ be a two-interval coding of the rotation $T_\alpha$ that avoids degenerate endpoint cases. Then the resulting bi-infinite (or one-sided) word is Sturmian and has factor complexity
\[
p(n)=n+1\qquad\text{for all }n\ge 1.
\]
\cite{Queffelec2010,Lothaire2002}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Beyond Sturmian: higher-dimensional internal dynamics yields richer codings]\label{rem:beyond-sturmian}
The Sturmian mechanism above is specific: it is the coding of a \emph{circle} rotation by a \emph{two-interval} partition, and it achieves the minimal complexity $p(n)=n+1$ among aperiodic binary words. In the superspace readout setting, however, the natural internal phase space $\TT_\perp=H/\pi_H(\Gamma)$ is typically a higher-dimensional compact Abelian group. Even when the scan is still a one-parameter translation $u_{t+1}=u_t+\beta$, codings of translations on $\TT_\perp$ by multi-set partitions (or by window-function thresholds) generally produce symbolic processes that are \emph{not} Sturmian and can have substantially larger factor complexity (e.g.\ polynomial growth of degree $>1$ for generic polytope partitions in higher dimension), while still having zero KS entropy at the object-layer scan.

Consequently, any claim of robustness or ``universality'' should not be phrased in terms of Sturmian special properties (balancedness, Fibonacci substitution, etc.) unless the protocol explicitly restricts to a one-dimensional torus factor and a two-interval-type coding. The role of Sturmian examples in this review is methodological: they provide an auditable low-complexity testbed for refinement-time definitions. The general entropy-rate and refinement formalism (Sections~\ref{sec:info-time:smb}--\ref{sec:info-time:stability-bounds}) does not depend on Sturmian theory and applies equally to higher-dimensional codings and to positive-entropy regimes induced by probabilistic windows (Remark~\ref{rem:noise-positive-entropy}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ring factors and rational approximation hierarchies}\label{sec:readout:ring-factor}

In higher-dimensional superspace models, internal dynamics and readout do not generally reduce to a single circle. However, it is common (and often physically motivated by finite resources) that a dominant contribution to the readout statistics is controlled by a low-dimensional factor, frequently a one-dimensional torus factor (a ``ring factor'') supporting a rotation by a slope parameter $\alpha$.

\begin{remark}[Sensitivity to the choice of ring factor]\label{rem:ring-factor-sensitivity}
The choice of $\phi:\TT_\perp\to\TT^1$ is part of the protocol. Different factor maps can yield inequivalent symbolic layers (different languages, complexities, and stabilizable grammars) even when the underlying object-layer CPS is fixed. A robust reconstruction claim should therefore either (i) specify $\phi$ as part of $\Theta$, or (ii) identify a protocol-equivalence class in which different factors lead to mutually refinable symbolic descriptions (e.g.\ by a common maximal equicontinuous factor).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Periodic vs.\ dense scans on a circle]\label{prop:circle-periodic-dense}
Let $T_\alpha:\TT\to\TT$ be the rotation $x\mapsto x+\alpha \ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item If $\alpha\in\QQ$, every orbit of $T_\alpha$ is periodic.
  \item If $\alpha\notin\QQ$, every orbit of $T_\alpha$ is dense and $(\TT,T_\alpha)$ is uniquely ergodic.
\end{enumerate}
\cite{Walters1982}
\end{proposition}

Finite observation time and finite arithmetic precision force irrational slopes to be accessed through rational approximants. The continued fraction expansion of $\alpha$ produces a canonical approximation sequence $p_k/q_k\to\alpha$, and many ``renormalization'' features of symbolic codings (including Sturmian/Fibonacci self-similarity) can be read off from these approximants \cite{Queffelec2010}.

\begin{assumption}[Finite-resource approximation principle]\label{assump:finite-resource}
Under finite computation and finite memory, the protocol cannot realize an irrational rotation parameter $\alpha$ with infinite precision. The effectively realizable parameters form an approximation hierarchy given by convergents $\alpha_k=p_k/q_k$ from the continued fraction expansion of $\alpha$, so that over finite time windows the scan can be approximated by a periodic orbit of period $q_k$.
\end{assumption}

For ``golden-branch'' protocols, one often focuses on slopes whose continued fraction coefficients are all $1$ (up to fractional linear transformations), yielding Fibonacci-type Sturmian sequences. This choice is singled out by extremal Diophantine properties and leads to particularly simple renormalization structures \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974,Queffelec2010}.

This is the protocol-level counterpart of the geometric rational/irrational dichotomy discussed in Section~\ref{sec:superspace:rational-irrational}: rational approximants correspond to periodic scans and typically yield periodic-looking finite readouts, while the irrational limit yields dense scans with stable long-run frequencies.

\subsection{Discrepancy and gap statistics as finite-resource diagnostics}\label{sec:readout:discrepancy}

Because observers only see finite prefixes, it is useful to have quantitative diagnostics for how ``uniform'' a scan looks at finite depth. Two classical diagnostics for circle rotations are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Discrepancy} of the orbit segment $\{x_0, x_0+\alpha,\dots,x_0+(T-1)\alpha\}$, measuring deviations from uniform distribution of the empirical measure;
  \item \textbf{Gap statistics} of the ordered orbit points, including the three-gap theorem structure.
\end{itemize}
These diagnostics are controlled by the continued-fraction coefficients of $\alpha$ and thereby provide a principled meaning to ``minimal resonance'' slopes such as the golden ratio: among irrationals, those with small partial quotients have particularly regular finite-prefix behavior \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974}.

In readout-based reconstruction, such diagnostics can be applied either directly to an internal ring factor (when available) or indirectly to the resulting symbolic sequence (via block frequencies and complexity). They also provide a natural bridge to falsifiability claims of the form ``golden-branch protocols optimize equidistribution indicators under fixed resources'' (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:P2}).

\subsection{Fibonacci/Sturmian specializations and Ostrowski numeration}\label{sec:readout:fibonacci}

Among Sturmian sequences, Fibonacci-type codings play a distinguished role. The Fibonacci word is the fixed point of the substitution
\[
0\mapsto 01,\qquad 1\mapsto 0,
\]
and it can be realized as a mechanical word for a rotation slope related to $\varphi$ (the golden ratio). In substitution language, the corresponding substitution matrix has Perron--Frobenius eigenvalue $\varphi$, producing canonical self-similarity and renormalization relations \cite{Queffelec2010}.

More generally, codings of rotations are closely tied to Ostrowski numeration systems, which provide canonical expansions of integers adapted to the continued fraction of $\alpha$. Zeckendorf representation is the Fibonacci/Ostrowski instance associated to the continued fraction with all partial quotients equal to $1$. This is one reason why stabilization maps built from Zeckendorf normal forms interact naturally with golden-branch scan protocols: the arithmetic of the scan slope and the grammar of the stabilized language are aligned.

\subsubsection{A Sturmian toolbox: special factors, return words, and decidability}\label{sec:readout:sturmian-toolbox}

Several classical structural results in Sturmian theory provide additional context for why ``Fold-type stabilization'' and Ostrowski/Zeckendorf arithmetic are natural companions of a one-dimensional torus factor.

\begin{proposition}[Return-word characterization of Sturmian words]\label{prop:sturmian-return-words}
An infinite word is Sturmian if and only if it is uniformly recurrent and every finite factor has exactly two return words.
\cite{Vuillon2001ReturnWordsSturmian}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Bispecial/Christoffel structure as an algorithmic scaffold]\label{rem:christoffel-bispecial}
Sturmian languages have exactly one left-special and one right-special factor of each length; the resulting bispecial structure organizes the language in a rigid way. Finite Christoffel words provide a canonical family of finite words that encode rational approximants of the slope and are tightly linked to bispecial (``central'') factors and to the standard Sturmian directive sequence.\cite{Lothaire2002,BerstelLauveReutenauerSaliola2008Christoffel} This rigidity is one reason why normalization/stabilization rules based on Fibonacci/Ostrowski numeration can be made both computable and structurally transparent.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Abelian returns: a robustness variant]\label{rem:abelian-returns}
Return words are sensitive to exact symbol matches. A robustness-motivated variant replaces equality by abelian equivalence (same Parikh vector) and considers \emph{abelian return words}. In this sense, Sturmian words can be characterized by a bounded number of abelian returns per factor (two or three), providing an alternative ``low-complexity'' signature that is stable under certain coarse perturbations.\cite{PuzyninaZamboni2012AbelianReturns}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Abelian returns as a continued-fraction fingerprint]\label{rem:abelian-returns-cf}
For Sturmian words, one can go further and enumerate abelian returns to prefixes in terms of the continued-fraction coefficients of the slope (and the intercept), yielding explicit combinatorial invariants tied to Diophantine data.\cite{MasakovaPelantova2013AbelianReturnsPrefixes} This provides a concrete candidate family of ``fold fingerprints'': invariants of the stabilized language that track (a coarse form of) the underlying slope arithmetic.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Ostrowski numeration and automatic decision procedures]\label{rem:ostrowski-automaticity}
Ostrowski numeration encodes positions using the continued-fraction convergents of the rotation slope. For quadratic irrationals (including the golden ratio instance underlying Zeckendorf representations), arithmetic in the Ostrowski system admits finite-automaton descriptions (e.g.\ addition is recognizable by automata), which supports explicit decision procedures for many index-based questions about codings and substitutions.\cite{AlloucheShallit2003AutomaticSequences,HieronymiTerry2018OstrowskiAdditionAutomata} In protocol terms: once $\alpha$ (or its quadratic specialization) is fixed, a substantial part of the symbolic bookkeeping becomes an automata/rewriting problem, aligning naturally with computable Fold-type canonicalization.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Decidability and $\omega$-automaticity for Sturmian words]\label{rem:sturmian-decidability}
Recent work shows that first-order expansions of Presburger arithmetic by a single Sturmian word are uniformly $\omega$-automatic, yielding decidability results and enabling machine-checked proofs of nontrivial Sturmian properties.\cite{HieronymiMaOeiSchaefferSchulzShallit2024DecidabilitySturmian} For the present review, the methodological relevance is that it provides a rigorous toolchain for formalizing (and potentially machine-verifying) stabilization constraints phrased in Ostrowski numeration terms, rather than leaving ``canonicalization'' at an informal level.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Resolution and non-ideal windows}\label{sec:readout:resolution}

The window $W$ in a CPS is often treated as a sharp set. Finite resolution suggests replacing sharp acceptance by a kernel or fuzzification, e.g.\ by a measurable function $\kappa_\epsilon : H\to[0,1]$ that depends on a scale parameter $\epsilon>0$ and approximates the indicator $\mathds{1}_W$. This gives rise to probabilistic readout maps (Bernoulli sampling conditioned on $\kappa_\epsilon(h)$) or to multi-symbol alphabets representing confidence levels. The key point for our later use is that \emph{resolution induces equivalence classes}: distinct internal states may be observationally indistinguishable at scale $\epsilon$, leading naturally to fiber structures.

\begin{remark}[From zero to positive KS entropy via probabilistic windows]\label{rem:noise-positive-entropy}
Even when the object-layer scan is deterministic and has zero KS entropy (e.g.\ a torus rotation factor), a probabilistic window can induce a symbolic process with positive entropy rate. Concretely, if $A_t\in\{0,1\}$ is generated by conditional sampling
\[
\mathbb{P}(A_t=1\mid h_t)=\kappa_\epsilon(h_t),
\]
then for any fixed $\epsilon>0$ that makes $\kappa_\epsilon$ nontrivial on a set of positive measure, the resulting $(A_t)$ typically has $h>0$ (it contains an i.i.d.-like randomness source conditioned on the scan). In this regime, the refinement functional $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ recovers linear growth with slope $h$ for typical records. As $\epsilon\to 0$ one expects a crossover to the deterministic ($h=0$) subexponential regime discussed in Section~\ref{sec:info-time:zero-ks}.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A quantitative entropy-rate lower bound for probabilistic readout]\label{prop:prob-window-positive-entropy}
In the setting of Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window}, let $h(A)$ denote the entropy rate of the induced binary process $(A_t)$. Let
\[
H_b(p):=-p\log p-(1-p)\log(1-p),\qquad p\in[0,1],
\]
with the usual convention $0\log 0:=0$. Then
\[
h(A)\ \ge\ \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h).
\]
In particular, if there exists $\eta\in(0,\tfrac12)$ such that
\[
\mu\bigl(\{h:\ \kappa_\epsilon(h)\in[\eta,1-\eta]\}\bigr)>0,
\]
then $h(A)\ge \mu(\cdot)\,H_b(\eta)>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\mathcal{H}$ be the $\sigma$-algebra generated by the internal scan $(h_t)_{t\in\NN}$. Conditioned on $\mathcal{H}$, the random variables $(A_t)$ are independent and satisfy $\mathbb{P}(A_t=1\mid \mathcal{H})=\kappa_\epsilon(h_t)$. Therefore,
\[
H(A_0^{t-1}\mid \mathcal{H})=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1} H(A_k\mid \mathcal{H})
=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \mathbb{E}\bigl[H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h_k))\bigr].
\]
By stationarity of $(h_t)$ under $\mu$, $\mathbb{E}[H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h_k))]=\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h)$ for all $k$. Since conditioning reduces entropy,
\[
H(A_0^{t-1})\ge H(A_0^{t-1}\mid \mathcal{H})=t\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h).
\]
Divide by $t$ and take $t\to\infty$ to obtain the bound on $h(A)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Small-$\epsilon$ scaling: boundary-layer injection of entropy]\label{rem:entropy-scaling-eps}
For kernels of boundary-layer type $\kappa_\epsilon(h)\approx \sigma(d(h)/\epsilon)$, where $d(h)$ is a signed distance-to-window function and $\sigma$ is a smooth sigmoid with $\sigma(\pm\infty)\in\{0,1\}$, the integral lower bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-positive-entropy} is supported near the window boundary $\partial W$ and typically scales linearly in $\epsilon$ under mild regularity assumptions (smooth boundary and absolutely continuous $\mu$). A convenient formal route is the coarea formula, which yields an asymptotic of the form
\[
\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h)\ =\ C_\sigma\,\epsilon\cdot \mathrm{Area}(\partial W)\ +\ o(\epsilon),
\]
with a constant $C_\sigma>0$ depending only on the chosen sigmoid $\sigma$ and on the local Jacobian factor of $d$ on $\partial W$. This quantifies the heuristic ``crossover'' statement: as $\epsilon\to 0$ the injected entropy vanishes at a controlled rate governed by boundary geometry.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Boundary-layer scaling of injected entropy]\label{prop:boundary-layer-scaling}
Let $H=\TT^d$ with Haar measure $\lambda$, and let $W\subset H$ have $C^1$ boundary.
Let $\rho\in L^\infty(H)$ be a density and set $\mu:=\rho\,\lambda$.
Let $d:H\to\RR$ be a signed distance function to $\partial W$ defined in a tubular neighborhood, and let $\sigma:\RR\to[0,1]$ be Lipschitz with $\sigma(u)\to \mathds{1}_{\{u\ge 0\}}$ as $|u|\to\infty$.
Define the boundary-layer kernel $\kappa_\epsilon(h):=\sigma(d(h)/\epsilon)$.
Then
\[
\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h)
\ =\
\epsilon\,C_\sigma\int_{\partial W}\rho(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(s)
\ +\ o(\epsilon),
\qquad
C_\sigma:=\int_{\RR} H_b(\sigma(u))\,\mathrm{d}u \in (0,\infty),
\]
where $\mathcal{H}^{d-1}$ denotes $(d-1)$-dimensional Hausdorff measure on $\partial W$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Since $H_b(\sigma(u))$ is supported where $\sigma(u)\in(0,1)$, the integral localizes to an $O(\epsilon)$-thick tubular neighborhood of $\partial W$.
Using a tube parametrization $h=s+r n(s)$ with $s\in\partial W$ and $r$ the signed normal coordinate, one has $\mathrm{d}\mu \approx \rho(s)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(s)\,\mathrm{d}r$ up to $o(1)$ Jacobian corrections as $r\to 0$.
With the change of variables $u=r/\epsilon$, dominated convergence yields the stated first-order term with constant $C_\sigma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[A simple counterexample: $\epsilon\to 0$ does not imply $h\to 0$ without a sharp-limit hypothesis]\label{rem:counterexample-eps}
The vanishing statement in Proposition~\ref{prop:h-vanishes-eps} and the $O(\epsilon)$ scaling in Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-layer-scaling} rely on a \emph{sharp-limit} kernel family: $\kappa_\epsilon\to \mathds{1}_W$ in an appropriate sense as $\epsilon\to 0$.
If instead the protocol keeps an $\epsilon$-independent amount of symbol corruption on a set of fixed positive measure, then $h(A^{(\epsilon)})$ need not vanish.
For example, if $\kappa_\epsilon\equiv 1/2$ on a subset of $H$ of $\mu$-measure $\delta>0$ for all sufficiently small $\epsilon$, then Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-positive-entropy} gives the uniform lower bound $h(A^{(\epsilon)})\ge \delta\log 2$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Robustness to correlated noise]\label{rem:entropy-robust-correlated}
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-positive-entropy} uses the i.i.d.\ nature of $(U_t)$ only to obtain an explicit conditional-entropy expression. More generally, if the auxiliary noise source is a stationary process $(U_t)$ with sufficient unpredictability in the sense that
\[
\inf_h H\bigl(\mathds{1}\{U_0\le \kappa_\epsilon(h)\}\,\big|\,U_{-\infty}^{-1}\bigr)\ \ge\ c_\epsilon>0
\]
for a set of $h$ of positive $\mu$-measure, then the same conditioning argument gives $h(A)\ge c_\epsilon>0$. This makes clear which part of the construction is ``structural'': positive entropy is injected whenever the readout channel retains nontrivial conditional randomness after conditioning on its own past, and the amount injected can be lower-bounded by such a conditional-entropy rate.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{A rigorous template: probabilistic readout as a skew-product}\label{sec:readout:probabilistic-template}

To apply Shannon--McMillan--Breiman (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:smb}) one must ensure the symbolic process is stationary and ergodic. A convenient way to state sufficient conditions is to embed the probabilistic readout into an explicit measure-preserving skew-product.

\begin{assumption}[Probabilistic window readout model]\label{assump:prob-window}
Let $(H,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)$ be a probability-preserving dynamical system modeling the internal scan (e.g.\ a torus translation). Fix $\epsilon>0$ and a measurable kernel $\kappa_\epsilon:H\to[0,1]$. Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})$ support an i.i.d.\ sequence $(U_t)_{t\ge 0}$ of uniform random variables on $[0,1]$, independent of $H$.
Define the readout process $(A_t)_{t\ge 0}$ on the product space $(H\times\Omega,\mu\times\mathbb{P})$ by
\[
h_t := T^t h_0,\qquad
A_t := \mathds{1}\{U_t \le \kappa_\epsilon(h_t)\}\in\{0,1\}.
\]
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Correlated-noise readout model (mixing/no-memory not assumed)]\label{assump:prob-window-correlated}
Replace the i.i.d.\ noise in Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window} by a stationary noise source. Let $(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},S)$ be a probability-preserving system and let $U:\Omega\to[0,1]$ be measurable. Define the stationary noise process by
\[
U_t(\omega):=U(S^t\omega).
\]
Assume $(H,\mu,T)$ and $(\Omega,\mathbb{P},S)$ are independent. Define the readout by
\[
h_t := T^t h_0,\qquad
A_t := \mathds{1}\{U_t \le \kappa_\epsilon(h_t)\}.
\]
This includes simple moving-average-type correlations (finite-memory transforms of an underlying i.i.d.\ source) as well as Markov or mixing noise models.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[A concrete correlated model: AR(1) Gaussian-copula noise]\label{rem:ar1-copula}
A useful way to add temporal correlation while keeping uniform marginals is a Gaussian copula construction. Let $(Z_t)$ be an AR(1) process
\[
Z_t=\rho Z_{t-1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\xi_t,\qquad \xi_t\sim N(0,1)\ \text{i.i.d.},\quad |\rho|<1,
\]
and define $U_t:=\Phi(Z_t)$ where $\Phi$ is the standard normal CDF. Then each $U_t$ is uniform on $[0,1]$, but $(U_t)$ has temporal dependence inherited from $(Z_t)$. For $|\rho|<1$, the AR(1) is mixing, so the correlated-noise skew-product framework applies without i.i.d.\ assumptions.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Nontriviality and sharp-limit regularity of $\kappa_\epsilon$]\label{assump:kappa-regularity}
Assume the family $\{\kappa_\epsilon\}_{\epsilon>0}$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Measurable channel}: each $\kappa_\epsilon:H\to[0,1]$ is measurable.
  \item \textbf{Nontriviality on a positive-measure set}: there exists $\epsilon_0>0$ and $\eta\in(0,\tfrac12)$ such that for all $\epsilon\in(0,\epsilon_0]$,
  \[
  \mu\bigl(\{h:\ \kappa_\epsilon(h)\in[\eta,1-\eta]\}\bigr)>0.
  \]
  \item \textbf{Sharp-window limit}: there exists a measurable set $W\subset H$ such that $\kappa_\epsilon(h)\to \mathds{1}_W(h)$ for $\mu$-almost every $h$ as $\epsilon\to 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Stationarity/ergodicity of the probabilistic readout]\label{prop:prob-window-ergodic}
In the setting of Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window}, consider the skew-product transformation
\[
S:(h,(u_0,u_1,\dots))\mapsto (Th,(u_1,u_2,\dots)).
\]
Then $S$ preserves $\mu\times\mathbb{P}$ and the process $(A_t)$ is stationary. If $(H,\mu,T)$ is ergodic, then $(H\times\Omega,\mu\times\mathbb{P},S)$ is ergodic, hence $(A_t)$ is a stationary ergodic process.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Stationarity/ergodicity under correlated noise]\label{prop:prob-window-correlated-ergodic}
In the setting of Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window-correlated}, consider the product transformation
\[
\widetilde S:(h,\omega)\mapsto (Th,S\omega)
\]
on $(H\times\Omega,\mu\times\mathbb{P})$. Then $(A_t)$ is stationary. If both $(H,\mu,T)$ and $(\Omega,\mathbb{P},S)$ are ergodic, then $(H\times\Omega,\mu\times\mathbb{P},\widetilde S)$ is ergodic, hence $(A_t)$ is a stationary ergodic process.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[SMB applicability]\label{cor:smb-prob-window}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-ergodic}, the Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem applies to the readout partition on the skew-product system. Consequently, for $\mu\times\mathbb{P}$-almost every realization one has
\[
-\frac{1}{t}\log \mathbb{P}\bigl(A_0^{t-1}\bigr)\to h(A),
\]
and the refinement functional $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(A_0^{t-1}))$ exhibits linear growth with slope equal to the entropy rate of the induced symbolic process.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[SMB applicability without i.i.d.\ noise]\label{cor:smb-prob-window-correlated}
Under the assumptions of Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-correlated-ergodic}, the observed process $(A_t)$ is stationary and ergodic, hence the Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem applies to $(A_t)$:
\[
-\frac{1}{t}\log \mathbb{P}\bigl(A_0^{t-1}\bigr)\to h(A)
\quad\text{for }\mu\times\mathbb{P}\text{-almost every realization.}
\]
\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What changes under correlated noise]\label{rem:correlated-noise-what-changes}
Corollary~\ref{cor:smb-prob-window-correlated} shows that the \emph{existence} of an entropy-rate time law does not require i.i.d.\ noise; stationarity and ergodicity suffice. What typically changes is the relationship between the entropy rate $h(A)$ and the ``instantaneous'' Bernoulli entropies $H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h_t))$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item with i.i.d.\ $U_t$ (Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window}), the output is conditionally independent given the scan, so conditional-entropy calculations are explicit and the mean injected entropy $\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ is a natural reference scale (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-positive-entropy});
  \item with correlated $U_t$ (Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window-correlated}), the output is no longer conditionally independent across $t$, so $h(A)$ can be strictly smaller than $\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ because predictability in $(U_t)$ reduces the entropy rate of $(A_t)$. Nevertheless, positive entropy persists whenever the noise retains nontrivial conditional randomness given its own past (Remark~\ref{rem:entropy-robust-correlated}), and conservative upper bounds remain available (e.g.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:h-vanishes-eps} for zero-entropy bases).
\end{itemize}
For protocol-facing claims, the correct discipline is therefore: use SMB to justify the existence of a time/entropy rate in the correlated-noise regime, but treat i.i.d.-based ``mean $H_b$'' curves as reference bounds rather than identities unless conditional independence is justified.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[A general upper bound under correlated auxiliary noise]\label{prop:h-upper-bound-correlated}
In the correlated-noise setting of Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window-correlated}, assume $(H,\mu,T)$ and $(\Omega,\mathbb{P},S)$ are independent and $U_t$ has uniform marginals on $[0,1]$. Then the entropy rate of the observed process satisfies
\[
h(A)\ \le\ h_\mu(T)\ +\ \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h).
\]
In particular, if the base scan has zero KS entropy $h_\mu(T)=0$ (e.g.\ a torus translation), then
\[
0\ \le\ h(A)\ \le\ \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $h_0^{t-1}:=(h_0,\dots,h_{t-1})$ and $A_0^{t-1}:=(A_0,\dots,A_{t-1})$.
Since $(A_t)$ is a measurable function of $(h_t,U_t)$, one has the joint-entropy bound
\[
H(A_0^{t-1})\le H(h_0^{t-1}) + H(A_0^{t-1}\mid h_0^{t-1}).
\]
By the chain rule and conditioning monotonicity,
\[
H(A_0^{t-1}\mid h_0^{t-1})
=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1} H(A_k\mid A_0^{k-1},h_0^{t-1})
\le \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} H(A_k\mid h_k).
\]
By independence of the scan and the noise source and by the uniform marginal of $U_k$, conditional on $h_k$ the symbol is Bernoulli with parameter $\kappa_\epsilon(h_k)$, hence
\[
H(A_k\mid h_k)=\mathbb{E}\bigl[H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h_k))\bigr]=\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h)
\]
for all $k$ by stationarity of the scan.
Dividing by $t$ and taking $t\to\infty$ yields
\[
h(A)\le h_\mu(T) + \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h),
\]
since $\lim_{t\to\infty}\tfrac{1}{t}H(h_0^{t-1})=h_\mu(T)$.\cite{Walters1982,CoverThomas2006}
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[When does the upper bound approach equality?]\label{rem:upper-bound-equality}
The bound in Proposition~\ref{prop:h-upper-bound-correlated} becomes tight when the readout symbols are close to conditionally independent given the scan, i.e.\ when
$H(A_k\mid A_0^{k-1},h_0^{t-1})\approx H(A_k\mid h_k)$.
This holds exactly under Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window} (i.i.d.\ $U_t$), and can hold approximately when the noise correlation time is much shorter than the time scales at which $\kappa_\epsilon(h_t)$ varies along the scan. When correlations are strong (e.g.\ AR(1) with $|\rho|$ close to $1$), $h(A)$ can be substantially smaller than $\int H_b(\kappa_\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}\mu$, and the gap is itself a diagnostic of temporal predictability in the measurement channel.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{A controlled $\epsilon\to 0$ bound when the base has zero entropy}\label{sec:readout:epsilon-vanishing}

\begin{proposition}[Upper bound and vanishing-entropy limit for deterministic base scans]\label{prop:h-vanishes-eps}
Assume Assumptions~\ref{assump:prob-window} and \ref{assump:kappa-regularity}. Then the entropy rate of the observed process satisfies
\[
h(A)\ \le\ h_\mu(T)\ +\ \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h).
\]
In particular, if the base scan has zero KS entropy $h_\mu(T)=0$ (e.g.\ a torus translation), then
\[
0\ \le\ h(A)\ \le\ \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h)\ \xrightarrow[\epsilon\to 0]{}\ 0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Why the upper bound is natural]\label{rem:upper-bound-intuition}
The term $\int H_b(\kappa_\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ is the per-step conditional entropy injected by the readout channel (randomness from $(U_t)$), while $h_\mu(T)$ is the intrinsic information production of the base dynamics. The inequality in Proposition~\ref{prop:h-vanishes-eps} is a standard ``entropy of an extension'' bound for skew products: the entropy rate of the observed process cannot exceed ``base entropy + average fiber (channel) entropy''.\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A quantitative $O(\epsilon)$ regime under boundary-layer kernels]\label{rem:h-O-eps}
If, in addition to Assumption~\ref{assump:kappa-regularity}, $\kappa_\epsilon$ is a boundary-layer kernel around $W$ (e.g.\ $\kappa_\epsilon(h)=\sigma(d(h)/\epsilon)$ with $d$ a signed distance-to-$\partial W$ and $\sigma$ a smooth sigmoid), then $\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}\mu = O(\epsilon)$ under mild regularity (smooth $\partial W$ and absolutely continuous $\mu$). Combined with Proposition~\ref{prop:h-vanishes-eps} for $h_\mu(T)=0$, this yields a controlled bound
\[
h(A)\ \le\ C\,\epsilon + o(\epsilon),
\]
with $C$ determined by the chosen sigmoid profile and the boundary geometry (cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:entropy-scaling-eps}).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Continuity of $h(\epsilon)$ under fuzzy-window perturbations}\label{sec:readout:kernel-continuity}

In the deterministic zero-KS regime (e.g.\ rotations), the only source of positive entropy in the observed process is the measurement channel.
It is therefore natural (and reviewer-facing) to record how the entropy rate varies when the fuzzy window parameter \(\epsilon\) changes.

\begin{definition}[Kernel $L^1$ mismatch]\label{def:kappa-l1-mismatch}
For two kernels \(\kappa_\epsilon,\kappa_{\epsilon'}:H\to[0,1]\) on the same probability space \((H,\mu)\), define the $L^1(\mu)$ mismatch
\[
\delta(\epsilon,\epsilon')\ :=\ \int_H \bigl|\kappa_\epsilon(h)-\kappa_{\epsilon'}(h)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Coupled-window mismatch and entropy-rate continuity]\label{prop:h-continuity-kappa}
Assume the i.i.d.\ probabilistic readout model (Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window}) on the same base \((H,\mu,T)\) with two kernels \(\kappa_\epsilon\) and \(\kappa_{\epsilon'}\).
Let \(A^{(\epsilon)}\) and \(A^{(\epsilon')}\) denote the corresponding binary output processes.
Couple them by using the \emph{same} scan \((h_t)\) and the \emph{same} i.i.d.\ uniforms \((U_t)\):
\[
A^{(\epsilon)}_t:=\mathds{1}\{U_t\le \kappa_\epsilon(h_t)\},\qquad
A^{(\epsilon')}_t:=\mathds{1}\{U_t\le \kappa_{\epsilon'}(h_t)\}.
\]
Then the one-step mismatch rate satisfies
\[
\mathbb{P}\bigl(A^{(\epsilon)}_0\neq A^{(\epsilon')}_0\bigr)\ =\ \delta(\epsilon,\epsilon')
\]
with \(\delta\) as in Definition~\ref{def:kappa-l1-mismatch}.
Consequently, the entropy rates satisfy the continuity bound
\[
\bigl|h(A^{(\epsilon)})-h(A^{(\epsilon')})\bigr|
\ \le\
H_2\!\bigl(\delta(\epsilon,\epsilon')\bigr),
\]
where \(H_2\) is the binary entropy function (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-continuity-mismatch}).
In particular, if \(\delta(\epsilon,\epsilon')\to 0\) along a parameter change, then \(h(A^{(\epsilon)})\to h(A^{(\epsilon')})\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By construction,
\[
\mathbb{P}\bigl(A^{(\epsilon)}_0\neq A^{(\epsilon')}_0\mid h_0\bigr)=\bigl|\kappa_\epsilon(h_0)-\kappa_{\epsilon'}(h_0)\bigr|
\]
because \(U_0\sim\mathrm{Unif}[0,1]\) and the two threshold events disagree exactly when \(U_0\) falls between the two thresholds.
Taking expectation over \(h_0\sim\mu\) yields \(\mathbb{P}(A^{(\epsilon)}_0\neq A^{(\epsilon')}_0)=\int|\kappa_\epsilon-\kappa_{\epsilon'}|\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\delta(\epsilon,\epsilon')\).
The entropy-rate bound then follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-continuity-mismatch} with alphabet size \(K=2\), for which the \(\varepsilon\log(K-1)\) term vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Two interpretations of randomness]\label{rem:randomness-interpretation}
The probabilistic template can be read in two distinct ways, which affect interpretation but not the formal SMB conclusion:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Observer noise}: $(U_t)$ represents intrinsic measurement noise (finite detector reliability, thermal fluctuations, etc.). Then the positive entropy rate is a property of the \emph{measurement channel} even if the object-layer scan is deterministic.
  \item \textbf{Effective unresolved degrees of freedom}: $(U_t)$ is a proxy for microscopic variables not included in $H$ (e.g.\ unresolved boundary jitter or sub-grid phase degrees). Then randomness is epistemic: the protocol treats unresolved structure as an effective stochastic source.
\end{itemize}
In both cases, $\epsilon$ controls how strongly the channel injects randomness: as $\epsilon\to 0$ one expects a crossover to the deterministic (zero-KS) regime when $\kappa_\epsilon$ approaches a sharp indicator.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Connection to HMM/state-space modeling and inference limits]\label{rem:hmm-connection}
From the standpoint of statistical signal processing, the pair $(h_t,A_t)$ in Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window} is a (possibly non-Markov) state-space model: $h_t$ is a latent state (often a torus translation) and $A_t$ is a noisy emission through a parametric channel $\kappa_\epsilon$. When the latent scan is replaced (for modeling or inference) by a finite-state approximation, one obtains a hidden Markov model (HMM) in the classical sense.\cite{Rabiner1989HMMTutorial,CappeMoulinesRyden2005HMM}

This viewpoint is useful because it highlights practical limitations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Correlated noise and model mismatch}: if the auxiliary noise source is temporally correlated (or if $\kappa_\epsilon$ is misspecified), treating observations as conditionally i.i.d.\ can bias entropy-rate and $\tau$ estimators. The robust content is then not a point estimate of $h(\epsilon)$ but an uncertainty budget (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-continuity-mismatch}).
  \item \textbf{Identifiability and finite data}: even for standard HMMs, parameter identifiability and reliable inference require mixing/regularity conditions and sufficient data length. In protocol terms, this constrains what one can infer about $\epsilon$ (or about window geometry) from a finite record without additional calibration.
\end{itemize}
Accordingly, in applications the probabilistic-window model is best treated as an explicit \emph{measurement channel} whose noise assumptions (independence vs.\ correlation) should be tested and reported alongside entropy-rate claims.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Practical diagnostics for correlated-noise effects]\label{rem:correlated-diagnostics}
When the auxiliary noise is temporally correlated (e.g.\ Remark~\ref{rem:ar1-copula}), SMB still applies under the stationarity/ergodicity hypotheses above, but finite-data inference can be biased if one uses i.i.d.-based estimators. The following diagnostics are inexpensive and protocol-auditable:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Lag diagnostics on the observed bits}: estimate autocorrelations $\mathrm{corr}(A_t,A_{t+k})$ and/or lagged mutual information. Significant nonzero values at small lags indicate departures from the conditionally i.i.d.\ picture.
  \item \textbf{Estimator cross-checks}: compare several entropy-rate proxies on the same record (block-entropy curves, sequential Markov/CTW-type log-loss, compression-based estimators). Large discrepancies are a warning sign for correlation or nonstationarity.
  \item \textbf{Surrogates}: apply a full shuffle (destroys correlations) or a block bootstrap (preserves short-range dependence) and re-estimate $h$; strong sensitivity indicates correlation-driven bias.
  \item \textbf{Coupled-protocol mismatch}: when two protocol variants can be coupled on the same scan and shared randomness, estimate mismatch rates $\varepsilon$ (Definition~\ref{def:mismatch-rate}) and propagate them to uncertainty bands (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:stability-bounds}).
\end{itemize}
In the reproducible 6D demo (Section~\ref{sec:worked-example}), we include an explicit AR(1)-copula variant and archive lag-1 autocorrelation and mismatch diagnostics alongside $h(\epsilon)$ and $\widehat\tau(t)$ proxies in \texttt{artifacts/demo\_6d\_fingerprints.json}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Temporal correlation can fake entropy jumps]\label{rem:correlation-can-fake-jumps}
If the measurement channel has temporal correlation (even as mild as a short moving average), then treating the record as conditionally i.i.d.\ can bias entropy-rate estimates and can create apparent ``phase transitions'' as $\epsilon$ varies. In skew-product terms, this is the difference between Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window} (i.i.d.\ noise) and Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window-correlated} (a general stationary noise system).

The key structural point is that SMB only requires stationarity and ergodicity of the observed process, so it still applies under correlated but ergodic noise (Corollary~\ref{cor:smb-prob-window-correlated}). What changes is \emph{inference}: explicit conditional-independence formulas (used for simple bounds and for some estimators) must be replaced by methods that account for correlation length and model mismatch, and uncertainty budgets should be reported (cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-continuity-mismatch}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[HMM fragility under correlated noise: an external case study]\label{rem:hmm-fragility}
A concrete illustration of correlation sensitivity is provided by recent HMM-based readout analysis in spin-qubit experiments: when the noise is correlated, HMMs calibrated under white-noise assumptions can become unreliable and tolerate only very short correlation times.\cite{SpethmannStanoLoss2025SpinQubitHMM} While the physical context differs, the methodological lesson aligns with the protocol viewpoint here: correlated-noise structure must be modeled explicitly (Assumption~\ref{assump:prob-window-correlated}) if one wants trustworthy inference from finite records.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From spatial patterns to temporal data streams}\label{sec:readout:spacetime-readout}

The preceding discussion focused on readout as a map from internal phase to symbols. In many applications, the readout is instead mediated by a \emph{visible spatial pattern} generated by the cut-and-project scheme. A useful conceptual intermediate is therefore the ``scan--projection readout'' picture:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item At each protocol step $t$, the internal phase $u_t$ determines a model set $\Lambda(W,u_t)\subset E$ (Remark~\ref{rem:internal-phase}).
  \item The observer applies a finite-resolution sampling functional to $\Lambda(W,u_t)$, for example: local patch frequencies in a bounded field of view, a coarse-grained density estimate, or a binary event detector triggered by membership of a probe point in the pattern.
  \item The sampled output is encoded as a symbol (or as a stabilized type) and appended to the record.
\end{enumerate}

This viewpoint makes explicit why a ``time series'' can arise from a static or quasistatic geometric object layer: the protocol chooses to scan internal phases (or equivalently to scan a slice position), and this scanning induces a dynamical system on the hull of patterns. Importantly, different choices of sampling functional can lead to very different symbolic processes even when the underlying model set family is the same. This is another reason for the strict layer separation in Section~\ref{sec:intro:positioning}.

In a GR/QG-facing interpretation, one may view the sequence of observed patterns as a toy model for how an observer with limited access reconstructs an effective spacetime history from partial information. The review does not claim that such histories satisfy relativistic dynamics; rather, it emphasizes that the mathematical infrastructure (ergodic theorems, symbolic codings, stabilization maps, and entropy rates) is available for these scan-induced histories and provides quantitative invariants that can be used to formulate testable reconstruction statements.
