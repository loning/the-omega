\section{Discussion and limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What is explained and what is not}
\label{sec:what_explained}

The $m=6$ folding template is a \emph{minimal} arithmetic layer.
It provides:
\begin{itemize}
\item a fixed $64\to 21$ compression space $X_6$ with a $18\oplus 3$ cyclic/boundary split;
\item a finite encoding search space ($24$) for the unknown two-bit nucleotide dictionary;
\item a rigid start--stop symmetry at the unique encoding selected by the control-boundary objective.
\end{itemize}

It does not claim to derive the full biochemical translation apparatus, tRNA recognition, or evolutionary pathways.
Rather, it posits that if a finite-resolution readout constraint exists at a low layer, then many higher-level degrees of freedom are pre-constrained by arithmetic topology.

\subsection{Why the control-boundary objective is a minimal prior}
\label{sec:why_control_objective}

The reverse-compilation step requires a criterion to select one encoding among $24$ discrete candidates.
Our objective uses only translation-control semantics and the folding-defined boundary sector.
Two structural facts make it a particularly weak form of supervision:
\begin{itemize}
\item the boundary sector is extremely sparse at $m=6$ (only six codons, independent of encoding);
\item the control set $\mathcal{K}=\{\mathrm{AUG,UAA,UAG,UGA}\}$ is the smallest standard set tied directly to start/stop control, without invoking amino-acid physicochemical properties, codon-usage statistics, or evolutionary assumptions.
\end{itemize}

Maximizing the boundary-hit count $S(\mu)$ therefore asks only whether the known control symbols preferentially occupy the rare topological endpoints of the $64\to 21$ folding space.
Importantly, the objective does not enforce the $14/48$ start--stop symmetry; the observed coincidence of $\mathrm{AUG}$ and $\mathrm{UAA}$ on the same boundary word arises as a consequence of the folding preimage structure and the finite search.
Alternative weak objectives (e.g.\ combining boundary hits with mutual information or with sequence-level context statistics) can be layered on later, but are not required for the closed $m=6$ identifiability result.
In particular, we do not use codon-usage frequencies or codon-bias objectives as supervision at this stage \cite{SharpLi1987,HershbergPetrov2008}.

\subsection{Why three stop codons do not all lie in the boundary sector}
\label{sec:stops_not_all_boundary}

The boundary sector contains exactly six microstate indices (two per boundary word), independent of the nucleotide encoding (Appendix~\ref{app:fold6_preimages}).
In particular, under the standard stop set $\{\mathrm{UAA,UAG,UGA}\}$ it is impossible to place all three stop codons inside the boundary sector, and in fact at most one stop can be boundary for any bijective encoding (Proposition~\ref{prop:stop_hits_at_most_one}).
\medskip

Thus, in the minimal $m=6$ template, the stop set is forced into a rigid arithmetic stratification (Definition~\ref{def:stop_classes}): $\mathrm{UAA}$ is the unique boundary stop, $\mathrm{UAG}$ is a non-boundary stop with $\Delta=34$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$ is a non-boundary stop with maximal uplift $\Delta=55$.
Any biological mechanism that differentiates the three stop codons must therefore act through context-sensitive factors beyond boundary occupancy at $m=6$.

\subsection{Recoding and stop-context sensitivity as an uplift interface}
\label{sec:recoding_uplift_interface}

At the arithmetic level, the three standard stop codons have distinct $(V,\Delta)$ signatures under $\mu^\ast$ (Section~\ref{sec:stop_fine_structure}):
$\mathrm{UAA}$ is boundary-aligned with $\Delta=34$, $\mathrm{UAG}$ is non-boundary with the same $\Delta=34$, and $\mathrm{UGA}$ has maximal uplift $\Delta=55$.
This yields a quantitative interface prediction: context-sensitive termination and recoding should correlate with the uplift class $\Delta$ and with local stop-context uplift windows.
Empirically, translation termination is known to depend on the nucleotide context following the stop codon \cite{McCaughanBrownDalphinBerryTate1995}, and recoding phenomena exist in which $\mathrm{UGA}$ can specify selenocysteine and $\mathrm{UAG}$ can specify pyrrolysine in specialized contexts \cite{HatfieldGladyshev2002,SrinivasanJamesKrzycki2002}.

The framework here does not attempt to derive these biochemical mechanisms.
Instead it provides an explicit quantitative signal family $(Z,U)$ that can be extracted from sequences and compared across stop classes and contexts using the protocols in Appendix~\ref{app:stat_tests}.

\paragraph{Baseline dependence and scale.}
In this dataset, the most stable separation is an upstream uplift shift: recoding sites exhibit substantially larger $\overline{U}_{\mathrm{before}}$ than CDS-deduplicated terminal stops across multiple radii $k$, while the downstream contrast is more $k$-dependent (Appendix~\ref{app:recoding_sites}).
This motivates multi-$k$ reporting and caution against treating a single-$k$ after-window contrast as an invariant signature.

\paragraph{Multi-resolution sensitivity.}
Because uplift $\Delta$ is defined relative to a finite Zeckendorf window, its empirical separability can change with resolution.
Accordingly, we also report rank-based discrimination summaries (AUC) across Fold$_m$ for $m\in\{6,7,8,9\}$ in Appendix~\ref{app:recoding_sites}.
At the codon scale ($N\in\{0,\dots,63\}$), $m=9$ is a saturating window and yields $\Delta_m\equiv 0$ (see Section~\ref{sec:resolution_upgrade}); the corresponding AUC entries therefore collapse to $0.5$ as a sanity check rather than a biological signal.

\paragraph{Boundary-sector check.}
A boundary-hit enrichment would be a distinct and stronger mechanism than an uplift shift.
Instead, boundary-hit rates in the $k=10$ windows around Sec sites are significantly \emph{depleted} relative to the coding background in the same dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:boundary_enrichment_results}), indicating that the recoding-context signal is not reducible to boundary-sector proximity at $m=6$.

\paragraph{CDS-level terminal-stop baselines and stratification.}
Because a single CDS can contain multiple \texttt{transl\_except} sites, terminal-stop contexts are treated at CDS resolution: each CDS contributes at most one terminal-stop window to the reference distribution.
We also report codon- and domain-stratified tests to make explicit which subsets drive the aggregate signal; in the present dataset, recoding is dominated by $\mathrm{UGA}$/Sec events and non-eukaryotic strata are small.

\paragraph{Within-CDS controls.}
In coding regions, the stop codons $\mathrm{UGA}$ and $\mathrm{UAG}$ are normally forbidden as internal codons; thus an internal same-codon control (Control-B) is typically unavailable once \texttt{transl\_except} sites are excluded.
Instead we compare each recoding site to random internal non-stop codon positions from the same CDS (Control-C), which provides a within-gene baseline for uplift-context windows.
The resulting signal persists under this within-CDS random-internal baseline, indicating that the aggregate contrast is not explained solely by between-gene variation.
However, composition-matched within-CDS controls can invert the direction of the comparison, showing that $U$-window statistics are strongly coupled to local GC and dinucleotide structure.
Accordingly, we treat composition-adjusted analyses against terminal-stop baselines as the more interpretable summary for the recoding dataset (Appendix~\ref{app:composition_controls}).

\subsection{Resolution upgrade and context}
\label{sec:resolution_upgrade}

Two limitations point to clear extensions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Higher resolution.} The window length $m=6$ is the minimal case matching $20+\mathrm{Stop}$ by cardinality.
Higher windows $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ introduce new degrees of freedom, richer boundary structure, and potentially a closer match to amino-acid degeneracy patterns \cite{Ma2025RF}.
\item \textbf{Sequence context.} Translation is context-sensitive (wobble pairing, recoding signals, local RNA structure).
In the folding language, context dependence naturally corresponds to conditioning the spectrum traces on neighborhoods and on reading-frame-aligned patterns.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Saturating resolution at the codon scale.}
In this paper the microstate index is codon-scale: $N\in\{0,\dots,63\}$ under a two-bit encoding.
The Zeckendorf expansion of any $N\le 63$ uses at most nine Fibonacci weights ($1,2,3,5,8,13,21,34,55$), hence the length-$9$ window contains the full admissible digit string.
Therefore $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ is lossless for $m\ge 9$ at this scale: $V_m(N)=N$ and the uplift vanishes identically, $\Delta_m(N)=N-V_m(N)\equiv 0$.
Consequently, uplift-based statistics are nontrivial only for $m\le 8$ in the codon-scale model; including $m=9$ is useful as an internal consistency check but cannot carry discriminative signal in $\Delta_m$.

\subsection{A research program}
\label{sec:program}

The scripts provided here are designed to support a sustained experimental program:
\begin{itemize}
\item scan known nonstandard genetic codes to test the boundary-migration prediction (P3);
\item compute $(Z,U)$ statistics around known recoding sites (selenocysteine, pyrrolysine) to test the uplift hypothesis (P2) \cite{HatfieldGladyshev2002,SrinivasanJamesKrzycki2002};
\item compare codon-usage biases with the folding-derived spectra to probe whether organisms preferentially select codons with certain $(Z,U)$ profiles under ecological or translational constraints.
\end{itemize}
