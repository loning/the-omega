\begin{abstract}
Linear presentations of arithmetic take additive translation as the primitive operation. While locally economical, this choice systematically obscures multiplicative scaling, phase and periodicity, and spectral data, and it pushes ``infinity'' outside the object domain, where endpoint pathologies (singularity and divergence) are then handled by external prescriptions. This paper proposes a self-contained coordinate upgrade---\emph{Holographic Polar Arithmetic} (HPA)---in which multiplication is ontological and addition becomes a derived, lossy readout.

Starting from the $\mathbb{F}_1$-inspired multiplicative monoid skeleton $M=(\NN_{>0},\cdot)$, we define a radial character $\rho$ and a multiplicative phase $\theta_\times$ which is additive under multiplication, yielding a polar embedding
$$
\mathcal{Z}(n)=\rho(n)\e^{\iu\theta_\times(n)}\in\CC^*.
$$
We show that any nontrivial such embedding is multiplicative but cannot be additive in the classical linear sense: no map $\Phi:\NN_{>0}\to\CC^*$ can simultaneously preserve multiplication and linear addition except the identity. Consequently, vector synthesis $\mathcal{Z}(a)+\mathcal{Z}(b)$ is not constrained to land on the discrete image $\mathcal{Z}(M)$. This mismatch between continuous vector composition and discrete integer coordinates defines a \emph{quantum gap}. Macroscopic ``addition'' is therefore modeled as a probabilistic projection of synthesized vectors onto a discrete coordinate lattice, canonically realized by Zeckendorf/Ostrowski coding.

To decouple intrinsic multiplicative phase from time/readout, we introduce a genuine unitary scan operator $\Theta$ (a Koopman operator for an irrational rotation on $L^2(S^1)$), with iteration count as time. The scan model naturally yields a Weyl pair: together with the basic phase multiplication operator $V$, the shift $U=\Theta$ satisfies $UV=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}VU$, i.e. the irrational rotation algebra. A binary readout is obtained by a window projection $\Pi_W$, producing a two-letter mechanical word. The golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ is singled out by a binary-minimality requirement: its continued fraction coefficients are all $1$, so the characteristic Sturmian word is the Fibonacci word and Ostrowski numeration degenerates to the binary Zeckendorf representation. On this basis we construct an orbit-wise ``cyclic calculus'' (difference, ergodic trace, and Abel-type finite part) and package three reusable modules---topologizing singularities, extracting finite parts from divergences, and replacing ill-posed continuum path integrals by controlled sums over Zeckendorf-coded lattices---closing a consistent loop from multiplicative skeleton to discrete readout.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords}: $\mathbb{F}_1$, polar arithmetic, multiplicative phase, unitary scan, irrational rotation, Sturmian mechanical word, cut-and-project, Ostrowski numeration, Zeckendorf decomposition, orbit trace, finite part.

\section{Introduction: upgrading the coordinate system of arithmetic}
\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Linear minimality and its structural side effects}
In the classical one-dimensional presentation, integers live on a line and the primitive generator is the successor map $n\mapsto n+1$. Additive structure is represented as translation, and multiplicative structure is typically introduced as iterated addition. This ``Cartesian'' minimality is effective for local linearization, but it exhibits systematic side effects in three directions.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Open endpoints.} The ``point at infinity'' is not part of the object domain. Limit procedures therefore develop endpoint pathologies (singularity and divergence) that are extrinsic to the algebraic objects being studied.
    \item \textbf{Flattening multiplicativity.} Prime factorization encodes multiplicative topology and spectral information. In a purely additive coordinate these become highly nonlocal, motivating auxiliary prescriptions (cutoffs and regularization) when one attempts analytic or spectral constructions.
    \item \textbf{Conflation of readout and ontology.} Using a single linear index to encode both ``time/scan'' (iteration count) and intrinsic multiplicative structure forces conflicts between phase conventions and multiplicative homomorphisms. For instance, a naive phase $\theta(n)=2\pi\alpha n$ is generally incompatible with multiplicative additivity $\theta(mn)\equiv\theta(m)+\theta(n)$.
\end{enumerate}

The goal of this paper is not to patch these issues within the linear coordinate, but to upgrade the coordinate system so that multiplication and phase become primitive, and ``time/scan'' is represented by an explicit unitary dynamics.

\subsection{Polar minimality: a generator and a canonical readout}
We make ``minimal complexity'' explicit as the following design criteria.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(C1) Compactification.} Replace open linear endpoints by a compact phase space (circle or torus), so that ``infinity'' becomes an internal boundary point of an extended object domain.
    \item \textbf{(C2) Anti-resonance.} Avoid short periodic lock-in; prefer irrational rotations that are difficult to approximate by rationals.
    \item \textbf{(C3) Binary readout.} The observational alphabet is minimized to $\{0,1\}$.
    \item \textbf{(C4) Canonical coordinates.} Discrete readouts should correspond to a unique, computable, locally constrained integer coding.
    \item \textbf{(C5) Single generator.} Use a single unitary scan generator rather than multi-parameter assembly.
\end{itemize}

Under these constraints, we adopt the skeleton ``irrational rotation + binary window readout''. For irrational $\alpha$ and interval windows, the resulting mechanical word is Sturmian and hence has minimal factor complexity $p(n)=n+1$ among aperiodic binary sequences; see~\cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}. The golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ is then distinguished because its continued fraction coefficients are all $1$. This choice is extremal for anti-resonance (it attains the optimal Hurwitz constant among irrational rotations; see e.g.~\cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}) and it yields the Fibonacci word together with the binary Zeckendorf/Ostrowski canonical encoding (Sections~\ref{sec:readout}--\ref{sec:zeckendorf}).

\subsection{Contributions and organization}
This paper constructs and organizes a closed-loop toolkit.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Sections~\ref{sec:skeleton}--\ref{sec:embedding} define a radial character $\rho$ and a multiplicative phase $\theta_\times$ from the multiplicative skeleton $M=(\NN_{>0},\cdot)$, and introduce the holographic polar embedding $\mathcal{Z}$.
    \item Section~\ref{sec:interference} reframes addition as a lossy geometric readout: we prove a no-go theorem for additive completeness, formalize lattice mismatch, and define a projection-induced notion of additive readout together with the associated quantum gap.
    \item Sections~\ref{sec:theta}--\ref{sec:cyclic_calculus} introduce the unitary scan operator $\Theta$, formalize time as iteration count, and construct orbit-wise operators (difference, trace, finite part).
    \item Sections~\ref{sec:readout}--\ref{sec:zeckendorf} define window readouts and derive Sturmian/Fibonacci structure, then connect to Ostrowski numeration and its golden degeneration to Zeckendorf decompositions.
    \item Sections~\ref{sec:modules}--\ref{sec:conclusion} package reusable modules (singularity compactification, finite-part extraction, lattice path sums) and summarize limitations and testable intermediate claims.
\end{itemize}

\section{Minimal ontological commitments: a reference state and the \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{F}_1$}{F1} skeleton}
\label{sec:skeleton}

\subsection{Reference state \texorpdfstring{$\ketomega$}{|Omega>} as minimal mathematical commitment}
Fix a separable Hilbert space $\hilbert$ and a unit vector $\ketomega\in\hilbert$. In this paper $\ketomega$ plays the role of a global reference state for later operator-theoretic constructions.

We do not assign $\ketomega$ a physical interpretation as a theorem. The only requirement is that, under the subsequent scan and projection operations, $\ketomega$ can generate observable readout structures.

\begin{remark}[A concrete choice in the minimal scan model]
\label{rem:omega_choice}
In the minimal rotation model of Section~\ref{sec:theta} we take $\hilbert=L^2(X,\mu)$ with $X=\RR/\ZZ$ and $\mu$ the Lebesgue probability measure. For $x_0\in X$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$ define the normalized bump
$$
\omega_{x_0,\delta}(x):=\frac{\ind_{[x_0,x_0+\delta)}(x)}{\sqrt{\delta}}\in L^2(X).
$$
Let $\Pi_W$ be the multiplication operator $(\Pi_W f)(x)=\ind_W(x)f(x)$. Then the scalar
$$
p_k(\delta):=\langle \omega_{x_0,\delta},\,\Pi_W\,\Theta^k\,\omega_{x_0,\delta}\rangle
$$
is a bona fide Hilbert-space observable. Moreover, for any $x_0$ that is a Lebesgue point of $W$ one has
$$
\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0} p_k(\delta)=\ind_W(x_0+k\alpha),
$$
so the binary orbit readout arises as a sharp localization limit of a standard projection measurement. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, for any measurable $W$ almost every $x_0$ is a Lebesgue point of $W$; for interval windows the only exceptional points are the boundary points; see e.g.~\cite{SteinShakarchi2005}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{F}_1$}{F1} viewpoint: the multiplicative monoid skeleton}
Let
$$
M := (\NN_{>0},\cdot)
$$
be the multiplicative monoid of positive integers. By ``$\mathbb{F}_1$ skeleton'' we only use the following minimal core.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prime generation.} Every $n\in M$ admits a unique prime factorization
    $$
    n=\prod_{p\in\PP} p^{a_p(n)},\qquad a_p(n)\in\NN,\quad a_p(n)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }p.
    $$
    \item \textbf{Multiplication as first-order structure.} All ``geometric'' quantities introduced below (radial and phase) are required to be homomorphic or covariant with respect to multiplication on $M$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Radial structure \texorpdfstring{$\rho$}{rho}: metrizing the multiplicative skeleton}
\label{sec:radial}

\subsection{Radial weights and the radial character}
Fix a weight function $w:\PP\to\RR$. Define the radial character
$$
\rho_w(n) := \exp\!\Big(\sum_{p\in\PP} a_p(n)\, w(p)\Big),
$$
where $n=\prod_{p} p^{a_p(n)}$.

\begin{proposition}[Radial homomorphism]
\label{prop:radial_hom}
For all $m,n\in M$ one has $\rho_w(mn)=\rho_w(m)\rho_w(n)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using $a_p(mn)=a_p(m)+a_p(n)$ and additivity of the logarithm,
$$
\log\rho_w(mn)=\sum_{p\in\PP} (a_p(m)+a_p(n))w(p)=\log\rho_w(m)+\log\rho_w(n).
$$
Exponentiating yields the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Minimal choice and tunable families}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Minimal choice.} If $w(p)=\log p$, then
    $$
    \rho_w(n)=\exp\Big(\sum_p a_p(n)\log p\Big)=n.
    $$
    In this case we simply write $\rho(n)=n$.
    \item \textbf{Tunable family.} Other choices of $w$ correspond to alternative ``radial scales'' and can be used to couple the arithmetic skeleton to external weighting schemes (e.g., spectral weights). Unless stated otherwise, we adopt the minimal choice $\rho(n)=n$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Multiplicative phase \texorpdfstring{$\theta_\times$}{theta\_times} and the holographic polar embedding \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{Z}$}{Z}}
\label{sec:embedding}

\subsection{Definition of multiplicative phase}
Fix a phase weight $\beta:\PP\to\RR$. Define the multiplicative phase (valued in $\RR/2\pi\ZZ$) by
$$
\theta_\times(n)\equiv \sum_{p\in\PP} a_p(n)\,\beta(p)\pmod{2\pi}.
$$

\begin{proposition}[Phase additivity under multiplication]
\label{prop:phase_hom}
For all $m,n\in M$,
$$
\theta_\times(mn)\equiv\theta_\times(m)+\theta_\times(n)\pmod{2\pi}.
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This follows from $a_p(mn)=a_p(m)+a_p(n)$ and additivity of the sum, exactly as in Proposition~\ref{prop:radial_hom}.
\end{proof}

A minimal parametrized choice is $\beta(p)=\beta_0\log p$ for some real constant $\beta_0$, in which case
$$
\theta_\times(n)\equiv \beta_0\log n\pmod{2\pi}.
$$

\subsection{Holographic polar embedding}
\begin{definition}[HPA embedding]
\label{def:Z_embedding}
Define a map $\mathcal{Z}:M\to\CC^*$ by
$$
\mathcal{Z}(n):=\rho(n)\,\e^{\iu\theta_\times(n)}.
$$
\end{definition}

Under the minimal choices $\rho(n)=n$ and $\theta_\times(n)\equiv\beta_0\log n\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 2\pi)$,
$$
\mathcal{Z}(n)=n\,\e^{\iu\beta_0\log n}=n^{1+\iu\beta_0},
$$
so $\mathcal{Z}(M)$ lies on a family of logarithmic spirals in the complex plane.

\begin{theorem}[Multiplicative homomorphism of the embedding]
\label{thm:Z_mult}
For all $m,n\in M$,
$$
\mathcal{Z}(mn)=\mathcal{Z}(m)\mathcal{Z}(n).
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:radial_hom} and Proposition~\ref{prop:phase_hom},
$$
\mathcal{Z}(mn)=\rho(mn)\e^{\iu\theta_\times(mn)}=\rho(m)\rho(n)\e^{\iu(\theta_\times(m)+\theta_\times(n))}=\mathcal{Z}(m)\mathcal{Z}(n).
$$
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:Z_mult} is the first structural pillar: multiplicativity is decomposed into radial scaling and angular additivity.

\section{Geometric interference and the quantum gap}
\label{sec:interference}

\subsection{Vector synthesis}
In $\CC$ the interaction of two holographic integers is expressed as Euclidean vector composition. For $a,b\in M$ define the synthesized vector
$$
v(a,b):=\mathcal{Z}(a)+\mathcal{Z}(b)\in\CC.
$$
In polar form, $\mathcal{Z}(a)$ and $\mathcal{Z}(b)$ are vectors; synthesis depends on their relative phase.

\begin{itemize}
    \item If $\theta_\times(a)\approx\theta_\times(b)$, magnitudes reinforce.
    \item If $\theta_\times(a)\approx\theta_\times(b)+\pi$, partial cancellation occurs.
\end{itemize}

The central point is strategic: in a multiplicative ontology we do not ask addition to be complete. Instead, we ask how and why it fails, and what kind of readout must replace it.

\subsection{Lattice mismatch: why linear addition is not preserved}
The embedding $\mathcal{Z}$ is designed to be multiplicative (Theorem~\ref{thm:Z_mult}). A naive expectation is that it should also respect linear addition, but this target is impossible except in a degenerate trivial case.

\begin{theorem}[No-go for additive completeness]
\label{thm:no_go_additive}
Let $\Phi:\NN_{>0}\to\CC$ satisfy
$$
\Phi(mn)=\Phi(m)\Phi(n)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\Phi(a+b)=\Phi(a)+\Phi(b)
$$
for all $m,n,a,b\in\NN_{>0}$. Then either $\Phi\equiv 0$ or $\Phi(n)=n$ for all $n\in\NN_{>0}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $z:=\Phi(1)$. Multiplicativity gives $z=\Phi(1)=\Phi(1\cdot 1)=\Phi(1)^2=z^2$, hence $z\in\{0,1\}$.
If $z=0$, then for any $n\in\NN_{>0}$ one has $\Phi(n)=\Phi(n\cdot 1)=\Phi(n)\Phi(1)=0$, so $\Phi\equiv 0$.
If $z=1$, then by additivity,
$$
\Phi(n)=\Phi(\underbrace{1+\cdots+1}_{n\ \text{terms}})=n\Phi(1)=n.
$$
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Non-additivity is necessary]
\label{cor:nonadditive}
Let $\mathcal{Z}:M\to\CC^*$ be multiplicative and satisfy $\mathcal{Z}(1)=1$.
If $\mathcal{Z}$ is not the identity map, then it cannot satisfy $\mathcal{Z}(a+b)=\mathcal{Z}(a)+\mathcal{Z}(b)$ for all $a,b\in M$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\mathcal{Z}$ were additive on $M=\NN_{>0}$, then it would satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:no_go_additive}. Since $\mathcal{Z}$ takes values in $\CC^*$ it is not identically zero, hence $\mathcal{Z}(n)=n$ for all $n\in M$, contradicting the assumption that $\mathcal{Z}$ is not the identity.
\end{proof}

This is the \emph{lattice mismatch}: vector synthesis takes place in the continuous plane $\CC$, while integer readout is constrained to the discrete point set $\mathcal{Z}(M)\subset\CC$. A quantitative obstruction is the distance-to-image function
$$
g(v):=\inf_{n\in M}\bigl|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)\bigr|.
$$
When $v=v(a,b)$, the value $g(v)$ measures the residual error incurred when one attempts to interpret synthesis as an exact integer operation. If $v\in\mathcal{Z}(M)$ then $g(v)=0$; otherwise $g(v)>0$.

\subsection{Quantum projection: lossy additive readout}
Since $v(a,b)$ is not constrained to lie in $\mathcal{Z}(M)$, any macroscopic ``additive'' bookkeeping must be implemented as a \emph{projection} back to $M$. A minimal deterministic choice is a nearest-point readout.

\begin{definition}[Nearest-point readout]
\label{def:nearest_point}
For $v\in\CC$ define
$$
\pi(v):=\operatorname*{argmin}_{n\in M}\bigl|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)\bigr|,
$$
with a fixed tie-breaking rule (e.g. choose the smallest $n$ among minimizers).
\end{definition}

This produces a lossy ``sum readout'' $c=\pi(v(a,b))$. The residual
$$
\delta(a,b):=v(a,b)-\mathcal{Z}\!\bigl(\pi(v(a,b))\bigr)
$$
is the \emph{quantum gap}: the part of synthesis that cannot be represented inside the integer image. In physical terms, $\delta$ encodes the fluctuations that remain when a continuous interference pattern is coerced into a discrete lattice readout.

A probabilistic refinement replaces the hard nearest-point rule by a soft projection kernel.

\begin{definition}[Projection kernel]
\label{def:projection_kernel}
Fix a scale $\varepsilon>0$. For $v\in\CC$ define a probability distribution on $M$ by
$$
\pi_\varepsilon(c\mid v)
:=
\frac{\exp\!\bigl(-|v-\mathcal{Z}(c)|^2/\varepsilon^2\bigr)}
{\sum_{n\in M}\exp\!\bigl(-|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|^2/\varepsilon^2\bigr)}.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Well-definedness of the projection readout]
\label{prop:projection_well_defined}
Assume the minimal radial choice $\rho(n)=n$. Then for every $v\in\CC$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The minimizer set
    $$
    \operatorname*{Argmin}_{n\in M}\,|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|
    $$
    is nonempty and finite, hence the nearest-point rule $\pi(v)$ is well-defined.
    \item For every $\varepsilon>0$ the normalizing sum
    $$
    Z_\varepsilon(v):=\sum_{n\in M}\exp\!\bigl(-|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|^2/\varepsilon^2\bigr)
    $$
    converges and satisfies $0<Z_\varepsilon(v)<\infty$, hence $\pi_\varepsilon(\cdot\mid v)$ is a well-defined probability distribution on $M$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under $\rho(n)=n$ we have $|\mathcal{Z}(n)|=n$, so $|\mathcal{Z}(n)|\to\infty$ as $n\to\infty$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Fix $v\in\CC$ and set $R:=|v|+1$. If $|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|\le 1$, then $|\mathcal{Z}(n)|\le |v|+|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|\le R$, hence $n\le R$. Therefore only finitely many $n\in M$ can lie within distance $1$ of $v$, and the minimum distance is achieved by at least one of them. Finiteness of the minimizer set follows from the same bound.
    \item For all $n\in M$ we have
    $$
    |v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|\ge \bigl||\mathcal{Z}(n)|-|v|\bigr|=|n-|v||,
    $$
    hence
    $$
    \exp\!\bigl(-|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|^2/\varepsilon^2\bigr)\le \exp\!\bigl(-|n-|v||^2/\varepsilon^2\bigr).
    $$
    The comparison series $\sum_{n\ge 1}\exp(-|n-|v||^2/\varepsilon^2)$ converges, so $Z_\varepsilon(v)<\infty$. Positivity is immediate since each summand is positive.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Sharp projection limit]
\label{prop:sharp_projection_limit}
Assume the minimal radial choice $\rho(n)=n$. Fix $v\in\CC$ and let
$$
d_{\min}(v):=\min_{n\in M}|v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|.
$$
Define the minimizer set $D(v):=\{n\in M:\ |v-\mathcal{Z}(n)|=d_{\min}(v)\}$.
Then as $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ the kernel $\pi_\varepsilon(\cdot\mid v)$ converges to the uniform distribution on $D(v)$.
In particular, if $D(v)$ is a singleton, then $\pi_\varepsilon(\cdot\mid v)$ converges to a point mass at that minimizer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $d=d_{\min}(v)$. For $c\in D(v)$ one has $\exp(-|v-\mathcal{Z}(c)|^2/\varepsilon^2)=\exp(-d^2/\varepsilon^2)$.
For $m\notin D(v)$ set $\Delta_m:=|v-\mathcal{Z}(m)|^2-d^2>0$. Then
$$
\frac{\exp(-|v-\mathcal{Z}(m)|^2/\varepsilon^2)}{\exp(-d^2/\varepsilon^2)}
=\exp(-\Delta_m/\varepsilon^2)\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\downarrow 0]{}0.
$$
Since $D(v)$ is finite by Proposition~\ref{prop:projection_well_defined}(1), dividing numerator and denominator by $\exp(-d^2/\varepsilon^2)$ yields
$$
\pi_\varepsilon(c\mid v)
=
\frac{1}{|D(v)|+\sum_{m\notin D(v)}\exp(-\Delta_m/\varepsilon^2)}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\downarrow 0]{}\frac{1}{|D(v)|}
$$
for $c\in D(v)$, and $\pi_\varepsilon(m\mid v)\to 0$ for $m\notin D(v)$.
\end{proof}

Given $a,b\in M$, set $v=v(a,b)$ and draw $C\sim \pi_\varepsilon(\,\cdot\,\mid v)$. The random gap
$$
\delta:=v-\mathcal{Z}(C)
$$
realizes a probability cloud around the synthesized vector. After readout, the integer outcome $C$ is canonically encoded in Zeckendorf/Ostrowski coordinates (Section~\ref{sec:zeckendorf}), making explicit that ``addition'' in the observable layer is a statistical projection onto a discrete coding lattice rather than a closed microscopic law.

Any statement about divergent sums or ``finite residuals'' must be made using the orbit-trace and finite-part machinery introduced in Section~\ref{sec:cyclic_calculus}.

\section{The scan operator \texorpdfstring{$\Theta$}{Theta}: time/readout must arise from a genuine unitary}
\label{sec:theta}

\subsection{Why a scalar phase is insufficient}
If $\Theta$ is merely a complex number $\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}$, then for any state $|\psi\rangle$ we have $\Theta^k|\psi\rangle=\e^{2\pi\iu k\alpha}|\psi\rangle$, which differs only by a global phase and produces no observable structure. Therefore a scan that drives readout must be represented by a nontrivial unitary operator on a nontrivial Hilbert space.

\subsection{A minimal model: the Koopman operator for an irrational rotation}
Let $X:=\RR/\ZZ\cong S^1$ with Lebesgue measure $\mu$, and set $\hilbert:=L^2(X,\mu)$. Fix an irrational $\alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\QQ$. Define
$$
T_\alpha(x):=x+\alpha\pmod{1},
$$
and the Koopman operator $\Theta$ by
$$
(\Theta f)(x):=f(T_\alpha(x))=f(x+\alpha).
$$

\begin{proposition}[Unitarity]
\label{prop:theta_unitary}
The operator $\Theta$ is unitary on $\hilbert$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $f,g\in L^2(X)$,
$$
\langle \Theta f,\Theta g\rangle=\int_X \overline{f(x+\alpha)}\,g(x+\alpha)\,d\mu(x)=\int_X \overline{f(u)}\,g(u)\,d\mu(u)=\langle f,g\rangle,
$$
using invariance of Lebesgue measure under rotations.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A Weyl pair and intrinsic noncommutativity}
\label{subsec:weyl_pair}
On $\hilbert=L^2(X,\mu)$ define a second unitary $V$ by multiplication with the basic character,
$$
(Vf)(x):=\e^{2\pi\iu x} f(x).
$$
Then $U:=\Theta$ and $V$ satisfy the Weyl commutation relation
$$
UV=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}VU.
$$
Indeed,
$$
(UVf)(x)=(Vf)(x+\alpha)=\e^{2\pi\iu(x+\alpha)}f(x+\alpha)=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}(VUf)(x).
$$
The $C^*$-algebra generated by $U$ and $V$ is the irrational rotation algebra (noncommutative $2$-torus) for irrational $\alpha$; see e.g.~\cite{Rieffel1981,Connes1994}. In particular, scan shift and phase multiplication cannot be simultaneously diagonalized.

\begin{corollary}[No common eigenvectors]
\label{cor:no_common_eigenvectors}
If $\alpha\notin\QQ$, there is no nonzero $f\in\hilbert$ such that $Uf=\lambda f$ and $Vf=\mu f$ for some $\lambda,\mu\in S^1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $Uf=\lambda f$ and $Vf=\mu f$, then $UVf=\lambda\mu f$ and $VUf=\mu\lambda f$. The Weyl relation gives $UVf=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}VUf$, hence $(1-\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha})\lambda\mu f=0$. Since $\alpha\notin\QQ$ implies $\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}\ne 1$, it follows that $f=0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[A variance-type uncertainty relation for a Weyl pair]
\label{thm:weyl_uncertainty}
Let $U$ and $V$ be unitary operators satisfying
$$
UV = \e^{\iu\Phi} VU,\qquad U^\dagger V = \e^{-\iu\Phi} VU^\dagger,
\qquad 0\le \Phi\le \pi.
$$
For a normalized state $|\psi\rangle$, define the unitary variances
$$
\Delta U^2 := 1-|\langle \psi|U|\psi\rangle|^2,\qquad
\Delta V^2 := 1-|\langle \psi|V|\psi\rangle|^2,
$$
and set $A:=\tan(\Phi/2)$. Then
$$
(1+2A)\,\Delta U^2\,\Delta V^2 + A^2(\Delta U^2+\Delta V^2)\ \ge\ A^2.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This is Theorem~1 of~\cite{MassarSpindel2008}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Quantitative irreducibility for irrational rotations]
In the rotation model one has $UV=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}VU$. Choosing the representative $\Phi\in(0,\pi]$ with $\e^{\iu\Phi}=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}$ (equivalently, $\Phi=2\pi\min\{\alpha,1-\alpha\}$) and noting that $\Delta U^2$ depends only on $|\langle U\rangle|$, Theorem~\ref{thm:weyl_uncertainty} applies. In particular, for $\alpha\notin\QQ$ one has $\Phi\ne 0$, hence $A>0$, which forces a strictly nontrivial tradeoff between localization in the $U$- and $V$-modes.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Separating multiplicative phase from scan time}
Define scan time as the iteration index $k\in\ZZ_{\ge 0}$ and the orbit
$$
x_k:=T_\alpha^k(x_0)=x_0+k\alpha\pmod{1},\qquad f_k:=\Theta^k f_0.
$$
Define a scan phase increment $\delta:=2\pi\alpha$ and a scan phase $\theta_t(k):=k\delta$. The separation principle is:

\begin{itemize}
    \item $\theta_\times(n)$ is determined by the multiplicative structure of $n$ and satisfies $\theta_\times(mn)\equiv\theta_\times(m)+\theta_\times(n)$.
    \item $\theta_t(k)$ is determined by scan time $k$ and is independent of $n$.
\end{itemize}

This avoids the structural conflict that arises when one attempts to use a single index simultaneously as multiplicative coordinate and time/scan coordinate.

\subsection{The golden branch as a minimal grammar choice}
Let $\varphi:=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}$ and choose
$$
\alpha:=\varphi^{-1}=\varphi-1.
$$
This choice is not treated as mystical. It is singled out by two concrete extremal features.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anti-resonance (Diophantine extremality).} The golden ratio attains the optimal Hurwitz constant: among all irrational $\alpha$, it maximizes the Diophantine ``gap'' to rational resonances (equivalently, it is maximally badly approximable); see e.g.~\cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}.
    \item \textbf{Binary-minimal canonical encoding.} In Ostrowski numeration (Section~\ref{sec:zeckendorf}) the digit bounds are controlled by the continued fraction coefficients $a_{k+1}$. Since $a_{k+1}\ge 1$ for all $k$, the smallest possible digit alphabet is binary $\{0,1\}$, and this occurs precisely when $a_{k+1}=1$ for all $k$, i.e. $\alpha=[0;1,1,1,\dots]=\varphi^{-1}$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Binary-minimality of the golden slope]
\label{prop:golden_binary_minimal}
Let $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\dots]\in(0,1)\setminus\QQ$ and let $(b_{k+1})$ be the digits in its Ostrowski representation (Section~\ref{sec:zeckendorf}). If one requires a canonical encoding with binary digits $b_{k+1}\in\{0,1\}$ for all $k$, then necessarily $a_i=1$ for all $i$, hence $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$. Conversely, for $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ the Ostrowski constraints degenerate to the Zeckendorf constraints (Section~\ref{sec:zeckendorf}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
In \eqref{eq:ostrowski_digits} the digits satisfy $0\le b_{k+1}\le a_{k+1}$ for all $k\ge 1$. If $b_{k+1}\in\{0,1\}$ is required for all $k$, then necessarily $a_{k+1}=1$ for all $k\ge 1$ (and also $a_1=1$ since $0\le b_1<a_1$). Thus $\alpha=[0;1,1,1,\dots]=\varphi^{-1}$. The converse is exactly the golden degeneration described in Section~\ref{sec:zeckendorf}.
\end{proof}

\section{Cyclic calculus: difference, trace, and finite parts along \texorpdfstring{$\Theta$}{Theta}-orbits}
\label{sec:cyclic_calculus}

This section provides the second structural pillar: statements about regularization and finite residuals are formulated via orbit functionals rather than by informal limiting intuition.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\Theta$}{Theta}-difference as a derivative surrogate}
For sufficiently regular $f:X\to\CC$, define the orbit difference operator
$$
(\mathcal{D}_\Theta f)(x):=\frac{f(x+\alpha)-f(x)}{\alpha}.
$$
When $\alpha$ is small this approximates a derivative, but here $\alpha$ is fixed and irrational, so $\mathcal{D}_\Theta$ is an intrinsic ``discrete derivative'' along the scan step.

\subsection{Orbit trace: ergodic averages as an integral surrogate}
Define the orbit average
$$
A_N(f;x_0):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} f(x_0+k\alpha).
$$

\begin{theorem}[Unique ergodicity of irrational rotations]
\label{thm:unique_ergodic}
If $f$ is continuous, the limit
$$
\mathrm{Tr}_\Theta(f):=\lim_{N\to\infty} A_N(f;x_0)
$$
exists, is independent of $x_0$, and equals the space average:
$$
\mathrm{Tr}_\Theta(f)=\int_X f(x)\,d\mu(x).
$$
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:unique_ergodic} is a standard consequence of unique ergodicity/equidistribution of irrational circle rotations and provides a canonical replacement for ``integration'' and ``summation'' along the scan orbit; see e.g.~\cite{Weyl1916,KuipersNiederreiter1974}.

\subsection{An Abel-type finite part for divergent orbit sums}
Let $u_k:=f(x_0+k\alpha)$ be a (possibly non-absolutely summable) sequence. Define its Abel generating function
$$
S_f(r):=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} u_k r^k,\qquad 0<r<1.
$$
If as $r\uparrow 1$ the function admits an asymptotic expansion of the form
$$
S_f(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{J} \frac{c_j}{(1-r)^j} + C + o(1),
$$
then we define its orbit finite part by
$$
\FP_\Theta(f):=C.
$$
The constant term $C$ is unique whenever such an expansion exists, so $\FP_\Theta$ is well-defined (see Lemma~\ref{lem:fp_unique}).

\begin{lemma}[Uniqueness of the finite part]
\label{lem:fp_unique}
Suppose $S_f(r)$ admits two expansions as $r\uparrow 1$ of the form
$$
S_f(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{J} \frac{c_j}{(1-r)^j} + C + o(1)
$$
and
$$
S_f(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{J'} \frac{c'_j}{(1-r)^j} + C' + o(1).
$$
Then $C=C'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Subtract the two expansions to obtain
$$
0=\sum_{j=1}^{J''}\frac{d_j}{(1-r)^j}+(C-C')+o(1)
$$
as $r\uparrow 1$, where $J'':=\max\{J,J'\}$ and $d_j:=c_j-c'_j$ (with missing coefficients treated as $0$).
Multiplying by $(1-r)^{J''}$ and taking $r\uparrow 1$ gives $d_{J''}=0$. Repeating inductively yields $d_j=0$ for all $j\ge 1$, so $C-C'+o(1)=0$ and therefore $C=C'$.
\end{proof}

This template shifts ``divergence'' from being a defect of the object to being a dependence on a consistent limiting and pole-subtraction scheme.

\section{Projection readout: binary windows imply Sturmian and Fibonacci structure}
\label{sec:readout}

\subsection{Window projection and binary readout}
Fix a half-open window interval $W\subset X$ and define the binary readout
$$
s_k := \ind_W(x_0+k\alpha)\in\{0,1\}.
$$
Equivalently, on $\hilbert=L^2(X,\mu)$ one may regard $\Pi_W$ as the orthogonal projection (multiplication operator) $(\Pi_W f)(x)=\ind_W(x)f(x)$, so that orbit sampling admits an operator-theoretic interpretation; cf.~Remark~\ref{rem:omega_choice}.
This realizes criterion (C3) and provides a minimal alphabet for scan observables.

\subsection{Mechanical word form and Sturmian minimal complexity}
Fix the interval window $W=[1-\alpha,1)$. Then the binary sequence can be written as a mechanical word: there exists an intercept $\beta\in\RR$ (determined by $x_0$) such that
$$
s_k=\big\lfloor (k+1)\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor k\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor.
$$

\begin{proposition}[Window coding as a mechanical word]
\label{prop:window_mechanical}
Let $W=[1-\alpha,1)$ and set $\beta:=x_0$ (viewed as a real representative in $[0,1)$). Then the orbit coding $s_k=\ind_W(x_0+k\alpha)$ satisfies
$$
s_k=\big\lfloor (k+1)\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor k\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor
$$
for all $k\ge 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $x_k=\{k\alpha+\beta\}\in[0,1)$ for the fractional part. One has $s_k=1$ iff $x_k\in[1-\alpha,1)$, i.e. iff $\{k\alpha+\beta\}+\alpha\ge 1$. This is equivalent to
$$
\lfloor k\alpha+\beta+\alpha\rfloor=\lfloor k\alpha+\beta\rfloor+1,
$$
which is exactly the claimed difference-of-floors identity.
\end{proof}

When $\alpha$ is irrational, this sequence is Sturmian, i.e. its factor complexity satisfies $p(n)=n+1$, which is minimal among aperiodic binary sequences; see~\cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}.

\subsection{The golden branch yields the Fibonacci word}
For $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$, the associated Sturmian sequence is the Fibonacci word. Equivalently, it arises as the fixed point of the substitution
$$
0\mapsto 01,\qquad 1\mapsto 0.
$$
Geometrically, this encodes a Fibonacci chain with two spacings (Long/Short), ordered aperiodically but with strong combinatorial constraints.

\section{Cut-and-project geometry: from symbolic readout to discrete point sets}
\label{sec:cut_project}

\subsection{A cut-and-project scheme for an irrational slope}
Fix an irrational slope $\alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\QQ$. Consider the lattice $L:=\ZZ^2\subset\RR^2$. Let
$$
E_\parallel:=\mathrm{span}\{(1,\alpha)\},\qquad E_\perp:=\mathrm{span}\{(-\alpha,1)\},
$$
and let $\pi_\parallel$ and $\pi_\perp$ denote the orthogonal projections onto $E_\parallel$ and $E_\perp$, respectively. Given a bounded interval window $W\subset E_\perp$ with boundary of $\mu$-measure zero, the associated model set is
$$
\Lambda(W):=\{\pi_\parallel(\ell)\ :\ \ell\in L,\ \pi_\perp(\ell)\in W\}.
$$
This is the standard cut-and-project construction; see~\cite{Moody2000}. For the golden slope $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ and a standard half-open interval window in internal space, $\Lambda(W)$ is the Fibonacci model set, whose gap sequence yields the Fibonacci tiling (two spacings in a nonperiodic order).

\subsection{A canonical staircase coordinate from the readout}
The window coding $s_k\in\{0,1\}$ defines a natural integer coordinate by prefix summation. Set
$$
S_N:=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} s_k,\qquad N\ge 1.
$$
Then the lattice path $\gamma_N:=(N,S_N)\in\ZZ^2$ advances by steps $(1,0)$ and $(1,1)$, encoding the local short/long pattern.

\begin{proposition}[Prefix-sum coordinate and the staircase line]
\label{prop:prefix_sum}
If $s_k$ is given by the mechanical word formula of Proposition~\ref{prop:window_mechanical}, then
$$
S_N=\big\lfloor N\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor \beta\big\rfloor.
$$
In particular, when $\beta\in[0,1)$ one has $S_N=\lfloor N\alpha+\beta\rfloor$, so the staircase $\{(N,S_N)\}$ is a digital approximation of the line $y=\alpha x+\beta$ and provides an explicit bridge between symbolic readout and lattice geometry.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By telescoping,
$$
S_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\Big(\big\lfloor (k+1)\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor k\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor\Big)
=\big\lfloor N\alpha+\beta\big\rfloor-\big\lfloor \beta\big\rfloor.
$$
\end{proof}

\section{Ostrowski numeration and Zeckendorf coordinates: canonical discrete arithmetic}
\label{sec:zeckendorf}

\subsection{Ostrowski representation for a general irrational slope}
Let $\alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\QQ$ have continued fraction expansion
$$
\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,a_3,\dots],\qquad a_i\in\NN.
$$
Let $q_k$ be the denominators of convergents, defined by
$$
q_{-1}=0,\quad q_0=1,\quad q_{k+1}=a_{k+1}q_k+q_{k-1}.
$$
Ostrowski numeration asserts that every $N\in\NN_{>0}$ admits a unique expansion (see e.g.~\cite{AlloucheShallit2003,Lothaire2002})
$$
N=\sum_{k=0}^{m} b_{k+1} q_k,
$$
where the digits satisfy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ostrowski_digits}
0\le b_1<a_1,\qquad
0\le b_{k+1}\le a_{k+1}\ (k\ge 1),\qquad
b_{k+1}=a_{k+1}\ \Rightarrow\ b_k=0\ (k\ge 1).
\end{equation}
This numeration shares a common source with Sturmian mechanical words: both are controlled by the continued fraction data $(a_i)$.

\subsection{Golden degeneration: Zeckendorf as the binary canonical form}
For $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}=[0;1,1,1,\dots]$, we have $a_i=1$ for all $i$, hence
$$
q_{k+1}=q_k+q_{k-1},
$$
so $(q_k)$ is Fibonacci-type. The digit constraints degenerate to
$$
b_1=0,\qquad b_{k+1}\in\{0,1\}\ (k\ge 1),\qquad b_{k+1}=1\ \Rightarrow\ b_k=0\ (k\ge 1),
$$
i.e. no adjacent ones.

\begin{lemma}[Convergent denominators and Fibonacci bases]
\label{lem:qk_fib}
For $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ the convergent denominators satisfy $q_0=1$, and for every $k\ge 1$ one has
$$
q_k=F_k,
$$
where $(F_k)$ is the Zeckendorf Fibonacci base defined below by $F_1=1$, $F_2=2$, $F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
From the recurrence $q_{k+1}=q_k+q_{k-1}$ with $q_0=1$ and $q_1=1$, we obtain
$$
(q_k)_{k\ge 0}=(1,1,2,3,5,\dots).
$$
The Zeckendorf base satisfies the same recurrence with initial data $F_1=1$ and $F_2=2$, hence
$$
(F_k)_{k\ge 1}=(1,2,3,5,\dots).
$$
Thus $q_1=1=F_1$ and by induction $q_k=F_k$ for all $k\ge 1$.
\end{proof}

To align with the classical Zeckendorf convention, define the Fibonacci base
$$
F_1=1,\qquad F_2=2,\qquad F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n.
$$

\begin{theorem}[Zeckendorf uniqueness]
\label{thm:zeckendorf}
Every $N\in\NN_{>0}$ admits a unique representation
$$
N=\sum_{k=1}^{m} c_k F_k,
\qquad c_k\in\{0,1\},\qquad c_k c_{k+1}=0.
$$
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
See~\cite{Zeckendorf1972}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Algorithms and interface to readout}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Canonical encoding.} Given $N$ and the convergent denominators $(q_k)$ of $\alpha$, one computes the Ostrowski digits $(b_{k+1})$ by a greedy algorithm subject to the constraints in \eqref{eq:ostrowski_digits}. In the golden branch this degenerates to the Zeckendorf greedy algorithm, yielding the canonical bitstring $(c_k)$.
    \item \textbf{Readout interface.} Given a finite readout prefix $(s_0,\dots,s_{N-1})$, form the staircase coordinate $S_N=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}s_k$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:prefix_sum}). In the golden branch, canonicalization is achieved by Zeckendorf normalization (Theorem~\ref{thm:zeckendorf}); for a general irrational slope, the analogous canonical coding is given by Ostrowski digits $(b_{k+1})$.
\end{itemize}

This completes the scan--projection--canonical-coordinate loop.

\section{A modular toolbox: singularities, divergences, and lattice path sums}
\label{sec:modules}

\subsection{Topologizing singularities on the Riemann sphere}
On the extended complex plane $\widehat{\CC}:=\CC\cup\{\infty\}$, the inversion map
$$
\mathcal{I}(z):=\frac{1}{z}
$$
is a biholomorphic automorphism satisfying $\mathcal{I}(0)=\infty$ and $\mathcal{I}(\infty)=0$. Thus ``division by zero'' is replaced by a compactified pole within the object domain, shifting singularities from ``undefined'' to ``boundary points'' of a compactified geometry.

\subsection{Finite-part extraction: an Abel prototype}
Consider the Abel family
$$
S(\varepsilon):=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,\e^{-n\varepsilon},\qquad \varepsilon>0.
$$
A direct computation yields
$$
S(\varepsilon)=\frac{\e^{-\varepsilon}}{\bigl(1-\e^{-\varepsilon}\bigr)^2}.
$$
As $\varepsilon\to 0^+$,
$$
S(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2}-\frac{1}{12}+O(\varepsilon^2).
$$
Therefore, in the finite-part sense (subtracting the pole terms and taking the limit),
$$
\FP_{\varepsilon\to 0^+} \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n\,\e^{-n\varepsilon}=-\frac{1}{12}.
$$
More generally, any divergent object that admits a controlled Abel asymptotic can be treated within the orbit finite-part template of Section~\ref{sec:cyclic_calculus}.

\subsection{Lattice path sums over Zeckendorf-coded configuration spaces}
Suppose a class of configurations is coded by Zeckendorf bitstrings $(c_k)$ subject to the local constraint $c_k c_{k+1}=0$. Then a formally ill-posed continuum path integral may be replaced by a controlled sum over a discrete, locally constrained code space:
$$
\int \mathcal{D}[\mathrm{path}]\,\e^{\iu S[\mathrm{path}]}
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\sum_{(c_k)\in\mathcal{C}} W(c)\,\e^{\iu S(c)},
$$
where $\mathcal{C}$ is the set of legal bitstrings and $W(c)$ is a weight induced by the projection density or an application-specific measure.

\section{Discussion: meaning, boundaries, and testable intermediate claims}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Two notions of minimality}
The classical picture realizes a Cartesian minimality in which addition and multiplication are primitive and orthogonal. HPA aims at a polar minimality:

\begin{itemize}
    \item radial/phase separation $(\rho,\theta_\times)$ linearizes multiplicative structure;
    \item scan time is supplied by a genuine unitary $\Theta$ and is decoupled from multiplicative phase;
    \item binary window readout together with canonical numeration (Zeckendorf in the golden branch) yields a computable, locally constrained coordinate system.
\end{itemize}

In this sense, ``minimal complexity'' refers to the shortest generative description: one unitary generator, one binary window, one canonical encoding.

\subsection{Core prediction: emergence of addition}
HPA predicts that addition is not a fundamental primitive of the object domain. At the $\mathbb{F}_1$ ontological layer only multiplicative structure is intrinsic, while additive bookkeeping arises only after imposing a readout: synthesized vectors in $\CC$ must be projected back to a discrete coordinate lattice, and that projection is necessarily lossy (Section~\ref{sec:interference}). In this view, the linear conservation laws of low-energy physics are effective statements about projected statistics rather than exact microscopic identities. A concrete consequence is that at sufficiently high resolution one should expect additive bookkeeping to be replaced by a more refined holographic conservation principle in which conservation is expressed through the geometry of $\mathcal{Z}$, the scan dynamics of $\Theta$, and the statistics of projection gaps.

\subsection{Testable intermediate statements}
Rather than asserting global claims, HPA motivates concrete intermediate problems.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Readout--encoding consistency.} Quantify the stability of Sturmian factors and Zeckendorf/Ostrowski encodings under window perturbations.
    \item \textbf{Error bounds for finite parts.} For function classes specified by regularity or Fourier decay, derive explicit bounds on the error terms in $\FP_\Theta$.
    \item \textbf{Statistics of projection gaps.} For specified phase weights $\beta(p)$ and sampling models on $(a,b)$, study the distribution of the quantum gap $\delta$ and its dependence on the projection scale $\varepsilon$.
    \item \textbf{Spectral meaning of multiplicative phase choices.} Study how different prime weights $\beta(p)$ shape the distribution of $\mathcal{Z}(M)$ in $\CC^*$ and how this interfaces with spectral statistics.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The projection step is intentionally left as an application-layer choice: different physical or statistical models correspond to different kernels, scales, and tie-breaking conventions (Definitions~\ref{def:nearest_point}--\ref{def:projection_kernel}).
    \item Orbit trace/finite-part mechanisms provide a uniform template, but application to concrete models requires specifying weights and actions compatible with the chosen readout and projection scheme.
    \item The present construction keeps $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\Theta$ minimally separated; stronger couplings may exist but may sacrifice minimality or require additional axioms.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced Holographic Polar Arithmetic (HPA) as a self-contained toolkit.

\begin{itemize}
    \item From the $\mathbb{F}_1$-inspired multiplicative skeleton we defined a radial character $\rho$ and a multiplicative phase $\theta_\times$, yielding an embedding $\mathcal{Z}(n)=\rho(n)\e^{\iu\theta_\times(n)}$ that linearizes multiplication.
    \item We proved a no-go theorem: no nontrivial holographic embedding into $\CC^*$ can preserve linear addition together with multiplication. Additive readout therefore appears as a lossy projection from vector synthesis in $\CC$ back to a discrete coordinate lattice, with an intrinsic quantum gap.
    \item We introduced a genuine unitary scan operator $\Theta$ (irrational-rotation Koopman operator), defined time as iteration count, and constructed an orbit-wise cyclic calculus (difference, trace, finite part).
    \item Via binary window readout we obtained Sturmian/Fibonacci structures and, in the golden branch, a canonical coordinate system given by Zeckendorf decompositions (Ostrowski degeneration).
    \item We packaged three reusable modules: singularity compactification, finite-part extraction, and Zeckendorf-coded lattice path sums.
\end{itemize}

Under the minimal-complexity criteria, the golden branch is singled out not by numerology but by two concrete extremal features: Hurwitz anti-resonance and binary-minimal canonical encoding (Proposition~\ref{prop:golden_binary_minimal}). Future work can focus on robustness bounds, explicit error control for finite parts, and interfaces to multiplicative/spectral statistics.
