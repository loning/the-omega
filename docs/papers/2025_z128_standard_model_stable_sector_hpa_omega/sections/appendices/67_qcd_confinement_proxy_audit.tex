\section{QCD confinement proxy from finite Wilson-loop diagnostics (audit)}
\label{app:qcd_confinement_proxy_audit}

This appendix records an \emph{audit-only} confinement proxy constructed from the finite holonomy diagnostics already used to close the continuum Yang--Mills representative (Appendix~\ref{app:continuum_yang_mills_from_holonomy}).
It does \emph{not} claim a nonperturbative confinement mechanism or a Yang--Mills mass gap.

\paragraph{Input.}
We use the loop-scale Wilson-loop style diagnostics $W=\frac13\RePart\Tr(Q)$ defined in \eqref{eq:wilson_loop_style_W} (Appendix~\ref{app:continuum_yang_mills_from_holonomy}), summarized for $k\times k$ square loops on the $n=3$ grid in Table~\ref{tab:holonomy_wilson_loop}.

\paragraph{Proxy model (area/perimeter).}
\AuditTag In standard lattice gauge theory, an area law has the schematic form $\langle W(C)\rangle\sim \exp(-\sigma\,\mathrm{Area}(C))$ (with perimeter corrections).
To keep the audit fully finite and to avoid introducing continuous fit freedom, we adopt the bounded two-parameter proxy
\[
|W_k|\approx \exp(-\sigma k^2-\mu k),
\]
where $k^2$ is the square-loop area in plaquette units and $k$ is a perimeter proxy.

\begin{proposition}[Finite confinement proxy selection (audit)]\label{prop:qcd_confinement_proxy_selection}
\AuditTag Let $|W_k|$ denote the absolute value of the mean Wilson-loop diagnostic at loop size $k$, extracted from Table~\ref{tab:holonomy_wilson_loop} for $k\in\{1,\dots,6\}$.
Within the bounded rational candidate family $\sigma=p/q$, $\mu=r/s$ with $1\le q,s\le 20$ and $0\le p,r\le 40$, selecting the unique minimizer of the mean absolute log mismatch
\[
E(\sigma,\mu):=\frac{1}{6}\sum_{k=1}^6\left|\log\!\left(\frac{\exp(-\sigma k^2-\mu k)}{|W_k|}\right)\right|
\]
with deterministic tie-break rules (smaller denominators, then lexicographic numerators) yields a stabilized proxy pair $(\widehat\sigma,\widehat\mu)$ reproduced by the generator script \texttt{scripts/exp\_qcd\_confinement\_proxy\_audit.py}.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Consistency bridge to the Pad\'e pole-barrier audit (analytic continuation viewpoint)}
\label{subsec:qcd_proxy_pade_bridge}
\AuditTag The proxy above is an explicit finite-domain fit in the $(\sigma,\mu)$ family and therefore tests an \emph{area/perimeter} heuristic.
Separately, Appendix~\ref{app:qcd_confinement_pade_pole_barrier_audit} records an Abel-first / pole-barrier style audit by treating the same finite loop magnitudes as coefficients of a sparse series and probing Pad\'e denominators for interior poles.
\par
\AuditTag These are different diagnostics and neither is a theorem-level confinement/mass-gap result.
However, they can be reported together as a consistency check:
\begin{itemize}
\item the proxy selection currently yields $\widehat\sigma=0$ and a nonzero perimeter parameter $\widehat\mu$ (Summary fragment in this appendix), i.e.\ no detectable area-law slope at this anchor under the declared bounded family;
\item the Pad\'e audit records whether any bounded-order approximants produce interior-unit-disk denominator roots (Appendix~\ref{app:qcd_confinement_pade_pole_barrier_audit}).
\end{itemize}
In future refinements (larger-$n$ grids, richer loop families, denser series), one can require qualitative agreement between these two viewpoints as an additional audit gate, while keeping the strict confinement/mass-gap problem explicitly not closed.

\subsection{Proxy$\leftrightarrow$pole-barrier consistency loop (mutual-exclusion style audit)}

\AuditTag We summarize the two diagnostics into a small audit-facing gate table to make failure modes explicit.

Neither gate is a theorem-level statement; both are finite diagnostics on a finite dataset.

\paragraph{Minimal conflict reasons (why this does not close confinement/mass gap).}
\AuditTag Even when the two gates appear mutually consistent within the declared bounded families, this does not upgrade the confinement/mass-gap status.
The minimal failure points are:
\begin{itemize}
\item (QG1) \textbf{Sparsity/short-series ambiguity.} The Pad\'e pole-barrier check is performed on a sparse series with coefficients only at $n=k^2$ up to $36$; spurious poles/zeros and order-instability are expected and must be treated as a diagnostic-only phenomenon.
\item (QG2) \textbf{Proxy family narrowness.} The area/perimeter proxy $|W_k|\approx \exp(-\sigma k^2-\mu k)$ is a two-parameter family on $k\in\{1,\dots,6\}$; $\widehat\sigma=0$ within this family does not imply absence of an area law in a richer family or at larger loop sizes.
\item (QG3) \textbf{Finite-grid non-identifiability.} Both diagnostics are computed from the same finite holonomy table at fixed $n=3$; without a controlled refinement limit (larger $n$, richer loop families), neither diagnostic can be promoted beyond an audit-facing consistency check.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Audit hooks for future refinements.}
\AuditTag A minimal upgrade path (still audit-only) is to add one bounded extension at a time:
(i) increase the loop family density (more $k$ and non-square loops), (ii) extend Pad\'e order families with stability gates, and (iii) add a bounded proxy-family enlargement, while requiring the gate verdict to stabilize across these bounded extensions.
