
\begin{abstract}
We propose a linear-ontology formulation of the Holographic Phase Arithmetic (HPA) scan--projection paradigm: the primitive description is a single unitary scan order (a one-dimensional readout stream), while ``space'' and ``spacetime'' refer to protocol-level addressing bases that fold this order into locality-preserving neighborhoods.
Time is not imposed as an external parameter; it is realized operationally as the iteration count along the scan.
In this paper, the conversion from this dimensionless scan rate to a physical velocity scale (e.g.\ the measured constant $c$) is treated only as a matching-layer and units dictionary, not as a theorem-level output of the folding core.

Within this framework we reformulate the Standard Model (SM) as a problem of \emph{stable readout sectors} under finite resolution.
On the physical identification layer, we treat the intrinsic rigidity of $3$D geometry---specifically the $6$ degrees of freedom (three translations plus three rotations) required to specify a local frame---as a geometric lock that selects a $6$-bit fundamental readout window $\Omega_6:=\{0,1\}^6$.
This is not an arbitrary choice but the unique balanced instance in which scan information capacity matches addressing capacity ($2^m=4^n$), namely $m=2n$ with $(m,n)=(6,3)$ and $|\Omega_6|=64$.
Introducing three commuting stability channels, denoted $\varphi$--$\pi$--$\e$, and defining stability by their joint readout constraints, we obtain a rigid \emph{resolution folding} $64\to 21$: the $\varphi$-stable type set $X_6$ has cardinality $|X_6|=21$ and admits a canonical $18\oplus 3$ split into cyclic and boundary types induced by the $\pi$-channel.
At this minimal anchor, several interface choices are strongly constrained by geometric rigidity and by bounded-complexity audit rules: the stable-sector skeleton is exact, and the remaining identifications are reduced to finite, reproducible selection problems with explicit candidate families and deterministic tie-break rules.

We then propose a \emph{scan--chirality locking} principle: the orientation class of Hilbert addressing (a choice among the dihedral layout families $D_4$) fixed at $t=0$ turns parity into a protocol change at the readout level.
A discrete Hilbert-path chirality index flips sign under both reflection (parity) and traversal reversal (time reversal), providing an auditable finite-resolution sense in which the arrow of time and spatial handedness couple to the same sign datum.
Gauge fields are interpreted as compensating connections enforcing cross-site phase consistency under the three defect channels.

In the closed constant-geometry sector of the HPA--$\Omega$ program, the same protocol language yields quantitative rigidity targets: the theorem-level value $\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi\approx 137.0363037759$ has relative deviation $\sim 2.22\times 10^{-6}$ from CODATA 2022; electroweak volume quantization yields $\alpha^{-1}(\mu_Z)=13\pi^2\approx 128.3048572142$ and $\sin^2\theta_W(\mu_Z)=3/13\approx 0.2307692308$ with explicit PDG deviations at the per-mille level; and CP violation admits the rigid reference $J_{\mathrm{geo}}=1/(11\pi^7)\approx 3.00994\times 10^{-5}$ compared to the PDG CKM Jarlskog value.
These closed values are recorded as \emph{low-complexity rigidity targets} with explicit mismatch factors (in the log-mismatch norm used throughout the paper) and are accompanied by bounded-complexity audit certificates, uncertainty-robustness checks, and counterfactual baselines reproduced by deterministic scripts.

Finally, we provide minimal standard-library Python scripts that reproduce the Fold$_6$ surjectivity, the rigid $(2,3,4)$ degeneracy histogram, the Hilbert chirality-index transformation law, and a minimal parity contrast between parity-odd and coarse-grained parity-even observables, and we record falsifiable interface predictions.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Conventions.}
Unless otherwise stated, $\log$ denotes the natural logarithm.
We use Fibonacci numbers $F_1=F_2=1$, $F_{k+1}=F_k+F_{k-1}$.
For $N\in\NN$, the Zeckendorf digits $c_k\in\{0,1\}$ satisfy $c_k c_{k+1}=0$ and
\[
N=\sum_{k\ge 1} c_k F_{k+1}.
\]
We write $w=w_1\cdots w_m$ for a finite binary word with letters $w_i\in\{0,1\}$.
We reserve $m$ for Zeckendorf window length and reserve $n$ for Hilbert order.
The standard Abel path refers to the limit process $r\uparrow 1$ with $r\in(0,1)$.

\paragraph{Dimensional language.}
Throughout the paper we use conventional terms such as ``space'', ``spacetime'', ``grid'', and ``dimension'' only as shorthand for protocol-level addressing and locality structure induced by the chosen readout basis (e.g.\ Hilbert addressing at fixed order).
No ontic assumption of a pre-existing continuum manifold or a privileged physical dimension is used as a premise for the theorem-level folding statements.
References to ``4D spacetime'' and to the $6$-DoF phase-space lock are likewise interface language: time refers to scan iteration count, while the spatial dictionary refers to the chosen locality basis; these identification statements are not used as premises for the folding core.

\paragraph{Layered audit rule.}
We separate a \emph{mathematical layer} (definitions and provable finite-resolution statements) from a \emph{physical identification layer} (interfaces and falsifiable mapping hypotheses).
