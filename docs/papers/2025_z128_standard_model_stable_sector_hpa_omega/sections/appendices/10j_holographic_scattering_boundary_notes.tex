\section{Holographic scattering boundary notes (conditional interface)}
\label{app:holo_scattering_boundary_notes}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
\AuditTag This appendix records how the manuscript organizes scattering objects as \emph{boundary observables} in the holographic dictionary,
without asserting a theorem-level reconstruction of a 4D interacting $S$-matrix from the folding core.
All statements here are conditional on the explicit scattering prerequisites and failure points already recorded in the paper.

\subsection{Holo5: scattering observables as boundary data}
\label{subsec:holo5_scattering_as_boundary_data}

\paragraph{Declared scattering interface.}
\InterfaceTag Whenever a scattering representation is declared, the interface object is the frequency-resolved scattering matrix $S(\omega)$
(Appendix~\ref{app:scattering_haag_ruelle_lsz_interface}).
In the holographic dictionary of Appendix~\ref{app:holo_scope_contract}, we treat $S(\omega)$ and its derived delay invariants
as \emph{boundary-accessible data}: they are functions of external in/out channels and do not assume access to internal bookkeeping variables.

\paragraph{Delay as a boundary invariant.}
\InterfaceTag On any band where $S(\omega)$ is (approximately) unitary, the Wigner--Smith operator
\[
Q(\omega)=-\iu\,S(\omega)^\dagger \frac{\dd S}{\dd \omega},
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)=\Tr Q(\omega),
\]
provides a basis-invariant operational boundary observable (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}).
Within the paper's delay dictionary, this $\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}$ is the canonical boundary proxy for latency/overhead across channels.

\subsection{Holo5: compatibility with the boundary algebra package}
\label{subsec:holo5_boundary_algebra_compatibility}

\paragraph{Algebraic packaging viewpoint.}
\AuditTag The boundary-algebra package (Appendix~\ref{app:holo_boundary_algebra_from_screens}) is region-indexed and protocol-native.
The scattering interface is \emph{not} derived from it, but may be \emph{declared} as an additional boundary readout channel:
in that case, one can treat scattering-derived quantities (e.g.\ $\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)$ on a finite grid) as elements of a finite outcome algebra
or as expectation values of effects in a finite register model (Appendix~\ref{app:observable_algebra_state_base}).
This is a bookkeeping alignment step, not a theorem.

\subsection{Holo5: minimal failure points and fallbacks}
\label{subsec:holo5_failure_points}

\paragraph{Scattering gates.}
\AuditTag The scattering interface is conditional on the S1--S3 failure-point gates
(Appendix~\ref{app:minimal_failure_point_templates} and Appendix~\ref{app:scattering_haag_ruelle_lsz_interface}):
\begin{itemize}
\item (S1) approximate unitarity window,
\item (S2) one-particle stability/mass gap,
\item (S3) asymptotic completeness.
\end{itemize}
If any of these fail, the manuscript reverts to delay-as-proxy (S1) or to AQFT bookkeeping (S2--S3).

\paragraph{Holographic comparability gate.}
\AuditTag Any attempt to compare scattering-derived boundary observables to bulk reportables must satisfy the holographic comparability gate H0b
(Appendix~\ref{app:holo_scope_contract} and the H0b record in Appendix~\ref{app:minimal_failure_point_templates}):
units/scheme/threshold conventions must be declared, or the statement must be downgraded to a matching-envelope report.
