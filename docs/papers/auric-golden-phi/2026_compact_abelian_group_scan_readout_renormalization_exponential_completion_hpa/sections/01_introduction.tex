\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Research Objective}

Under finite information and finite resolution, the discrete structures and stable patterns observed in practice can be understood as projections and coarse-grainings of continuous ontic dynamics. The objective of this paper is to provide an auditable mathematical framework that places the following objects in one formal system:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Scan dynamics:} translation iterations on compact groups (or tori) as a minimal dynamical prototype;
  \item \textbf{Readout mechanism:} window kernels and finite-resolution symbolic readout map continuous states to visible sequences and statistics;
  \item \textbf{Cross-scale stability:} substitution systems and block renormalization describe stable structures, frequencies, and growth rates;
  \item \textbf{Geometric interface:} cut-and-project unifies ``hidden periodic structure'' and ``visible aperiodic order'' as window-membership relations;
  \item \textbf{Continuous completion:} exponential mapping from discrete iteration to continuous flow introduces $e$ as a structural base.
\end{enumerate}
For the provable part we rely strictly on classical theorems in ergodic theory and symbolic dynamics. For non-theorem components we state assumptions explicitly and provide falsifiable tests.

\subsection{Main Contributions}

The contributions can be summarized in six points:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formalize a three-layer ``scan--readout--renormalization'' architecture and provide coherent definitions on compact Abelian groups.
  \item We prove that translations modulo $L$ and modulo $1$ are conjugate under a scale homeomorphism, reducing ``$\varphi$-step scanning modulo $\pi$'' to an irrational-ratio parameter problem.
  \item We show that multidimensional synchronous equal-increment scans degenerate to a diagonal subtorus, and we provide non-resonant step-vector constructions satisfying the Kronecker--Weyl condition.
  \item We establish a closed-loop consistency chain: rotation coding $\Rightarrow$ Sturmian sequence $\Rightarrow$ Fibonacci special case $\Rightarrow$ substitution-renormalization fixed point (agreement of frequency, growth rate, and window measure).
  \item Under Pontryagin duality, we represent concepts as observable functions in $L^2(G)$, encode conceptual differences by Fourier coefficients, and construct metrics consistent with discrepancy/spectral energy.
  \item We formalize ``continuous completion of discrete iteration'' via exponential maps and Euler limits, explaining why $e$ appears structurally in this protocol class.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Relation to HPA (Conceptual Correspondence, Not Citation Dependency)}

HPA, viewed as a coordinate system of ``multiplicative ontology--unit scan--projective readout--canonical coding,'' has four key protocol-layer components:
\begin{itemize}
  \item an intrinsic discrete ``time scale'' (iteration count) generated by a unitary scan operator;
  \item finite-resolution readout induced by projection/window kernels and associated probability structure;
  \item canonical coding (e.g., Ostrowski/Zeckendorf) that maps continuous phase information to auditable discrete strings;
  \item multichannel constraints that organize stable subspaces and observable structure.
\end{itemize}

The compact-group translation scan and window-readout model developed here provides a fully rigorous mathematical instance of such an HPA protocol layer:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\varphi$ enters readout and renormalization through ``minimal-complexity symbolization + anti-locking (Diophantine extremality) + Zeckendorf constraints'';
  \item $\pi$ corresponds to periodic closure and modular structure of phase space (especially the distinction between a ``circle'' and ``cyclic admissibility'');
  \item $e$ appears via exponential completion from discrete iteration to continuous flow and via Abel/Euler-type limits in analytic normalization.
\end{itemize}
Hence, the ``framework in this paper--HPA'' relation can be stated as follows: this paper provides a provable protocol core in which scan, readout, and multichannel stability can be fully formalized in the language of compact Abelian groups, symbolic dynamics, and harmonic analysis.
