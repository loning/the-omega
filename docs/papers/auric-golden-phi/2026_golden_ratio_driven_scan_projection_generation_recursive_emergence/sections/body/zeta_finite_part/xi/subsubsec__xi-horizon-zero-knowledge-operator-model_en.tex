\subsubsection{Horizon zero knowledge and transcript operators: relatively independent extensions, closed-loop \texorpdfstring{$\zeta$}{zeta}-ification, and critical slice symmetry}\label{subsubsec:xi-horizon-zero-knowledge-operator-model}
The preceding paragraph closed the auditable decidability of the ``single-slice protocol'' to the read-out reflector \(R_{\mathrm{read}}\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-read-reflector-factorization}).
This paragraph further demonstrates that when interactive transcripts close on the horizon factor, this closure is not merely a predicate-level factorization but can be precisely promoted to \emph{perfect zero knowledge} in the information-theoretic sense;
its direct consequence is that the protocol induces on the horizon level an auditable family of transfer operators and a Fredholm determinant interface, and under the ``invertible/dual \(+\) detailed balance'' hypothesis, automatically carries a functional equation-type symmetry, thereby organizing ``critical line symmetry--spectrum--zeros'' into a single closed-loop expansion chain.
\par

\paragraph{Setup: bulk--boundary factor, transcript kernel, and simulator}
Let \((\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mu)\) be the bulk (microscopic state) probability space, let \((Y,\mathcal{Y})\) be the horizon visible space, and let \(\pi:\Omega\to Y\) be a measurable factor map (horizon projection); denote \(y=\pi(\omega)\), \(\nu:=\pi_{\#}\mu\).
Let \((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{G})\) be the interactive transcript space: given a microstate \(\omega\), the conditional distribution of the protocol output transcript \(\tau\in\mathcal{T}\) is denoted by the kernel \(P_{\omega}(\dd\tau)\).
This gives the joint measure
\[
\mathbf{P}(\dd\omega,\dd\tau):=\mu(\dd\omega)\,P_{\omega}(\dd\tau)
\qquad(\text{on }\Omega\times\mathcal{T}).
\]
Take a disintegration \(\{\mu_y\}_{y\in Y}\) of \(\mu\) with respect to \(\pi\), so that \(\mu(\dd\omega)=\nu(\dd y)\,\mu_y(\dd\omega)\).
\par
In the context of this paper, an important special case is taking \(\pi=\mathrm{read}\) or \(\pi=(\mathrm{read},a)\) (the addressed visible readout; see Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null} and Definition \ref{def:multi_axis_address}), in which case the ``horizon factor'' is the stable type space visible to the protocol.

\begin{definition}[Perfect zero knowledge on the horizon factor]\label{def:xi-horizon-perfect-zero-knowledge}
The protocol is called \textbf{perfect zero knowledge} relative to the factor \(\pi\) if there exists a simulation kernel \(S_y(\dd\tau)\) depending only on \(y\) such that for \(\mu\)-almost every \(\omega\),
\[
P_{\omega}(\cdot)=S_{\pi(\omega)}(\cdot).
\]
In other words, given the visible data \(y\), the conditional distribution of the transcript no longer depends on any additional information about the bulk microstate \(\omega\).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Equivalent characterizations of perfect zero knowledge: conditional independence and relatively independent splicing]\label{thm:xi-horizon-perfect-zk-equivalences}
Under the above setting, the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Horizon perfect zero knowledge (Definition \ref{def:xi-horizon-perfect-zero-knowledge}).
  \item \(\tau\) and \(\omega\) are conditionally independent given \(y=\pi(\omega)\): for any bounded measurable functions \(f:\Omega\to\CC\), \(g:\mathcal{T}\to\CC\),
  \[
  \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{P}}\bigl[f(\omega)\,g(\tau)\mid y\bigr]
  =
  \mathbb{E}_{\mu}\bigl[f(\omega)\mid y\bigr]\cdot
  \mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{P}}\bigl[g(\tau)\mid y\bigr]
  \quad(\nu\text{-a.e. }y).
  \]
  \item The joint measure is a \textbf{relatively independent splicing} along the factor \(Y\): there exists a kernel \(S_y(\dd\tau)\) such that
  \[
  \mathbf{P}(\dd\omega,\dd\tau)
  =
  \nu(\dd y)\,\mu_y(\dd\omega)\,S_y(\dd\tau).
  \]
  \item The conditional mutual information vanishes: under \(\mathbf{P}\), \(I(\omega;\tau\mid y)=0\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore, the rigorous translation of ``the horizon as an information barrier'' is: \(\mathbf{P}\) is a relatively independent extension along \(Y\); all decidable statistics of the protocol on the horizon level can be generated by \(\{S_y\}\) without accessing additional degrees of freedom within the bulk microstate fibers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{(1)\(\Rightarrow\)(3).}
If \(P_{\omega}=S_{\pi(\omega)}\), then
\[
\mathbf{P}(\dd\omega,\dd\tau)
=
\mu(\dd\omega)\,S_{\pi(\omega)}(\dd\tau)
=
\nu(\dd y)\,\mu_y(\dd\omega)\,S_y(\dd\tau).
\]
\textbf{(3)\(\Rightarrow\)(2).}
From \(\mathbf{P}(\dd\omega,\dd\tau\mid y)=\mu_y(\dd\omega)\,S_y(\dd\tau)\), the multiplicative factorization of conditional independence follows directly.
\textbf{(2)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(4).}
This is the standard characterization of conditional mutual information: \(I(\omega;\tau\mid y)=0\) if and only if \(\omega\perp \tau\mid y\).
\textbf{(3)\(\Rightarrow\)(1).}
Comparing \(\mathbf{P}(\dd\omega,\dd\tau)=\mu(\dd\omega)\,P_{\omega}(\dd\tau)\) with (3) shows that for \(\mu\)-almost every \(\omega\), \(P_{\omega}(\cdot)=S_{\pi(\omega)}(\cdot)\).
\qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Threshold zero knowledge and horizon secret sharing: from local simulability to global coupleability}
In multi-radiation partitioning/multi-verifier contexts, the horizon visible space often admits a product decomposition \(Y=\prod_{i=1}^{m}Y_i\), where each \(Y_i\) corresponds to a subsystem's visible readout.
In this case, ``zero knowledge'' can be refined according to subset viewpoints into a threshold form: any subset viewpoint of size \(\le t\) can be locally simulated; while the ``secret sharing/error correction'' picture corresponds to: although any proper subset does not carry internal witnesses in the visible semantics, the joint readout of all subsystems can recover the interior (thereby making the global readout possess reconstruction capability in the information-theoretic sense).

\begin{definition}[Multiparty zero knowledge at threshold \(t\) (horizon aperture)]\label{def:xi-threshold-zk}
Let \(Y=\prod_{i=1}^{m}Y_i\) and \(\pi=(\pi_1,\dots,\pi_m):\Omega\to Y\).
Suppose the transcript space also decomposes as \(\mathcal{T}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathcal{T}_i\), and for any \(S\subseteq[m]:=\{1,\dots,m\}\) denote
\[
Y_S:=\prod_{i\in S}Y_i,\qquad
\mathcal{T}_S:=\prod_{i\in S}\mathcal{T}_i,
\qquad
y_S:=\pi_S(\omega):=(\pi_i(\omega))_{i\in S},
\qquad
\tau_S:=\operatorname{pr}_S(\tau).
\]
The protocol satisfies \textbf{multiparty zero knowledge at threshold \(t\)} if for every subset \(S\subseteq[m]\) with \(|S|\le t\), there exists a simulation kernel
\(
S_S(\dd\tau_S\mid y_S)
\)
depending only on \(y_S\) such that for \(\mu\)-almost every \(\omega\),
\[
\mathsf{Law}\bigl(\tau_S\mid \pi_S(\omega)=y_S\bigr)=S_S(\cdot\mid y_S).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Projective consistency of local simulation kernel families]\label{def:xi-threshold-zk-projective-consistency}
In the notation of Definition \ref{def:xi-threshold-zk}, the local simulation kernel family
\(
\{\,S_S(\dd\tau_S\mid y_S):|S|\le t\,\}
\)
is said to satisfy \textbf{projective consistency} if for any \(S\subseteq T\subseteq[m]\) with \(|T|\le t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-threshold-zk-projective}
\boxed{\;
(\operatorname{pr}_{T\to S})_{\#}S_T(\cdot\mid y_T)=S_S(\cdot\mid y_S),
\qquad y_S=\operatorname{pr}_{T\to S}(y_T),\;}
\end{equation}
where \(\operatorname{pr}_{T\to S}\) is the natural projection (applicable to both \(Y\) and \(\mathcal{T}\)).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Globally coupleable threshold simulator]\label{def:xi-threshold-zk-global-coupler}
The threshold simulation kernel family \(\{S_S\}_{|S|\le t}\) is said to admit a \textbf{global coupling simulator} if there exists a kernel
\(
S(\dd\tau\mid y)
\)
such that for all \(|S|\le t\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-threshold-zk-global-marginals}
\boxed{\;
(\operatorname{pr}_{S})_{\#}S(\cdot\mid y)=S_S(\cdot\mid y_S),
\qquad y_S=\operatorname{pr}_S(y).\;}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Consistency criterion and coupling existence (marginal extension problem)]\label{prop:xi-threshold-zk-consistency-coupling}
Under standard Borel (or separable complete metric) assumptions, if a global coupling simulator exists (Definition \ref{def:xi-threshold-zk-global-coupler}), then the local simulation kernel family necessarily satisfies projective consistency \eqref{eq:xi-threshold-zk-projective}.
Conversely, when the local simulation kernel family can be \emph{simultaneously} realized as the marginal family of some joint kernel \(S(\dd\tau\mid y)\) at \(\nu\)-almost every \(y\) (i.e., the marginal extension problem is feasible), a global coupling simulator exists.
\par
In particular, when \(t=m\) (or more generally, when the local kernel family is given on a projective system that contains all coordinates and is closed under inclusion), projective consistency and global kernel existence are equivalent.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Extreme threshold of horizon secret sharing and ``full-set reconstructibility'']\label{rem:xi-threshold-zk-secret-sharing}
In the ``radiation partition'' model, the property ``any proper subset cannot reconstruct the interior while the full set can'' may be viewed as an extreme threshold secret sharing picture with \(t=m-1\): all viewpoints with \(|S|\le m-1\) can be locally simulated in the visible semantics, while when taking the full set \(S=[m]\), the joint readout may acquire interior information and trigger completeness reconstruction.
The geometrized version can be found in the error correction/secret sharing discussion of \cite{BalasubramanianEtAl2021SecretSharing}; in the aperture of this paper, it corresponds to the structural tension between ``local zero knowledge (simulable)'' and ``global coupling/reconstruction (recoverable).''
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Horizon transcript transfer operator and ``protocol \texorpdfstring{$\zeta$}{zeta} function'' interface}
Under perfect zero knowledge, any ``single-round transcript--visible update'' statistic can be closed on the horizon space.
To align its closed-loop structure with the \(\zeta\)/spectral interface, introduce an additive length/action functor \(\ell:\mathcal{T}\to\RR_{\ge 0}\) satisfying composition additivity
\(
\ell(\tau_1\circ\tau_2)=\ell(\tau_1)+\ell(\tau_2)
\)
(for example, taking the number of steps or cost potential as the length).

\begin{definition}[Horizon transcript transfer operator family and protocol determinant]\label{def:xi-horizon-transcript-transfer-operator}
Suppose a single-round kernel \(Q_y(\dd y',\dd\tau)\) closing on the horizon is given (given the current visible state \(y\), outputting the joint distribution of the next visible state \(y'\) and transcript \(\tau\)).
For \(s\in\CC\), define the weighted horizon kernel
\[
k_s(y,\dd y')
:=
\int_{\mathcal{T}} e^{-s\ell(\tau)}\,Q_y(\dd y',\dd\tau),
\]
and define its kernel operator on \(L^2(Y,\nu)\)
\[
(\mathcal{K}_s f)(y):=\int_Y f(y')\,k_s(y,\dd y').
\]
If \(\mathcal{K}_s\) is trace class in some open domain and analytic in \(s\), then one can define the Fredholm determinant
\[
D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s):=\det(I-\mathcal{K}_s),
\qquad
\zeta_{\mathrm{prot}}(s):=D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)^{-1}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Closed-loop expansion identity and closed orbit weight sums]\label{prop:xi-horizon-protocol-zeta-loop-expansion}
Under the trace class analyticity assumption of Definition \ref{def:xi-horizon-transcript-transfer-operator}, one always has
\[
\log D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)=-\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{1}{n}\Tr(\mathcal{K}_s^{n}).
\]
Under a finite/countable partition approximation of the horizon (\(Y\) is a finite set or a countable Markov partition), \(\Tr(\mathcal{K}_s^{n})\) equals the weight sum of all ``transcript closed loops'' of length \(n\); hence \(\zeta_{\mathrm{prot}}\) semantically corresponds to a Ruelle/Ihara-type \(\zeta\)-ification object driven by verifiable transcript closed loops.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The first formula is a direct application of the trace class Fredholm determinant Definition \ref{def:fredholm-determinant} at \(z=1\).
\par
When \(Y\) is a finite set, \(\mathcal{K}_s\) is a matrix; \(\Tr(\mathcal{K}_s^{n})\) is the sum over all closed orbits
\(
y_0\to y_1\to\cdots\to y_{n-1}\to y_0
\)
of the product of weights, where the weight of each step is given by \(k_s(y_i,y_{i+1})\); writing \(k_s\) back in its integral representation over \(\tau\) allows this weight to be interpreted as a weighted sum over ``transcript closed loops.''
The countable Markov partition case is analogous, using the integral representation of kernel operator composition and Fubini exchange to write the orbit integral as a closed-loop summation. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Microscopic reversibility and zero-knowledge duality \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} functional equation-type symmetry}
Assume the protocol carries a ``time reversal/duality'' structure such that at the horizon level a conjugation symmetry analogous to completion self-duality arises: there exists an invertible bounded operator \(J_Y:L^2(Y,\nu)\to L^2(Y,\nu)\) such that for all \(s\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-hzom-detailed-balance}
\boxed{\ \mathcal{K}_s^{*}=J_Y\,\mathcal{K}_{1-\overline{s}}\,J_Y^{-1}\ }.
\end{equation}
This relation can be viewed as a unified expression of ``horizon detailed balance \(+\) parameter duality'': it realizes the functional equation-type symmetry \(s\mapsto 1-\overline{s}\) as the adjoint conjugation of the kernel operator family.

\begin{proposition}[Detailed balance conjugation \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} determinant reflection symmetry]\label{prop:xi-hzom-functional-equation}
Under the hypothesis \eqref{eq:xi-hzom-detailed-balance}, the protocol determinant satisfies
\[
\boxed{\ \overline{D_{\mathrm{prot}}(1-\overline{s})}=D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)\ }.
\]
Therefore its zero set is mirror-symmetric about the line \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the basic property of trace class determinants, for any trace class operator \(A\) one has \(\overline{\det(I-A)}=\det(I-A^{*})\).
Therefore
\[
\overline{D_{\mathrm{prot}}(1-\overline{s})}
=\det\!\bigl(I-\mathcal{K}_{1-\overline{s}}^{*}\bigr).
\]
By \eqref{eq:xi-hzom-detailed-balance} (replacing \(s\) by \(1-\overline{s}\)),
\(
\mathcal{K}_{1-\overline{s}}^{*}=J_Y\,\mathcal{K}_{s}\,J_Y^{-1}
\).
The determinant is invariant under conjugation, so
\[
\det\!\bigl(I-\mathcal{K}_{1-\overline{s}}^{*}\bigr)
=
\det\!\bigl(I-J_Y\mathcal{K}_sJ_Y^{-1}\bigr)
=
\det(I-\mathcal{K}_s)
=D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s).
\]
\qedhere
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Protocol translation of the contraction criterion: reducing zero concentration to spectral contractivity]\label{rem:xi-hzom-contraction-template}
If on some right half-plane \(\Re(s)\ge \sigma_0\) one can establish spectral contractivity \(\rho(\mathcal{K}_s)<1\), then \(1\notin\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{K}_s)\), so \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)\neq 0\);
by the reflection symmetry of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hzom-functional-equation}, the left half-plane \(\Re(s)\le 1-\sigma_0\) is also zero-free.
Therefore, for any \(\epsilon>0\), if one can prove
\(
\Re(s)\ge \tfrac12+\epsilon\Rightarrow \rho(\mathcal{K}_s)<1
\),
then all zeros of the protocol determinant are compressed onto the critical slice \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
This template is formally isomorphic to the ``congruence symmetry--boundary zero principle'' of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-contractive-boundary-zero} later in this section: both reduce the zero geometry to a purely operator-theoretic contraction/invertibility certificate problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Zero--soundness duality: closed-loop counting errors and spectral singularities]\label{rem:xi-hzom-zero-soundness-duality}
Under the closed-loop expansion of Definition \ref{def:xi-horizon-transcript-transfer-operator}, \(\Tr(\mathcal{K}_s^n)\) can be understood as the weighted count of ``admissible transcript closed loops of length \(n\).''
When analytic continuation and growth conditions are satisfied, the zero/pole positions of \(\zeta_{\mathrm{prot}}\) control the error term of the closed-loop count through explicit formula-type contour shifting: zeros deviating from the critical slice introduce exponentially divergent modes, while critical slice concentration suppresses visible deviations to critical-type fluctuations.
In this semantics, the critical line statement can be reformulated as: all forgeable advantages can only accumulate in a critical-type manner, and no non-critical exponential amplification channel exists.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Unitary slice and spectral crossing: zeros as spectral collisions with \(1\)]\label{cor:xi-hzom-unitary-slice}
If on the critical slice \(s=\tfrac12+it\), the horizon operator can be reparametrized as a family of unitary operators \(U(t)\) satisfying
\(
D_{\mathrm{prot}}(\tfrac12+it)=\det(I-U(t))
\)
(or differing by an everywhere nonzero analytic factor),
then the zero event is equivalent to \(1\in\mathrm{Spec}(U(t))\).
In the completed determinant bridge kernel case of this paper, this is precisely the abstract translation of the unitary slice spectral crossing criterion (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-unitary-slice-spectral-crossing}): critical line zeros are realized as spectral collisions of the horizon unitary evolution at the same point \(1\).
This is compatible with the route of understanding zeros as spectral lines/resonances; see the spectral interpretation of ``absorption spectral lines/resonances'' in \cite{Connes1999TraceFormulaNCG}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{definition}[Horizon zero-knowledge operator model (HZOM)]\label{def:xi-hzom}
The data \((Y,\nu;\mathcal{K}_s,J_Y)\) is said to constitute a \textbf{horizon zero-knowledge operator model} if it satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Zero-knowledge closure)} There exists a horizon factor \(\pi:\Omega\to Y\) such that the protocol is perfect zero knowledge relative to \(\pi\) (Definition \ref{def:xi-horizon-perfect-zero-knowledge}), and this induces a closed kernel operator family \(\mathcal{K}_s\) on \(Y\) (Definition \ref{def:xi-horizon-transcript-transfer-operator}).
  \item \textbf{(Trace class analyticity)} \(\mathcal{K}_s\) is trace class in some open domain and analytic in \(s\), so that \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)=\det(I-\mathcal{K}_s)\) is well-defined.
  \item \textbf{(Detailed balance duality)} \(\mathcal{K}_s\) satisfies \eqref{eq:xi-hzom-detailed-balance}, so that \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}\) possesses functional equation-type reflection symmetry (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hzom-functional-equation}).
  \item \textbf{(Arithmetic consistency)} There exists an everywhere nonzero entire function \(E(s)\) such that
  \[
  \Xi_{\zeta}(s)=E(s)\,D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)
  \]
  (or equivalently, both belong to the same uniquely determined analytic equivalence class), so that \(\Xi_{\zeta}\) and \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}\) share the same zero set.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Abstract alignment with cryptographic zero knowledge]\label{rem:xi-hzom-crypto-alignment}
The requirement of Definition \ref{def:xi-horizon-perfect-zero-knowledge} that ``the simulator depends only on the visible factor \(y\)'' is isomorphic to the classical requirement in cryptographic zero-knowledge arguments: the simulator can only access the public statement and not the witness (fiber-internal information), so the transcript does not leak the witness in the information-theoretic sense.
Under this correspondence, the limit efficiency and lower bound results for certificate length/verification complexity (e.g., size bounds for pairing-based preprocessing SNARK/SNARG) can be viewed as external references for ``the compilability budget of the horizon visible interface'' \cite{Groth2016SizePairingNIZK};
this is structurally compatible with the characterization in this paper of ``the irreplaceable cost of verifiable upgrading'' in terms of \(\kappa\)/resolution/orthogonal axis budgets.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[HZOM verifiable contraction template: spectral radius contraction \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} zeros forced to criticalize]\label{thm:xi-hzom-rh-reduction}
Under the setting of Definition \ref{def:xi-hzom}, if for any \(\epsilon>0\) one can prove
\[
\Re(s)\ge \tfrac12+\epsilon\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \rho(\mathcal{K}_s)<1,
\]
then all zeros of \(\Xi_{\zeta}\) satisfy \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By spectral contractivity, \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)\neq 0\) for \(\Re(s)\ge \tfrac12+\epsilon\);
by the reflection symmetry of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hzom-functional-equation}, \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}(s)\neq 0\) for \(\Re(s)\le \tfrac12-\epsilon\).
Since \(\epsilon>0\) is arbitrary, the zeros of \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}\) can only lie on \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
By arithmetic consistency (condition (4) of Definition \ref{def:xi-hzom}), \(\Xi_{\zeta}\) and \(D_{\mathrm{prot}}\) share the same zero set, so the conclusion holds. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Off-critical-line zeros and exponential-scale distinguishers: analytic evidence of zero-knowledge breakdown}
In the ``horizon operator--determinant'' aperture, off-critical-line zeros are not merely spectral anomalies but also produce exponential amplification modes in auditable statistics through poles/residues.
This mechanism has strict correspondences in the multipole moment temperedness dichotomy (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-horizon-multipole-temperedness-dichotomy}) and the discrete Abel--Weil expansion (Theorem \ref{thm:discrete-abel-weil-expansion}) of this paper: once a zero with \(\Re(\rho)>\tfrac12\) appears, it induces a pole inside the unit disk of the corresponding generating function, thereby forcing the tail coefficients to grow exponentially.

\begin{proposition}[Exponential-scale distinguishability theorem for off-critical-line zeros: inevitable breakdown of zero knowledge at exponential scale]\label{prop:xi-hzom-offcritical-zero-distinguisher}
Let \(D(s)=\det(I-\mathcal{K}_s)\) be analytic in some domain and satisfy the trace class expansion of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-horizon-protocol-zeta-loop-expansion}.
Suppose there exists a Laplace/Mellin readout interface aligned with transcript length statistics, such that some real statistic \(A(T)\) (e.g., a truncated linear functional of the closed-loop count) is given by the Mellin--Perron inversion of \(\frac{D'(s)}{D(s)}\), and the integration contour can be shifted leftward to pick up zero residue terms (this assumption is isomorphic to the explicit formula-type derivation for \(\zeta/\Xi\); see \cite{Connes1999TraceFormulaNCG} as well as the discrete Abel--Weil version in \S\ref{subsec:discrete-abel-weil-polar-rigidity} of this paper).
\par
If \(D\) has a zero at \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) with \(\beta>\tfrac12\) (multiplicity \(m(\rho)\ge 1\)), then there exists a family of bounded test functions \(\{\Phi_T\}_{T\to\infty}\) (which can be taken as truncated inversion kernels centered at \(\rho\)) and a constant \(c>0\) such that for any simulation distribution \(Q_T\) depending only on horizon data (i.e., satisfying visible closure in the threshold zero-knowledge sense), the lower bound holds:
\[
\left|\mathbb{E}_{P_T}[\Phi_T]-\mathbb{E}_{Q_T}[\Phi_T]\right|
\ \ge\
c\,T^{m(\rho)-1}\,e^{(\beta-\frac12)T}.
\]
Therefore, in such models, if exponential-scale zero-knowledge indistinguishability is required for all test families of growing scale \(T\), the zeros must be forced to concentrate in the neighborhood of the critical slice; with reflection symmetry (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hzom-functional-equation}), this condition further aligns with ``all zeros lie on \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).''
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Quadratic-test zero knowledge and Weil positivity: complete positivity as distinguisher closure}
In practical protocols and spectral models, a natural subclass of distinguishers consists of quadratic statistics: they only read the second-order energy of ``linear readout quantities.''
In the horizon positivity language of this paper, such quadratic tests correspond to complete positivity of a Hermitian form (or operator-valued form), thereby translating ``zero-knowledge indistinguishability'' into ``positivity certificate feasibility.''

\begin{definition}[Quadratic-test distinguishers and correlation form]\label{def:xi-hzom-quadratic-distinguishers}
Let \(M_{\tau}\) be the random measure induced by the transcript \(\tau\) (e.g., the closed-loop counting measure/random distribution reduced to the length variable), and take a test function space \(\mathcal{F}\) (e.g., compactly supported or Schwartz class, chosen according to the specific inversion interface).
For \(f\in\mathcal{F}\), define the quadratic test statistic
\[
\mathcal{D}_{f}(\tau):=\left|\int f\,\dd M_{\tau}\right|^{2}.
\]
Define the Hermitian correlation form
\[
Q(f,g):=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left(\int f\,\dd M_{\tau}\right)\overline{\left(\int g\,\dd M_{\tau}\right)}\right],
\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{F},
\]
where the expectation is taken under the transcript distribution conditioned on the horizon (or given \(y\)).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Complete positivity characterization of quadratic-test zero knowledge: distinguisher closure \texorpdfstring{$\Leftrightarrow$}{<->} Gram representation]\label{prop:xi-hzom-weil-positivity-cp}
Under the setting of Definition \ref{def:xi-hzom-quadratic-distinguishers}, the following statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For any finite collection \(\{f_j\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{F}\), the matrix \(\bigl[Q(f_i,f_j)\bigr]_{i,j=1}^{N}\) is positive semidefinite;
  \item For any choice of coefficients \(\{z_j\}_{j=1}^{N}\subset\CC\),
  \[
  \sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\overline{z_i}z_j\,Q(f_i,f_j)\ \ge\ 0;
  \]
  \item There exists a Hilbert space-valued linear map \(f\mapsto \mathsf{X}(f)\) (in the second-order sense) such that \(Q(f,g)=\langle \mathsf{X}(f),\mathsf{X}(g)\rangle\).
\end{enumerate}
Therefore, ``zero-knowledge indistinguishability at the quadratic test level'' can be compressed into a complete positivity certificate for \(Q\).
When \(D\) and the completed object belong to the same analytic equivalence class as Connes' trace formula/Weil positivity framework, this complete positivity further pushes to a critical constraint on zero distribution (see the spectral geometry route of \cite{Connes1999TraceFormulaNCG}), and docks isomorphically with the Toeplitz--PSD/complete positivity interface (Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}, \S\ref{subsubsec:xi-operator-valued-carath-toeplitz-complete-positivity}) of this paper.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For any \(\{z_j\}\),
\[
\sum_{i,j}\overline{z_i}z_j\,Q(f_i,f_j)
=
\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left|\int \Bigl(\sum_j z_j f_j\Bigr)\dd M_{\tau}\right|^{2}\right]\ge 0,
\]
so (1)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(2).
And (2) is equivalent to the existence of a Gram representation \(Q(f,g)=\langle \mathsf{X}(f),\mathsf{X}(g)\rangle\) (taking \(\mathsf{X}(f)\) as the equivalence class of the \(L^2\)-random variable \(\int f\,\dd M_{\tau}\)) yielding (3). \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{de Bruijn--Newman heat flow as ``simulation noise threshold''}
In the classical \(\Xi\) context, the de Bruijn--Newman heat flow organizes zero reality/critical stability into a phase transition constant varying with noise intensity;
in the protocol aperture of this paper, this constant can be interpreted as the ``minimum noise threshold for zero-knowledge stability'': when the noise is strong enough to eliminate amplifiable distinguishing modes, the zero structure enters the critical stable phase.

\begin{definition}[Zero-knowledge threshold interpretation of the de Bruijn--Newman constant: the critical stable phase at noise intensity is the ZK stability domain]\label{def:xi-hzom-zk-newman-constant}
Apply Gaussian blurring of intensity \(t\ge 0\) to the protocol length variable (or its logarithmic scale), and denote the resulting deformed determinant by \(D_t(s)\).
If there exists \(t_0\in\RR\) such that when \(t\ge t_0\), all zeros of \(D_t\) are in the critical stable phase (e.g., all lie on \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) or an equivalent slice), then define
\[
\Lambda_{\mathrm{ZK}}:=\inf\{t\in\RR:\ D_t\ \text{is in the critical stable phase}\}\in[-\infty,\infty].
\]
When \(D_t\) and the classical \(\Xi\) de Bruijn--Newman deformation \(H_t\) belong to the same analytic equivalence class, \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ZK}}\) coincides with the de Bruijn--Newman constant \(\Lambda\); and \(\Lambda\ge 0\) (\cite{RodgersTao2018NewmanNonnegative}; see also the effective approximation and upper bound of \cite{Polymath2019NewmanUpper}) can be interpreted as: the system at the zero-noise limit is at (or exactly at the boundary of) the zero-knowledge stability threshold.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Newman threshold of finite kernels and noise phase transition point]\label{rem:xi-hzom-kernel-newman-threshold}
In the finite kernel/finite matrix aperture, analogous ``noise thresholds'' already appear as kernel-version Newman thresholds: in sync kernel weighted examples, the phase transition point is exactly given by \(\beta_{\star}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:sync-kernel-weighted-newman-threshold}; see also Definition \ref{def:kernel-newman-threshold}).
This phase transition can be interpreted as: when the potential parameter (or equivalently noise intensity) crosses the threshold, the Ramanujan/RH clause transitions from strictly holding to failing, thereby producing a visible non-critical amplification channel.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Graph horizon discretization: Ihara--ZK duality and Ramanujan soundness exponent}
When \(Y\) is a finite set discretized by a finite graph (or finite Markov chain), the spectral structure of the transfer operator \(\mathcal{K}_s\) is strictly coupled with the pole geometry of the Ihara \(\zeta\) (\S\ref{subsec:operator-zeta-interface}; see also the Ramanujan--toy-RH dictionary in \S\ref{subsubsec:xi-horizon-ramanujan-feasible-reduction}).
In this discrete model, the ``soundness amplification rate'' can be directly aligned with the ``spectral gap/second largest eigenvalue modulus.''

\begin{proposition}[Ihara--ZK duality: spectral gap gives the optimal soundness exponent, Ramanujan achieves square-root optimality]\label{prop:xi-ihara-zk-soundness-exponent}
Let \(G\) be a finite \(d\)-regular graph, \(A\) its adjacency matrix, \(P:=A/d\) the simple random walk transition matrix.
Consider a class of iterative verification protocols with random walk mixing and local consistency spot-checking as the core: let \(n\) be the number of checking rounds, and suppose the forgery pass rate is controlled by the second largest eigenvalue modulus as
\(
\mathrm{Pr}[\text{forge pass}]\lesssim \|P^{n}-\Pi\|
\)
(\(\Pi\) is the stationary projection).
Then there exists a constant \(c>0\) such that
\[
\mathrm{Pr}[\text{forge pass}]\ \le\ c\,|\lambda_2(P)|^{n}
\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
\text{soundness exponent}\ \gtrsim\ \log\!\Bigl(\frac{1}{|\lambda_2(P)|}\Bigr)=\log\!\Bigl(\frac{d}{|\lambda_2(A)|}\Bigr).
\]
Therefore, when \(G\) is a Ramanujan graph (\(|\lambda_2(A)|\le 2\sqrt{d-1}\), equivalent to the Ihara--RH circle band constraint), this exponent achieves the square-root optimal bound;
deviations correspond to observable amplification channels of forgeable advantage.
Moreover, for the reciprocity relation between the partition function of the fermionic model on the graph and the graph \(\zeta\), as well as the counting interpretation of coefficients for fermionic closed loops, see \cite{MatsuuraOhta2025FermionsZetaGraph}.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Two-channel complementarity and critical line modulus-square decomposition: common origin of reality and zero-knowledge complementarity}
Critical line reality (Hardy real-valuedness) can in this paper be directly derived from completion and the unitary slice (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-unitary-slice-spectral-crossing} and Remark \ref{rem:xi-unitary-slice-operator-audit}).
If one further introduces a zero-knowledge structure with two complementary channels, then this reality can be strengthened to a modulus-square decomposition: the determinant value on the critical line is necessarily a nonnegative real number.

\begin{proposition}[Critical line modulus-square decomposition from two-channel complementary zero knowledge: critical line reality is strengthened to nonnegativity]\label{prop:xi-hzom-two-channel-modsquare}
Suppose the witness decomposes as \(\omega=(\omega_{+},\omega_{-})\), and there exist two non-communicating proof channels generating \(\tau_{+},\tau_{-}\) such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Each channel is perfect zero knowledge relative to its visible factor \(y_{\pm}\);
  \item Conditional independence: \((\omega_{+},\tau_{+})\perp(\omega_{-},\tau_{-})\mid y\);
  \item Horizon duality symmetry gives a conjugate pairing of factor determinants
  \(
  D_{-}(s)=\overline{D_{+}(1-\overline{s})}.
  \)
\end{enumerate}
Then the full determinant decomposes as \(D(s)=D_{+}(s)D_{-}(s)\), and on the critical slice satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;
D\!\left(\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}t\right)=\left|D_{+}\!\left(\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}t\right)\right|^{2}\in\RR_{\ge 0}\; }.
\]
Therefore, ``critical line reality'' can be viewed as forced by the ``conjugate pairing of complementary zero-knowledge channels''; at the same time, whether zeros leave the critical slice is reduced to the zero control problem for the one-sided factor \(D_{+}\) in the right half-plane.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
From conditional independence and determinant multiplicativity, \(D=D_{+}D_{-}\).
Taking \(s=\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}t\) gives \(1-\overline{s}=s\), so
\(
D_{-}(s)=\overline{D_{+}(s)}
\).
Hence \(D(s)=D_{+}(s)\overline{D_{+}(s)}=|D_{+}(s)|^{2}\ge 0\). \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Integrable ``impurity scattering on a ring'' template: self-adjoint extension parameters and simulator degrees of freedom}
In integrable scattering models, the Bethe equations for impurity scattering on a ring can produce a family of unitary transfer matrices whose quantized energy levels can be aligned with the imaginary parts of critical zeros, thereby providing explicit examples of ``critical line \(=\) unitary slice spectrum.''
More importantly, the phase parameters introduced by self-adjoint extensions/boundary conditions change the transcript distribution without altering the verifiable language (completeness constraints), and hence can be interpreted as the simulator's degrees of freedom at the horizon level.

\begin{remark}[Scattering realization and simulator parametrization]\label{rem:xi-hzom-impurity-scattering-simulator-freedom}
LeClair--Mussardo constructed an integrable ``impurity scattering on a ring'' model such that the Bethe Ansatz energy levels correspond to the imaginary parts of the critical zeros of \(\zeta\), and interpreted GRH as the completeness of the Bethe equations \cite{LeClairMussardo2024RiemannZerosImpurities}.
In the aperture of this paper, the corresponding family of unitary transfer matrices can be viewed as verification kernels \(U(t)\) on the unitary slice (aligned with Corollary \ref{cor:xi-hzom-unitary-slice}), while the phase degrees of freedom introduced by boundary conditions/self-adjoint extension parameters correspond to: the simulator parameter family that changes the transcript distribution while preserving the ``verifiable predicate/completion constraint.''
This observation is compatible with the ``boundary unitarization \(\Rightarrow\) inner factor carries defects'' picture in the scattering/Blaschke/horizon readout of this paper (\S\ref{subsubsec:selfdual-blaschke-defect-fixed-slice}), and provides an operationalizable realization template for the spectral picture ``non-critical zeros \(=\) resonance leakage modes.''
\end{remark}
