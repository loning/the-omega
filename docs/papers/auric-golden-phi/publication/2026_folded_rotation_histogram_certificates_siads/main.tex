\title{Folded Histograms of Irrational Rotations: Finite-Sample Control and a Low-Complexity Barrier to the Parry Measure}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Finite windows of an irrational rotation generate binary words, and the
canonical fold studied here sends these words to the golden-mean shift by
Fibonacci weighting followed by Zeckendorf normalization. We use this explicit
model to study a broader question: how should a low-complexity dynamical source
be compared with an ambient maximal-entropy equilibrium measure after a
deterministic coarse-graining? The paper has two aims. The first is
finite-sample control: star discrepancy bounds the empirical microstate law and
its folded pushforward in total variation, with a universal \(2m\) coefficient
and the sharp \(m+1\) coefficient in the canonical Sturmian slice
\(\beta=\alpha\); for metallic means \(\alpha_A=[0;A,A,\dots]\), the golden
branch \(A=1\) uniquely minimizes the leading discrepancy constant. The second
aim is structural comparison with the Parry measure. In the canonical Sturmian
slice the fold is transparent on the realized language, so the Parry mismatch is
not created by folding: it comes from comparing an \(m+1\)-state Sturmian law
with the \(F_{m+2}\)-state maximal-entropy equilibrium on the ambient
golden-mean shift. The endpoint collapse of Parry cylinders then yields an exact
formula for \(\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)\) for every law \(\nu\) on \(X_m\), explicit
support-constrained minima for total variation and relative entropy, and an
exact complexity-divergence law. Consequently, any family on \(X_m\) with
support exponent \(\alpha\) stays at asymptotic Parry cost at least
\(h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-\alpha\); in particular every subexponential family,
including folded Sturmian histograms, remains at extensive distance
\(\log\varphi\) per symbol from \(\pi_m\). For bounded-type slopes this sharpens
to \(\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)=m\log\varphi-\log m+O_\alpha(1)\). At the
microstate level, the canonical fold also yields an exact information-projection
decomposition into a hidden fiber residual and an observable macro divergence.
Numerical experiments corroborate the deterministic bounds and show directly
that comparison with the Parry baseline deteriorates with \(m\) even when the
finite-sample discrepancy certificate is already tiny. Thus the paper uses one
fully explicit rotation model to exhibit a broader equilibrium-barrier
principle, with the canonical fold contributing exact relabeling on realized
support and a computable off-support degeneracy ledger.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Finite words cut from irrational rotations lie at the intersection of symbolic
dynamics, discrepancy theory, and ergodic sampling. They also provide a clean
test case for a broader question: after a deterministic coarse-graining, how
should one compare a low-complexity dynamical source with the ambient
maximal-entropy equilibrium of the symbolic state space in which it now lives?
This paper studies that question in one explicit model. Starting from a
length-$m$ binary rotation word, we apply a canonical fold map defined by
Fibonacci weighting followed by Zeckendorf normalization, thereby producing an
admissible word in the golden-mean shift.

There are two distinct problems. The first is a finite-sample one: given \(N\)
windows along a single orbit, how close is the empirical folded histogram to
its deterministic limit law? The second is a reference-measure problem: how
should that folded law be compared with the Parry measure, the natural
maximal-entropy equilibrium on the golden-mean shift? In the canonical
Sturmian slice \(\beta=\alpha\), the folded law is supported on only \(m+1\)
words while the ambient shift contains \(F_{m+2}\) admissible words, so a
large deviation from the Parry baseline need not be a sampling artifact.

The main message is that, in this canonical slice, the Parry mismatch is not
created by folding. On the realized Sturmian language, the fold is transparent:
it acts only as an arithmetic relabeling, without merging distinct realized
factors. The extensive gap to the Parry equilibrium comes instead from low
Sturmian complexity against an ambient maximal-entropy reference. Thus the
paper separates two effects that are often conflated in numerical practice:
finite-sample fluctuation, which discrepancy can control, and structural
mismatch, which persists even as the sampling certificate becomes tiny.

The first contribution is deterministic finite-sample control. Star discrepancy
bounds the empirical microstate law and its folded pushforward in total
variation, and the Sturmian slice sharpens the universal \(2m\) coefficient to
\(m+1\). A smallest-cylinder estimate and data processing then yield explicit
relative-entropy certificates. Within the metallic-mean family
\(\alpha_A=[0;A,A,\dots]\), the golden branch \(A=1\) uniquely minimizes the
leading discrepancy constant.

The second contribution is a sharp comparison with the Parry baseline. Because
Parry cylinder weights on the golden-mean language collapse to the endpoints of
a word, one obtains an exact formula for \(\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)\) for every law
\(\nu\) on \(X_m\). This turns the Parry comparison into an exact support
optimization problem and yields an explicit complexity-divergence law. In the
form most relevant to the present paper, if \((\nu_m)\) is any sequence of laws
on \(X_m\) with effective support exponent at most \(\alpha\), then
\[
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\KL(\nu_m\|\pi_m)
\ge
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-\alpha.
\]
Thus every subexponential family on \(X_m\), folded or not, stays at positive
entropy density from the Parry equilibrium. The folded Sturmian laws realize
the extremal case \(\alpha=0\), so their extensive \(\log\varphi\) penalty is a
structural consequence of low complexity rather than a defect of the canonical
normalization rule.

The third contribution is model-specific but conceptually useful. The canonical
fold localizes its incompatibility with prefix truncation at a single explicit
boundary event, explaining why cross-resolution residuals can remain visible
even when orbit discrepancy is already small. On realized Sturmian support, the
fold preserves all label-invariant entropies and divergences because it is a
support bijection. At the microstate level, it also induces a natural
fiber-uniform model family, and relative entropy splits exactly into a hidden
within-fiber residual and the observable divergence of the folded law. This
identifies what information is lost by passing from microstates to folded
histograms without altering the Parry-comparison conclusions.

The ingredients entering these statements are classical in isolation. Sturmian
codings, factor complexity, and factor frequencies go back to
\cite{MorseHedlund1940,Slater1950DistributionIntegers,Slater1967GapsSteps} and
fit naturally into the substitution and symbolic-dynamical viewpoints
\cite{LindMarcus1995,QueffelecSubstitution,Fogg2002}. The Parry measure and the
thermodynamic formalism of shifts of finite type are standard
\cite{LindMarcus1995,WaltersErgodicTheory,ParryPollicott1990Zeta}. Fibonacci
numeration and its finite-state normalization mechanisms are likewise classical
\cite{Zeckendorf1972,Frougny1991FibonacciFiniteAutomata,Frougny1992NumbersAutomata},
with more recent work on dynamical properties of Zeckendorf-type codings in
\cite{ManiboMiroRustTadeo2020ZeckendorfMixing}. What seems specific here is the
way these structures lock together: endpoint collapse converts the Parry
comparison into an exact information-theoretic identity, and the low-complexity
support of the folded rotation law then forces a sharp equilibrium barrier.

We deliberately focus on one explicit model rather than a general theory of
fold maps. The gain is that every term in the barrier can be computed sharply,
and the numerical section can compare the finite-sample certificate with the
direct Parry mismatch on the same data. The limitation is equally clear: the
exact KL identity depends on the endpoint collapse of the golden-mean Parry
cylinders, and we do not claim an equally sharp closed formula for arbitrary
shifts of finite type or non-canonical folds. Background on discrepancy and
quantitative Kronecker sampling may be found in
\cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974,Goda2024QuasiUniformKronecker}; for empirical
divergence bounds we use
\cite{WeissmanEtAl2003L1Empirical,SasonVerdu2016fDivergence,
DemboZeitouni2010LargeDeviations}. The remaining sections develop the model,
the deterministic estimates, the Parry comparison, and the numerical
illustrations in this order.

\section{Model and Target Measures}

\subsection{Rotations, Windows, and the Folded Observable}

Let $\alpha\in(0,1)$ be irrational, let $\beta\in(0,1)$, and let
$x_0\in\TT:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$.  Define the Kronecker orbit
\[
x_t:=x_0+t\alpha \pmod 1,\qquad t\ge 0,
\]
and the binary readout
\[
s_t:=\mathbf{1}_{[0,\beta)}(x_t)\in\{0,1\}.
\]
For a fixed resolution $m\ge 1$, let
\[
\Omega_m:=\{0,1\}^m,\qquad
X_m:=\{u=(u_1,\dots,u_m)\in\{0,1\}^m:\ u_i u_{i+1}=0\text{ for }1\le i<m\}.
\]

\begin{definition}[Canonical fold]\label{def:canonical-fold}
Let \(F_1=F_2=1\) and \(F_{k+2}=F_{k+1}+F_k\). For
\(\omega=\omega_1\cdots\omega_m\in\Omega_m\), define its Fibonacci value by
\[
N(\omega):=\sum_{k=1}^m \omega_k F_{k+1}.
\]
By Zeckendorf's theorem \cite{Zeckendorf1972}, there is a unique sequence
\(Z(N(\omega))=(c_1,c_2,\dots)\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\) such that
\[
N(\omega)=\sum_{k\ge 1} c_k F_{k+1},
\qquad
c_kc_{k+1}=0\ \text{ for all }k\ge 1.
\]
Let
\[
\pref_m(c_1,c_2,\dots):=(c_1,\dots,c_m)\in X_m.
\]
The canonical fold at resolution \(m\) is
\[
\Fold_m(\omega):=\pref_m(Z(N(\omega)))\in X_m.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Basic properties of the canonical fold]\label{prop:canonical-fold-basic}
For every \(m\ge 1\), the map \(\Fold_m:\Omega_m\to X_m\) is well defined,
surjective, and satisfies \(\Fold_m|_{X_m}=\mathrm{id}\). Moreover,
\(\Fold_m\) admits an order-independent local implementation by
value-preserving Zeckendorf rewrites.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Well-definedness is immediate from Definition~\ref{def:canonical-fold}. If
\(\omega\in X_m\), then \(\omega\) is already a legal Zeckendorf prefix, so the
uniqueness of the Zeckendorf expansion gives \(\Fold_m(\omega)=\omega\). Hence
\(\Fold_m\) fixes \(X_m\) pointwise, and surjectivity follows by taking
\(\omega=x\in X_m\) for any \(x\in X_m\). The order-independent local
implementation is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:canonical-fold}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Residue characterization of the canonical fold]\label{prop:fold-residue-characterization}
For every \(m\ge 1\) and \(\omega\in\Omega_m\), write
\[
N(\omega)=qF_{m+2}+r,
\qquad q\in\{0,1\},
\qquad 0\le r<F_{m+2}.
\]
Then
\[
\Fold_m(\omega)=\pref_m(Z(r)).
\]
Equivalently, for \(x\in X_m\),
\[
\Fold_m(\omega)=x
\iff
N(\omega)\equiv N(x)\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since
\[
N(\omega)\le \sum_{k=1}^m F_{k+1}=F_{m+3}-2<F_{m+2}+F_{m+2},
\]
the quotient \(q\) is either \(0\) or \(1\). If \(q=0\), then \(r=N(\omega)\)
and the claim is immediate.

Assume \(q=1\). Then
\[
0\le r=N(\omega)-F_{m+2}\le F_{m+1}-2<F_{m+1},
\]
so the Zeckendorf expansion \(Z(r)\) has no nonzero digit in position \(m\) or
beyond. Hence appending a \(1\) in position \(m+1\) produces another legal
Zeckendorf digit string, namely the expansion of \(N(\omega)=F_{m+2}+r\). Its
first \(m\) digits are therefore exactly those of \(Z(r)\), which proves the
first statement.

If \(x\in X_m\), then
\[
N(x)\le \sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor (m-1)/2\rfloor}F_{m-2j+1}<F_{m+2},
\]
so \(x=\pref_m(Z(N(x)))\). Applying the first statement with
\(r\equiv N(\omega)\pmod{F_{m+2}}\) yields
\[
\Fold_m(\omega)=x
\iff
\pref_m(Z(r))=\pref_m(Z(N(x)))
\iff
r=N(x),
\]
which is equivalent to the congruence condition.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Microstates and folded histograms]
For $t\ge 0$, the length-$m$ microstate is
\[
\omega_m(t):=s_t s_{t+1}\cdots s_{t+m-1}\in\Omega_m.
\]
The folded output is
\[
y_m(t):=\Fold_m(\omega_m(t))\in X_m.
\]
For a sample length $N$, define the empirical microstate law and folded
histogram by
\[
\hat\mu_{m,N}(u):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{\omega_m(t)=u\}},
\qquad u\in\Omega_m,
\]
\[
\hat\pi_{m,N}(v):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{y_m(t)=v\}},
\qquad v\in X_m.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Scope]\label{rem:scope-fold-assumptions}
We fix the canonical fold throughout the paper. Sections~3 and~4 use only that
\(\Fold_m\) is deterministic. The specific Zeckendorf structure enters in the
cross-resolution boundary law of
Theorem~\ref{prop:multiscale-residual-by-boundary}, in the numerical examples,
and in the appendix implementation. The sharp \(m+1\) complexity statements in
Section~5 use the Sturmian slice \(\beta=\alpha\), while
Theorem~\ref{thm:parry-kl-identity} is a statement about the Parry reference
family on \(X_m\) and is independent of the fold once a law on \(X_m\) has
been specified. This separation is deliberate: Sections~3 and~4 isolate the
finite-sample and coarse-graining interfaces of the model, whereas Section~5 is
the dynamical core in which the ambient equilibrium structure of the
golden-mean shift is combined with the low-complexity support created by the
folded rotation coding.
The paper does not compare alternative Zeckendorf-based folds: the residue
characterization of Proposition~\ref{prop:fold-residue-characterization} and
the rigidity theorem on realized Sturmian support are specific to the canonical
normalization, and no analogous transparency statement is claimed here for
non-canonical procedures.
\end{remark}

Because irrational rotations are uniquely ergodic, the empirical microstate
law converges to a deterministic target law induced by Lebesgue measure.

\begin{definition}[Target laws]
Let $\mu_m$ be the length-$m$ microstate distribution induced by Lebesgue
measure on $\TT$:
\[
\mu_m(u):=\leb\{x\in\TT:\omega_m(x;0)=u\},\qquad u\in\Omega_m.
\]
Its folded pushforward is
\[
\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}:=(\Fold_m)_*\mu_m\in\Delta(X_m).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Spaces, maps, and laws]\label{rem:model-spaces-maps}
The basic objects of the paper may be summarized by
\[
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Omega_m & \xrightarrow{\Fold_m} & X_m\\[0.3em]
\mu_m \downarrow\phantom{(\Fold_m)_*} &  & \phantom{(\Fold_m)_*}\downarrow \pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\\[0.3em]
\hat\mu_{m,N} & \xrightarrow{(\Fold_m)_*} & \hat\pi_{m,N}
\end{array}
\qquad
\begin{array}{ccc}
\Omega_{m+1} & \xrightarrow{\Fold_{m+1}} & X_{m+1}\\[0.3em]
\pref_m \downarrow &  & \downarrow \rho_{m+1\to m}\\[0.3em]
\Omega_m & \xrightarrow{\Fold_m} & X_m.
\end{array}
\]
The left diagram separates microstate space \(\Omega_m\) from folded space
\(X_m\). The right diagram records the truncation interface whose failure to
commute is measured in Section~3.2 by the projective residual.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Parry Measure}

As a reference measure on \(X_m\) we use the Parry measure of the golden-mean
shift. Let
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\varphi=\frac{1+\sqrt5}{2}.
\]
The associated two-state Parry chain has transition matrix
\[
P=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi^{-1} & \varphi^{-2}\\ 1&0\end{pmatrix},
\]
and stationary distribution
\[
\pi(1)=\frac{1}{\varphi^2+1},\qquad
\pi(0)=1-\pi(1).
\]
For $u=u_1\cdots u_m\in X_m$, define the length-$m$ Parry cylinder law
\[
\pi_m(u):=\pi(u_1)\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}P_{u_j u_{j+1}}.
\]
We emphasize that \(\pi_m\) is used as a reference measure only. We do not
assume that \(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}=\pi_m\) for irrational rotations.
Since \(|X_m|=F_{m+2}\), the ambient shift has topological entropy
\[
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac1m\log |X_m|=\log\varphi,
\]
and the Parry measure is its unique measure of maximal entropy
\cite{LindMarcus1995,WaltersErgodicTheory}.

\begin{proposition}[Endpoint collapse of Parry cylinders]\label{prop:parry-endpoint-collapse}
For every legal word \(u=u_1\cdots u_m\in X_m\),
\[
\pi_m(u)
=
\pi(0)\varphi^{-m+\mathbf{1}_{\{u_1=0\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u_m=1\}}}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\pi_m(u)=
\begin{cases}
\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)}, & (u_1,u_m)=(0,0),\\
\pi(0)\varphi^{-m}, & (u_1,u_m)\in\{(0,1),(1,0)\},\\
\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m+1)}, & (u_1,u_m)=(1,1).
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, the Parry cylinder weight depends only on the endpoints of the
word.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(r_0:=\varphi\) and \(r_1:=1\). For every legal transition
\((i,j)\in\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0)\}\), one has
\[
P_{ij}=\frac{r_j}{\varphi r_i}.
\]
Hence for a legal word \(u=u_1\cdots u_m\),
\[
\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}P_{u_j u_{j+1}}
=
\varphi^{-(m-1)}\frac{r_{u_m}}{r_{u_1}}.
\]
Multiplying by \(\pi(u_1)\) and using \(\pi(1)=\pi(0)\varphi^{-2}\) gives the
three cases above.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Discrepancy of the Rotation Orbit}

Our only quantitative input is the one-dimensional star discrepancy of the
rotation orbit:
\[
D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)
:=
\sup_{0\le a\le 1}
\left|
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_{[0,a)}(x_t)-a
\right|.
\]
This quantity is effective because length-$m$ rotation words are determined by a
finite partition of the circle into interval atoms.

\begin{theorem}[Bounds for Sturmian cylinder lengths]\label{thm:spg-sturmian-cylinder-measure-continued-fraction-sandwich}
Let $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\dots]$ and let $p_n/q_n$ be its convergents.  If
$q_n\le t<q_{n+1}$, then every length-$t$ Sturmian cylinder $C(w)$ satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{q_{n+1}+q_n}
\;<\;
\leb(C(w))
\;<\;
\frac{1}{q_n}+\frac{1}{q_{n+1}}
\;<\;
\frac{2}{q_n}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\log\!\Bigl(\frac{q_n}{2}\Bigr)
\;<\;
-\log\leb(C(w))
\;<\;
\log(q_{n+1}+q_n).
\]
If $\alpha$ has bounded partial quotients, then
$-\log\leb(C(w))=\log t+O(1)$ uniformly in $w$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The endpoints of length-$t$ cylinders are among the orbit points
$\{-k\alpha:0\le k\le t\}$, so cylinder lengths are the adjacent gaps in this
finite rotation set.  Standard continued-fraction gap estimates and the
three-gap theorem give
\[
\min_{1\le k\le t}\|k\alpha\|
=
\|q_n\alpha\|
\in
\left(\frac{1}{q_{n+1}+q_n},\frac{1}{q_{n+1}}\right),
\]
while the largest gap is at most
$\|q_{n-1}\alpha\|+\|q_n\alpha\|<q_n^{-1}+q_{n+1}^{-1}$; see
\cite{Slater1950DistributionIntegers,Slater1967GapsSteps}.  Taking negative
logarithms yields the second display.
\end{proof}

\section{Total Variation Bounds}

\subsection{Discrepancy Bounds}

The first step is to observe that discrepancy controls the empirical mass of
each relevant cylinder atom and hence the total variation distance of both the
microstate and folded laws.

\begin{theorem}[Discrepancy control of microstate and folded laws]\label{thm:tv-certificate-hist}
In the rotation model above, for every $m,N\ge 1$,
\[
\TV(\hat\mu_{m,N},\mu_m)\le 2m\,D_N^*(\alpha;x_0).
\]
Consequently,
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
\le
2m\,D_N^*(\alpha;x_0).
\]
If \(\beta=\alpha\), then the sharper bounds
\[
\TV(\hat\mu_{m,N},\mu_m)\le (m+1)D_N^*(\alpha;x_0),
\qquad
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})\le (m+1)D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)
\]
also hold.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For fixed $m$, the length-$m$ rotation words are constant on the interval atoms
of the partition of \(\TT\) cut out by the points
\[
0,\ \beta,\ -\alpha,\ \beta-\alpha,\ \dots,\ -(m-1)\alpha,\ \beta-(m-1)\alpha
\pmod 1.
\]
There are at most \(2m\) such endpoints and therefore at most \(2m\) interval
atoms. For any interval \(I=[a,b)\subset\TT\),
\[
\left|
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_I(x_t)-\leb(I)
\right|
\le
\left|
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_{[0,b)}(x_t)-b
\right|
{}+
\left|
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_{[0,a)}(x_t)-a
\right|
\le 2D_N^*(\alpha;x_0).
\]
Taking half the sum of the atomwise errors gives
\(\TV(\hat\mu_{m,N},\mu_m)\le 2mD_N^*(\alpha;x_0)\).

If \(\beta=\alpha\), the coding is Sturmian and the number of distinct
length-\(m\) factors is exactly \(m+1\)
\cite{MorseHedlund1940,LindMarcus1995}. Summing over the \(m+1\) nonempty
cylinder atoms yields the sharper constant. The folded estimates follow because
deterministic pushforward cannot increase total variation.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
Once \(D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)\) is controlled, either arithmetically or numerically,
the same estimate immediately bounds the folded histogram. No additional
probabilistic approximation is needed.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal leading constant within the metallic-mean family]
\label{cor:metallic-mean-golden-unique-optimal-tv-constant}
For $A\ge 1$, let
\[
\alpha_A=[0;A,A,\dots],\qquad
\lambda_A:=\frac{A+\sqrt{A^2+4}}{2},\qquad
\kappa(A):=\frac{A}{\log\lambda_A}.
\]
Then there is a constant $C(A)<\infty$ such that for every $N\ge 2$ and every
$x_0\in\TT$,
\[
D_N^*(\alpha_A;x_0)\le \frac{\kappa(A)\log N+C(A)}{N}.
\]
Consequently, for every fixed resolution $m\ge 1$,
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
\le
2m\frac{\kappa(A)\log N+C(A)}{N}.
\]
If \(\beta=\alpha_A\), this sharpens to
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
\le
(m+1)\frac{\kappa(A)\log N+C(A)}{N}.
\]
Moreover, $\kappa(A)$ is strictly increasing on $[1,\infty)$, so the golden
branch $A=1$ uniquely minimizes the leading $(\log N)/N$ coefficient among
metallic means.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $p_n/q_n$ be the convergents of $\alpha_A$.  The standard discrepancy bound
\cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974} gives
\[
D_N^*(\alpha_A;x_0)\le \frac{3+nA}{N}
\qquad \text{whenever } q_n\le N<q_{n+1}.
\]
Since $q_{n+1}=Aq_n+q_{n-1}$ and $\lambda_A$ is the Perron root of
$x^2-Ax-1=0$, one has $q_n\ge \lambda_A^{n-1}$ for $n\ge 1$, hence
$n\le 1+\log N/\log\lambda_A$.  Substituting yields the discrepancy estimate
with $C(A)=3+A$.  The TV bound follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:tv-certificate-hist}.  The monotonicity of
$\kappa(A)=A/\log\lambda_A$ on $[1,\infty)$ is elementary, so its minimum is
attained uniquely at $A=1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cross-Resolution Residuals}

Let $r_{m+1\to m}:\Omega_{m+1}\to\Omega_m$ denote prefix truncation,
$r_{m+1\to m}(u_1\cdots u_{m+1})=u_1\cdots u_m$, and let
$\rho_{m+1\to m}:X_{m+1}\to X_m$ be the analogous truncation on folded words.
This residual is not a second reference metric competing with the Parry
comparison. Rather, it isolates a specifically dynamical effect of the canonical
fold: finite-resolution aliasing across adjacent window lengths.
Define the projective residual
\[
E_{m,N}
:=
\TV\!\left(\hat\pi_{m,N},(\rho_{m+1\to m})_*\hat\pi_{m+1,N}\right).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Boundary law for the canonical fold]\label{prop:multiscale-residual-by-boundary}
For the canonical fold of Definition~\ref{def:canonical-fold}, let
\[
f_m(\omega):=\Fold_m(r_{m+1\to m}(\omega)),
\qquad
g_m(\omega):=\rho_{m+1\to m}(\Fold_{m+1}(\omega)),
\]
and define the boundary event set
\[
\mathcal{B}_{m+1}:=\{\omega\in\Omega_{m+1}: f_m(\omega)\neq g_m(\omega)\}.
\]
Then
\[
E_{m,N}\le \hat\mu_{m+1,N}(\mathcal{B}_{m+1}).
\]
Moreover, if \(\omega=u_1\cdots u_{m+1}\) and \(v:=u_1\cdots u_m\), then
\[
\omega\in\mathcal{B}_{m+1}
\iff
u_{m+1}=1\ \text{and}\ N(v)\ge F_{m+1}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both $\hat\pi_{m,N}$ and $(\rho_{m+1\to m})_*\hat\pi_{m+1,N}$ are pushforwards
of $\hat\mu_{m+1,N}$, respectively by $f_m$ and $g_m$.  Therefore
\[
\TV((f_m)_*\nu,(g_m)_*\nu)\le \nu(f_m\neq g_m)
\]
for any probability measure $\nu$, and the first claim follows with
$\nu=\hat\mu_{m+1,N}$.

For the second claim, write \(N:=N(v)\). Since
\[
0\le N\le \sum_{k=1}^m F_{k+1}=F_{m+3}-2,
\]
the Zeckendorf expansion of \(N\) has the form
\[
Z(N)=(c_1,\dots,c_{m+1},0,0,\dots).
\]
If \(u_{m+1}=0\), then \(N(\omega)=N\), so \(f_m(\omega)=g_m(\omega)\).

Assume next that \(u_{m+1}=1\), so \(N(\omega)=N+F_{m+2}\). If \(N<F_{m+1}\),
then \(c_m=c_{m+1}=0\), hence
\[
Z(N+F_{m+2})=(c_1,\dots,c_m,1,0,0,\dots)
\]
is already a legal Zeckendorf expansion. Therefore
\[
g_m(\omega)=\rho_{m+1\to m}(\Fold_{m+1}(\omega))=(c_1,\dots,c_m)=f_m(\omega),
\]
so \(\omega\notin\mathcal{B}_{m+1}\).

Conversely, suppose \(u_{m+1}=1\) and \(\omega\notin\mathcal{B}_{m+1}\). Write
\[
Z(N+F_{m+2})=(d_1,\dots,d_{m+2},0,0,\dots).
\]
Since the first \(m\) digits agree, one has \(d_j=c_j\) for \(1\le j\le m\).
Subtracting the two Zeckendorf expansions gives
\[
F_{m+2}
=
(d_{m+1}-c_{m+1})F_{m+2}+d_{m+2}F_{m+3}.
\]
Because \(F_{m+3}>F_{m+2}\), necessarily \(d_{m+2}=0\) and
\(d_{m+1}-c_{m+1}=1\). Hence \(c_{m+1}=0\) and \(d_{m+1}=1\). Since
\((d_1,\dots,d_{m+2})\) is a legal Zeckendorf digit string and \(d_m=c_m\), one
must also have \(c_m=d_m=0\). Thus \(Z(N)\) has no nonzero digit in positions
\(m\) or \(m+1\), which is equivalent to \(N<F_{m+1}\).

We have proved
\[
\omega\notin\mathcal{B}_{m+1}
\iff
u_{m+1}=0\ \text{or}\ \bigl(u_{m+1}=1\ \text{and}\ N(v)<F_{m+1}\bigr),
\]
which is exactly the stated characterization.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Residuals relative to the Parry measure]\label{prop:multiscale-residual-parry-bound}
Let $\pi_m$ be the Parry measure on $X_m$.  Since the Parry family is
projectively consistent,
\[
\pi_m=(\rho_{m+1\to m})_*\pi_{m+1},
\]
one has
\[
E_{m,N}
\le
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m)+\TV(\hat\pi_{m+1,N},\pi_{m+1}).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Apply the triangle inequality around the pair
$(\pi_m,(\rho_{m+1\to m})_*\pi_{m+1})$.
\end{proof}

\section{Relative-Entropy Bounds}

\subsection{From Total Variation to Relative Entropy}

To pass from total variation to relative entropy one needs a lower bound on the
smallest atom of the target distribution.

\begin{lemma}[Relative entropy from total variation under strict positivity]\label{lem:kl-from-tv-qmin}
Let $p,q$ be probability measures on a finite set $S$, assume $p\ll q$, and
suppose that $q$ is strictly positive on $S$. Let
\[
q_{\min}:=\min_{s\in S}q(s)>0.
\]
Then
\[
\KL(p\|q)
\le
\log\!\left(1+\frac{\|p-q\|_1^2}{q_{\min}}\right)
\le
\frac{1}{q_{\min}}\|p-q\|_1^2
=
\frac{4}{q_{\min}}\TV(p,q)^2.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The standard inequality \(\KL\le \log(1+\chi^2)\) gives
\[
\KL(p\|q)\le \log\bigl(1+\chi^2(p,q)\bigr),
\qquad
\chi^2(p,q):=\sum_{s\in S}\frac{(p(s)-q(s))^2}{q(s)}.
\]
Since \(q(s)\ge q_{\min}\) for every \(s\in S\),
\[
\chi^2(p,q)
\le
\frac{1}{q_{\min}}\sum_{s\in S}(p(s)-q(s))^2
\le
\frac{1}{q_{\min}}\|p-q\|_1^2.
\]
The second inequality uses \(\log(1+u)\le u\). See
\cite{SasonVerdu2016fDivergence}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Lower Bounds on the Smallest Rotation Atom}

\begin{definition}[Diophantine lower bound]
For a badly approximable irrational $\alpha$, let $c(\alpha)>0$ be a constant
such that
\[
\|k\alpha\|\ge \frac{c(\alpha)}{k}\qquad (k\ge 1),
\]
where $\|x\|:=\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|x-n|$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Lower bound on the smallest microstate atom]\label{prop:qmin-lowerbound-markov}
Assume $\beta=\alpha$ and let
\[
q_{\min}(m):=\min\{\mu_m(u):\mu_m(u)>0\}.
\]
Then
\[
q_{\min}(m)\ge \min_{1\le k\le m}\|k\alpha\|\ge \frac{c(\alpha)}{m}.
\]
For the golden slope $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ one may take
$c(\alpha)=1/\sqrt5$, hence
\[
q_{\min}(m)\ge \frac{1}{\sqrt5\,m}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
When $\beta=\alpha$, the partition endpoints for length-$m$ microstates reduce
to the set $\{-k\alpha:0\le k\le m\}$.  Thus the positive atoms of $\mu_m$ are
exactly the adjacent gaps determined by these points, and the smallest one is
at least $\min_{1\le k\le m}\|k\alpha\|$.  The badly approximable lower bound
then yields $c(\alpha)/m$.  For $\varphi^{-1}$ the optimal Markov constant is
$1/\sqrt5$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Microstate and Folded Relative-Entropy Bounds}

\begin{corollary}[Microstate relative-entropy bound]\label{cor:rotation-microstate-kl-certificate}
Assume $\beta=\alpha$ and $\alpha$ is badly approximable.  Then
\[
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|\mu_m)
\le
\frac{4}{q_{\min}(m)}\TV(\hat\mu_{m,N},\mu_m)^2
\le
\frac{4(m+1)^2}{q_{\min}(m)}D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)^2.
\]
For $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ this becomes
\[
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|\mu_m)
\le
4\sqrt5\,m(m+1)^2 D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let \(S_m:=\supp(\mu_m)\). In the Sturmian slice \(\beta=\alpha\), every
observed length-\(m\) word belongs to the Sturmian language, hence
\(\hat\mu_{m,N}\) and \(\mu_m\) may both be viewed as probability measures on
\(S_m\). On this set, \(\mu_m\) is strictly positive and
\[
\min_{u\in S_m}\mu_m(u)=q_{\min}(m).
\]
The claim therefore follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:tv-certificate-hist},
Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-from-tv-qmin}, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:qmin-lowerbound-markov}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[KL monotonicity under pushforward]\label{lem:kl-monotone-pushforward}
Let $F:S\to T$ be a map between finite sets and let $p,q$ be probability
measures on $S$ with $p\ll q$.  Then
\[
\KL(F_*p\|F_*q)\le \KL(p\|q).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the data-processing inequality for relative entropy under deterministic
coarse graining.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Folded relative-entropy bound]\label{cor:rotation-folded-kl-certificate}
Under the assumptions of
Corollary~\ref{cor:rotation-microstate-kl-certificate},
\[
\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N}\|\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
\le
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|\mu_m)
\le
\frac{4(m+1)^2}{q_{\min}(m)}D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)^2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:kl-monotone-pushforward} with $F=\Fold_m$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sturmian Rigidity on Realized Support}

In the canonical Sturmian slice, the fold does not merge the words that are
actually realized by the rotation. The reason is arithmetic: along the
Sturmian cylinder partition, the Fibonacci values of the factors remain
distinct modulo \(F_{m+2}\).

\begin{theorem}[Sturmian no-collision rigidity]\label{thm:sturmian-no-collision-rigidity}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\), and define
\[
S_m(\alpha):=\supp(\mu_m)\subset\Omega_m,
\qquad
Y_m:=\Fold_m(S_m(\alpha))\subset X_m.
\]
Then the restriction
\[
\Fold_m\!\upharpoonright_{S_m(\alpha)}:S_m(\alpha)\to Y_m
\]
is a bijection.

More precisely, let
\[
\Xi_m:=\{(1-j)\alpha \bmod 1:\ 0\le j\le m\}\subset\TT,
\]
let \(I_0,\dots,I_m\) be the open atoms of \(\TT\setminus \Xi_m\) in
counterclockwise order, and choose \(x_\ell\in I_\ell\). Writing
\[
u^{(\ell)}:=\omega_m(x_\ell;0)\in S_m(\alpha),
\qquad
V_\ell:=N(u^{(\ell)}),
\]
the cyclic jump multiset
\[
\Delta_\ell:=V_{\ell+1}-V_\ell
\qquad (\ell\!\!\!\!\pmod{m+1})
\]
consists of exactly one positive jump \(+F_{m+1}\) and the \(m\) negative
jumps
\[
-1,\ -F_1,\ -F_2,\ \dots,\ -F_{m-1},
\]
counted with multiplicity. After a cyclic relabeling that starts immediately to
the right of the unique positive jump, the values \(V_\ell\) form a strictly
decreasing integer chain. In particular, the residues \(V_\ell\bmod F_{m+2}\)
are pairwise distinct.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The proof has two steps: compute the Fibonacci jump produced by crossing each
Sturmian boundary atom, and then observe that after the unique positive jump
the remaining cycle is a strictly decreasing chain whose total drop is smaller
than \(F_{m+2}\), so residue collisions cannot occur.

For \(0\le k\le m-1\), the \(k\)-th symbol of \(\omega_m(x;0)\) is
\[
\mathbf{1}_{[0,\alpha)}(x+k\alpha),
\]
so its discontinuities occur at the two points \(-k\alpha\) and
\(\alpha-k\alpha=(1-k)\alpha\) on \(\TT\). The union of all such discontinuity
points is exactly \(\Xi_m\). Hence \(\omega_m(x;0)\) is constant on each atom
\(I_\ell\), and \(S_m(\alpha)=\{u^{(\ell)}:0\le \ell\le m\}\).

Fix a boundary point \(\xi_j:=(1-j)\alpha\in\Xi_m\).
If \(1\le j\le m-1\), then crossing \(\xi_j\) from left to right changes the
symbols in positions \(j-1\) and \(j\) from \(01\) to \(10\), while all other
positions stay fixed. Therefore the Fibonacci value jump is
\[
F_{j+1}-F_{j+2}=-F_j.
\]
At \(\xi_0=\alpha\), only the first symbol changes, from \(1\) to \(0\), so
the jump is \(-F_1=-1\). At \(\xi_m=(1-m)\alpha\), only the last symbol
changes, from \(0\) to \(1\), so the jump is \(+F_{m+1}\). This proves the
stated jump multiset.

Now start with the atom immediately to the right of \(\xi_m\). Along the
remaining cycle, every subsequent jump is negative, so the corresponding values
form a strictly decreasing integer chain. Since
\[
1+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}F_j=F_{m+1},
\]
the total drop before the cycle closes is exactly \(F_{m+1}<F_{m+2}\). Hence
no two values in this chain can be congruent modulo \(F_{m+2}\).
Proposition~\ref{prop:fold-residue-characterization} shows that
\(\Fold_m\) is constant on residue classes modulo \(F_{m+2}\), so its
restriction to \(S_m(\alpha)\) is injective. Bijectivity onto \(Y_m\) is then
tautological.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact relabeling on the realized Sturmian language]\label{cor:sturmian-exact-relabeling}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\), and let
\[
B_m:=\Fold_m\!\upharpoonright_{S_m(\alpha)}:S_m(\alpha)\to Y_m.
\]
Then \(B_m\) is a bijection, and for every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}=(B_m)_*\mu_m,
\qquad
\hat\pi_{m,N}=(B_m)_*\hat\mu_{m,N}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
=
\TV(\hat\mu_{m,N},\mu_m),
\]
\[
\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N}\|\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
=
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|\mu_m),
\]
and, writing \(H_q\) for the Renyi entropy of order \(q\), for every
\(q\in[0,\infty]\),
\[
H_q(\hat\pi_{m,N})=H_q(\hat\mu_{m,N}),
\qquad
H_q(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})=H_q(\mu_m).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The pushforward identities follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:sturmian-no-collision-rigidity}.
Since \(B_m\) is a bijection between the realized supports, total variation,
relative entropy, and all Renyi entropies are invariant under the relabeling
\(B_m\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pure degeneracy ledger on the Sturmian slice]\label{cor:sturmian-pure-degeneracy-ledger}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\), and let \(\mu_m^e:=Q_m\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\). Then
\[
\KL(\mu_m\|\mu_m^e)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}}\bigl[\log d_m(Y)\bigr].
\]
Likewise, for every \(N\ge 1\), if \(\hat\mu_{m,N}^e:=Q_m\hat\pi_{m,N}\), then
\[
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|\hat\mu_{m,N}^e)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\hat\pi_{m,N}}\bigl[\log d_m(Y)\bigr].
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Corollary~\ref{cor:sturmian-exact-relabeling} gives
\[
H(\mu_m)=H(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}),
\qquad
H(\hat\mu_{m,N})=H(\hat\pi_{m,N}).
\]
Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:fold-entropy-ledger} to \(\mu_m\) and
\(\hat\mu_{m,N}\) yields the two identities.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Fiber-Uniform Lift Geometry}

This subsection records a complementary microstate interpretation of the folded
observable. It is not needed for the support-barrier results of Section~5, but
it identifies which part of the mismatch survives after the data are compressed
to folded histograms. The canonical fold therefore induces a natural quotient
geometry on the microstate simplex, and the corresponding lift family is the
correct microstate-level model class for any comparison that keeps only the
folded observable.

\begin{definition}[Fiber multiplicity and uniform lift]\label{def:fiber-uniform-lift}
For \(x\in X_m\), define the fiber multiplicity
\[
d_m(x):=\bigl|\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\bigr|.
\]
For \(\eta\in\Delta(X_m)\), define its fiber-uniform lift
\[
(Q_m\eta)(a):=\frac{\eta(\Fold_m(a))}{d_m(\Fold_m(a))},
\qquad a\in\Omega_m.
\]
Then \((\Fold_m)_*(Q_m\eta)=\eta\). For \(\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)\), let
\[
\pi:=(\Fold_m)_*\mu\in\Delta(X_m),
\qquad
\mu^e:=Q_m\pi.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Exact entropy ledger for the fold quotient]\label{prop:fold-entropy-ledger}
For every \(\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)\), with \(\pi=(\Fold_m)_*\mu\) and
\(\mu^e=Q_m\pi\),
\[
H(\mu)
=
H(\pi)+\sum_{x\in X_m}\pi(x)\log d_m(x)-\KL(\mu\|\mu^e).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
H(\mu)-H(\pi)
=
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[\log d_m(X)]-\KL(\mu\|\mu^e).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write \(x_a:=\Fold_m(a)\). By Definition~\ref{def:fiber-uniform-lift},
\[
\mu^e(a)=\frac{\pi(x_a)}{d_m(x_a)}.
\]
Hence
\[
\begin{aligned}
\KL(\mu\|\mu^e)
&=
\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log\frac{\mu(a)}{\mu^e(a)}\\
&=
-H(\mu)-\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log\pi(x_a)
+\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log d_m(x_a).
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\sum_{a:\,x_a=x}\mu(a)=\pi(x)\), the two sums become
\[
\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log\pi(x_a)=\sum_{x\in X_m}\pi(x)\log\pi(x)=-H(\pi)
\]
and
\[
\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log d_m(x_a)
=
\sum_{x\in X_m}\pi(x)\log d_m(x).
\]
Rearranging gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[KL isometry of fiber-uniform lifts]\label{prop:fold-fiber-lift-kl-isometry}
For every \(\pi,\eta\in\Delta(X_m)\),
\[
\KL(Q_m\pi\|Q_m\eta)=\KL(\pi\|\eta).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Again write \(x_a:=\Fold_m(a)\). Then
\[
\frac{(Q_m\pi)(a)}{(Q_m\eta)(a)}
=
\frac{\pi(x_a)}{\eta(x_a)},
\]
so
\[
\begin{aligned}
\KL(Q_m\pi\|Q_m\eta)
&=
\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\frac{\pi(x_a)}{d_m(x_a)}
\log\frac{\pi(x_a)}{\eta(x_a)}\\
&=
\sum_{x\in X_m}
\left(\sum_{a:\,x_a=x}\frac{\pi(x)}{d_m(x)}\right)
\log\frac{\pi(x)}{\eta(x)}
=
\KL(\pi\|\eta).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Pythagorean decomposition for the canonical fold]\label{thm:fold-kl-pythagoras}
Let \(\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)\), let \(\pi=(\Fold_m)_*\mu\), and let
\(\mu^e=Q_m\pi\). Then for every \(\eta\in\Delta(X_m)\),
\[
\KL(\mu\|Q_m\eta)
=
\KL(\mu\|\mu^e)+\KL(\pi\|\eta).
\]
Consequently, \(\mu^e\) is the unique I-projection of \(\mu\) onto the
fiber-uniform model family
\[
\mathcal E_m:=\{Q_m\eta:\eta\in\Delta(X_m)\}\subset\Delta(\Omega_m),
\]
and
\[
\inf_{\eta\in\Delta(X_m)}\KL(\mu\|Q_m\eta)=\KL(\mu\|\mu^e).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(x_a:=\Fold_m(a)\). Since
\[
\mu^e(a)=\frac{\pi(x_a)}{d_m(x_a)},
\qquad
(Q_m\eta)(a)=\frac{\eta(x_a)}{d_m(x_a)},
\]
one has
\[
\log\frac{\mu^e(a)}{(Q_m\eta)(a)}=\log\frac{\pi(x_a)}{\eta(x_a)}.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\KL(\mu\|Q_m\eta)-\KL(\mu\|\mu^e)
&=
\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log\frac{\mu^e(a)}{(Q_m\eta)(a)}\\
&=
\sum_{a\in\Omega_m}\mu(a)\log\frac{\pi(x_a)}{\eta(x_a)}\\
&=
\sum_{x\in X_m}
\left(\sum_{a:\,x_a=x}\mu(a)\right)\log\frac{\pi(x)}{\eta(x)}
=
\KL(\pi\|\eta).
\end{aligned}
\]
This proves the decomposition. Since \(\KL(\pi\|\eta)\ge 0\), with equality if
and only if \(\eta=\pi\), the minimizer is unique and equals \(\mu^e=Q_m\pi\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Relation to classical information projection]\label{rem:fold-kl-pythagoras-classical}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fold-kl-pythagoras} is the finite-state specialization of the
classical Pythagorean identity for I-projections onto model families determined
by a sufficient statistic \cite{Csiszar1975IdivergenceGeometry}. In the present
paper the sufficient statistic is the folded observable \(\Fold_m\), and the
fiber-uniform family \(\mathcal E_m\) is exactly the family of laws that are
uniform within each fiber of this coarse-graining.
\end{remark}

\section{Comparison with the Parry Measure}

The Parry measure is the unique maximal-entropy equilibrium state of the
ambient golden-mean shift, whose topological entropy is
\(h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)=\log\varphi\). The point of this section is therefore not
merely to compare two probability measures on \(X_m\), but to quantify how far
folded rotation laws sit from ambient equilibrium when their effective support
is much smaller than the full shift language. This section is intentionally
model-specific: the exact identities below rely on endpoint collapse for the
golden-mean Parry cylinders, and no comparable closed formula is claimed here
for arbitrary shifts of finite type or non-canonical folds. Within this explicit
setting, the bounded-type case sharpens the extensive Parry gap to the
second-order asymptotic \(m\log\varphi-\log m+O_\alpha(1)\).

\begin{corollary}[Decomposition relative to the Parry measure]\label{cor:tv-decomposition-parry}
For every $m,N\ge 1$,
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m)
\le
2m\,D_N^*(\alpha;x_0)+\TV(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}},\pi_m).
\]
If \(\beta=\alpha\), one may replace \(2m\) by \(m+1\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:tv-certificate-hist} gives
$\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})\le 2mD_N^*(\alpha;x_0)$, and the
triangle inequality completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact divergence identity relative to the Parry measure]\label{thm:parry-kl-identity}
Let \(\nu\) be a probability measure on \(X_m\), and define the endpoint masses
\[
\eta_{ij}(\nu):=\nu\bigl(\{u\in X_m:u_1=i,\ u_m=j\}\bigr),
\qquad i,j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
If, with the convention \(0\log 0:=0\),
\[
H(\nu):=-\sum_{u\in X_m}\nu(u)\log \nu(u)
\]
denotes the Shannon entropy of \(\nu\), then
\[
\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
=
m\log\varphi-H(\nu)-\log\pi(0)
{}+(\eta_{11}(\nu)-\eta_{00}(\nu))\log\varphi.
\]
Consequently,
\[
m\log\varphi-H(\nu)-\log\pi(0)-\log\varphi
\le
\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
\le
m\log\varphi-H(\nu)-\log\pi(0)+\log\varphi.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:parry-endpoint-collapse},
\[
\log \pi_m(u)
=
\log\pi(0)-m\log\varphi
{}+\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{\{u_1=0\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{u_m=1\}}\bigr)\log\varphi.
\]
Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
&=
\sum_{u\in X_m}\nu(u)\log\frac{\nu(u)}{\pi_m(u)}\\
&=
-H(\nu)-\sum_{u\in X_m}\nu(u)\log\pi_m(u)\\
&=
m\log\varphi-H(\nu)-\log\pi(0)
{}+\bigl(\eta_{11}(\nu)-\eta_{00}(\nu)\bigr)\log\varphi.
\end{aligned}
\]
Since \(\eta_{11}(\nu)-\eta_{00}(\nu)\in[-1,1]\), the two-sided bound follows.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Specificity of the endpoint collapse]\label{rem:endpoint-collapse-specificity}
Theorem~\ref{thm:parry-kl-identity} depends essentially on
Proposition~\ref{prop:parry-endpoint-collapse}. For a general shift of finite
type, Parry cylinder weights are controlled by boundary states in an edge
presentation but need not collapse to a scalar endpoint statistic of the kind
available here. Accordingly, we do not claim an equally sharp closed formula
outside collapse geometries of this type; the exact identity is part of what
makes the golden-mean setting unusually rigid.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[No fold-entropy loss on the Sturmian slice]\label{cor:no-fold-entropy-loss}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\). Then
\[
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
=
m\log\varphi-H(\mu_m)-\log\pi(0)
{}+\bigl(\eta_{11}(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})
-\eta_{00}(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})\bigr)\log\varphi.
\]
Likewise, for every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N}\|\pi_m)
=
m\log\varphi-H(\hat\mu_{m,N})-\log\pi(0)
{}+\bigl(\eta_{11}(\hat\pi_{m,N})
-\eta_{00}(\hat\pi_{m,N})\bigr)\log\varphi.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply Theorem~\ref{thm:parry-kl-identity} with
\(\nu=\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\) and \(\nu=\hat\pi_{m,N}\), and use
Corollary~\ref{cor:sturmian-exact-relabeling} to replace
\(H(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})\) by \(H(\mu_m)\) and
\(H(\hat\pi_{m,N})\) by \(H(\hat\mu_{m,N})\).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint class counts]\label{lem:endpoint-class-counts}
Extend the Fibonacci sequence by \(F_0:=0\), \(F_1:=1\), and
\(F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_n\). For \(m\ge 2\), let
\[
X_m^{ij}:=\{u\in X_m:u_1=i,\ u_m=j\},
\qquad i,j\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Then
\[
|X_m^{00}|=F_m,\qquad
|X_m^{01}|=|X_m^{10}|=F_{m-1},\qquad
|X_m^{11}|=F_{m-2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The admissible length-\(m\) words in \(X_m\) are the paths of length \(m-1\) in
the golden-mean graph with adjacency matrix
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For the standard Fibonacci extension above,
\[
A^n=
\begin{pmatrix}
F_{n+1} & F_n\\
F_n & F_{n-1}
\end{pmatrix}
\qquad (n\ge 1).
\]
Hence the number of admissible words of length \(m\) with endpoints \((i,j)\) is
\((A^{m-1})_{ij}\), which gives the displayed formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Sharp support barrier against the Parry measure]\label{thm:parry-support-barrier}
Let \(m\ge 2\) and \(1\le k\le F_{m+2}\). Define
\[
M_m(k):=\max\{\pi_m(S):S\subset X_m,\ |S|\le k\}.
\]
Then
\[
M_m(k)=
\begin{cases}
k\,\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)},
& 1\le k\le F_m,\\[0.4em]
F_m\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)}+(k-F_m)\pi(0)\varphi^{-m},
& F_m<k\le F_m+2F_{m-1},\\[0.4em]
1-(F_{m+2}-k)\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m+1)},
& F_m+2F_{m-1}<k\le F_{m+2}.
\end{cases}
\]
Moreover,
\[
\inf_{\substack{\nu\in\Delta(X_m)\\ |\supp(\nu)|\le k}}\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
=
-\log M_m(k),
\]
and
\[
\inf_{\substack{\nu\in\Delta(X_m)\\ |\supp(\nu)|\le k}}\TV(\nu,\pi_m)
=
1-M_m(k).
\]
Each infimum is attained by conditioning \(\pi_m\) on a maximizing set
\(S\subset X_m\); equivalently, one takes all words from the heaviest endpoint
classes until the budget \(k\) is exhausted.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The argument has three layers. One first orders individual Parry cylinder
weights by endpoint class, then maximizes the mass of a \(k\)-point set by a
greedy filling of these endpoint classes, and finally observes that
conditioning the Parry law on an optimal set attains both the KL and TV
infima.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:parry-endpoint-collapse}, every word in \(X_m^{00}\) has
Parry weight \(\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)}\), every word in \(X_m^{01}\cup X_m^{10}\)
has weight \(\pi(0)\varphi^{-m}\), and every word in \(X_m^{11}\) has weight
\(\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m+1)}\). These three values are strictly ordered. Together
with Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoint-class-counts}, this implies that the largest
possible Parry mass of a \(k\)-point subset is obtained greedily by exhausting
the endpoint classes in the order
\[
X_m^{00},\qquad X_m^{01}\cup X_m^{10},\qquad X_m^{11},
\]
which gives the formula for \(M_m(k)\).

Now let \(S\subset X_m\) and let \(\nu\) be supported on \(S\). Writing
\(\pi_m^S:=\pi_m(\cdot\mid S)\), one has
\[
\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
=
\KL(\nu\|\pi_m^S)-\log \pi_m(S)
\ge
-\log \pi_m(S),
\]
with equality if and only if \(\nu=\pi_m^S\). Therefore
\[
\inf_{\substack{\nu\in\Delta(X_m)\\ |\supp(\nu)|\le k}}\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
=
-\log\max_{\substack{S\subset X_m\\ |S|\le k}}\pi_m(S)
=
-\log M_m(k).
\]

For total variation, if \(\nu\) is supported on \(S\), then
\[
\TV(\nu,\pi_m)
\ge
\pi_m(S^c)
=
1-\pi_m(S).
\]
Equality holds for \(\nu=\pi_m^S\), since then
\[
\TV(\pi_m^S,\pi_m)
=
\frac12\sum_{u\in X_m}\bigl|\pi_m^S(u)-\pi_m(u)\bigr|
=
1-\pi_m(S).
\]
Taking the best support set \(S\) gives the total-variation formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Exact complexity-divergence law]\label{cor:exact-complexity-divergence-law}
Let \(m\ge 2\) and \(1\le k\le F_m\). Then
\[
\inf_{\substack{\nu\in\Delta(X_m)\\ |\supp(\nu)|\le k}}\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
=
(m-1)\log\varphi-\log\pi(0)-\log k,
\]
and
\[
\inf_{\substack{\nu\in\Delta(X_m)\\ |\supp(\nu)|\le k}}\TV(\nu,\pi_m)
=
1-k\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)}.
\]
The KL minimizers are exactly the uniform laws on \(k\)-point subsets of
\(X_m^{00}\), while the TV minimizers are the conditioned Parry laws on such
subsets.

Consequently, if \(k_m\le F_m\) and
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log k_m=\alpha\in[0,\log\varphi],
\]
then
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}
\inf_{\substack{\nu\in\Delta(X_m)\\ |\supp(\nu)|\le k_m}}
\frac{1}{m}\KL(\nu\|\pi_m)
=
\log\varphi-\alpha
=
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-\alpha.
\]
In particular, every subexponential support budget still incurs the full rate
\(\log\varphi\), and cancelling the Parry penalty requires support growth of
order \(\varphi^m\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first branch of Theorem~\ref{thm:parry-support-barrier} gives the displayed
formulas. The description of the minimizers follows because all words in
\(X_m^{00}\) carry the same Parry weight \(\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)}\). The limit
statement is obtained by dividing the KL formula by \(m\) and letting
\(m\to\infty\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Equilibrium barrier from effective complexity]\label{cor:equilibrium-barrier-effective-complexity}
Let \((\nu_m)_{m\ge 1}\) be any family with \(\nu_m\in\Delta(X_m)\). Assume
that for some \(\alpha\in[0,\log\varphi)\),
\[
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log |\supp(\nu_m)|\le \alpha.
\]
Then
\[
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\KL(\nu_m\|\pi_m)\ge h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-\alpha.
\]
In particular, every family with subexponential effective complexity satisfies
\[
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\KL(\nu_m\|\pi_m)\ge h_{\mathrm{top}}(X),
\]
so low-complexity folded laws remain at positive entropy density from the
ambient maximal-entropy equilibrium.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Choose any \(\varepsilon\in(0,\log\varphi-\alpha)\). By hypothesis,
\[
|\supp(\nu_m)|\le \exp((\alpha+\varepsilon)m)
\]
for all sufficiently large \(m\). Since \(F_m=\varphi^m/\sqrt5+o(\varphi^m)\),
one has \(|\supp(\nu_m)|\le F_m\) for all sufficiently large \(m\). Applying
Corollary~\ref{cor:exact-complexity-divergence-law} with
\(k_m:=|\supp(\nu_m)|\) yields
\[
\frac{1}{m}\KL(\nu_m\|\pi_m)
\ge
\log\varphi-\frac{1}{m}\log k_m-\frac{\log\pi(0)}{m}.
\]
Taking \(\liminf\) gives
\[
\liminf_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\KL(\nu_m\|\pi_m)
\ge
h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)-(\alpha+\varepsilon).
\]
Since \(\varepsilon>0\) is arbitrary, the first claim follows. The
subexponential case is \(\alpha=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the support barrier says about alternative folds]
\label{rem:alternative-folds-support-barrier}
Corollary~\ref{cor:equilibrium-barrier-effective-complexity} is stated only in
terms of laws on \(X_m\); it does not depend on how those laws were produced.
Accordingly, changing the local normalization rule cannot by itself erase the
Parry mismatch if the resulting folded family still has subexponential support.
To beat the extensive rate \(h_{\mathrm{top}}(X)\), one must change the
effective support exponent, not merely the labels inside a fixed low-complexity
model class.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Bounded-type Sturmian entropy]\label{prop:bounded-type-sturmian-entropy}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\), and suppose that \(\alpha\) has bounded partial
quotients, equivalently that \(\alpha\) is badly approximable in dimension one
\cite{Cassels1957,Schmidt1969BadlyApproximableSystems}. Then there exist
constants \(c_\alpha,C_\alpha>0\) such that for
every \(m\ge 1\) and every \(u\in S_m(\alpha)\),
\[
\frac{c_\alpha}{m}\le \mu_m(u)\le \frac{C_\alpha}{m}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
H(\mu_m)=\log m+O_\alpha(1).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(p_n/q_n\) be the convergents of \(\alpha\), and choose \(n\) so that
\(q_n\le m<q_{n+1}\). By
Theorem~\ref{thm:spg-sturmian-cylinder-measure-continued-fraction-sandwich},
every length-\(m\) Sturmian cylinder satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{q_{n+1}+q_n}<\mu_m(u)<\frac{2}{q_n}.
\]
If the partial quotients of \(\alpha\) are bounded by \(A\), then
\(q_{n+1}\le (A+1)q_n\), and since \(q_n\le m<q_{n+1}\), one has
\[
\frac{1}{(A+2)m}\le \mu_m(u)\le \frac{2(A+1)}{m}.
\]
This proves the uniform \(m^{-1}\) bounds.

Because \(|S_m(\alpha)|=m+1\), the lower bound gives
\[
H(\mu_m)\le \log(m+1)=\log m+O(1),
\]
while the upper bound \(\mu_m(u)\le C_\alpha/m\) implies
\[
H(\mu_m)
=
\sum_{u\in S_m(\alpha)}\mu_m(u)\log\frac1{\mu_m(u)}
\ge
\log m-\log C_\alpha.
\]
Combining the two estimates yields \(H(\mu_m)=\log m+O_\alpha(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Explicit bounded-type KL window]\label{prop:explicit-bounded-type-kl-window}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\), and suppose that the partial quotients of \(\alpha\)
are bounded by \(A\). Then for every \(m\ge 1\),
\[
\log m-\log(2(A+1))
\le
H(\mu_m)
\le
\log(m+1).
\]
Consequently,
\[
m\log\varphi-\log(m+1)-\log\pi(0)-\log\varphi
\le
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
\]
\[
\le
m\log\varphi-\log m+\log(2(A+1))-\log\pi(0)+\log\varphi.
\]
In particular,
\[
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
-
\bigl(m\log\varphi-\log m\bigr)
\]
remains bounded by explicit constants depending only on \(A\) and \(\pi(0)\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:bounded-type-sturmian-entropy} already gives
the explicit entropy bounds
\[
\frac{1}{(A+2)m}\le \mu_m(u)\le \frac{2(A+1)}{m}
\qquad (u\in S_m(\alpha)).
\]
Because \(|S_m(\alpha)|=m+1\), the upper entropy bound is
\[
H(\mu_m)\le \log(m+1),
\]
while the pointwise upper mass bound yields
\[
H(\mu_m)
=
\sum_{u\in S_m(\alpha)}\mu_m(u)\log\frac1{\mu_m(u)}
\ge
\log m-\log(2(A+1)).
\]
Corollary~\ref{cor:no-fold-entropy-loss} gives
\[
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
=
m\log\varphi-H(\mu_m)-\log\pi(0)
+(\eta_{11}(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})-\eta_{00}(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}))\log\varphi.
\]
Since \(\eta_{11}-\eta_{00}\in[-1,1]\), combining the entropy bounds with the
endpoint correction gives the displayed KL window.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp second-order asymptotics on bounded-type slopes]\label{cor:sharp-second-order-bounded-type}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\), and suppose that \(\alpha\) has bounded partial
quotients. Then
\[
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
=
m\log\varphi-\log m+O_\alpha(1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:explicit-bounded-type-kl-window}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Microstate decomposition relative to the Parry lift]\label{cor:microstate-parry-lift-decomposition}
Let \(\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)\), let \(\pi=(\Fold_m)_*\mu\), and let
\(\mu^e=Q_m\pi\). Then
\[
\KL(\mu\|Q_m\pi_m)
=
\KL(\mu\|\mu^e)+\KL(\pi\|\pi_m).
\]
If \(|\supp(\pi)|\le k\), then
\[
\KL(\mu\|Q_m\pi_m)
\ge
\KL(\mu\|\mu^e)-\log M_m(k).
\]
In particular, assume \(\beta=\alpha\) and \(m\ge 6\). Then
\[
\KL(\mu_m\|Q_m\pi_m)
=
\mathbb E_{\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}}\big[\log d_m(Y)\big]
+\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m),
\]
\[
\KL(\mu_m\|Q_m\pi_m)
\ge
\mathbb E_{\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}}\big[\log d_m(Y)\big]
+(m-1)\log\varphi-\log\pi(0)-\log(m+1),
\]
and for every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|Q_m\pi_m)
=
\mathbb E_{\hat\pi_{m,N}}\big[\log d_m(Y)\big]
+\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N}\|\pi_m),
\]
\[
\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|Q_m\pi_m)
\ge
\mathbb E_{\hat\pi_{m,N}}\big[\log d_m(Y)\big]
+(m-1)\log\varphi-\log\pi(0)-\log(m+1).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The first identity is Theorem~\ref{thm:fold-kl-pythagoras} with \(\eta=\pi_m\).
If \(|\supp(\pi)|\le k\), then
Theorem~\ref{thm:parry-support-barrier} gives
\(\KL(\pi\|\pi_m)\ge -\log M_m(k)\), which yields the second inequality.

Under \(\beta=\alpha\), Corollary~\ref{cor:sturmian-pure-degeneracy-ledger}
identifies \(\KL(\mu_m\|\mu_m^e)\) and \(\KL(\hat\mu_{m,N}\|\hat\mu_{m,N}^e)\)
with the corresponding degeneracy averages. The folded laws
\(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\) and \(\hat\pi_{m,N}\) are supported on at most \(m+1\)
words, and for \(m\ge 6\), one has \(m+1\le F_m\). Hence
Corollary~\ref{cor:exact-complexity-divergence-law} applies with \(k=m+1\),
yielding the lower bounds.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Universal extensive divergence in the Sturmian slice]\label{cor:parry-extensive-rate-sturmian}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\). Then for every \(m\ge 1\),
\[
|\supp(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})|\le m+1,
\]
and for every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
|\supp(\hat\pi_{m,N})|\le m+1.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)=m\log\varphi+O(\log m),
\qquad
\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N}\|\pi_m)=m\log\varphi+O(\log m),
\]
where the \(O(\log m)\) terms are deterministic and uniform in \(N\) and
\(x_0\). In particular, along every sampling schedule \(N=N(m)\),
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
=
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N(m)}\|\pi_m)
=
\log\varphi.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Under \(\beta=\alpha\), the length-\(m\) rotation language is Sturmian and
therefore has exactly \(m+1\) factors
\cite{MorseHedlund1940,LindMarcus1995}. Deterministic folding cannot enlarge
support, so both displayed support bounds follow. Hence
\[
H(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}})\le \log(m+1),
\qquad
H(\hat\pi_{m,N})\le \log(m+1).
\]
Applying Theorem~\ref{thm:parry-kl-identity} to
\(\nu=\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\) and \(\nu=\hat\pi_{m,N}\), and using the bounded
endpoint correction, gives the \(m\log\varphi+O(\log m)\) estimates. Division
by \(m\) yields the limit.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Optimal support obstruction for folded Sturmian laws]\label{cor:folded-optimal-support-obstruction}
Assume \(\beta=\alpha\) and \(m\ge 6\). Then
\[
\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)
\ge
(m-1)\log\varphi-\log\pi(0)-\log(m+1),
\]
and for every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
\KL(\hat\pi_{m,N}\|\pi_m)
\ge
(m-1)\log\varphi-\log\pi(0)-\log(m+1).
\]
Likewise,
\[
\TV(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}},\pi_m)
\ge
1-(m+1)\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)},
\]
and for every \(N\ge 1\),
\[
\TV(\hat\pi_{m,N},\pi_m)
\ge
1-(m+1)\pi(0)\varphi^{-(m-1)}.
\]
Thus the mismatch to the Parry baseline is not merely large; it lies above the
sharp variational floor for every \((m+1)\)-state law on \(X_m\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the Sturmian slice, both \(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\) and \(\hat\pi_{m,N}\) are
supported on at most \(m+1\) words. For \(m\ge 6\), one has \(m+1\le F_m\), so
Corollary~\ref{cor:exact-complexity-divergence-law} applies with \(k=m+1\).
\end{proof}

obstruction: after folding into \(X_m\), low effective complexity prevents
convergence to the maximal-entropy baseline. This is why the paper does not try
to benchmark many alternative local normalization rules numerically. By
Remark~\ref{rem:alternative-folds-support-barrier}, any alternative fold that
still produces a subexponential-support family on \(X_m\) must retain a
positive Parry entropy density.

structural term persists and eventually dominates.
No systematic benchmark against alternative folds is included here. The purpose
of the numerical section is to corroborate the canonical theory developed in
Sections~2--5, and the support-barrier results already show what any such
benchmark could and could not change: a different fold may alter constants or
the effective support exponent, but it cannot erase the Parry penalty while
remaining inside the same low-complexity regime.

\section{Discussion}

The paper isolates a single mechanism behind the Parry mismatch in folded
histograms of irrational rotations. In the canonical Sturmian slice
\(\beta=\alpha\), Parry divergence is not created by folding: once
finite-sample error has been controlled by discrepancy, the remaining gap comes
from low-complexity Sturmian geometry against the ambient maximal-entropy
equilibrium. Both the folded limit law and every empirical folded histogram
therefore remain at the universal KL rate \(\log\varphi\) per symbol from the
Parry baseline, so a large Parry discrepancy is a structural signal of the
model rather than evidence of insufficient sampling.

At the same time, the canonical Fibonacci fold is transparent on the realized
Sturmian language. In the slice \(\beta=\alpha\), it does not collapse distinct
realized factors; it merely relabels them by distinct residues modulo
\(F_{m+2}\). The Parry divergence therefore comes from Sturmian arithmetic
geometry itself, not from an additional entropy loss created by folding.

The variational picture is now equally rigid. The Parry endpoint collapse makes
it possible to solve exactly the best low-complexity approximation problem:
under a support budget \(k\), the optimal law is obtained by conditioning the
Parry measure on the \(k\) heaviest admissible words, and the resulting KL and
TV costs are explicit. The complexity exponent therefore trades against the
Parry divergence rate with slope one: a support growth
\(\exp(\alpha m+o(m))\) lowers the unavoidable KL rate precisely to
\(\log\varphi-\alpha\). In particular, the folded Sturmian laws do not merely
have too little support to look Parry; they already sit above the exact
complexity-constrained optimum for any \((m+1)\)-state model.

At the same time, the canonical fold admits an exact quotient information
geometry on the microstate simplex. The fiber-uniform lift family is a canonical
I-projection family associated with the fold, and the relative entropy to the lifted Parry baseline
splits orthogonally into two terms: a hidden within-fiber residual and the
observable Parry divergence of the folded law. This identifies precisely which
part of the mismatch is visible at the histogram level and which part remains
confined to unresolved microstructure. This viewpoint is complementary to the
main support-barrier results: it does not replace the Parry comparison, but
explains how much of that comparison is already determined once one passes to
the folded observable. In this sense, Theorem~\ref{thm:fold-kl-pythagoras}
should be read as a canonical coarse-graining identity in the classical
information-projection sense of \cite{Csiszar1975IdivergenceGeometry}, with the
folded observable acting as the sufficient statistic.

For bounded-type slopes, the same rigidity sharpens the asymptotics of the
Parry mismatch. Because the fold preserves the entropy of the realized
Sturmian law exactly, the second term is governed by cylinder geometry alone:
\(\KL(\pi_m^{\mathrm{fold}}\|\pi_m)=m\log\varphi-\log m+O_\alpha(1)\). Thus both
the leading equilibrium barrier and its logarithmic correction are already
encoded in the Sturmian cylinders before any off-support fiber structure is
taken into account.

Within the metallic-mean family, the golden slope plays a double role. It
minimizes the leading deterministic discrepancy constant, yet the same constant
\(\varphi\) also fixes the unavoidable divergence rate from the Parry baseline.
More generally, the paper isolates a complexity-versus-equilibrium principle:
once a folded model on the golden-mean language has effective support exponent
strictly below \(\log\varphi\), its KL distance from the ambient equilibrium
remains extensive. In that sense, the present irrational-rotation model should
be read as a fully computable realization of a broader low-complexity barrier:
what is special here is not merely that the folded Sturmian laws are far from
Parry, but that the gap can be decomposed exactly into finite-sample error,
structural support deficit, and fiber-level degeneracy.

The same perspective clarifies the status of alternative folds. The canonical
normalization is important because it admits exact residue formulas,
transparency on realized Sturmian support, and the boundary law for
cross-resolution residuals. But the extensive Parry penalty is less fragile
than those model-specific identities. Any alternative coarse-graining that
still outputs a subexponential-support family on \(X_m\) faces the same
equilibrium obstruction. What a different fold could change is the support
exponent, the constants, or the availability of exact formulas; what it cannot
do is restore proximity to the Parry equilibrium without leaving the
low-complexity regime.

The main limitation is therefore precise rather than diffuse. The exact KL
identity relies on endpoint collapse of the golden-mean Parry cylinders, and no
general extension to arbitrary shifts of finite type is claimed. The natural
next question is to determine which other low-complexity codings admit an
analogous collapse mechanism or, failing that, whether one can still prove a
quantitative equilibrium barrier with weaker but robust constants. That is the
point at which the explicit theory developed here would have to give way to a
more general coarse-graining framework.

\section{Canonical Fold as Local Zeckendorf Normalization}\label{app:canonical-fold}

This appendix records a local implementation of the canonical fold used in
Definition~\ref{def:canonical-fold}. It is included only to make the model
object completely explicit and reproducible.

Let \(\mathcal{D}\) be the set of finitely supported digit configurations
\[
a=(a_1,a_2,\dots),\qquad a_k\in\mathbb{N},
\]
and define the Fibonacci value
\[
\mathrm{Val}(a):=\sum_{k\ge 1} a_k F_{k+1}.
\]
Embed \(\omega=\omega_1\cdots \omega_m\in\Omega_m\) as
\[
\iota_m(\omega):=(\omega_1,\dots,\omega_m,0,0,\dots)\in\mathcal{D}.
\]
Consider the following value-preserving local rewrites:
\[
e_k+e_{k+1}\to e_{k+2},
\qquad
2e_1\to e_2,
\qquad
2e_2\to e_1+e_3,
\qquad
2e_k\to e_{k-2}+e_{k+1}\ \ (k\ge 3),
\]
where \(e_k\) is the \(k\)-th unit vector in \(\mathcal{D}\).

\begin{proposition}[Order-independent local normalization]\label{prop:canonical-fold-rewrite}
Starting from \(\iota_m(\omega)\), every maximal rewrite sequence terminates at
the same normal form \(\mathrm{NF}(\iota_m(\omega))\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}\)
with no adjacent \(1\)'s. This normal form is the Zeckendorf expansion
\(Z(N(\omega))\). Consequently,
\[
\Fold_m(\omega)=\pref_m\bigl(\mathrm{NF}(\iota_m(\omega))\bigr)
\]
is independent of rewrite order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each rewrite preserves \(\mathrm{Val}\) because it encodes one of the Fibonacci
identities
\[
F_{k+1}+F_{k+2}=F_{k+3},
\qquad
2F_2=F_3,
\qquad
2F_3=F_2+F_4,
\qquad
2F_{k+1}=F_{k-1}+F_{k+2}\ \ (k\ge 3).
\]
Termination follows from a lexicographic descent argument. Let
\[
A(a):=\sum_{k\ge 1} a_k,
\qquad
B(a):=\sum_{k\ge 1} k\,a_k,
\qquad
C(a):=a_2.
\]
The rules \(e_k+e_{k+1}\to e_{k+2}\) and \(2e_1\to e_2\) decrease \(A\); the
rules \(2e_k\to e_{k-2}+e_{k+1}\) for \(k\ge 3\) preserve \(A\) and decrease
\(B\); and the rule \(2e_2\to e_1+e_3\) preserves \(A\) and \(B\) but decreases
\(C\). Hence \((A,B,C)\) decreases lexicographically at each step, so rewriting
must terminate.

A normal form has no adjacent \(1\)'s and no digit \(\ge 2\), hence is a
Zeckendorf representation. Since every rewrite preserves \(\mathrm{Val}\) and
Zeckendorf representation is unique \cite{Zeckendorf1972}, all terminating
branches have the same normal form. The final formula for \(\Fold_m\) is then
just the definition of the canonical fold through prefix truncation.
\end{proof}
