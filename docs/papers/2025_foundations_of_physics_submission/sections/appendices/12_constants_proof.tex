\section{Geometric ans\"atze for dimensionless constants}
\label{app:constants_proof}

This appendix records the working geometric ans\"atze for two dimensionless constants discussed in Part~IV and clarifies the matching assumptions and error budgets. These expressions are \emph{not} derived here from a unique microscopic measure; they are to be read as explicit model targets for a future dynamical completion.

\subsection{Regularization and Scale Definition}
In a fully specified microscopic model, dimensionless couplings should arise as ratios of normalized geometric/topological invariants of the internal sector, potentially defined via an appropriate measure on a moduli space $\mathcal{M}_{int}$. In the present manuscript we do not fix such a measure. Instead, we work with concrete closed-form ans\"atze and interpret them as defining \emph{effective} low-energy matching data for the corresponding couplings.
\par
\noindent
In the scan-based viewpoint, such normalized quantities can be expressed (in concrete models) as orbit traces and, when necessary, as finite parts of regulated orbit sums. Whenever a constant term depends on regularization choice, we adopt the orbit finite-part convention of Convention~R1 (Section~\ref{conv:orbit_regularization} and Appendix~\ref{app:orbit_calculus}).

\subsection{Fine Structure Constant \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}}
Motivated by the appearance of $U(1)$ as a circle fiber in standard Hopf-type constructions, we introduce a simple geometric inverse-coupling ansatz as a dimensionless combination of elementary fiber-volume factors.

\begin{definition}[Alpha ansatz (working)]
\label{def:alpha_ansatz}
We use a phenomenological geometric ansatz for the inverse coupling:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha^{-1}_{\mathrm{geo}} \equiv 4\pi^3 + \pi^2 + \pi,
\end{equation}
which numerically gives $\alpha^{-1}_{\mathrm{geo}}\approx 137.03630$. This expression should be understood as an effective summary of discrete/topological weights associated with internal fiber data; a first-principles derivation from a well-defined measure on the relevant moduli space remains an open problem.
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{Matching and error budget}
Comparing with the low-energy value $\alpha^{-1}(0)\approx 137.035999$, one finds
\begin{equation}
    \Delta \alpha^{-1}\equiv \alpha^{-1}_{\mathrm{geo}}-\alpha^{-1}(0)\approx 3.0\times 10^{-4},
\end{equation}
corresponding to a relative discrepancy $|\Delta \alpha^{-1}|/\alpha^{-1}(0)\sim 2\times 10^{-6}$. In a controlled EFT embedding, one would interpret $\alpha^{-1}_{\mathrm{geo}}$ as a boundary condition at a matching scale $\mu_\ast$ and run the Standard Model renormalization group to $\mu=0$, including thresholds. Since the microscopic Omega construction is not yet specified to that level, we do not perform this RG matching here; instead we treat $\Delta \alpha^{-1}$ as an empirical error budget that a future completion must account for (e.g.\ via normalization choices, threshold corrections, or additional geometric contributions).

\subsection{Proton-Electron Mass Ratio \texorpdfstring{$\mu$}{mu}}
We record the working geometric ansatz for the proton--electron mass ratio $\mu=m_p/m_e$ as a dimensionless constant associated with internal-cycle data. In the absence of a derived hadronic/EW/QED matching computation, we treat the following as a phenomenological target:
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{\mathrm{geo}} = 6\pi^5 \approx 1836.1181.
\end{equation}
Comparing with the experimental value $\mu_{\mathrm{exp}}\approx 1836.1526$, the absolute discrepancy is $|\Delta\mu|\approx 3.5\times 10^{-2}$ and the relative discrepancy is $|\Delta\mu|/\mu_{\mathrm{exp}}\approx 1.9\times 10^{-5}$. Any serious derivation must explain both the functional form and the size/sign of $\Delta\mu$ once QCD binding effects, electroweak symmetry breaking, and radiative corrections are included.

\subsection{Rigidity bound for local temporal drift of coarse-grained couplings}
\label{app:rigidity_drift}
The drift estimates in Part~VI use only a minimal ``averaging'' mechanism: if an effective coupling is a coarse-grained average over many microscopic contributions, then any bounded-rate microscopic update produces a parametrically suppressed macroscopic drift.

\begin{proposition}[Averaging bound]
\label{prop:averaging_bound}
Let $g(t)$ be an effective (dimensionless) coupling defined as an average over $N_{\mathrm{dof}}$ bounded microscopic contributions (not to be confused with the lapse $N(t)$ used elsewhere in this manuscript),
\begin{equation}
    g(t)=\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{dof}}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{dof}}} g_i(t),
\end{equation}
and assume that during a time interval of duration $\Delta t$ at most $M$ of the $g_i$ change, each by at most $|\Delta g_i|\le \delta$. Then
\begin{equation}
    |\Delta g|\ \le\ \frac{M}{N_{\mathrm{dof}}}\,\delta.
\end{equation}
If $g(t)$ is strictly positive and bounded below by $g_{\min}>0$, then the fractional drift obeys
\begin{equation}
    \left|\frac{\Delta g}{g}\right|\ \le\ \frac{M}{N_{\mathrm{dof}}}\,\frac{\delta}{g_{\min}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is immediate from the triangle inequality and the assumed bounds on the number and magnitude of microscopic changes.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Holographic scaling of $N_{\mathrm{dof}}$.}
In holographic near-saturation regimes, one may identify $N_{\mathrm{dof}}$ with the number of independent boundary degrees of freedom in a causal patch, $N_{\mathrm{dof}}\sim A/(4l_P^2\ln 2)$, so that any bounded-rate microscopic update yields a very small macroscopic drift. The estimate is purely kinematic; the remaining dynamical question is to determine the effective update rate $M$ and the microscopic step size $\delta$ for a concrete Omega network.
