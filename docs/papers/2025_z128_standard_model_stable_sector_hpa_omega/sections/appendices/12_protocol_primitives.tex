\section{Protocol primitives and regularization conventions}
\label{app:protocol_primitives}

This appendix records, in one place, a compact set of protocol primitives and analytic conventions that are used throughout the paper.
It introduces \emph{no new axioms or assumptions} beyond the declared input set in the main text; it only restates standard definitions and short supporting lemmas.

\subsection{A Weyl pair viewpoint for scan dynamics}

The scan orbit $x_n=x_0+n\alpha\pmod{1}$ can be encoded algebraically by a Weyl pair.
One convenient realization is on $L^2(\TT)$ with the shift and multiplication operators.

\begin{definition}[Weyl pair]\label{def:weyl_pair}
Fix $\alpha\in\RR$.
A \emph{Weyl pair} is a pair of unitary operators $(U,V)$ satisfying
\[
UV=\e^{2\pi\iu \alpha}\,VU.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[A canonical realization]\label{rem:weyl_pair_realization}
On $L^2(\TT)$ with coordinate $x\in\RR/\ZZ$, define
\[
(Uf)(x):=f(x+\alpha),\qquad (Vf)(x):=\e^{2\pi\iu x}f(x).
\]
Then $U$ and $V$ are unitary and satisfy the Weyl relation in Definition~\ref{def:weyl_pair}.
This algebraic encoding is used only as a bookkeeping device for ``shift'' and ``phase'' operations consistent with Axiom~\ref{ax:readout_sequentiality}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Window projection as a readout map}

Given a window $W\subset\TT$, define the indicator kernel $K_W:\TT\to\{0,1\}$ by $K_W(z)=\ind\{z\in W\}$.
The (deterministic) binary readout induced by $W$ is
\[
w_n=K_W(z_n)=\ind\{\e^{2\pi\iu x_n}\in W\}\in\{0,1\}.
\]
Finite observability at window length $m$ is the restriction to words $w=w_1\cdots w_m\in\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m$.

\subsection{Zeckendorf admissibility and the golden branch}

On the golden branch, admissibility is enforced by the forbidden word ``11'':
\[
X_m=\{w\in\Omega_m:\text{$w$ contains no adjacent ones}\}.
\]
Equivalently, if $c_k\in\{0,1\}$ are Zeckendorf digits, the admissibility constraint is $c_kc_{k+1}=0$.
The Fibonacci count $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:xm_fib}) is the combinatorial backbone of the $\varphi$-channel.

\subsection{Dyadic phase registers}

For a phase resolution parameter $p\ge 1$, we model phases by the finite ring
\[
\ZZ_{2^p}=\ZZ/2^p\ZZ,
\]
equipped with the embedding into $\TT$ given by
\[
k\;\longmapsto\;\e^{2\pi\iu k/2^p}.
\]
In the holonomy diagnostics, the dyadic denominator $\mathrm{denom}=2^p$ is treated as an explicit refinement knob and is swept in bounded families rather than fixed as a hidden degree of freedom (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy} and Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}).
The label $\ZZ_{128}$ is the baseline choice at $p=7$ (Section~\ref{subsec:z128_label}).

\subsection{Abel normalization and pole barriers}

The $\e$-channel uses an Abel-type viewpoint: one studies an analytic generating function on the open unit disk and takes a limit along the \emph{Abel path} $\rho\uparrow 1$ with $\rho\in(0,1)$.
This isolates the location of the first singularity (the ``pole barrier'') that obstructs extending the analytic object to the boundary.

\begin{definition}[Abel-normalized sum]\label{def:abel_sum}
Let $(a_n)_{n\ge 0}$ be a complex sequence and define, for $\rho\in(0,1)$,
\[
F(\rho):=\sum_{n\ge 0} a_n \rho^n,
\]
whenever the series converges.
If the limit exists (finite or infinite), the \emph{Abel limit} is
\[
\lim_{\rho\uparrow 1} F(\rho).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Root-test pole barrier]\label{lem:abel_pole_barrier_root_test}
Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence with
\[
\lambda:=\limsup_{n\to\infty} |a_n|^{1/n}\in[0,\infty].
\]
Then $F(\rho)=\sum_{n\ge 0} a_n \rho^n$ converges absolutely for $0\le \rho<1/\lambda$ (with the convention $1/0=\infty$) and diverges for $\rho>1/\lambda$.
In particular, if $\lambda>1$ then the Abel path $\rho\uparrow 1$ necessarily encounters a singularity barrier at $\rho=1/\lambda<1$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the standard root test applied to the power series with coefficients $a_n$.
For $\rho<1/\lambda$, one has $\limsup_n |a_n\rho^n|^{1/n}=\rho\lambda<1$, so the series converges absolutely.
For $\rho>1/\lambda$, the limsup exceeds $1$, so the terms do not tend to zero and the series diverges.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Connection to the $\e$-channel language]\label{rem:abel_pole_barrier_connection}
In this paper, the phrase ``Abel pole barrier'' refers to the first singularity that obstructs analytic continuation along the Abel path in the relevant generating function (Section~\ref{subsec:e_channel}).
The Artin--Mazur zeta framework supplies a canonical class of such generating functions in symbolic dynamics and provides a standard way to encode stability information through analyticity and singularity structure \cite{ArtinMazur1965,ParryPollicott1990,Ruelle1978}.
\end{remark}
