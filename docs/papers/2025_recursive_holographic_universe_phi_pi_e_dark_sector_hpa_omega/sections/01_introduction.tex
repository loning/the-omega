
\section{Introduction: from continuous fields to discrete readout}
\label{sec:introduction}

The $\Lambda$CDM model provides an extraordinarily successful effective description of cosmological observations, from the cosmic microwave background to large-scale structure \cite{Planck2018CosmoParams}.
At the same time, three structural tensions persist at the level of microscopic interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
\item dark matter and dark energy enter as effective components without a unified geometric--informational origin;
\item early-universe inflation is often implemented by adding extra degrees of freedom and tuning a potential;
\item black-hole information puzzles suggest that ``local quantum fields on a smooth spacetime manifold'' may not be the final ontology.
\end{itemize}
For background on black-hole thermodynamics and the information problem, see \cite{Bekenstein1973,Hawking1975,Page1993}.

Motivated by holography, tensor-network models, causal sets, and computational viewpoints, we adopt a concrete working thesis:
\emph{spacetime is a readout effect}.
For representative entry points, see \cite{tHooft1993,Susskind1995,Bousso2002,Vidal2007MERA,Swingle2012MERA,BombelliLeeMeyerSorkin1987}.
More precisely, macroscopic geometry is treated as the output of a finite-resolution scanning process projected onto a locality-preserving address grid.
In this view, the primary objects are not continuous field values but the discrete structure of the readout hardware and its stability selection mechanisms.

\subsection{A layered model: phase arithmetic, readout grids, and folding}
\label{subsec:intro_layered}

We organize the framework into three layers.

\paragraph{Ontic layer (phase arithmetic).}
The microscopic substrate is modeled by discrete phase arithmetic, abstracted by finite rings such as $\ZZ_{2^p}$.
Throughout this paper, $\ZZ_{128}$ is used as a representative resolution label: it emphasizes that phases are discrete registers rather than continuous angles.
The specific choice of $128$ is not essential; it serves as a minimal nontrivial example of a finite phase alphabet compatible with recursion.

\begin{definition}[$\ZZ_{128}$ phase register and a minimal readout interface]
\label{def:z128_interface}
Let $\theta\in\ZZ_{128}$ denote a discrete phase register.
A minimal deterministic readout interface from $\ZZ_{128}$ to a local $6$-bit word is any fixed map
\[
R:\ZZ_{128}\to \Omega_6,
\]
which may represent a device-specific coarse quantization (e.g.\ thresholding or a fixed lookup).
The $\varphi$--$\pi$--$\e$ stability selection then acts on the readout word $R(\theta)$ rather than on $\theta$ directly.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Phenomenal layer (Hilbert-addressed readout).}
The observed ``spacetime'' is a readout on a Hilbert-addressed grid.
At spatial order $n$, a Hilbert map $H_n$ assigns a one-dimensional scan index to a two-dimensional $2^n\times 2^n$ lattice, with the defining property that consecutive indices map to nearby lattice points \cite{Hilbert1891,Sagan1994}.

\paragraph{Interface (projection and resolution folding).}
The interface between the two layers is implemented by a stability projection and a finite-resolution folding map.
The projection selects a stable type set (a subspace of the local readout Hilbert space), while the folding map sends arbitrary microstates to their stable representatives.
This interface is both computable and scalable: it can be iterated across increasing resolution.

\subsection{Minimal falsifiable anchor: the \texorpdfstring{$64\to 21$}{64->21} stable projection}
\label{subsec:intro_minimal}

The paper is anchored at a fully explicit, minimal finite model.
We model a local readout window by a $6$-bit word space
\[
\Omega_6:=\{0,1\}^6,\qquad |\Omega_6|=64,
\]
and its linearization $\mathcal{H}_6:=\ell^2(\Omega_6)$.
Stability is organized by three commuting constraint channels, denoted $\varphi$--$\pi$--$\e$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{($\varphi$) grammar stability.} The golden-mean (Zeckendorf) constraint forbids adjacent ones and selects the admissible set $X_6\subset\Omega_6$ with $|X_6|=21$ \cite{Zeckendorf1972,LindMarcus1995}.
\item \textbf{($\pi$) topological closure.} A cyclic (wrap-around) refinement splits $X_6$ canonically as $21=18\oplus 3$, separating cyclic states from boundary states.
\item \textbf{($\e$) analytic stability.} An Abel--zeta pole-barrier viewpoint characterizes stability in spectral terms and naturally extends to weighted pressure mechanisms \cite{Ruelle1978,ParryPollicott1990}.
\end{itemize}

The decisive feature of the minimal model is that it is entirely computable:
we use a folding map $\mathrm{Fold}_6:\{0,\dots,63\}\twoheadrightarrow X_6$ defined by Zeckendorf normalization and length-$6$ truncation \cite{Ma2025RF}.
Its full preimage structure is explicit and rigid, yielding a fixed $(2,3,4)$ degeneracy law and a complete table (Appendix~\ref{app:fold6_table}).

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{subsec:intro_contributions}

The contributions are organized around falsifiable identifications rather than purely narrative interpretation:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Dark-sector definition from finite folding.}
We define ghost microstates as the hidden degrees of freedom eliminated by the stable projection: at window length $6$, the count is $64-21=43$.
\item \textbf{Three-channel stability unification.}
We treat matter stability as the intersection of three commuting channels: syntactic legality ($\varphi$), topological closure ($\pi$), and analytic pole-barrier stability ($\e$).
\item \textbf{A dynamical interface via routing overhead.}
Using the HPA--$\Omega$ action framework \cite{Ma2025CAP,Ma2025FoP}, we couple folding statistics to an effective routing/compilation overhead field, which in turn couples to the metric via an effective lapse.
\item \textbf{Falsifiable predictions.}
We state data-level tests: Hilbert-address clustering statistics for the dark sector, geometric drift of effective constants in high-overhead regions \cite{Ma2025PCG}, and scanline artifacts and spectral-template biases tied to discrete readout recursion.
\end{itemize}
