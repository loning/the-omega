\section{Causal Structure Interface: Light-Cone Boundary Readout and the Role of ``Constant Propagation Speed''}

\subsection{Light-Cone Boundary Readout}

In the relativistic context, the information available to an observer is constrained by causal structure: accessible data is primarily located at field data on the past light-cone or light-like boundary. This paper formulates this fact at the protocol layer as: \textbf{empirical records can be approximately modeled as restriction and re-encoding of the global state on light-like boundaries}. Formally, one can extend the readout map as
\[
\mathcal{R}_{\theta}:\ (\text{global state/lattice configuration}) \ \longrightarrow\  (\text{light-cone boundary data stream})\ \longrightarrow\  (\text{discrete records}).
\]
In this interface, ``constant propagation speed'' is not interpreted as ``everything consists of light,'' but rather as: it sets the calibration ratio from scan iteration (protocol time) to causally-reachable set, and constrains the geometric shape of observable information channels.

\subsection{Compatibility with This Framework}

The cut-and-project--scan readout framework does not require reducing all degrees of freedom to massless propagating modes. Rather, it allows:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Mass, interactions, etc. to appear as structural cost of stable sectors and defect channels;
  \item Light-cone boundary readout as an interface layer for ``observational accessibility constraints'' to be added, without replacing the ontological-layer high-dimensional phase lattice assumption.
\end{itemize}
Therefore, under strict formulation, the empirically-observed ``readout records dominated by boundary data under light-cone constraints'' is compatible with this framework, but does not logically entail ``everything is merely light-like projection.''
