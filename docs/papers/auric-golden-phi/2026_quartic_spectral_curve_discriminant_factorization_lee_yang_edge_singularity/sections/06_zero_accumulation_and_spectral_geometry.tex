\section{Zero accumulation and the spectral geometry of the boundary}\label{sec:zeros}

\subsection{Background: the Beraha--Kahane--Weiss theorem}

The accumulation behavior of zeros of recursively defined polynomial families is governed by the classical theorem of Beraha, Kahane, and Weiss~\cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1980}.
In its simplest form, the theorem states that if $\{P_m(y)\}$ satisfies a fixed-order linear recurrence with polynomial coefficients, then the limiting set of zeros (as $m\to\infty$) consists of parameter values $y$ for which two or more dominant roots of the characteristic polynomial are equimodular.
This result has been applied extensively in graph theory and statistical mechanics: Beraha, Kahane, and Weiss~\cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1980} used it to study accumulation points of chromatic-polynomial zeros, and Sokal~\cite{Sokal2001} applied related techniques to antiferromagnetic Potts models.

For our model, the characteristic polynomial is $\Pi(\lambda,y)$ and the family $\{Z_m(y)\}$ is defined by the order-$4$ recurrence~\eqref{eq:recurrence}.
We state the relevant specialization below.

\subsection{Application to the present model}

For each fixed $y$, the sequence $\{Z_m(y)\}$ admits a spectral representation
\[
Z_m(y)=\sum_{i=1}^4 a_i(y)\lambda_i(y)^m,
\]
with $\lambda_i(y)$ the roots of $\Pi(\lambda,y)$ and coefficient functions $a_i(y)$ determined by the initial values.
When $y$ varies, the limiting geometry of the zero sets $\{y: Z_m(y)=0\}$ is controlled by equimodular competition among the dominant roots.

\begin{theorem}[Beraha--Kahane--Weiss-type zero accumulation]\label{thm:bkw}
Assume $y\in\CC\setminus\Branch$ and that the roots $\lambda_i(y)$ of $\Pi(\cdot,y)$ are simple.
If there exist indices $i\neq j$ such that
\[
\abs{\lambda_i(y)}=\abs{\lambda_j(y)}=\max_k \abs{\lambda_k(y)},
\]
and the corresponding coefficient functions do not vanish simultaneously, then $y$ belongs to the limiting accumulation set of zeros of $\{Z_m\}$ as $m\to\infty$.
Moreover, points in the branch locus $\Branch$ may arise as endpoints of such accumulation curves in the complex $y$-plane.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
Theorem~\ref{thm:bkw} is a direct specialization of the general Beraha--Kahane--Weiss theory~\cite{BerahaKahaneWeiss1975,BerahaKahaneWeiss1980} to the present order-$4$ setting.
The theorem itself is not new; our contribution is the observation that for this particular spectral polynomial, the branch locus $\Branch$ is completely determined by Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor}, which makes the endpoint structure of the equimodular curves explicitly computable.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Equimodular structure on the real axis}

In the present model, the discriminant factorization (Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor}) gives a complete algebraic description of $\Branch$.
Numerical root-tracking reveals the following picture for $y$ on the real axis:

\begin{enumerate}
\item For $y>0$ sufficiently close to $1$, the dominant spectral root $\lambda_+(y)$ is real and strictly larger in modulus than all other roots (Theorem~\ref{thm:lambda-plus}).
\item As $y$ decreases through $0$ (a degenerate branch point), the root configuration reorganizes: for $y$ slightly below $0$, a complex conjugate pair emerges among the spectral roots.
\item At a non-branch parameter value $y_{\mathrm{eq}}\approx -0.16$, the modulus of this complex conjugate pair overtakes the real dominant root, creating a pair of equimodular dominant roots.
Since complex conjugate roots of a real polynomial automatically satisfy $\abs{\lambda_i}=\abs{\bar\lambda_i}$, the equimodular condition holds for all $y<y_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
\item This equimodular dominance persists through the branch point $\yLY\approx -1.13$, where the subdominant pair undergoes a real-to-complex transition (Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision}), and continues for all $y<\yLY$.
\end{enumerate}

By Theorem~\ref{thm:bkw}, zeros of $Z_m(y)$ accumulate along the negative real axis in the region where the dominant pair is equimodular.
The branch locus $\Branch$ controls the endpoints and topology of equimodular curves in the full complex $y$-plane; a complete numerical study of these curves is beyond the scope of this paper.
