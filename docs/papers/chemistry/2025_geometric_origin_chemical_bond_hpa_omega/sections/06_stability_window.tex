\section{A rigorous stability window: why chemistry is not destroyed by collapse}
\label{sec:stability_window}

Sections~\ref{sec:alpha_geo}--\ref{sec:chemistry_scaling} showed that $(\alpha_{\mathrm{em}},\mu)$ rigidly fix the absolute atomic-unit scale and the BO hierarchy. A reviewer will rightly ask a sharper question:
\begin{quote}
Do these inputs merely set \emph{scales}, or do they also ensure \emph{stability} (no catastrophic collapse) in the underlying Coulombic matter model?
\end{quote}
This section records the relevant rigorous facts from mathematical physics. We do not reprove them; they are mature results and are used as external anchors.

\subsection{Nonrelativistic Coulomb matter is stable (bounded below)}
Consider the standard nonrelativistic many-body Coulomb Hamiltonian for electrons and nuclei (with fixed nuclear charges and masses). The \emph{stability of matter} problem asks whether the ground-state energy is bounded below linearly in particle number (so that macroscopic matter does not collapse as $N\to\infty$). This is a classic theorem: stability holds for nonrelativistic Coulomb matter, established in foundational works and summarized in modern expositions \cite{DysonLenard1967,DysonLenard1968,LiebThirring1975,LiebSeiringer2010}.

For the present paper, the consequence is simple: in the standard nonrelativistic Coulomb model that underlies quantum chemistry, the Hamiltonian is well-defined and extensive. Therefore, once the absolute scale is fixed by $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ and the nuclear slowness by $\mu$, there is no hidden ``instability loophole'' that would forbid chemistry.

\paragraph{Minimal assumptions (external theorem input).}
The stability-of-matter theorems are statements about the standard quantum many-body Coulomb model under physically standard hypotheses: fermionic electrons (Pauli principle), Coulomb interactions, and (for thermodynamic stability statements) appropriate charge constraints and/or neutrality conditions. Precise formulations and assumptions are given in the cited mathematical physics literature \cite{LiebSeiringer2010}.

\paragraph{Quantitative form (stability of the second kind).}
One standard formulation is that the ground-state energy $E_0(N,K)$ of a Coulomb system with $N$ electrons and $K$ nuclei admits a linear lower bound
\begin{equation}
E_0(N,K)\ge -C\,(N+K),
\end{equation}
for a constant $C$ independent of $N,K$ (under the stated hypotheses). This is the mathematical statement that macroscopic matter does not collapse as particle number grows \cite{LiebSeiringer2010}.

\subsection{Relativistic constraints give an \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}-window}
When relativistic kinematics are included (Dirac-type operators and/or magnetic fields), stability becomes nontrivial and depends on $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ and nuclear charges $Z_A$. There are sharp one-particle thresholds and distinguished self-adjointness regimes for Dirac--Coulomb operators, as well as sufficient conditions for many-body stability; see \cite{Thaller1992,Thirring2002,LiebSeiringer2010} for detailed statements and references. A widely used stability indicator is the requirement that $Z\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ remain below the critical $O(1)$ thresholds associated with Dirac--Coulomb collapse (point-nucleus heuristics often quote $Z\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}<1$ as the basic bound-state regime).

In particular, $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}\approx 1/137$ lies deep in the weak-coupling regime relative to the known relativistic thresholds (which are $O(1)$), and therefore does not trigger relativistic collapse for the range of nuclear charges realized in chemistry. The inequality $Z\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}<1$ is used here only as an intuitive window marker; rigorous statements depend on the precise operator model (point nucleus vs.\ extended charge, single-particle vs.\ many-body, projection choices) and are detailed in the cited references.

\paragraph{A concrete numerical bound for the periodic table.}
For the largest nuclear charges realized in nature ($Z\le 118$), one has
\[
Z\,\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}\lesssim \frac{118}{137}\approx 0.861,
\]
which lies below the standard point-nucleus Dirac--Coulomb bound-state threshold $Z\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}<1$ and (numerically) even below the classical essential self-adjointness window quoted in the mathematical literature (see, e.g., \cite{Thaller1992} for precise statements). This makes the ``relativistic collapse'' concern quantitatively irrelevant for chemistry at observed $Z$.

\subsection{Closed-theory implication: \texorpdfstring{$\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}$}{alpha\_geo} and \texorpdfstring{$\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}$}{mu\_geo} land inside the window}
In the HPA--$\Omega$ constants program, the minimal closed-theory model fixes
\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi,
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}=6\pi^5.
\]
Numerically, $\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}\simeq 7.297\times 10^{-3}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}\simeq 1.836\times 10^3$ \cite{Ma2025PCG}. Two derived quantities control chemistry directly:
\begin{equation}
\frac{E_h}{m_e c^2}=\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^2,
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BO}}\sim \mu^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
Thus the closed-theory prediction implies
\[
\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^2\simeq 5.33\times 10^{-5},
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1/2}\simeq 2.33\times 10^{-2},
\]
which simultaneously (i) pushes the chemical scale down from MeV to eV and (ii) enforces a robust electronic/vibrational/rotational hierarchy. Moreover, the small interface mismatches between $(\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}},\mu_{\mathrm{geo}})$ and operational constants are at the $10^{-6}$--$10^{-5}$ level \cite{Ma2025PCG,CODATA2022RMP}; their propagated effect on chemical scales is correspondingly tiny.

\paragraph{Propagation to chemical scales.}
At the level of atomic units,
\[
a_0\propto \alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1},
\qquad
E_h\propto \alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{2},
\qquad
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BO}}\sim \mu^{-1/2}.
\]
Therefore a relative perturbation $\delta\alpha/\alpha$ induces
\[
\frac{\delta a_0}{a_0}=-\frac{\delta\alpha}{\alpha},
\qquad
\frac{\delta E_h}{E_h}=2\,\frac{\delta\alpha}{\alpha},
\]
while a relative perturbation $\delta\mu/\mu$ induces
\[
\frac{\delta(\mu^{-1/2})}{\mu^{-1/2}}=-\frac12\,\frac{\delta\mu}{\mu}.
\]
With the interface sizes quoted in \cite{Ma2025PCG}, the propagated effects remain at the few-ppm level for $(a_0,E_h)$ and at the $\sim 10^{-5}$ level for $\mu^{-1/2}$.
