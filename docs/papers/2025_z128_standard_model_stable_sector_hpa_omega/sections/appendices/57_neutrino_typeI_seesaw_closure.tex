\section{Type-I seesaw scale (audit)}
\label{app:neutrino_typeI_seesaw_closure}

This appendix records a standard Type-I seesaw bridge as a UV completion of the Weinberg operator.
The goal is to make explicit what is (and is not) fixed by the protocol-selected low-energy neutrino spectrum under conventional EFT assumptions.

\paragraph{Seesaw assumption (standard).}
\AuditTag We assume heavy gauge-singlet fermions (right-handed neutrinos) exist in the EFT completion (already present as $\nu_R$ in the closed labeling; Appendix~\ref{app:yukawa_beta_protocol_closure}).
In a minimal one-scale estimate, the light neutrino scale is related to a heavy Majorana scale $M_R$ and an effective Dirac Yukawa magnitude $y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}$ by \cite{Minkowski1977Seesaw}
\[
m_{\nu,\max}\;\approx\;\frac{v^2}{2M_R}\,y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}^2,
\qquad
M_R=\frac{v^2\,y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}^2}{2\,m_{\nu,\max}}.
\]
This approximation is sufficient to expose the scale logic; determining full textures requires additional flavor assumptions beyond the minimal content fixed by oscillation observables.

\paragraph{Bounded Yukawa-magnitude candidate family.}
\AuditTag To keep the hypothesis class finite and auditable, we consider a bounded candidate set for $y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}$:
\begin{itemize}
\item $y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}=1$ (a conventional ``order-one'' benchmark);
\item $y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}$ equal to one of the closed Yukawa eigenvalues of the up-type sector $u_R^{(g)}$ and the charged-lepton sector $e_R^{(g)}$ derived from the depth template (Appendix~\ref{app:yukawa_beta_protocol_closure}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Two selection modes and a minimal failure point.}
\AuditTag We report:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{cap}: a protocol-only complexity rule (prefers the benchmark $y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}=1$);
\item \texttt{match}: a threshold-alignment rule that minimizes mismatch to the closed staircase thresholds $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(m)$.
\end{itemize}
Their divergence quantifies the \emph{minimal underdetermination}: without an additional physics assumption that fixes $y_{\nu,\mathrm{eff}}$ (or the flavor structure of $Y_\nu$ and $M_R$), the seesaw scale is not uniquely determined by the low-energy spectrum alone.
