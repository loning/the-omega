\section{From $(\alpha_{\mathrm{em}},\mu)$ to chemistry: atomic units and Born--Oppenheimer hierarchy}
\label{sec:chemistry_scaling}

This section isolates a key structural fact: in a nonrelativistic Coulombic molecule, $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ fixes the absolute units (Hartree and Bohr) while $\mu$ fixes the small parameter controlling nuclear motion. Consequently, once $(\alpha_{\mathrm{em}},\mu)$ are treated as rigid protocol invariants rather than empirical inputs, two necessary prerequisites of chemistry---an absolute eV-scale and a robust BO hierarchy---become rigid consequences of protocol geometry (with stability anchored separately in Section~\ref{sec:stability_window}).

\subsection{Atomic units: why chemistry lives at the eV scale}
In standard physics, atomic units are defined by the Bohr radius and Hartree energy \cite{CODATA2022RMP}:
\begin{equation}
a_0=\frac{\hbar}{m_e c\,\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}},
\qquad
E_h=m_e c^2\,\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^2,
\qquad
\mathrm{Ry}=\frac12 E_h.
\end{equation}
Numerically, $a_0\approx 0.529\,\text{\AA}$ and $E_h\approx 27.21\,\mathrm{eV}$ \cite{CODATA2022RMP}.
In SI units, $m_e c^2$ is a MeV-scale quantity, and $E_h$ is suppressed by $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^2\sim 10^{-4}$, landing at the characteristic chemical scale of tens of eV. Typical bond energies are fractions of a Hartree, so eV-scale chemistry is a direct consequence of a dimensionless weak coupling.
See, e.g., standard molecular quantum mechanics discussions in \cite{AtkinsFriedman2011}.

In the HPA--$\Omega$ constants program, $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ is not a free input: the minimal closed-theory model predicts $\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:alpha_three_channel_chem}). Hence the absolute chemical energy scale is locked by protocol geometry, up to the explicit interface matching $s_\alpha$.

\subsection{Dimensionless molecular Hamiltonian in atomic units (model assumptions)}
Consider a molecule with $N$ electrons (mass $m_e$) and nuclei labeled by $A$ with charges $Z_A$ and masses $M_A$. In atomic units (length in $a_0$, energy in $E_h$), the nonrelativistic Coulomb Hamiltonian takes the dimensionless form \cite{SzaboOstlund1996}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:H_atomic_units}
\frac{H}{E_h}
=
-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{N}\nabla_i'^2
-\frac12\sum_A \frac{1}{\mu_A}\nabla_A'^2
-\sum_{i,A}\frac{Z_A}{r_{iA}'}
\,+\sum_{i<j}\frac{1}{r_{ij}'}
\,+\sum_{A<B}\frac{Z_AZ_B}{R_{AB}'},
\end{equation}
where $\mu_A:=M_A/m_e$ and primes denote coordinates in units of $a_0$.
This is the standard nonrelativistic point-charge Coulomb model used as the baseline of quantum chemistry; relativistic, QED, and finite-nuclear-size effects are treated as corrections at higher precision \cite{AtkinsFriedman2011,SzaboOstlund1996}.

Two points are structurally decisive:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ no longer appears in the \emph{dimensionless} Hamiltonian \eqref{eq:H_atomic_units}; it only survives through the choice of absolute units $a_0$ and $E_h$.
\item All nuclear slowness enters through the large parameters $\mu_A$ (in particular $\mu$ for hydrogenic nuclei).
\end{itemize}
Thus $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ fixes the overall absolute scale of chemistry, while $\mu$ fixes the expansion parameter governing nuclear dynamics.

\subsection{Born--Oppenheimer parameter and spectral hierarchy}
For diatomic molecules with identical nuclei of mass $M\sim m_p$, a standard BO small parameter is
\begin{equation}
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{BO}}\sim \sqrt{\frac{m_e}{M}} \sim \mu^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
This parameter controls (i) nonadiabatic couplings between electronic surfaces and (ii) the hierarchy of energy scales. Dimensionally, one obtains the standard estimates
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{elec}}\sim E_h,
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{vib}}\sim \frac{E_h}{\sqrt{\mu}},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{rot}}\sim \frac{E_h}{\mu}.
\end{equation}
For $\mu\simeq 1836$, one has $\mu^{-1/2}\simeq 0.023$, consistent with the robust empirical hierarchy (electronic $\gg$ vibrational $\gg$ rotational).
See standard molecular quantum mechanics texts for these scalings and their refinements \cite{AtkinsFriedman2011,SzaboOstlund1996}.

\subsection{A stability criterion: zero-point motion versus well depth}
Molecular ``geometry'' is meaningful only if nuclear zero-point motion does not wash out the electronic potential well. Let $D_e$ denote a typical electronic well depth (in Hartree units) and approximate the vibrational frequency near equilibrium by $\omega\sim \sqrt{k/\mu_{\mathrm{red}}}$, where $\mu_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a nuclear reduced mass in electron-mass units and $k$ is the dimensionless curvature of the BO curve.
Then the vibrational zero-point energy scales as
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{ZPE}}\sim \frac12 \omega \propto \mu^{-1/2}.
\end{equation}
A minimal stability requirement is $E_{\mathrm{ZPE}}\ll D_e$, which becomes a scale inequality of the form
\begin{equation}
\mu^{1/2}\gg \frac{1}{\eta},
\qquad
D_e\sim \eta\,E_h,\ \ \eta\in(10^{-2},10^{-1})\ \text{(typical)}.
\end{equation}
With $\mu^{1/2}\sim 43$, the inequality is naturally satisfied for a wide range of chemical $\eta$, explaining why stable molecular geometries are abundant.
The order-of-magnitude range for $\eta$ is consistent with textbook bond-energy scales in atomic units \cite{AtkinsFriedman2011}.

\subsection{Isotope effects as $\mu$-controlled interface}
Replacing a nucleus by an isotope changes $M$ and hence $\mu_A$ while leaving $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ unchanged. Consequently vibrational frequencies and zero-point energies shift as $M^{-1/2}$, producing isotopic shifts in spectra and kinetic isotope effects. In the HPA--$\Omega$ viewpoint, this is not an empirical coincidence: isotope effects are direct consequences of the same internal phase-volume invariant $\mu$ that enforces the BO hierarchy.
