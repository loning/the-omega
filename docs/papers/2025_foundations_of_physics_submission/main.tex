\begin{abstract}
We propose and systematize a unified cosmological framework---Omega Theory---starting from the geometry of Hilbert space and the principle of finite information. Based on the ``Omega Axiom,'' the physical universe is ontologically isomorphic to a single, static, normalized vector $|\Omega\rangle$ in an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space. All dynamics and history are merely results of spectral decomposition and phase rearrangement of this vector under different orthogonal bases. Combining the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy with the Holographic Principle, we introduce the ``Finite Information Axiom'' and the ``Holographic Mapping Axiom,'' proving that the effective Hilbert space dimension of any finite causal region is finite and can be encoded as area and entropy on a lower-dimensional holographic boundary, thus unifying the black hole area law with the detailed cosmic information budget.

Building on this, we introduce a Quantum Cellular Automaton (QCA) on a quasicrystal background and its Dirac limit (DQCA). We demonstrate that under the constraints of finite information and causal locality, the Dirac equation and Lorentz symmetry must emerge in the continuum limit, reducing the wavefunction to the statistical appearance of discrete informational jumps. By introducing the ``Golden Unitary Operator'' driven by a Golden Ratio spectrum, we construct an evolution that is ergodic but non-periodic in the projective Hilbert space, discussing how it manifests as a Fibonacci spiral universe that infinitely approaches an ``Omega Point'' without closure in the holographic projection.

At the dynamical level, we define the Omega Action containing Fisher information terms and holographic constraints, providing a unified geometric-informational derivation for gravitational field equations, variable-constant cosmology, and the dark energy dilution law. At the computational ontological level, we treat the universe as an Interactive Quantum Automaton. We prove that a strictly closed unitary evolution must encounter Gödelian dynamical incompleteness; we therefore introduce a self-referential ``Observer Operator'' term, clarifying the physical necessity of measurement in maintaining complexity growth. Finally, we provide several testable predictions, including the spatial dipole drift of the fine-structure constant, energy-dependent dispersion of high-energy gamma-ray photons, and holographic ``pixel noise'' in gravitational wave waveforms, qualitatively comparing these with standard $\Lambda$CDM predictions. Detailed mathematical proofs for the octonionic algebra ($G_2$), geometric constant derivations ($\alpha^{-1}, \mu$), and the DQCA continuum limit are provided in the Appendices.
\end{abstract}
