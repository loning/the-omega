\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We reframed the ``existence of chemistry'' problem in the HPA--$\Omega$ scan--readout paradigm. In standard quantum chemistry, chemical bonding and molecular stability depend critically on two empirical dimensionless inputs: the fine-structure constant $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ and the proton--electron mass ratio $\mu$. In the HPA layer discipline, these are instead treated as protocol-geometric invariants.

Using the companion constants-geometry results, we recorded the closed-theory theorem-level values $\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}=6\pi^5$ and showed how they control the two structural pillars of chemistry:
(i) $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ fixes atomic units and hence the absolute eV-scale of chemical energies, and
(ii) $\mu$ fixes the BO small parameter $\mu^{-1/2}$ and thereby the hierarchical separation of electronic/vibrational/rotational spectra, molecular geometry stability, and isotope effects.

We further anchored these scale statements with rigorous stability-of-matter results, placing $\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}$ deep inside known stability regimes, and used the textbook $\mathrm{H}_2^+$ Born--Oppenheimer curve as the minimal molecule-level reference that exhibits a bound minimum and the $\mu^{-1/2}$ control of nuclear zero-point motion. An optional mismatch-based interface template consistent with HPA phase thermodynamics is recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:interface_phase_friction}; specifying and auditing concrete models for $\mathcal{D}_\varepsilon$ and $S_{\mathrm{pf}}$ is a natural next step.
