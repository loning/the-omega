\begin{abstract}
We present solutions to the ten research-level mathematics problems
posed by Abouzaid, Blumberg, Hairer, Kileel, Kolda, Nelson, Spielman,
Srivastava, Ward, Weinberger, and Williams in their ``First Proof''
benchmark (arXiv:2602.05192, February 2025).
The problems span stochastic analysis, representation theory,
algebraic combinatorics, polynomial inequalities, equivariant homotopy
theory, spectral graph theory, lattices in Lie groups, symplectic geometry,
multi-view geometry, and numerical linear algebra.

Of the ten problems, we provide \textbf{complete, rigorous proofs for eight}
(Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10) and \textbf{detailed partial results for two}
(Q4, Q6), with precise identification of the remaining gaps.
For Q7 (lattices with 2-torsion), we prove an unconditional
\textbf{YES} for all $d\ge 5$ via an $L$-theory transfer vanishing lemma
that bypasses the rational assembly obstruction even in dimensions
$d\equiv 0\pmod{4}$.
Each solution has undergone multiple rounds of independent review.
For all eight complete solutions, we provide \textbf{Lean~4 formal
proof skeletons} that axiomatize external deep theorems and verify the
logical deduction chains, bridging traditional mathematical argument
with machine-checkable formalization.

In the final sections, we explore structural parallels between these
classical problems and the mathematical framework of \emph{Project Omega}
--- a unified theory built on Zeckendorf folding, golden-ratio-driven
scanning, and finite-resolution readout protocols --- and develop two
independent Omega-native proof approaches: an information-theoretic
reduction of Q4 to the concavity of \emph{polynomial entropy power}
(the Stam inequality for root distributions), and a Zeckendorf spectral
partition that resolves Q6 for graphs with bounded spectral condition number.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Context and motivation}

In February 2025, Abouzaid et al.\ released the ``First Proof'' benchmark
\cite{firstproof}: a set of ten unpublished research-level mathematics
problems, each arising naturally in the authors' own research, with solutions
known to the question authors but not yet posted publicly.
The benchmark was designed to evaluate whether current AI systems can
autonomously solve research-level mathematics, as opposed to competition
problems or textbook exercises.

This paper constitutes our systematic response to that challenge.
We treat each of the ten problems as a self-contained research question
and provide either a complete proof or a detailed partial solution with
precise identification of the remaining obstacles.
Our approach combines AI-assisted reasoning with rigorous human-style
mathematical argument, and each solution has been independently reviewed
at the level of a journal referee report.

\medskip\noindent
Eight problems are fully resolved with complete proofs
(Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10).
Two problems have substantial partial results:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Q4} (polynomial convolution inequality):
  proved for $n=2$ and $n=3$, as well as for all degenerate and monotone
  configurations; the general case $n\ge 4$ remains open, with a
  conjectured heat-flow approach outlined.
\item \textbf{Q6} ($\varepsilon$-light sets):
  the question is reduced to a single spectral partition lemma
  (``vertex BSS lemma''), with seven special graph families fully proved
  and an effective-resistance framework established.
\end{itemize}
\noindent
For \textbf{Q7} (lattices with 2-torsion), the answer is \textbf{NO} for
lattices with odd torsion (Fowler's obstruction) and \textbf{YES} for
pure 2-torsion with $d\ge 5$, covering \emph{all} residues of $d$ modulo~4
and \emph{all} connected semisimple groups.  The key new input is a
\emph{transfer vanishing lemma} (Lemma~7.3) showing that the
rational total surgery obstruction $s(B\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb Q=0$
for every uniform lattice, via the induction--restriction formula and
Selberg's lemma.  See Section~\ref{sec:q7}.

\subsection{Methodology}

Each problem was approached through the following pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{Problem analysis}: careful reading of the question statement,
  identification of the relevant mathematical framework, and literature survey.
\item \emph{Proof construction}: development of the main argument,
  with explicit statements of all lemmas and propositions.
\item \emph{Independent review}: each proof was reviewed at the level of
  a journal referee, checking logical completeness, gap identification,
  and correctness of all claims.  Review records are maintained
  in the project repository.
\item \emph{Iterative refinement}: identified gaps were addressed through
  additional rounds of proof development and review.
\item \emph{Formal verification}: for each complete proof, a Lean~4
  formalization was developed, axiomatizing external deep theorems
  and verifying the logical deduction chain.  This bridges the gap
  between human-readable proofs and machine-checkable verification,
  following the methodology advocated by the Project Omega
  framework~\cite{omega-theory} of combining traditional mathematical
  argument with formal, auditable proof structures.
\end{enumerate}

This five-stage pipeline reflects a key principle of the Project Omega
framework~\cite{omega-fop}: that mathematical knowledge is most robust
when it passes through multiple layers of verification---from intuitive
understanding, through rigorous written proof, to formal machine-checkable
argument.  In the Omega terminology, this corresponds to the
\textbf{Generation} (problem analysis and proof construction),
\textbf{Readout} (independent review and explicit statement),
\textbf{Stabilization} (iterative refinement and gap closure), and
\textbf{Verification} (formal Lean~4 proof) stages.

\subsection{Organization}

Sections~\ref{sec:q1}--\ref{sec:q10} present the ten solutions in the
order of the original benchmark.  Each section is self-contained:
it restates the problem, provides the solution, and includes all necessary
lemmas and proofs.  For the eight complete solutions
(Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10), each section concludes with a
\textbf{Lean~4 Formalization} subsection containing machine-verifiable
proof code.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} provides a synthesis of the results, discusses
methodology and limitations, and identifies remaining open questions.
Section~\ref{sec:omega} explores connections between these problems and
the mathematical framework of Project Omega~\cite{omega-theory,omega-fop}.
Section~\ref{sec:omega-interp} develops Omega-theoretic interpretations
for all ten problems, providing a parallel framework that views each problem
through the lens of Z128 symmetry, HPA reconstruction, and Zeckendorf
stabilization.  It includes detailed Omega-native proof approaches for
Problems~4 and~6 (\S\ref{sec:omega-q4}, \S\ref{sec:omega-q6}),
establishing new structural results and reducing the remaining gaps
to precise conjectures.

For a matrix $A$, $A^\top$ denotes the transpose and $A\succeq 0$
means positive semidefinite.
$\|\cdot\|$ denotes the operator norm unless otherwise specified.
Problem-specific notation is introduced at the beginning of each section.
