\subsection{Finite holonomy as curvature proxy (protocol \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{->} interface)}
\label{subsec:op3_holonomy_curvature_proxy}

\paragraph{Finite connection and gauge redundancy.}
At fixed resolution $(m,n)$, the protocol constructs a deterministic edge transport between padded fibers, yielding an $S_r$-valued connection on the addressing graph (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}; at the minimal anchor $(m,n)=(6,3)$ one has $r=4$).
Local relabelings of fiber slots act by conjugation on loop products, so loop holonomy is physical only through conjugacy invariants such as cycle type (Proposition~\ref{prop:cycle_type_gauge_invariant}).
This is the finite core of ``gauge redundancy'': a compensating transport rule is required by finite fibers, but it is defined only up to local basis relabeling (Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge_compensation}).

\paragraph{Curvature semantics.}
Section~\ref{subsec:curvature_as_loops} records the semantic bridge:
in any continuum dictionary with a connection one-form $A$, holonomy around an infinitesimal loop is controlled by the curvature $F=\dd A + A\wedge A$.
Accordingly, the protocol loop invariants are read as coarse-grained curvature proxies at the readout scale, without claiming that a microscopic connection field exists as a primitive object.

\subsection{Phase-lifted Wilson-loop diagnostics and a local action-density proxy}
\label{subsec:op3_wilson_loop_proxy}

\paragraph{Phase lift and effective unitaries.}
The holonomy pipeline of Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy} upgrades the finite $S_r$ transport to a phase-lifted monomial unitary transport in $U(r)$ using a dyadic phase register (Definition~\eqref{eq:holonomy_phase_def} and equation \eqref{eq:phase_lift_edge_transport}), and then projects the resulting loop holonomy to an effective $3\times 3$ unitary matrix by the sum-zero projection and deterministic Gram--Schmidt normalization (Remark~\ref{rem:gram_schmidt_failures}).
Appendix~\ref{app:holonomy_sweeps_extended} records extended sweeps of these finite diagnostics across a balanced refinement chain $(n,m)=(1,2),(2,4),\dots,(8,16)$ and across loop scales.

\paragraph{Continuum dictionary: $1-W$ as a gauge-action proxy.}
In standard lattice gauge theory, the Wilson plaquette observable provides a local proxy for the continuum action density.
Let $U_\square\in SU(N)$ denote the holonomy around a small square plaquette of side length $a$ in the $\mu\nu$-plane, built from a smooth connection $A$.
Then one has the small-loop expansion (see, e.g., \cite{Wilson1974,Rothe2012})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wilson_small_loop_expansion}
U_\square
=\exp\!\bigl(\iu\,a^{2}F_{\mu\nu} + O(a^{3})\bigr),
\qquad
1-\frac{1}{N}\RePart\Tr(U_\square)
=\frac{a^{4}}{2N}\Tr\!\bigl(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\bigr)+O(a^{6}),
\end{equation}
where $F_{\mu\nu}$ is the curvature of $A$.
Equation \eqref{eq:wilson_small_loop_expansion} is used here purely as an \emph{interface dictionary}:
it explains why the protocol-level scalar $A=1-W$ is the natural coarse readout proxy for a local gauge kinetic term at the resolution scale implicit in the loop family.

\subsection{CAP-minimal gauge-sector representative and Yang--Mills dynamics}
\label{subsec:op3_cap_yang_mills_representative}

\paragraph{Closure problem.}
The protocol provides (i) a notion of locality via the addressing graph, (ii) a finite gauge redundancy (local relabelings) and loop invariants, and (iii) a bounded-complexity closure discipline (Axiom~\ref{ax:cap}).
The continuum representative problem is therefore: within a finite family of local covariant invariants (Section~\ref{subsec:cap_action_candidate_family}), select a minimal gauge-sector action that is consistent with the holonomy/curvature semantics and introduces no hidden continuous degrees of freedom.

\paragraph{Representative uniqueness (gauge sector).}
Once one adopts the local redundancy$\Rightarrow$connection dictionary (Proposition~\ref{prop:local_sun_connection}) and restricts to local bulk terms built from at most two derivatives, the gauge-sector kinetic term is fixed:
the unique local quadratic gauge-invariant term at this derivative order is proportional to $\Tr(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$.
This is exactly the gauge-sector entry in the CAP candidate family and the resulting CAP-minimal representative action skeleton (Proposition~\ref{prop:cap_minimal_action_skeleton}).
Varying the representative then yields the Yang--Mills equations (Proposition~\ref{prop:ym_equation}).

\paragraph{Relation to the protocol EFT embedding.}
The ``protocol EFT'' recorded in Section~\ref{subsec:protocol_eft} (and collected in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_eft_embedding}) is therefore not an extra physical input: it is the same continuum representative written in standard field-theory notation, after the stable-sector selection and labeling have fixed the light degrees of freedom and their gauge assignments (Section~\ref{sec:sm_labeling_closure}).
In this sense, the finite holonomy diagnostics supply an auditable protocol-level curvature proxy, while CAP closes a minimal local continuum representative whose gauge sector is the Yang--Mills action.

\subsection{What is not claimed (scope)}
\label{subsec:op3_not_claimed_scope}

The closure in this appendix is a \emph{representative} closure: it identifies the minimal local continuum gauge dynamics compatible with the protocol invariants and the stated CAP discipline.
It does not address nonperturbative questions such as confinement or a Yang--Mills mass gap \cite{JaffeWitten2000YangMillsMassGap,Greensite2011Confinement}, which are outside the present paper's theorem-level folding core and are not required for the finite audited closures reported here.
Within this paper, confinement-sensitive quantities (e.g.\ quark ``masses'') are therefore treated strictly at the matching layer (Appendix~\ref{app:quark_mass_scheme_notes}), and standard scheme/threshold running is used only as a translation dictionary in the $r$ coordinate (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).
