\section{Protocol connections and finite holonomy diagnostics on the Hilbert grid}
\label{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}

This section records a minimal, auditable connection model that makes the slogan ``gauge fields as compensating connections'' concrete at finite resolution.
The construction is discrete, finite, and fully computable at the minimal balanced scale $(m,n)=(6,3)$.
It is used here as a protocol-level diagnostic: every choice is explicit, every search domain is finite, and the reported fit objectives reuse the same log-mismatch norms as in Definition~\ref{def:bounded_complexity_closure}.
No continuum limit is assumed in the computations below; any continuum interpretation is an interface hypothesis (Axiom~\ref{ax:gauge_compensation}).

\subsection{Fibers under projection: stable types with finite degeneracy}
\label{subsec:fiber_under_projection}

At $m=6$, the folding map $\mathrm{Fold}_6:\{0,\dots,63\}\to X_6$ is surjective and each stable type $w\in X_6$ has a finite preimage
\[
P(w):=\mathrm{Fold}_6^{-1}(w)\subset\{0,\dots,63\},
\qquad |P(w)|\in\{2,3,4\}.
\]
From the protocol viewpoint, $P(w)$ is a finite \emph{fiber} of microstates that project to the same stable readout label.
Comparing stable labels across space requires a convention for how these fibers are identified, which is the discrete origin of a compensating connection.

\subsection{A deterministic discrete connection on grid edges}
\label{subsec:deterministic_edge_connection}

Fix Hilbert order $n=3$ and embed indices $k\in\{0,\dots,63\}$ into an $8\times 8$ grid via Hilbert addressing (Section~\ref{sec:hilbert_addressing}).
Label each site by its stable type $w=\mathrm{Fold}_6(k)$.
For each undirected nearest-neighbor grid edge $\{a,b\}$, we define a deterministic transport map between the two endpoint fibers by the following rule:
\begin{itemize}
\item For each stable type $w$, list its fiber $P(w)$ in increasing order and pad it deterministically to length $4$ (the maximal degeneracy at $m=6$) by repeating its last element.
\item Define a cost between microstates by Hamming distance on their $6$-bit binary words.
\item Choose the minimum-cost bijection between the two length-$4$ padded fibers; this yields a permutation in $S_4$.
\item For the reverse orientation, use the inverse permutation.
\end{itemize}
This produces a discrete, non-abelian edge connection on edges, designed to be auditable and fully reproducible by a finite exhaustive search over $4!$ matchings per edge.

\subsection{A minimal \texorpdfstring{$SO(3)\subset SU(3)$}{SO(3) subset SU(3)} representation bridge}
\label{subsec:holonomy_su3_bridge}

To connect the discrete $S_4$ holonomy values to a continuous group action (as a skeleton for later mixing-matrix work), we use the standard permutation representation and its $3$-dimensional ``standard'' subrepresentation \cite{Serre1977}.
Concretely, let $\rho:S_4\to O(4)$ act by permutation matrices on $\RR^4$, and let
\[
H:=\Bigl\{x\in\RR^4:\ \sum_{i=1}^4 x_i=0\Bigr\},
\]
the sum-zero subspace. Then $H$ is $\rho$-invariant and $\dim H=3$.

\begin{lemma}[Sign-twisted standard representation $S_4\to SO(3)$]\label{lem:s4_so3_twist}
Let $\rho_H:S_4\to O(H)\cong O(3)$ be the restriction of the permutation representation to $H$.
Then $\det(\rho_H(\sigma))=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)$.
Consequently the twisted representation
\[
\widetilde\rho(\sigma):=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,\rho_H(\sigma)
\]
lands in $SO(3)$.
Viewing $SO(3)$ matrices as real unitary matrices gives an inclusion $SO(3)\subset SU(3)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Permutation matrices satisfy $\det(\rho(\sigma))=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)$.
Moreover $\RR^4$ decomposes as an orthogonal direct sum
\[
\RR^4=\mathrm{span}\{(1,1,1,1)\}\ \oplus\ H,
\]
and $\rho(\sigma)$ fixes $(1,1,1,1)$.
Thus $\det(\rho(\sigma))=\det(\rho(\sigma)|_{\mathrm{span}\{(1,1,1,1)\}})\cdot \det(\rho_H(\sigma))=1\cdot \det(\rho_H(\sigma))$, proving $\det(\rho_H(\sigma))=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)$.
Finally, on the $3$-dimensional space $H$, multiplication by the scalar $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\in\{\pm 1\}$ contributes determinant $\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)^3=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)$, hence
\[
\det(\widetilde\rho(\sigma))=\det(\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,I_3)\,\det(\rho_H(\sigma))=\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\cdot \mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=1,
\]
so $\widetilde\rho(\sigma)\in SO(3)$.
The inclusion $SO(3)\subset SU(3)$ follows because a real orthogonal $3\times 3$ matrix is unitary over $\CC$, and determinant $1$ is preserved.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A phase-register lift and a CP-odd invariant}
\label{subsec:holonomy_phase_lift_cp}

To allow CP-odd signatures in a holonomy-based description, one must go beyond purely real orthogonal representations.
As a minimal protocol-level bridge consistent with the $\ZZ_{128}$ phase-register stance, we can attach a deterministic discrete phase to each microstate and lift the edge transport to a unitary (phase-weighted) transport.
We make this lift fully explicit.
Fix a dyadic denominator $\mathrm{denom}=2^p$ and a low-complexity index map $\tau:\{0,\dots,63\}\to\{0,\dots,63\}$ chosen from the family
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{id}}(k)=k,\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{gray}}(k)=k\oplus (k\!\gg\!1),\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{bitrev}}(k)=\mathrm{bitreverse}_6(k),\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{not}}(k)=63-k.
\]
Here $\oplus$ and $\gg$ denote bitwise XOR and right shift on $6$-bit integers, and $\mathrm{bitreverse}_6$ reverses the $6$-bit binary expansion.
Define the phase register embedding
\begin{equation}\label{eq:holonomy_phase_def}
\phi(k):=2\pi\,\frac{\tau(k)}{\mathrm{denom}}.
\end{equation}
For an oriented edge $a\to b$ with padded fibers $f_a(i)$ and $f_b(i)$ ($i=0,1,2,3$) and transport permutation $p\in S_4$ (Section~\ref{subsec:deterministic_edge_connection}), define the phase-lifted edge transport $U_{a\to b}\in U(4)$ by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phase_lift_edge_transport}
(U_{a\to b})_{p(i),i}:=\exp\!\Bigl(\iu\bigl(\phi(f_b(p(i)))-\phi(f_a(i))\bigr)\Bigr),
\qquad i=0,1,2,3,
\end{equation}
with all other entries equal to $0$.
Projecting the resulting $4\times 4$ unitary plaquette holonomy to the $3$-dimensional sum-zero subspace and renormalizing by a deterministic Gram--Schmidt procedure yields an effective $3\times 3$ unitary matrix.
From this effective unitary one can compute a rephasing-invariant CP-odd quantity, e.g.\ the Jarlskog-type invariant
\[
J:=\Im\!\bigl(U_{11}U_{22}U_{12}^\ast U_{21}^\ast\bigr).
\]
This is the standard rephasing-invariant combination for $3\times 3$ unitary mixing matrices \cite{Jarlskog1985,PDGRPP2024}.

\paragraph{Angle extraction (PDG convention, diagnostic).}
From a $3\times 3$ unitary matrix $U$ in the PDG standard parameterization \cite{PDGRPP2024}, one can extract mixing sines by the identities
\[
s_{13}=|U_{13}|,\qquad
c_{13}=\sqrt{1-s_{13}^2},\qquad
s_{12}=\frac{|U_{12}|}{c_{13}},\qquad
s_{23}=\frac{|U_{23}|}{c_{13}},
\]
and obtain a principal Dirac phase $\delta$ from the Jarlskog invariant via
\[
\sin\delta=\frac{J}{s_{12}s_{23}s_{13}c_{12}c_{23}c_{13}^2},
\qquad
c_{ij}=\sqrt{1-s_{ij}^2}.
\]
In the holonomy diagnostics below, because row/column identifications are not fixed \emph{a priori}, we also allow a global relabeling (a pair of permutations in $S_3\times S_3$) when comparing to PMNS/CKM targets.

\subsection{A soft transport variant (temperature-like smoothing)}
\label{subsec:holonomy_soft_transport}

The strict minimum-cost matching rule produces a discrete $S_4$ transport on edges.
As an optional robustness diagnostic, one can form a \emph{soft} transport matrix from the full $4\times 4$ cost matrix between padded fibers, weighted by a temperature-like parameter $\beta$ via $\exp(-\beta\,\mathrm{cost})$, and then deterministically orthonormalize columns to obtain a unitary edge transport.
Sweeping $\beta$ interpolates between a highly mixed transport ($\beta\to 0$) and a sharp near-minimum transport (large $\beta$).
Tables~\ref{tab:holonomy_soft_transport_pmns} and \ref{tab:holonomy_soft_transport_ckm} report permutation-robust fits to PMNS- and CKM-style target sines under this soft transport, together with the mean $|J|$ on the resulting effective holonomies.
