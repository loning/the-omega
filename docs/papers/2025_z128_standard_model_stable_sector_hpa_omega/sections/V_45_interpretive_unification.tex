\paragraph{Real versus imaginary exponents.}
For parameters $\lambda\in\RR$ and $\omega\in\RR$, the complex exponential splits as
\[
\e^{(\lambda+\iu\omega)t} = \e^{\lambda t}\,\e^{\iu\omega t}.
\]
\AuditTag In the protocol language, the real factor $\e^{\lambda t}$ is the canonical continuous representative of a memoryless semigroup weight (Section~\ref{app:arrow_of_time_semigroup_notes}), while the phase factor $\e^{\iu\omega t}$ is the canonical representative of a unitary rotation at frequency $\omega$ (Appendix~\ref{app:quantum_measurement_born}).

\paragraph{Unitary spheres as a representation choice.}
In the standard quantum interface, states may be represented as unit vectors in a complex Hilbert space, so unitary evolution preserves the norm and acts as a trajectory on the unit sphere.
\AuditTag We use this only as a compact language for the readout/POVM interface; we do not promote ``the universe is literally a Hilbert sphere'' to an additional axiom.

\subsubsection*{Discrete update time and the stroboscopic artifact (what is definable here)}
\AuditTag The paper is built on tick-indexed protocol time $t\in\NN_0$ (Axiom~\ref{ax:readout_sequentiality}).
Any conversion to laboratory units or to a ``Planck frequency'' is a matching-layer calibration, not a theorem-level consequence
(Appendix~\ref{app:arrow_of_time_semigroup_notes}, Subsection~\ref{subsec:arrow_time_calibration_constants}).

\begin{definition}[Eventful updates and a discrete proper-time proxy]\label{def:tick_eventful_proper_time_proxy}
\MathTag Let $\mathcal{X}$ be a declared readout/state space at fixed protocol knobs (e.g.\ fixed $(m,n,K)$) and let $(x_t)_{t\in\NN_0}$ be a state sequence.
Fix any declared pseudometric $d$ on $\mathcal{X}$ (e.g.\ the discrete metric, or a mismatch metric in an audit contract).
Define the event indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{ev}}(t):=\mathbf{1}\{d(x_{t+1},x_t)>0\}$ and the cumulative event-count
\[
\tau(T):=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{ev}}(t).
\]
\end{definition}

\AuditTag Definition~\ref{def:tick_eventful_proper_time_proxy} is the minimal safe substitute for informal claims of ``vanishing time'':
if a readout is constant at the chosen coarse-graining, then $\tau(T)\equiv 0$.
It deliberately does not assert a thermodynamic statement (entropy production) or a GR statement (proper time of a spacetime metric),
both of which would require additional declared structures (Appendix~\ref{sec:limitations_related_work}, Subsection~\ref{subsec:rigidity_dpi_second_law}).

\AuditTag The ``stroboscopic'' phrasing can now be made precise:
a perceived smooth flow is an observer-side reconstruction map that replaces the tick-indexed sequence $(x_t)$ by a continuous representative $\tilde x(\lambda)$,
with $\lambda\in\RR$, such that $\tilde x(t)\approx x_t$ at integer times.
This reconstruction is a declared interface convention, and its scope is governed by the windowed-comparability contract
(Appendix~\ref{app:qg9_m1_windowed_comparability}).

\subsubsection*{Tunneling and wormholes: two internal carriers and one optional spectral dictionary}
\paragraph{Carrier A (closed mathematics): switching as finite-family selection under updates.}
\AuditTag The theorem-facing carrier for ``switching'' language in this manuscript is finite-family selection under deterministic tie-breaks
and its update/switching constraints (Appendix~\ref{app:branching_selection_rigidity}).
In particular, any ``jump'' between alternatives is represented as a change of the selected minimizer inside a declared finite family,
and robustness is controlled by gap-stability certificates (RB-D).

\paragraph{Carrier B (audited interface): wormhole-like channels as pointer jumps with ledgers.}
\AuditTag The manuscript also contains a fully explicit protocol-level model of a ``wormhole-like'' shortcut:
a pointer jump is an added nonlocal edge in Hilbert-index space (Definition~\ref{def:wormhole_pointer_jump}),
audited by shortest-path distance reduction tables (Appendix~\ref{app:bh_wormholes_pointer}).
When embedded in dynamics, the corresponding closure family is required to satisfy hard gates:
an explicit shortcut cost ledger $E_{\mathrm{wh}}$, complementarity certificates, and counterfactual wormhole-off baselines
(Section~\ref{sec:qg_interface_full_fusion}).
This is the correct internal meaning of ``wormhole'' in the z128 manuscript: a paid, auditable shortcut channel in readout geometry,
not a traversable Lorentzian wormhole in vacuum GR.

\paragraph{Carrier C (dictionary): spectral packaging and pole-barrier language.}
\AuditTag A third vocabulary, used as a bookkeeping dictionary across modules, packages orbit and update data into resolvents/determinants.
The operator-mother-space appendix records the minimal interface: trace-class resolvents, Fredholm determinants, Abel-first pole barriers,
and finite-rank pointer-jump updates as trace-class perturbations (Appendix~\ref{app:operator_mother_space}).
In this language, ``spectral coincidence'' can be read as equality (or near-equality) of declared spectral summaries
(e.g.\ logdet/resolvent-trace proxies) between two candidate packages, optionally after a shared Abel normalization.
The pole-barrier discipline then supplies an auditable stability gate: interior poles $|r_0|<1$ are obstructions to unit-disk holomorphy
(Appendix~\ref{app:operator_mother_space}, Remark~\ref{rem:interior_pole_obstruction_operator}).
