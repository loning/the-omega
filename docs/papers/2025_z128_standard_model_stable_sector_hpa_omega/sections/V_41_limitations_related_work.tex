\section{Limitations, scope, and relation to prior work}
\label{sec:limitations_related_work}

\subsection{Mathematical results vs.\ physical identifications}
The paper is organized around an auditable separation (Section~\ref{subsec:audit_layering}).
The folding statements at $(m,n)=(6,3)$ (e.g.\ $64\to 21$ and the $18\oplus 3$ split) are mathematical-layer results with explicit tables and reproducible scripts.
By contrast, the physical identifications (gauge sectors, chirality, antimatter, and CP violation) are recorded as \emph{interface} statements stated in protocol language (e.g.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge_compensation}, Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}, Definition~\ref{def:scl}, Definition~\ref{def:particles_as_types}).
The finite combinatorics alone is not a Lagrangian; the Standard Model interface is fixed here by explicit closure rules that are stated at the protocol layer and are audited by finite searches.
Concretely, the paper provides a rigid finite substrate ($X_6$ with intrinsic invariants) on which any identification must act, closed auditable interface maps at the level of discrete assignments (e.g.\ the labeling closure in Section~\ref{sec:sm_labeling_closure}), and falsifiable protocol-level predictions that go beyond post-hoc parameter matching (Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability}).
Within the declared protocol class, the remaining selection freedom is closed by deterministic bounded-complexity rules (Definition~\ref{def:bounded_complexity_closure}) and by explicit refinement maps (Appendix~\ref{app:functorial_refinement}); uniqueness/stability is recorded via rigidity certificates and stabilization tables throughout.
Appendix~\ref{app:inference_ledger} records the resulting ``no-new-input'' dependency ledger in a compact audit form, separating theorem-level consequences, finite protocol constructions, conditional interface implications, and bounded-complexity closures.

\subsection{On the CAP-selected anchor \texorpdfstring{$(m,n)=(6,3)$}{(m,n)=(6,3)}}
For the chosen $2$D Hilbert readout screen, the balanced coupling condition $m=2n$ is used to match two independent finite cardinalities:
the local readout alphabet size $|\Omega_m|=2^m$ and the number of sites on the Hilbert-addressed grid $4^n$ (Section~\ref{subsec:microstate_space}).
This is a diagnostic convention used to attach spatial statistics; it is not a theorem-level necessity for the folding core (Remark~\ref{rem:balanced_coupling_convention}).
Other addressing dimensions correspond to $m=dn$ rather than $m=2n$ (Remark~\ref{rem:balanced_coupling_dimension}), and other space-filling curves can be used as alternative readout bases (Remark~\ref{rem:why_2d_hilbert}).
The choice $(m,n)=(6,3)$ is then the smallest nontrivial instance of this match:
$2^6=4^3=64$.
This choice is an \emph{anchor scale} for an auditable finite model, not a claim that the dynamics must select $m=6$.
On the physical identification layer, we record a CAP selection principle for this anchor: within the balanced family on the chosen $2$D screen, the smallest pair for which the deterministic holonomy diagnostic becomes nontrivial is $(n,m)=(3,6)$.
Concretely, the balanced-chain sweep in Table~\ref{tab:holonomy_balanced_chain_sweep} shows that $n=1,2$ yield only trivial (identity) plaquette holonomy while $n=3$ is the first scale with nontrivial 3/4-cycle content.
This provides a low-complexity interface constraint whose compatibility with uplift and coarse-graining behavior is audited and falsifiable.
We partially control parameter sensitivity by recording $m$-sweeps and balanced refinement chains (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables} and Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}), which show that several structural counts (Fibonacci admissible sizes, cyclic/boundary split) persist across $m$.
To close the energy$\leftrightarrow$m interface at the protocol layer, we fix a deterministic staircase selection rule $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu)$ in Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution_selection}, together with an auditable calibration of the step size $r_{\mathrm{step}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:p3_resolution_jumps}).
At the purely mathematical level, the persistence statements are theorem-level: $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ and $|X_m^{\mathrm{bdry}}|=F_{m-2}$ for all $m$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:xm_fib} and Proposition~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_size}), and the truncation map $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ is surjective onto $X_m$ for all $m\ge 1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:foldm_surjective}).

\subsection{Rigidity targets, look-elsewhere context, and counterfactual baselines}
Several quantitative statements are presented as low-complexity rigidity targets (Section~\ref{sec:couplings_cp}).
We do not interpret these as ``agreement within experimental error bars'' when the reference uncertainties are far smaller than the quoted mismatches (e.g.\ $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}$), but as matching-layer factors between an idealized closed normalization and scheme/scale-dependent reference conventions.
To reduce the risk of numerology, we treat each target as a \emph{bounded-complexity closure} with an explicit finite candidate family and a fully specified tie-break rule (Definition~\ref{def:bounded_complexity_closure}).
Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables} records audit context beyond point estimates:
candidate-domain sizes, second-best gaps, distribution quantiles, stability under target perturbations, and deterministic counterfactual baselines (Tables~\ref{tab:audit_closure_metrics}--\ref{tab:audit_counterfactual}).
These audits provide look-elsewhere information within stated hypothesis classes; they are not a substitute for a complete statistical analysis over unrestricted expression families.

\paragraph{Error control from protocol outputs to continuum fields.}
Several falsifiability channels in this paper involve reconstructing a field $\chi(x)$ from discrete protocol statistics (Section~\ref{app:chi_reconstruction_protocol}) and then applying derivative operators (e.g.\ $\chi'(r)$ or $\Delta\chi$) to form weak-field observables such as $\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (Section~\ref{app:overhead_to_gravity_closure}).
Such steps are intrinsically noise-amplifying: finite differences can trade $O(h^2)$ truncation bias against $O(\epsilon/h)$ or $O(\epsilon/h^2)$ noise amplification, so any empirical use must declare the discretization/smoothing rules and report stability under explicit counterfactual baselines.
Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_to_continuum_error_control} records explicit concentration and propagation bounds in a self-contained audit form (see, e.g., standard references \cite{LeVeque2007FDM,BoucheronLugosiMassart2013,vanDerVaart1998}).
\paragraph{A minimal model-selection interpretation (within stated families).}
If one treats a candidate family $\Theta$ as a discrete hypothesis class equipped with the uniform prior, then the empirical frequency
\[
p_{\le \epsilon}:=\frac{N_{\le \epsilon}}{|\Theta|}
\]
in Table~\ref{tab:audit_closure_metrics} gives the probability that a uniformly random candidate achieves minimax log-mismatch $E_\infty\le \epsilon$.
When the minimizer is unique, the probability that a uniformly random candidate matches \emph{as well or better} than the selected minimizer is $1/|\Theta|$.
If the paper reports $K$ closures from (possibly different) discrete families $\Theta_1,\dots,\Theta_K$ and one adopts the same uniform-within-family reading, then a conservative global look-elsewhere upper bound is given by the union bound:
\[
\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\exists\,i:\ E_{\infty}^{(i)}\le \epsilon_i\Bigr)
\;\le\;
\sum_{i=1}^{K} p^{(i)}_{\le \epsilon_i},
\]
and in the unique-minimizer case one may take $p^{(i)}_{\le \epsilon_i}=1/|\Theta_i|$ for the event ``a random candidate matches as well or better than the selected minimizer''.
This is a Bonferroni-style conservative correction; we emphasize that our audit tables are intended to provide transparent within-family look-elsewhere context, not a comprehensive search over unrestricted expression grammars.

\subsection{Rigidity constraints and why key interface choices are forced}
\label{subsec:rigidity_constraints_forced}

Some parts of the physical identification layer are not yet derived from CAP beyond the audited finite selection rules recorded here (Section~\ref{subsec:audit_layering}); nevertheless, at the chosen anchor scale the interface is subject to strong \emph{rigidity constraints} that sharply limit admissible choices.
This subsection records, in audit language, the sense in which several design choices are ``forced'' once one commits to the stated protocol primitives and to minimality.

\paragraph{(i) The (m,n)=(6,3) anchor is CAP-minimal for nontrivial holonomy on the chosen 2D screen (interface).}
The balanced rule $m=2n$ is the theorem-level cardinality match specialized to the $2$D Hilbert addressing used for the explicit finite diagnostics in this paper, namely $2^m=4^n$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:balanced_coupling_equiv}; see also Remark~\ref{rem:balanced_coupling_dimension} for the $d$-dimensional general form).
On the physical identification layer, we require a closed-loop (plaquette) diagnostic with nontrivial transport, as used in the holonomy constructions of Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}.
Within the balanced chain, Table~\ref{tab:holonomy_balanced_chain_sweep} shows that $n=1,2$ produce only identity plaquette holonomy (no 3/4-cycle content), while $n=3$ is the first scale with nontrivial 3/4 cycles.
Thus $(n,m)=(3,6)$ is selected as the minimal balanced holonomy anchor for the non-abelian holonomy diagnostic on the chosen screen.
Other addressing dimensions would select different balanced relations $m=dn$ (Remark~\ref{rem:balanced_coupling_dimension} and Remark~\ref{rem:why_2d_hilbert}).
This is the precise sense in which the anchor is not arbitrary within the declared protocol choices: it is the meeting point of the scan-bit budget, the chosen screen/dihedral audit structure, and the minimal closed-loop diagnostic requirement.

\paragraph{(ii) The \texorpdfstring{$18\oplus 3$}{18+3} split forces a minimal chiral/gauge allocation at \texorpdfstring{$m=6$}{m=6}.}
The $\pi$-channel induces a canonical cyclic/boundary split $X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}}\oplus X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}$ with sizes $18\oplus 3$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_6}).
If one insists that boundary types represent gauge-factor connection classes while cyclic types represent matter multiplets (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge} and Definition~\ref{def:particles_as_types}), then the minimal chiral content compatible with three generations is rigidly constrained by cardinality.
Closing $18$ cyclic labels with the smallest anomaly-neutral extension forces the addition of $\nu_R$ with $Y=0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:anomaly_nur}), because any charged extension would require further compensating matter by standard anomaly constraints.

\paragraph{(iii) The three boundary labels admit a unique monotone gauge-factor assignment.}
The boundary set has exactly three elements with distinct intrinsic values $V(w)$ (Corollary~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_6}).
The gauge factors $\{U(1),SU(2),SU(3)\}$ have distinct Lie-algebra dimensions $1,3,8$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge_dim_order}).
Among the $3!=6$ possible assignments, the monotonicity requirement ``larger intrinsic boundary value $\leftrightarrow$ larger gauge-sector complexity'' selects a unique assignment (Theorem~\ref{thm:labeling_unique}).
This eliminates a common source of post-hoc freedom.

\paragraph{(iv) The mass-depth template is rigid within the minimal invariant language.}
At $(m,n)=(6,3)$, the intrinsic stable-type invariants available for all $w\in X_6$ are discrete and low-entropy: $(V(w),g(w),|w|_1,D_\pi(w))$ with $V\in\{0,\dots,20\}$ and $g\in\{2,3,4\}$ (Definition~\ref{def:x6_invariants}).
Requiring that a depth assignment be built only from these invariants and be auditable as a bounded-integer closure leads naturally to the integer ansatz \eqref{eq:rhat_abc_ansatz}, whose coefficients are rigidly selected as $(2,5,1)$ under the stated objective and tie-break rules (Proposition~\ref{prop:rhat_rigidity}).
This is the precise, checkable meaning of ``mass is forced by rigidity'' within the declared hypothesis class.

\paragraph{(v) Phase registers and holonomy diagnostics are closed in CAP audit form.}
The dyadic phase-register family $\ZZ_{2^p}$ enters only through the finite phase-lift dictionary:
the phase denominator $\mathrm{denom}=2^p$ and the low-complexity index-map family $\tau$ are fixed as explicit bounded candidate sets, and their influence is audited by deterministic sweeps (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy} and Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}).
Thus, although the holonomy diagnostics are physical-layer constructs, they are \emph{not} free-form: the permitted choices are enumerated, tie-breaks are explicit, and sensitivity is reported.

\subsection{Status of the channel-to-gauge identification and anomaly constraints}
The strongest theorem-level statements in the paper are finite combinatorial and operator facts about admissibility and projection (Sections~\ref{sec:hpa_readout} and \ref{sec:folding_core}).
The mapping from the three stability channels to the Standard Model gauge factors is an \emph{interface closure} (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}): it is CAP-minimal within explicit compactness and factorization assumptions, but it is not used as a premise for the theorem-level folding core.
What the present paper supplies at the technical level is:
(i) a closed, computable labeling of the $21$ stable types into $18$ fermion multiplets and $3$ gauge-factor classes (Section~\ref{sec:sm_labeling_closure}),
(ii) explicit finite connection/holonomy diagnostics that realize a non-abelian transport structure at minimal resolution (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}),
and (iii) audit-level consistency checks against standard constraints such as anomaly cancellation and hypercharge sum rules (Lemma~\ref{lem:hypercharge_sum_10} and Proposition~\ref{prop:anomaly_nur}).
In this framework, anomaly cancellation is treated as a non-negotiable interface consistency requirement: any candidate identification of $X_6$ with chiral matter must satisfy the standard anomaly sums \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
Appendix~\ref{app:inverse_quantum_numbers} further records inverse diagnostics that probe to what extent quantum-number patterns can be recovered from intrinsic invariants by bounded-complexity rules (not used as premises).

\paragraph{Scalar sectors and symmetry breaking.}
The present paper closes a minimal \emph{chiral} labeling interface at $(m,n)=(6,3)$ and does not provide a stable-type label for the Higgs doublet (Remark~\ref{rem:higgs_not_in_21}).
When a renormalizable EFT embedding is recorded, the Higgs is introduced as an additional field (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_eft_embedding}).
The corresponding scalar-sector closure in protocol language (parity-even scalar observables by coarse graining/uplift, together with a standard EFT coupling dictionary) is recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar_sector_closed}.

\subsection{Scheme dependence and renormalization-group flow}
Several reported quantities are scheme and scale dependent (e.g.\ $\alpha(\mu)$ and $\sin^2\theta_W(\mu)$), and the paper therefore treats deviations as matching-layer effects rather than as direct ``within-error'' claims (Section~\ref{sec:couplings_cp} and Section~\ref{sec:mass_spectrum_closure}).
At present, the paper does not derive Standard Model $\beta$-functions from the finite combinatorics.
Instead, standard perturbative running is used only as a matching dictionary: an additive mismatch in $\alpha^{-1}$ corresponds to a logarithmic scale shift (Remark~\ref{rem:qed_running_scale_shift}), and Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow} records the $r$-coordinate RG chain rule together with a compact SM-$\beta$ reference pointer to standard sources.
The intrinsic protocol flow law used in this paper is fixed explicitly as a discrete uplift/coarse-graining flow together with the RG dictionary in the Fibonacci resolution coordinate (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_flow_step} and Proposition~\ref{prop:rg_dictionary_r}).

\subsection{Ledger-tagged items beyond the theorem-level core (audit-tagged)}
\label{subsec:open_problems_audit_tagged}
The following items are \emph{not} theorem-level consequences of the folding core and are recorded in an audit-facing form.
Some remain explicitly open (tagged \textbf{[Open]} in Appendix~\ref{app:inference_ledger}), while others are closed as interface representatives within declared CAP candidate families.
For a common technical question---how the finite holonomy/transport diagnostics connect to a continuum gauge-field representative---Appendix~\ref{app:continuum_yang_mills_from_holonomy} records the corresponding representative closure.

\paragraph{(OP1) Gauge-group candidate family / three-factor dictionary from protocol primitives.}
The paper closes a \emph{conditional} gauge-factor identification by CAP within an explicit compact three-factor family
(Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}).
What remains open is deriving the candidate family and the three-factor interface dictionary itself (compactness, factorization into three commuting redundancies, and the complexity label)
from a deeper microscopic scan/readout architecture, i.e., proving the admissibility of the declared family without hand-declaring it.
Within the stated family, the particular choice of common low-complexity labels is less fragile than it may appear: Appendix~\ref{app:gauge_complexity_sensitivity} shows the minimizer persists under several alternative labels in a bounded sweep, and Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge_label_robustness} gives a short classification-based reason for this robustness for the most natural labels.
Closing this would require either:
(i) a theorem-level derivation that any admissible local redundancy group must lie in the stated family under the tick-only primitives, or
(ii) a universality theorem showing that alternative admissible redundancy realizations coarse-grain to the same effective gauge triple.

\paragraph{(OP2) Uniqueness/inevitability of the folding map family (closed under protocol locality).}
The truncation map $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ used here is an explicit, deterministic bridge from dyadic indices to Zeckendorf-admissible digits
(Definition~\eqref{eq:foldm_def} and Remark~\ref{rem:fold_dyadic_zeckendorf}).
Appendix~\ref{app:fold_family_sensitivity} shows that alternative deterministic dyadic$\to X_m$ maps exist at fixed window length and can change fiber statistics.
Appendix~\ref{app:fold_family_uniqueness} closes the remaining selection gap by recording a minimal protocol-local/implementable contract (value consistency together with no-backreaction refinement under dyadic uplift) and proving that this contract uniquely forces the Zeckendorf-truncation family $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:fold_family_uniqueness}).
This is not an invariance claim under arbitrary nonlocal repairs of the dyadic$\to X_m$ bridge; rather, it shows that within the stated locality/implementability class the folding-map family introduces no residual choice.

\paragraph{(OP3) Continuum Yang--Mills/EFT representative from the finite connection (closed as an interface representative).}
Appendix~\ref{app:continuum_yang_mills_from_holonomy} records the closure chain that links the finite non-abelian transport/holonomy diagnostics (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy} and Appendix~\ref{app:holonomy_sweeps_extended}) to a CAP-minimal local continuum representative.
At the representative level, the gauge-sector kinetic term is fixed as $\Tr(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$ within the stated candidate family (Section~\ref{app:cap_continuum_action_closure}), yielding the Yang--Mills equations upon variation (Section~\ref{app:variational_field_equations}).
This representative closure does not address nonperturbative confinement or a Yang--Mills mass gap \cite{JaffeWitten2000YangMillsMassGap,Greensite2011Confinement}; scheme/threshold running is treated separately as a matching-layer dictionary (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).

\paragraph{(OP4) Global model-selection / look-elsewhere control across families (closed within a declared registry).}
Within each stated bounded candidate family, the paper records full audit context (domain sizes, gaps, counterfactual baselines)
(Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables} and Tables~\ref{tab:audit_closure_metrics}--\ref{tab:audit_counterfactual}).
Appendix~\ref{app:global_model_selection_mdl} closes the remaining cross-family gap in an explicit audit form:
it declares a finite registry of candidate families (mainline families used by the paper plus the already-audited counterfactual/null baselines),
assigns a prefix-code/MDL prior to families (no continuous hyperparameters), and computes the corresponding mixture bound on within-registry look-elsewhere probabilities (Table~\ref{tab:audit_global_mdl_family_registry}).
Expanding the registry beyond the declared set remains an explicit choice of a larger hypothesis space.

\paragraph{(OP5) Scalar/Yukawa sector and RG-running closure (closed within stated interface assumptions).}
At the anchor, the stable-type contract closes a minimal chiral labeling and does not allocate a primitive stable label to the Higgs
(Remark~\ref{rem:higgs_not_in_21}); scalar behavior is treated as uplift/coarse-graining dependent (Appendix~\ref{app:scalar_interface_audits}).
Yukawa physical observables and SM $\beta$-function coefficients are now closed within interface assumptions (Appendix~\ref{app:yukawa_beta_protocol_closure}):
Yukawa eigenvalue ratios follow the depth template $y_f/y_e = \varphi^{\hat r(f)}$;
mixing matrices (CKM/PMNS) are fixed by holonomy mechanisms already closed in Section~\ref{sec:couplings_cp} and Section~\ref{sec:pmns_neutrino_closure};
one-loop $\beta$-coefficients $(41/6, -19/6, -7)$ derive from representation counting on the closed $X_6$ labeling plus the minimal Higgs assumption.
The VEV $v$ (equivalently $y_e$) and right-handed rotations remain interface calibrations or unobservable redundancies, not protocol outputs (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).

\paragraph{Baryon asymmetry and out-of-equilibrium dynamics (not covered).}
The present paper does not address baryogenesis mechanisms or fit the observed baryon asymmetry.
In standard cosmology, any successful baryogenesis scenario requires the Sakharov conditions (baryon-number violation, $C/CP$ violation, and departure from equilibrium) \cite{Sakharov1967ViolationCAndBaryogenesis}.
Electroweak sphaleron transitions provide a canonical Standard-Model source of $B+L$ violation at high temperature \cite{KuzminRubakovShaposhnikov1985AnomalousBNonconservation}.
We record baryogenesis as an explicit beyond-scope target for future protocol-level closure: a tick-only mechanism would have to generate a non-equilibrium arrow and a CP-odd source in a way that is compatible with the finite holonomy diagnostics used here.

\paragraph{Strong $CP$ and EDM constraints (not covered).}
The paper does not model the QCD $\theta$ term, the strong-$CP$ problem, or electric-dipole-moment (EDM) constraints.
In the standard field-theoretic framework, the Peccei--Quinn mechanism and the associated axion provide a widely studied route to dynamical relaxation of the effective $\bar\theta$ parameter \cite{PecceiQuinn1977CPConservationPseudoparticles,PecceiQuinn1977ConstraintsCPConservation,Weinberg1978NewLightBoson,Wilczek1978StrongPTInstantons}.
Precision EDM bounds (e.g.\,electron and neutron) provide stringent complementary constraints on new CP-violating sources \cite{ACME2018ElectronEDM,AbelEtAl2020NeutronEDM,GranerChenLindahlHeckel2016HgEDM}.
Any future attempt to extend the present protocol closure toward strong-$CP$ would need an explicit protocol variable corresponding to $\bar\theta$ (or an equivalent selection mechanism) and an auditable map to hadronic/atomic EDM observables.

\paragraph{Black-hole information and Page-curve physics (not covered).}
Appendix~\ref{app:bh_wormholes_pointer} records boundary-entropy and throat templates as external rigidity targets, but the present paper does not treat evaporation dynamics or information recovery.
The Page-curve expectation for unitary evaporation and modern island/QES computations are reviewed in standard sources \cite{Page1993AverageEntropySubsystem,Page1993InformationInRadiation,AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2021EntropyHawkingRadiation,Penington2020EntanglementWedgeReconstruction,AlmheiriHartmanMaldacenaShaghoulianTajdini2020ReplicaWormholes}.
Within the current manuscript, no claim is made about Page-time behavior, replica-wormhole saddles, or microscopic unitarity restoration.

\paragraph{Cosmological tensions ($H_0$ and $S_8$) (not covered).}
The paper uses Planck-2018 parameters only as compact matching-layer reference targets in the $\gamma$ cross-observation audit (Appendix~\ref{app:gamma_crossobs_consistency}),
which is distinct from addressing the broader data-model tensions between early- and late-Universe inferences.
For orientation, see Planck-2018 cosmological parameters \cite{Planck2018Parameters} and representative late-time measurements and reviews \cite{Riess2019SH0ES,AsgariEtAl2021KiDS1000CosmicShear,DESY3Cosmo2022PRD,VerdeTreuRiess2019TensionsReview}.
A protocol-level treatment of these tensions would require an explicit systematics model and a declared global hypothesis space, neither of which is attempted here.

\subsection{Falsifiability beyond parameter matching}
While parts of the quantitative interface are expressed as parameter targets, the paper also records nontrivial protocol-level predictions that are not reducible to retrodictive matching:
chirality-domain defects and parity-odd statistical signatures (Section~\ref{subsec:p2_domain_walls}),

Each prediction is accompanied by an explicit observable channel in standard language: mixing and CP observables are compared directly to PDG/global-fit targets (Sections~\ref{sec:couplings_cp} and \ref{sec:pmns_neutrino_closure}), resolution uplifts are tied to energy thresholds by the calibrated staircase and the deterministic map $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution_selection}), and delay/lapse proxies are expressed via Wigner--Smith and GR reference dictionaries (Section~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}).

\subsection{Role of the \texorpdfstring{$\e$}{e}-channel at minimal resolution}
At $m=6$, admissibility is already enforced by the $\varphi$-grammar and the $\pi$-split, so the $\e$-channel is not used to further reduce $X_6$ (Remark~\ref{rem:e_channel_distinct}).
Its technical role at minimal resolution is to fix a standard analytic stability template (Artin--Mazur zeta, Abel normalization) and thereby fix the use of an Abel/pole-barrier viewpoint when defining resolution-flow coordinates and exponentially small weights.
At higher resolution, weighted/pressure-like variants can make the analytic channel genuinely constraining beyond a single forbidden-word predicate.

\subsection{Related discrete approaches and standard constraints}
Several mature lines of work share the broad goal of extracting continuum physics from discrete or finite data:
classical and quantum lattice gauge theory \cite{Wilson1974,KogutSusskind1975HamiltonianLGT,Rothe2012},
quantum cellular automata models for relativistic equations \cite{BialynickiBirula1994QCA},
and causal-set approaches to discrete spacetime structure \cite{Bombelli1987CausalSet}.
More recently, tensor-network and holographic-code frameworks provide discrete models in which geometry, error correction, and coarse graining are structurally linked \cite{Vidal2007MERA,Swingle2012EntanglementHolography,Pastawski2015HolographicCodes}.
The present work differs in emphasis: it treats \emph{finite readout} (window projection plus protocol audit) as the primitive, and records explicit finite invariants and closure rules at a minimal scale.
On the combinatorics/number-theory side, there is also a large literature on generalized Zeckendorf decompositions, digit statistics, and structural classifications beyond the classical Fibonacci case; see, e.g., \cite{DemontignyEtAl2013FDecompositions,BenAriMiller2014ProbabilisticZeckendorf,Dekking2020StructureZeckendorf}.

On the Standard Model side, the interface claims are constrained by standard consistency requirements such as anomaly cancellation and scheme/scale dependence (Sections~\ref{sec:sm_labeling_closure} and \ref{sec:mass_spectrum_closure}).
For modern high-precision discussions of scheme conventions and the extraction of Standard Model parameters in a fixed $\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$ prescription, see, e.g., \cite{MartinRobertson2019TadpoleFreeMSbar}.
For CP-violation constraints beyond mixing observables, electric-dipole-moment bounds provide an important complementary diagnostic; see, e.g., \cite{Fukuyama2013EDM}.
For modern treatments of flavor invariants in extended Standard Model settings, see, e.g., \cite{deLimaNishi2024FlavorInvariantsVLQ}.
For electroweak mixing, it is also useful to keep in view the standard grand-unification benchmark $\sin^2\theta_W=3/8$ at a unification scale in minimal $SU(5)$-type models \cite{GeorgiGlashow1974SU5,Langacker1981GUT}; see also Remark~\ref{rem:gutsin2_benchmark} for the scale/matching-layer boundary in this paper.
Our electroweak targets are stated at the $Z$ scale and are interpreted as matching-layer normalizations rather than unification-scale relations.
