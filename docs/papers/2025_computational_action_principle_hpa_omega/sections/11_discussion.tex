\section{Discussion: testable consequences and model boundaries}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{(1) Time dilation as implementation cost.}
If an operational estimate of the effective routing overhead $\kappa(x)$ of a region can be extracted (e.g.\ via compilation-depth proxies inferred from phase delays or scattering-time observables in an explicit microscopic model), then the computational lapse $\lapse=\kappa_0/\kappa$ predicts redshift and time delay in that region via the dictionary $\lapse\leftrightarrow \sqrt{-g_{00}}$.

\paragraph{(2) Phenomenology of information stress.}
Spatially inhomogeneous cost fields $\kappa(x)$ induce Fisher-gradient energy in the $\chi$ sector. In weak-field regimes this contributes positively to curvature through $T^{\mathrm{info}}_{\mu\nu}$ and can be comparable to the Newtonian potential in low-acceleration regions, providing a structured space for effective deviations without abandoning the Einsteinian skeleton.

\paragraph{(3) Topological sectors and dark-matter candidates.}
Stable defects carrying internal topological charge but neutral under long-range gauge flux could behave as collisionless matter. Their mass scales are set by microscopic maintenance budget and remain parameters until the internal spectrum is derived.

\paragraph{Model boundary.}
This paper closes the minimal backbone from scan--projection readout, discrepancy, and implementation overhead to an Einstein--Fisher action and its field equations. Completing the bridge to Standard Model parameters, a first-principles defect spectrum, and higher-dimensional quasicrystal/Dirac homotopy limits requires additional microscopic input; these are explicit open tasks in the $\Omega$ program \cite{Ma2025FoP}.
