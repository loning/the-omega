\section{Compute---Geometry Dictionary: Compilation Routing Overhead and Effective Lapse}

\subsection{One-Dimensional Compilation and Routing Overhead Field}

Compiling high-dimensional local updates (e.g., two-dimensional cellular automata or quantum cellular automata) to a one-dimensional chain typically requires routing and permutation, thus introducing position-dependent additional gate depth/communication cost. Let $\kappa(x)$ denote the compilation cost (routing overhead) required per unit logical step at position $x$. Treating it as a discrete field, we can define effective lapse
\[
N(x)=\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(x)},
\]
where $\kappa_0$ is a reference cost. This definition provides a purely computational-theoretic notion of ``time flow speed'': where compilation is more expensive, visible ticks are sparser, corresponding to smaller $N(x)$.

\subsection{Redshift Structure and Falsifiable Interface}

In the continuous limit, if the profile of $\kappa(x)$ is isomorphic to some class of geometric potential functions, then $N(x)$ can be compared to the lapse structure of classical gravitational fields. This paper emphasizes that this correspondence belongs to the interpretation layer and must be falsifiable through explicit compilation models and measurable outputs: for example, comparing shape stability of $N(x)$ under different routing strategies, whether observable transformation laws with information/entropy flow are consistent, and predictable corrections to external observer statistics.
