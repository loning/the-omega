\subsubsection{Dirichlet Variable Caliber (Fully Equivalent to the Abel Unit Disk)}\label{app:real-input-40-dirichlet}
To align with the ``orbit Dirichlet series / Abel finite part'' language of Section \ref{sec:zeta-finite-part} in the main text (Definition \ref{def:abel-finite-part}),
the primitive generating function $P$ can be rewritten as an orbit Dirichlet series in the $s$-variable. Define
\[
\mathcal{P}(s):=\sum_{n\ge 1}p_n\,\lambda^{-ns}=P(\lambda^{-s}),
\qquad
\mathcal{Z}(s):=\zeta_M(\lambda^{-s}).
\]
Note that the Abel normalized $r=\lambda z$ and the Dirichlet variable satisfy
\[
z=\lambda^{-s},\qquad r=\lambda^{1-s}\in(0,1)\quad(s>1),
\]
so $\mathcal{Z}(s)=\widetilde{\zeta}_M(\lambda^{1-s})$, and $\mathcal{P}(s)$ is likewise equivalent to $\mathcal{P}(r)$ evaluated at $r=\lambda^{1-s}$.
Since
\[
1-\lambda^{1-s}=1-e^{-(s-1)\log\lambda}\sim (s-1)\log\lambda\qquad(s\to 1^+),
\]
from the singularity expansion in the Abel caliber
\(
\mathcal{P}(r)=-\log(1-r)+\log\mathfrak{M}+o(1)
\)
one obtains the equivalent Dirichlet caliber:
\[
\mathcal{P}(s)=-\log(s-1)-\log\log\lambda+\log\mathfrak{M}+o(1)\qquad(s\to 1^+).
\]
Hence the Hadamard finite part constant on the $s$-plane satisfies
\[
\mathrm{fp}_{s=1}\bigl(\mathcal{P}(s)+\log(s-1)\bigr)=\log\mathfrak{M}-\log\log\lambda.
\]
On the other hand, from $\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r)=\frac{C}{1-r}+O(1)$ and $1-r\sim (s-1)\log\lambda$ one gets
\[
\mathcal{Z}(s)\sim \frac{C}{(s-1)\log\lambda}\qquad(s\to 1^+).
\]
This provides a complete equivalence between the two calibers: the Abel unit disk variable and the Dirichlet variable, expressed in closed form.

\paragraph{Angular variable in polar coordinates within the disk (parameter-free caliber)}
The above equivalence concentrates the critical singularity entirely on the radial parameter \(r\to 1^{-}\); if one additionally needs to organize an \emph{angular} variable within the same Abel disk, one may adopt the parameter-free area-phase compactification of Appendix \ref{app:unit-circle-phase-gate}: for any \(x\in\RR\), set
\[
e^{\mathrm{i}\vartheta(x)}=\iota(x)=\frac{1+\mathrm{i}x}{1-\mathrm{i}x}\in\TT,
\qquad
\vartheta(x)=2\arctan(x),
\]
so that points within the disk can be uniformly written as \(w=r\,e^{\mathrm{i}\vartheta(x)}\) (see \eqref{eq:unit-circle-phase-disk-polar}). When \(r=1\), this degenerates to the parameter-free parametrization \(w=\iota(x)\) of the unitary slice; this supplement does not alter the Dirichlet--Abel radial singularity analysis, but merely provides a source of angular variable aligned with the unitary-slice exposition.

\paragraph{Dirichlet--Witt dilation operator (a strictly commutative bridge package)}
From the Euler product / primitive decomposition identity
\(
\log\zeta(z)=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m}P(z^m)
\),
substituting $z=\lambda^{-s}$ and noting that $z^m=\lambda^{-ms}$, one obtains
\[
\log\mathcal{Z}(s)=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\,\mathcal{P}(ms).
\]
Therefore, introducing the Witt dilation operator on the Dirichlet variable
\[
(Wf)(s):=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\,f(ms),
\]
one has the strict identity
\[
\boxed{\ \log\mathcal{Z}(s)=(W\mathcal{P})(s)\ }.
\]
Applying M\"obius inversion (i.e., $W^{-1}$) to this identity yields the Dirichlet series of primitives directly strippable from $\mathcal{Z}$:
\[
\boxed{\ \mathcal{P}(s)=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\,\log\mathcal{Z}(ms)\ }.
\]
These two identities place the ``spectral/trace side'' $\mathcal{Z}(s)=\det(I-\lambda^{-s}M)^{-1}$ and the ``prime-like side'' $\mathcal{P}(s)$ under a single strictly aligned interface; therein the dilation $s\mapsto ms$ is precisely the equivalent translation of the power substitution $z\mapsto z^m$ in the Abel variable into the Dirichlet caliber.

\paragraph{Parameter-free Koenigs linearization coordinate of Witt dilation: translating the dilation \texorpdfstring{$(s\mapsto ms)$}{(s->ms)}}
Under the Dirichlet--Abel interface \(z=\lambda^{-s}\), the dilation \(s\mapsto ms\) and the power substitution \(z\mapsto z^{m}\) are strictly equivalent.
Therefore the Witt dilation chain can be linearized into a translation semigroup along a ``logarithm of logarithm'' coordinate.

\begin{theorem}[Koenigs linearization: translating the dilation \texorpdfstring{$(s\mapsto ms)$}{(s->ms)}]\label{thm:koenigs-linearization-witt-dilation}
On the real domain \(s>0\), define the parameter-free depth coordinate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-koenigs-depth-coordinate}
\boxed{\;
y(s):=\log\!\bigl(s\log\lambda\bigr)
=\log\!\bigl(-\log(\lambda^{-s})\bigr)\; }.
\end{equation}
Then for any integer \(m\ge 1\), the strict identity holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-koenigs-translation}
\boxed{\; y(ms)=y(s)+\log m\; }.
\end{equation}
Hence the Witt dilation \(s\mapsto ms\) is completely diagonalized in the \(y\)-coordinate as the translation semigroup \(y\mapsto y+\log m\), and this linearization introduces no external scale parameters (\(\lambda\) is already fixed by the Dirichlet--Abel interface).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:witt-koenigs-depth-coordinate},
\[
y(ms)=\log\!\bigl(ms\log\lambda\bigr)=\log\!\bigl(s\log\lambda\bigr)+\log m=y(s)+\log m,
\]
which yields \eqref{eq:witt-koenigs-translation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Translation convolution representation of the Witt dilation operator on the depth line]\label{cor:witt-depth-line-convolution}
Let \(f:(0,\infty)\to\CC\), and set
\[
s=\frac{e^{y}}{\log\lambda},
\qquad
g(y):=f\!\Bigl(\frac{e^{y}}{\log\lambda}\Bigr).
\]
Then the Witt dilation operator \(W\) satisfies the identity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-depth-line-convolution}
\boxed{\;
(Wf)\!\Bigl(\frac{e^{y}}{\log\lambda}\Bigr)
=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\,g(y+\log m)\; }.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, denoting the translation operator \(T_{\tau}g(y):=g(y+\tau)\), then \(W\) on the depth line \(y\) becomes a translation superposition operator generated by the discrete measure
\(
\nu:=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m}\delta_{\log m}
\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Theorem \ref{thm:koenigs-linearization-witt-dilation},
\(
g(y+\log m)=f(\tfrac{e^{y+\log m}}{\log\lambda})=f(ms)
\).
Substituting into \( (Wf)(s)=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m}f(ms)\) yields \eqref{eq:witt-depth-line-convolution}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Regularized depth convolution and prime local decomposition: operatorization of the Euler product}
To obtain a convergent symbolic caliber, for \(\sigma>0\) define the regularized depth convolution operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-depth-regularized-operator}
\boxed{\;
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}
:=\sum_{m\ge 1}\frac{1}{m^{1+\sigma}}\,T_{\log m}\; }.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Operator Euler decomposition: primes as translation generators]\label{thm:witt-depth-euler-product}
On any Banach space where \(T_{\tau}\) acts isometrically, if \(\sigma>0\) ensures \(\sum_{p}p^{-(1+\sigma)}<\infty\), then \(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}\) possesses a local decomposition convergent in operator norm:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-depth-euler-product}
\boxed{\;
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}
=\prod_{p}\Bigl(\sum_{k\ge 0}p^{-k(1+\sigma)}\,T_{k\log p}\Bigr)
=\prod_{p}\bigl(I-p^{-(1+\sigma)}T_{\log p}\bigr)^{-1}\; }.
\end{equation}
This decomposition lifts the Euler product to a locally invertible decomposition on the translation semigroup: primes correspond to fundamental translations \(T_{\log p}\), and composite scales correspond to their geometric series closures.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each prime \(p\), from \(\|T_{\log p}\|=1\) one gets \(\|p^{-(1+\sigma)}T_{\log p}\|=p^{-(1+\sigma)}<1\), hence
\[
\bigl(I-p^{-(1+\sigma)}T_{\log p}\bigr)^{-1}
=\sum_{k\ge 0}p^{-k(1+\sigma)}T_{k\log p}
\]
converges in operator norm.
\par
Expanding the product over a finite set of primes gives
\[
\prod_{p\le P}\Bigl(\sum_{k\ge 0}p^{-k(1+\sigma)}T_{k\log p}\Bigr)
=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{M}_P}\frac{1}{m^{1+\sigma}}\,T_{\log m},
\]
where \(\mathcal{M}_P\) is the set of all positive integers whose prime factors do not exceed \(P\).
By unique factorization \(m=\prod_{p\le P}p^{k_p}\), one has \(\log m=\sum_{p\le P}k_p\log p\) and \(m^{-(1+\sigma)}=\prod_{p\le P}p^{-k_p(1+\sigma)}\), so the weights and translation parameters match exactly.
Letting \(P\uparrow\infty\) and using the absolute convergence from \(\sigma>0\) to interchange summation and limits yields \eqref{eq:witt-depth-euler-product}.
\end{proof}
\begin{corollary}[Finite depth truncation: finite prime--finite power series window]\label{cor:witt-depth-finite-window-truncation}
For fixed \(\sigma>0\) and depth window scale \(L>0\), define the finite truncation operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:witt-depth-finite-window-trunc-operator}
\boxed{\;
\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{(L)}
:=\prod_{p\le e^{L}}
\Bigl(\sum_{0\le k\le \lfloor L/\log p\rfloor}p^{-k(1+\sigma)}\,T_{k\log p}\Bigr)\; }.
\end{equation}
Then \(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{(L)}\) depends on only finitely many primes and finitely many \(p\)-power translation layers.
Moreover, for any bounded \(g\), the remainder
\(
\mathcal{R}_{\sigma,L}:=(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}-\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}^{(L)})
\)
arises solely from (i) the prime tail \(p>e^{L}\) and (ii) the local geometric series tails \(k>L/\log p\); its uniform norm can be bounded above by the two explicit tail sums
\(
\sum_{p>e^{L}}p^{-(1+\sigma)}
\)
and
\(
\sum_{k> L/\log p}p^{-k(1+\sigma)}
\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{remark}[Local \(p\)-layer truncation and Frobenius defect integer certificates]\label{rem:witt-depth-local-p-frobenius}
Theorem \ref{thm:witt-depth-euler-product} decomposes \(\mathcal{K}_{\sigma}\) into prime-local factors
\[
\bigl(I-p^{-(1+\sigma)}T_{\log p}\bigr)^{-1}
=\sum_{k\ge 0}p^{-k(1+\sigma)}\,T_{k\log p},
\]
whose \(k\)-th term corresponds to the depth translation layer at \(p\)-power scale \(p^{k}\).
Hence truncating the local factor to \(k\le K\) is equivalent to retaining only finitely many layers of \(p\)-power scale data.
\par
On the other hand, in the coefficient-ring version of Big-Witt/M\"obius inversion, the Adams power substitution \(\psi^{p}\) is realized on the polynomial ring by \(u\mapsto u^{p}\), and inter-layer consistency can be audited via the Frobenius defect
\(
\Delta_{p,k}(u)
\)
(Definition \ref{def:witt-frobenius-defect}) and its divisibility depth
\(
\Delta_{p,k}(u)\in p^{k}\ZZ[u]
\)
(Proposition \ref{prop:witt-frobenius-defect-divisibility}).
When this divisibility depth holds for \(k\le K\), the local \(p\)-layer truncation is compatible with the Frobenius pushforward modulo \(p^{K}\); once this certificate fails, any further spectral layer interpretation of endpoint modular class channels (see Corollary \ref{cor:endpoint-tristate-compression} and Definition \ref{def:endpoint-frobenius-integer-certificate}) structurally loses its realization layer support.
\end{remark}
