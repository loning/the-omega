\subsubsection{Two-Atom--Two-Kink--Curvature Law: Constant-Scale Ledgerization and Budget Phase Transition under Resolution Deficiency}\label{subsubsec:discussion-two-atom-ledger-curvature-bias-toy-rh}
Building on \S\ref{subsubsec:discussion-phi-inversion-phase-ledger} and \S\ref{subsubsec:discussion-audit-kernel-hardening-kl-chernoff-resonance}, the solvable bin-fold branch yields a stronger ``constant-scale ledger'' paradigm:
under a two-state uniform asymptotic regime, output nonuniformity, tail thresholds, finite-scale budget decomposition, breakpoint phase diagrams, and even the emergence pattern of the toy-RH critical line can all be compressed into \emph{a small number of auditable constants} and a single discrete curvature law.
Here we only extract numbered conclusions already given in the main text/appendices for a unified interpretive synthesis, without introducing new proof assumptions.

\paragraph{Two-atom limit ledger: one-shot closed forms for arbitrary \texorpdfstring{$f$}{f}-divergence, \texorpdfstring{$L^p$}{Lp} distance, and dynamic range}
Let \(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}(w)=d_m^{\mathrm{bin}}(w)/2^m\) be the pushforward distribution on the stable layer \(X_m\), let \(u_m(w)=1/|X_m|\) be the uniform baseline, and define \(R_m=p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}/u_m\) as the relative density.
Theorem \ref{thm:xi-foldbin-pushforward-uniform-two-atom-ledger} gives a uniform two-atom collapse: there exist
\[
r_0=\frac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt5},\qquad r_1=\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt5},
\]
such that for any convex \(f\) (\(f(1)=0\)), the limit \(D_f(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)\) converges to the same two-atom closed form
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}D_f(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)
=\frac{1}{\varphi}f(r_0)+\frac{1}{\varphi^2}f(r_1),
\qquad
\text{error }O\!\bigl((\varphi/2)^m\bigr).
\]
This unified expression simultaneously contains the TV constant (same theorem), the \(L^p\) spectrum, and the \(L^\infty\) dynamic range:
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\|R_m\|_{L^\infty(u_m)}=r_0,\qquad
\lim_{m\to\infty}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u_m}R_m=r_1,\qquad
\frac{r_0}{r_1}=\varphi,
\]
and turns the Pinsker-gap into the constant
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{2D_{\mathrm{TV}}(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)^2}{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)}
=\frac{2C_{\mathrm{TV}}^2}{C_{\mathrm{KL}}}\in(0,1),
\]
where the closed form of \(C_{\mathrm{KL}}\) is given in Proposition \ref{prop:fold-bin-kl-constant}.
Hence the nonuniformity side of ``mechanism identity'' can be compressed into the \emph{unforgeable constant-scale ledger} of \((r_0,r_1)\) and weights \((1/\varphi,1/\varphi^2)\): any replacement implementation can be refuted offline as soon as it mismatches any divergence/norm/dynamic-range field.

\paragraph{Two-point threshold and zero-dispersion constant term: recurrence of the constant-scale phase-transition point \texorpdfstring{$\varphi/\sqrt5$}{phi/sqrt5}}
The same two-state mechanism fixes the tail/success ``knife-edge'' at a constant threshold.
On one side, the tail-quantile threshold undergoes a two-point jump at \(\varepsilon=\varphi/\sqrt5\) (Corollary \ref{cor:fold-bin-quantile-threshold-constant}).
On the other side, for the minimal additional information \(L_m(\varepsilon)\) under average error rate \(\le\varepsilon\), Corollary \ref{cor:xi-constant-bias-inversion-b-eps} gives the exact piecewise inversion of the zero-dispersion second-order term:
\[
L_m(\varepsilon)=m\log\!\Bigl(\frac{2}{\varphi}\Bigr)+b(\varepsilon)+o(1),
\]
and provides a piecewise closed form for \(b(\varepsilon)\) under \(s:=1-\varepsilon\) and threshold \(s_c:=\varphi/\sqrt5\).
Therefore, in the solvable bin-fold branch, ``principal slope \(\log(2/\varphi)\) + constant term \(b(\varepsilon)\)'' itself becomes an auditable object: the constant-scale cost jumps strictly with regime, and the threshold location is locked by the same two-point limit law.

\paragraph{Three-term additive ledger for the Chernoff certificate: geometric floor \texorpdfstring{$+$}{+} nonuniform penalty \texorpdfstring{$+$}{+} confidence term}
The budget phase transition is not an empirical tradeoff; at finite scale it already satisfies a strict three-term decomposition identity.
Remark \ref{rem:pom-quantile-budget-three-term} shows that for any \(q\ge 2\) and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), the finite-scale Chernoff certificate obeys the exact additive ledger
\[
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert,fin}}_{m,q}(\varepsilon)
=
\underbrace{\frac{1}{m}\log\!\Bigl(\frac{2^m}{|X_m|}\Bigr)}_{\text{geometric unavoidable loss}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{D}_q(w_m\Vert u_m)}_{\text{nonuniform penalty}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)}_{\text{confidence term}},
\]
and immediately yields a non-removable hard lower bound (same remark): the moment method cannot eliminate the first geometric-floor term by tuning \(q\); it can only make auditable tradeoffs between the nonuniform penalty and the confidence term.

\paragraph{Discrete curvature law of breakpoint phase diagram: optimal moment-order jumps are fully controlled by \texorpdfstring{$A_q$}{Aq}}
When the moment order \(q\) is treated as a design variable, the kink locations of the certificate lower envelope are given in closed form by the discrete-curvature increment scalar \(A_q\):
\[
m_{q\to q+1}(\varepsilon)=\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{A_q},
\qquad
A_q=(q-1)(\log r_{q+1}-\log2)-q(\log r_q-\log2),
\]
thereby yielding the jump phase diagram of \(q^\star(m,\varepsilon)\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-breakpoint-curvature-law-Aq}; Remark \ref{rem:pom-qstar-phase-diagram-breakpoints}).
Hence, in this framework, the ``budget phase-transition point'' is an auditable consequence of discrete geometry: the input is only the validated \(\{r_q\}\), and the output is a reproducible breakpoint table and optimal-order jump pattern.

\paragraph{Two-kink capacity spectrum: a common two-atom projection of the divergence ledger and the tail-spectrum ledger}
On the tail-count and capacity-curve side, Proposition \ref{prop:fold-bin-degeneracy-tail-capacity-kinks} gives a piecewise-constant/piecewise-linear structure for the scaled limit and proves that the rigid kinks are fixed at
\(
s=\varphi^{-1}
\)
and
\(
s=1
\).
Combining the two-atom divergence ledger (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-foldbin-pushforward-uniform-two-atom-ledger}) with the quantile-threshold jump (Corollary \ref{cor:fold-bin-quantile-threshold-constant}), this can be viewed as two projections of the same two-state mechanism along the ``distance/divergence axis'' and the ``tail-spectrum/capacity axis'': the former manifests as the two-atom spectrum \((r_0,r_1)\), and the latter as two non-removable kinks at \((\varphi^{-1},1)\).

\paragraph{Dirichlet log-image and critical line: a \texorpdfstring{$\log$}{log}-ratio identity of negative eigenvalues (toy-RH)}
On the spectral side, the \(s\)-plane zero-pole dictionary (Proposition \ref{prop:s-plane-spectrum-poles}) interprets the ``critical line'' as a pure \(\log\)-ratio identity: every nonzero eigenvalue \(\mu\) generates a sequence of Dirichlet poles whose vertical-line real part satisfies
\[
\Re(s)=\frac{\log|\mu|}{\log\lambda},
\qquad \lambda:=\rho(M).
\]
If one instead uses the Dirichlet logarithmic coordinate \(r=\lambda^{1-s}\), the same fact is equivalent to
\(
\Re(s)=1-\frac{\log|r_0|}{\log\lambda}
\)
where \(r_0=\lambda/\mu\) is the \(r\)-coordinate of the corresponding pole.
Hence the boundary negative eigenvalue \(\mu=-\sqrt{\lambda}\) directly generates the critical line \(\Re(s)=1/2\).
This generation mechanism, ``negative pole \(\Rightarrow\) critical line,'' is realized as an auditable fact on the real-input \(40\)-state kernel in this work (Proposition \ref{prop:rh-stratification-three-kernels}-(2); see also Proposition \ref{prop:finite-rh-40}).
