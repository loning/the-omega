\section{Reference state \texorpdfstring{$\ketomega$}{|Omega>} and a concrete choice in the minimal scan model}
\label{app:reference_state}

Fix a separable Hilbert space $\hilbert$ and a unit vector $\ketomega\in\hilbert$. In this paper $\ketomega$ plays the role of a global reference state for later operator-theoretic constructions.

We do not assign $\ketomega$ a physical interpretation as a theorem. The only requirement is that, under the subsequent scan and projection operations, $\ketomega$ can generate observable readout structures.

\begin{remark}[A concrete choice in the minimal scan model]
\label{rem:omega_choice}
In the minimal rotation model of Section~\ref{sec:theta} we take $\hilbert=L^2(X,\mu)$ with $X=\RR/\ZZ$ and $\mu$ the Lebesgue probability measure. For $x_0\in X$ and $\delta\in(0,1)$ define the normalized bump
$$
\omega_{x_0,\delta}(x):=\frac{\ind_{[x_0,x_0+\delta)}(x)}{\sqrt{\delta}}\in L^2(X).
$$
Let $\Pi_W$ be the multiplication operator $(\Pi_W f)(x)=\ind_W(x)f(x)$. Then the scalar
$$
p_k(\delta):=\langle \omega_{x_0,\delta},\,\Pi_W\,\Theta^k\,\omega_{x_0,\delta}\rangle
$$
is a bona fide Hilbert-space observable. Moreover, for any $x_0$ that is a Lebesgue point of $W$ one has
$$
\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0} p_k(\delta)=\ind_W(x_0+k\alpha),
$$
so the binary orbit readout arises as a sharp localization limit of a standard projection measurement. By the Lebesgue differentiation theorem, for any measurable $W$ almost every $x_0$ is a Lebesgue point of $W$; for interval windows the only exceptional points are the boundary points; see e.g.~\cite{SteinShakarchi2005}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Commutativity of cuts and spectral reality}
\label{app:commuting_cuts_spectra}

This subsection records a minimal operator-theoretic fact used in the discussion: composing a Hermitian generator with two positive cuts preserves real spectra only when the cuts commute, while noncommutativity can generate a genuinely complex spectral component.

\begin{proposition}[Real spectra under commuting positive cuts]
\label{prop:commuting_cuts_real_spectra}
Let $H$ be a Hermitian matrix and let $P,Q$ be positive semidefinite matrices. If $PQ=QP$, then every eigenvalue of $PHQ$ is real.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
If $P$ and $Q$ are positive definite, define the invertible matrix $S:=P^{1/2}Q^{-1/2}$. Since $P$ and $Q$ commute, their functional calculi commute and one computes
$$
S^{-1}(PHQ)S
=Q^{1/2}P^{1/2}HP^{1/2}Q^{1/2},
$$
which is Hermitian, hence has real eigenvalues. For the semidefinite case, set $P_\varepsilon:=P+\varepsilon I$ and $Q_\varepsilon:=Q+\varepsilon I$ for $\varepsilon>0$. Then $P_\varepsilon$ and $Q_\varepsilon$ are positive definite and commute, so $P_\varepsilon H Q_\varepsilon$ has real spectrum for every $\varepsilon>0$. As $\varepsilon\downarrow 0$ we have $P_\varepsilon H Q_\varepsilon\to PHQ$, and eigenvalues vary continuously with matrix entries, so the spectrum of $PHQ$ is a limit of real spectra and is therefore real.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Noncommuting cuts can generate an imaginary spectral component]
\label{rem:noncommuting_cuts_imaginary}
The commutativity hypothesis cannot be dropped. Consider
$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}18&2\\2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
Q=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}1&-\iu\\\iu&1\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Then $H$ is Hermitian and $P,Q$ are orthogonal projections (hence positive semidefinite), but $PQ\ne QP$. A direct computation gives
$$
PHQ=\begin{pmatrix}9+\iu&1-9\iu\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\operatorname{tr}(PHQ)=9+\iu,
$$
so $PHQ$ has the non-real eigenvalue $9+\iu$.
\end{remark}
