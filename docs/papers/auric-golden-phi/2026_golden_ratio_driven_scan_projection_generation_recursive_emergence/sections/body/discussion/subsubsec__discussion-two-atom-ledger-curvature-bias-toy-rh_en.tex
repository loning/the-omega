\subsubsection{Two-atom–two-kink–curvature law: constant-level ledger under insufficient resolution and budget phase transition}\label{subsubsec:discussion-two-atom-ledger-curvature-bias-toy-rh}
Building on the organization of \S\ref{subsubsec:discussion-phi-inversion-phase-ledger} and \S\ref{subsubsec:discussion-audit-kernel-hardening-kl-chernoff-resonance}, the solvable branch of bin-fold further yields a stronger ``constant-level ledger'' paradigm:
under the two-state uniformly asymptotic regime, output non-uniformity, tail thresholds, finite-scale budget decompositions, breakpoint phase diagrams, and even the emergence mode of the toy-RH critical line can all be compressed into \emph{a small set of auditable constants} together with a discrete curvature law.
This paragraph only consolidates numbered conclusions already given in the main text/appendices into a unified interpretive summary, without introducing new proof premises.

\paragraph{Two-atom limiting ledger: one-time closed forms for arbitrary \texorpdfstring{$f$}{f}-divergences, \texorpdfstring{$L^p$}{Lp} distances, and dynamic range}
Let \(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}(w)=d_m^{\mathrm{bin}}(w)/2^m\) be the pushforward distribution on the stable layer \(X_m\), \(u_m(w)=1/|X_m|\) the uniform baseline, and \(R_m=p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}/u_m\) the relative density.
Theorem~\ref{thm:xi-foldbin-pushforward-uniform-two-atom-ledger} gives a uniform two-atom collapse: there exist
\[
r_0=\frac{\varphi^2}{\sqrt5},\qquad r_1=\frac{\varphi}{\sqrt5},
\]
such that for any convex \(f\) with \(f(1)=0\), the limit \(D_f(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)\) converges to the same two-atom closed form
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}D_f(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)
=\frac{1}{\varphi}f(r_0)+\frac{1}{\varphi^2}f(r_1),
\qquad
\text{error }O\!\bigl((\varphi/2)^m\bigr).
\]
This unified formula simultaneously covers the TV constant (same theorem), \(L^p\) spectrum, and \(L^\infty\) dynamic range:
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\|R_m\|_{L^\infty(u_m)}=r_0,\qquad
\lim_{m\to\infty}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{u_m}R_m=r_1,\qquad
\frac{r_0}{r_1}=\varphi,
\]
and turns the gap constant in Pinsker's inequality into
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{2D_{\mathrm{TV}}(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)^2}{D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p_m^{\mathrm{bin}}\Vert u_m)}
=\frac{2C_{\mathrm{TV}}^2}{C_{\mathrm{KL}}}\in(0,1),
\]
where the closed form of \(C_{\mathrm{KL}}\) is given in Proposition~\ref{prop:fold-bin-kl-constant}.
Thus the non-uniformity side of ``mechanism identity'' can be compressed into an \emph{unforgeable constant-level ledger} of \((r_0,r_1)\) and their weights \((1/\varphi,1/\varphi^2)\): any alternative implementation that fails to match in any single field of divergence/norm/dynamic range can be refuted offline.

\paragraph{Two-point threshold and zero-dispersion constant term: reproducibility of the constant-level phase transition at \texorpdfstring{$\varphi/\sqrt5$}{phi/sqrt5}}
The same two-state mechanism fixes the tail/success rate ``knife edge'' as a constant threshold.
On one hand, the tail quantile threshold exhibits a two-point jump at \(\varepsilon=\varphi/\sqrt5\) (Corollary~\ref{cor:fold-bin-quantile-threshold-constant}).
On the other hand, for the minimal additional information \(L_m(\varepsilon)\) under average error rate \(\le\varepsilon\), Corollary~\ref{cor:xi-constant-bias-inversion-b-eps} provides the exact piecewise inversion of the zero-dispersion second-order term:
\[
L_m(\varepsilon)=m\log\!\Bigl(\frac{2}{\varphi}\Bigr)+b(\varepsilon)+o(1),
\]
and gives a piecewise closed form for \(b(\varepsilon)\) in the regime calibrated by \(s:=1-\varepsilon\) and the threshold \(s_c:=\varphi/\sqrt5\).
Thus in the bin-fold solvable branch, the ``main slope \(\log(2/\varphi)\) + constant term \(b(\varepsilon)\)'' itself becomes an auditable object: the constant-level overhead strictly jumps across regimes, while the threshold position is locked by the same two-point limiting law.

\paragraph{Three-term additive ledger of the Chernoff certificate: geometric floor \texorpdfstring{$+$}{+} non-uniform penalty \texorpdfstring{$+$}{+} confidence term}
Budget phase transitions are not empirical tradeoffs but possess a strict three-term decomposition identity at finite scale.
Remark~\ref{rem:pom-quantile-budget-three-term} shows that for any \(q\ge 2\) and \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), the finite-scale Chernoff certificate satisfies an exact additive ledger
\[
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert,fin}}_{m,q}(\varepsilon)
=
\underbrace{\frac{1}{m}\log\!\Bigl(\frac{2^m}{|X_m|}\Bigr)}_{\text{geometric inevitable loss}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{m}\mathrm{D}_q(w_m\Vert u_m)}_{\text{non-uniform penalty}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{q-1}\cdot\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)}_{\text{confidence term}},
\]
and immediately implies an unerasable hard lower bound (same remark): the moment method cannot erase the first geometric floor by scheduling \(q\), but can only perform auditable tradeoffs between the non-uniform penalty and confidence term.

\paragraph{Discrete curvature law of the breakpoint phase diagram: optimal moment order jumps fully controlled by \texorpdfstring{$A_q$}{Aq}}
When treating the moment order \(q\) as a design variable, the kink positions of the certificate lower envelope are given in closed form by the discrete curvature increment scalar \(A_q\):
\[
m_{q\to q+1}(\varepsilon)=\frac{\log(1/\varepsilon)}{A_q},
\qquad
A_q=(q-1)(\log r_{q+1}-\log2)-q(\log r_q-\log2),
\]
leading to the jump phase diagram of \(q^\star(m,\varepsilon)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:pom-breakpoint-curvature-law-Aq}; Remark~\ref{rem:pom-qstar-phase-diagram-breakpoints}).
Hence ``budget phase transition points'' are auditable discrete geometric consequences in this paper's regime: the input is only the verified \(\{r_q\}\), the output is a reproducible breakpoint table and optimal order jumps.

\paragraph{Dirichlet logarithmic image and critical line: \texorpdfstring{$\log$}{log} ratio identity of negative eigenvalues (toy-RH)}
On the spectral side, the \(s\)-plane zero-pole dictionary (Proposition~\ref{prop:s-plane-spectrum-poles}) interprets the ``critical line'' as a pure \(\log\)-ratio identity: any nonzero eigenvalue \(\mu\) generates a sequence of Dirichlet poles whose vertical real part satisfies
\[
\Re(s)=\frac{\log|\mu|}{\log\lambda},
\qquad \lambda:=\rho(M).
\]
Switching to the Dirichlet logarithmic coordinate \(r=\lambda^{1-s}\), the same fact is equivalent to
\(
\Re(s)=1-\frac{\log|r_0|}{\log\lambda}
\)
(where \(r_0=\lambda/\mu\) is the \(r\)-coordinate of the corresponding pole).
Thus a boundary-saturating negative eigenvalue \(\mu=-\sqrt{\lambda}\) directly generates the critical line \(\Re(s)=1/2\).
This ``negative pole \(\Rightarrow\) critical line'' generation mode is realized as an auditable fact in this paper's real-input 40-state kernel (Proposition~\ref{prop:rh-stratification-three-kernels}-(2); see also Proposition~\ref{prop:finite-rh-40}).
