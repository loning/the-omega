\section*{Unified spine (time, selection, updates, and resolution)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Unified spine (time, selection, updates, and resolution)}
\label{sec:unified_spine}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
\AuditTag This reader-facing section compresses a recurring set of structural themes into a single vocabulary:
(i) time as tick and as executed update order,
(ii) deterministic CAP closure as finite-family selection with explicit tie-breaks,
(iii) updates and branching as changes of admissible candidates/objectives under fixed constraints,
and (iv) resolution flow (uplift/RG/forced zoom) as an update trajectory of protocol states.
None of this section is used as a premise in theorem-level folding proofs; it is an organizational index.

\subsection*{Three time notions and their layer placement}
\AuditTag We distinguish three time notions throughout:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{tick time} $t_{\mathrm{tick}}$: the scan iteration index (Definition~\ref{def:tick});
\item \textbf{update time} $t_{\mathrm{upd}}$: the executed order of protocol updates (e.g.\ CAP closures or objective updates), indexed discretely and used to state monotonicity certificates (Section~\ref{subsec:wish_motive_generic_dynamics});
\item \textbf{physical time} $t_{\mathrm{phys}}$: any mapping from tick/update indices to SI units (seconds) or laboratory clock readings, which is always a matching-layer calibration (Remark~\ref{rem:tick_velocity_to_c} and Section~\ref{subsec:arrow_time_calibration_constants}).
\end{itemize}
\AuditTag The first two are intrinsic to the protocol language; the third is a dictionary layer.

\subsection*{Finite-family selection as the universal closure form}
\AuditTag The CAP rule is implemented throughout as deterministic selection on explicit finite candidate families.
Abstractly, a selection problem is a triple $(\mathcal{F},J,\prec)$ consisting of a finite candidate family $\mathcal{F}$,
an explicit objective functional $J:\mathcal{F}\to\RR$, and a deterministic tie-break order $\prec$.
The corresponding deterministic selector $\mathsf{Sel}(\mathcal{F},J,\prec)$ is formalized in Appendix~\ref{app:branching_selection_rigidity}
and instantiated in audit form across the tick\,+\,CAP derivation spine (Appendix~\ref{app:tick_cap_derivation}).

\paragraph{Gaps and robustness.}
\AuditTag When a quantitative objective gap is present, bounded perturbations cannot change the selected minimizer
(gap-stability; Appendix~\ref{app:branching_selection_rigidity} and the RB-D template in Appendix~\ref{app:rigidity_bridge_checklist}).
This is the paper's normalized way to state that a closure is robust rather than an accidental alignment.

\subsection*{Updates, branching, and prefix-consistency}
\AuditTag What is informally called ``branching'' is expressed in finite mathematics by prefix projections and extension fibers:
distinct continuations may share an audited prefix while differing in admissible extensions.
This is formalized by extension fibers $\mathrm{Ext}_m(u)$ under prefix projections $\pi_{m\to m_0}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:branching_selection_rigidity}).
\AuditTag Updates are modeled as maps $(\mathcal{F},J,\prec)\mapsto(\mathcal{F}',J',\prec')$; a ``switch'' occurs when the selected minimizer changes.
If a prefix constraint is preserved, any switch is confined to the corresponding extension fiber (Proposition~\ref{prop:prefix_consistent_switching_constraint}).

\subsection*{Where the HPA-integrated modules sit in the Sel/Upd/gap vocabulary (reader map)}
\label{subsec:sel_upd_gap_hpa_map}
\AuditTag Several companion-HPA modules are integrated into the main text in a way that preserves the same audit discipline.
Their placement in the unified Sel/Upd/gap vocabulary is as follows:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Embedding and projection readout (Sel + gap).}
The projection readout in Section~\ref{sec:embedding_gap_projection} is a concrete instance of deterministic finite-family selection:
for a fixed synthesized $v\in\CC$, the nearest-point rule $\pi(v)$ is a selector on an explicitly finite candidate subset (proved finite in that section), and the objective gap is the separation between the best and second-best distances.
The cohomology-based ``gap field'' viewpoint provides an additional \AuditTag failure mode not reducible to a scalar objective gap (a nontrivial $H^1$ class obstructs global gauging-away of residuals).
\item \textbf{Cut-and-project bridge (prefix/fiber carrier).}
Section~\ref{sec:cut_and_project_bridge} is the geometric carrier behind the prefix/fiber language:
window words induce staircase prefixes and therefore a canonical prefix projection hierarchy; model sets $\Lambda(W)$ provide a point-set realization of the same branching structure.
\item \textbf{Time-advance dispersion channel (dictionary update).}
Section~\ref{sec:dispersion_time_advance} records a kinematic signature once a specific energy/momentum readout is declared (Assumption~\ref{ass:dispersion_energy_momentum_from_scan}).
Interpreting this signature as a laboratory observable is a \MatchTag dictionary update and must be recorded as such, separate from overhead-induced delay channels.
\item \textbf{Octonionic internal lift and adelic prime-orbit modules (candidate-family updates).}
Sections~\ref{sec:octonion_g2_internal_phase} and \ref{sec:adelic_prime_orbit_module} are recorded as \AuditTag/\InterfaceTag candidate modules:
invoking them in any closure requires declaring an explicit finite family (of lifts/orbits/representatives) and a deterministic tie-break, so that the invocation itself is an instance of Sel/Upd under the IC-13 gate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Protocol objects, readout functors, and certificate families (deep unification)}
\label{subsec:protocol_sampling_certificate_unification}

\paragraph{Purpose (one sentence).}
\AuditTag Many parts of this manuscript are easiest to read as statements about the \emph{same} protocol object viewed through different readout functors and audited by different certificate families.
This subsection records the common object model and how it reuses the Sel/Upd/gap vocabulary already fixed above.

\begin{definition}[Protocol object (audit-facing packaging)]\label{def:protocol_object_packaging}
\AuditTag A protocol object is the tuple
\[
\mathsf{Prot}:=(X,\Theta;\ \mathcal{K};\ \mathcal{R}),
\]
where $(X,\Theta)$ is the scan system (state space and update operator),
$\mathcal{K}$ is an explicitly declared finite family of \emph{cuts/kernels/weights} (window projectors $\Pi_W$, soft kernels $\pi_\sigma$, scattering carriers, etc.),
and $\mathcal{R}$ is a declared \emph{readout functor} that maps $(X,\Theta,K)$ to a finite observable or to a finite certificate object.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Readout--certificate composition (minimal functorial form)]\label{def:readout_certificate_composition}
\AuditTag Fix a protocol object and a kernel/cut choice $K\in\mathcal{K}$.
Write the readout output as
\[
O_K:=\mathcal{R}(X,\Theta,K),
\]
and let $\mathcal{C}$ denote a declared certificate functional (or certificate generator) acting on readout outputs.
The induced certificate is the composition
\[
\mathrm{Cert}_{\mathcal{C}}(K):=(\mathcal{C}\circ\mathcal{R})(X,\Theta,K)=\mathcal{C}(O_K).
\]
When $\mathcal{C}$ is scalar-valued, it can be used directly as an objective $J$ in a Sel instance (Appendix~\ref{app:branching_selection_rigidity}).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Sel/Upd/gap reuse: certificates are objectives on finite families]\label{rem:certificates_as_sel_objectives_unified_spine}
\AuditTag Under Definition~\ref{def:protocol_object_packaging}, a ``certificate'' is not a separate ontology: it is typically a functional of the readout output (or a generating series built from it) and can be treated as an objective $J$ in a selection problem (Definition~\ref{def:selection_problem_tiebreak}).
Therefore, \emph{certificate extraction and parameter choice} are instances of the same Sel/Upd/gap discipline:
Sel chooses $K\in\mathcal{K}$ (or a finite surrogate output) by a declared objective and tie-break,
Upd changes $(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{R})$ along a resolution/update trajectory,
and gap-stability controls whether the selected certificate is robust under bounded perturbations (Appendix~\ref{app:branching_selection_rigidity}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Obstructions as non-commuting update diagrams (audit template)]\label{rem:obstruction_as_noncommuting_diagram}
\AuditTag In the audit language used here, many ``failure points'' can be summarized as a \emph{diagram not commuting} under an update:
one route updates the protocol (change $K$ or refine the readout), while another route takes a certificate first and then updates in the certificate coordinate.
When the two routes disagree beyond a declared tolerance, the disagreement is recorded as an obstruction channel.
Concrete instances recorded in this manuscript include:
(i) gap cohomology classes that obstruct global cancellation of projection residuals (Section~\ref{sec:embedding_gap_projection}),
(ii) regularization-coordinate dependence in Abel-first/finite-part prescriptions (Appendix~\ref{app:abel_finite_part_notes} and Appendix~\ref{app:cyclic_calculus_theta_fp}),
and (iii) order sensitivity under noncommuting cuts (Appendix~\ref{app:reference_state_and_cuts}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Obstruction functional (minimal audit form)]\label{def:obstruction_functional}
\AuditTag Fix a protocol object $\mathsf{Prot}=(X,\Theta;\mathcal{K};\mathcal{R})$ (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_object_packaging})
and a certificate functional (or generator) $\mathcal{C}$ as in Definition~\ref{def:readout_certificate_composition}.
Let $\mathsf{Upd}$ be an update that changes the protocol-side choice (kernel/cut/readout parameters) from $K$ to $K':=\mathsf{Upd}(K)$.
\AuditTag If the manuscript declares a corresponding induced update rule $\mathsf{Upd}_{\mathcal{C}}$ on the certificate coordinate,
define the \emph{obstruction} by the (signed) mismatch between the two routes:
\[
\mathrm{Obs}(\mathsf{Upd};\mathcal{C};K)
:=
\mathrm{Cert}_{\mathcal{C}}(K')\;-\;\mathsf{Upd}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathrm{Cert}_{\mathcal{C}}(K)).
\]
\AuditTag If no induced rule $\mathsf{Upd}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is declared (or if it is not invariant under the declared equivalences),
then the obstruction is \emph{undefined} and the certificate cannot be transported across that update without adding an explicit matching dictionary.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Three instantiated obstructions already present in this manuscript]\label{rem:obstruction_functional_instances}
\AuditTag Definition~\ref{def:obstruction_functional} is only a packaging device; its content is provided by concrete instantiations already recorded elsewhere:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(HPA gap field) cohomology obstruction.}
In Section~\ref{sec:embedding_gap_projection}, the residual gap field on an interaction complex admits gauge-like updates by adding coboundaries.
Here $\mathsf{Upd}$ is the gauge update, $\mathsf{Upd}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the coboundary action, and the obstruction is the cohomology class:
vanishing obstruction corresponds to $[\delta]=0$; a nontrivial class $[\delta]\in H^1$ is an auditable structured failure mode.
\item \textbf{(Abel/finite-part) regularization-coordinate obstruction.}
In Appendix~\ref{app:abel_finite_part_notes} and Appendix~\ref{app:cyclic_calculus_theta_fp}, $\mathsf{Upd}$ changes the Abel/regularization coordinate (e.g.\ the approach parameter to the pole barrier),
and $\mathsf{Upd}_{\mathcal{C}}$ is the declared finite-part/constant-term extraction rule.
The obstruction is the residual dependence of the extracted constant term across admissible coordinates, which must be recorded as a matching/audit boundary if nonzero.
\item \textbf{(Cut order) noncommutativity obstruction.}
In Appendix~\ref{app:reference_state_and_cuts}, swapping the order of positive cuts is an update $\mathsf{Upd}$; if one attempts to transport a spectral certificate across this update,
the mismatch can manifest as a complex spectral component (Remark~\ref{rem:cuts_noncommuting_imaginary_component}).
In this manuscript, this is treated as an order-sensitivity audit template and is not promoted to a physical identification without an explicit matching dictionary.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Reader map: one protocol object, many audited outputs.}
\AuditTag In this manuscript the following modules can be read as different readout functors/certificates on the same packaged protocol object:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Symbolic word readout.} $K=\Pi_W$ and $\mathcal{R}$ is the window-word map $w_n=\mathbf{1}\{z_n\in W\}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:window_projection}).
\item \textbf{Projection readout + quantum gap.} $K$ is a nearest-point/soft projection kernel $\pi,\pi_\sigma$ on an explicitly finite candidate set and the certificate is the residual $\delta$ (Section~\ref{sec:embedding_gap_projection}); a structured failure mode is recorded by a nontrivial $H^1$ class (same section).
\item \textbf{Cyclic calculus and Abel finite parts.} $\mathcal{R}$ is a finite-difference/trace package on scan orbits, and the certificate is a renormalized constant-term (Abel-first/finite-part) value (Appendix~\ref{app:abel_finite_part_notes} and Appendix~\ref{app:cyclic_calculus_theta_fp}).
\item \textbf{Det/trace/ζ bookkeeping and primitive-cycle refinement.} $\mathcal{R}$ packages periodic counts by $-\log\det(I-zM)=\sum z^k\mathrm{tr}(M^k)/k$ (Section~\ref{subsec:rigidity_det_trace_zeta}); primitive-cycle Euler products are recorded as a finite-state refinement (Appendix~\ref{app:zeckendorf_shift_prime_cycles}).
\item \textbf{Delay certificates as logdet derivatives.} On a declared unitary band, the WS trace is a frequency derivative of $\log\det S$ (Section~\ref{subsec:rigidity_scattering_delay_logdet}); this is a certificate of the same determinant/trace family with a different coordinate (derivative in $\omega$).
\item \textbf{Reference-state limit and cut order sensitivity.} Window words can be realized as a projection-measurement limit in $L^2(\RR/\ZZ)$, and commuting/noncommuting positive cuts provide a minimal order-sensitivity spectrum template (Appendix~\ref{app:reference_state_and_cuts}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Resolution flow as protocol-state updates}
\AuditTag Many interface outputs depend on a protocol state $(m,n,K)$ (window length, addressing order, readout kernel),
which is closed by CAP within explicit finite families (Section~\ref{sec:paper_contract} and Appendix~\ref{app:tick_cap_derivation}).
In this paper, three recurring narratives are treated as instances of the same update viewpoint:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{uplift}: changing the window length $m$ and selecting fiber representatives under refinement (Appendix~\ref{app:mass_flow_under_uplift});
\item \textbf{RG}: describing running in the resolution coordinate $r(\mu)$ with explicit scheme/threshold boundaries (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow});
\item \textbf{forced zoom}: engineered threshold crossing that changes the effective admissible protocol budget (Section~\ref{subsec:active_renormalization_synthesis}).
\end{itemize}
\AuditTag The mathematical-layer carrier for these statements is always finite (candidate families, prefix fibers, and deterministic keys);
any calibration to laboratory units remains at the matching layer.
