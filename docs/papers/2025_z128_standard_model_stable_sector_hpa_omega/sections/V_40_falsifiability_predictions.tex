
\section{Falsifiability: predictions in the protocol language}
\label{sec:falsifiability}

The folding counts and tables are mathematical-layer facts.
The statements below are physical-layer predictions: they can be tested by observational constraints, laboratory bounds, and reproducible protocol-level audits.

\paragraph{Time-first test strategy.}
In the tick-first dictionary of this paper (Section~\ref{sec:tick_calculus}), the primitive input is the sequential update count and the primitive closure rule is CAP.
Accordingly, the most direct experimental handles are \emph{time dictionaries}: delay and clock-rate proxies that test the overhead interpretation of mass and scale.
For a time-first reading, begin with P6 (scattering delay as a lapse proxy).
Predictions P1/P2/P4/P5 are protocol-level structural or rigidity statements that do not require a numerical staircase calibration.
Prediction P3 is special: its \emph{threshold locations} depend on the bounded-complexity calibration of $r_{\mathrm{step}}$ and the chosen reference anchors, and should therefore be read as a conditional prediction within that calibrated hypothesis class.

\paragraph{Protocol-state convention (what the predictions are conditional on).}
\AuditTag The falsifiability statements below are formulated in the protocol language and are therefore conditional on a declared protocol state $(m,n,K)$ (Appendix~\ref{subsec:tick_cap_joint_protocol_state}).
The $m$ and $n$ components determine which finite objects are available to the protocol (words, stable types, and spatial diagnostics), while the kernel component $K$ determines how finite outcomes are weighted when forming scalar readouts.
\AuditTag Predictions P1/P2/P4/P5 are intended to be largely kernel-robust within the minimal kernel families of Appendix~\ref{app:kernel_family_cap_closure} because they are structural (cardinality, split constraints, and symmetry-defect logic).
Predictions that depend on weighted scalars reconstructed from data (e.g.\ $\chi$-based channels and some RG-operator readouts) should be evaluated together with explicit kernel-family stability sweeps.

\subsubsection{P1: right-handed neutrinos as protocol-external / ghost modes}
\label{subsec:p1_rh_neutrino}

If weak chirality is tied to the Hilbert-protocol orientation bit and its mirror-protocol swap (Definition~\ref{def:scl}), then a neutral singlet $\nu_R$ can behave as a protocol-decoupled mode: it can be stable at the level of type labeling while remaining effectively unobservable through the weak compensation connection within a fixed protocol.
Operationally, this manifests as an ``invisible'' or ``ghost-like'' degree of freedom with extremely suppressed couplings at the readout level \cite{PDGRPP2024}.
\begin{remark}[Relation to standard sterile-neutrino extensions]
Gauge-singlet right-handed neutrinos are also the standard minimal extension used in neutrino-mass model building (e.g.\ seesaw completions of the dimension-five Weinberg operator); see, e.g., \cite{Weinberg1979WeinbergOperator,Minkowski1977Seesaw,PDGRPP2024}.
\end{remark}
\paragraph{A concrete interface reason at the chosen 2D anchor (m,n)=(6,3).}
In the closed labeling map, $\nu_R$ carries $(SU(3),SU(2))_Y=(1,1)_0$ (Table~\ref{tab:sm_labeling_table}), hence is neutral under all three gauge-factor connection classes.
In this sense, $\nu_R$ is the forced protocol-external candidate within the closed labeling: it can exist as a stable label without participating in the weak $SU(2)$ compensation transport within a fixed protocol.
\paragraph{Relation to the hidden microstates.}
At $m=6$, the protocol-unstable complement has size $|\Omega_6\setminus X_6|=64-21=43$.
In addition to protocol-decoupling within $X_6$, a stronger possibility is protocol-instability: if certain degrees of freedom are implemented as readout patterns outside $X_6$, they reside in this ghost sector and are absent from stable visible outputs.

\paragraph{Data channel.}
Laboratory sterile-neutrino searches (oscillation anomalies, beta-decay spectral distortions, missing-energy signatures) and cosmological bounds on extra relativistic degrees of freedom provide direct constraints on protocol-decoupled neutral singlets.

\paragraph{Fail condition.}
Evidence for an unsuppressed weakly coupled right-handed neutrino mode within the same protocol class (i.e.\ a $\nu_R$ that participates in the $SU(2)$ compensation transport comparably to left-handed leptons) would contradict the protocol-external/decoupled identification used here.

\subsubsection{P2: chirality-domain defects and large-scale statistical signatures}
\label{subsec:p2_domain_walls}

If early-universe initialization admitted domains with different Hilbert orientation classes, domain boundaries would act as protocol defects.
Such defects should leave parity-sensitive statistical signatures (e.g.\ polarization correlations) tied to the chirality index sign.
\emph{Remark:} domain walls as macroscopic defect remnants are standard in symmetry-breaking narratives; see, e.g., \cite{VilenkinShellard1994,ZeldovichKobzarevOkun1974}.
\paragraph{Test strategy.}
The prediction is protocol-level: one should search for statistics that are invariant under orientation-preserving layout changes but change sign under reflection-like protocol swaps, consistent with Proposition~\ref{prop:chi_flip}.
Conversely, parity-even observables provide a control: coarse-grained scalars such as those in Definition~\ref{def:coarse_grained_scalar} should be coherent across orientation domains up to pullback and should not exhibit an intrinsic sign flip.

\paragraph{Concrete observable channel: CMB parity-odd correlators and cosmic birefringence.}
A standard parity-sensitive testbed is CMB polarization: the $E/B$ decomposition separates parity-even and parity-odd modes \cite{ZaldarriagaSeljak1997,KamionkowskiKosowskyStebbins1997}.
In statistically isotropic parity-invariant models, the $TB$ and $EB$ cross-correlations vanish (up to foreground/systematics), while a parity-odd polarization-rotation (cosmic birefringence) angle $\beta$ mixes $E$ and $B$ and induces nonzero $TB/EB$ with a sign controlled by $\beta$ \cite{LueWangKamionkowski1999,MinamiKomatsu2020}.
In the present protocol language, different Hilbert orientation domains flip $\mathrm{sgn}(\chi)$; therefore any fitted parity-odd estimator whose sign tracks an effective rotation/birefringence sign is expected to flip sign between domains while parity-even control statistics remain coherent up to pullback.

\paragraph{Data channel.}
CMB polarization (parity-odd $TB/EB$ correlators), cosmic-birefringence estimators, and other large-scale parity-odd correlation searches provide concrete observational channels.

\paragraph{Fail condition.}
If improved multi-frequency data and systematics-controlled analyses exclude any domain-like sign-flip pattern in parity-odd estimators while the protocol-chirality mapping remains fixed, the chirality-domain defect scenario is disfavored.

\subsubsection{P3: resolution jumps and Fibonacci-structured spectrum thresholds}
\label{subsec:p3_resolution_jumps}

At window length $m$, the admissible stable type count is $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:xm_fib}).
Moreover, the $\pi$-channel cyclic/boundary split is itself Fibonacci-rigid:
for $m\ge 4$,
\[
|X_m^{\mathrm{bdry}}|=F_{m-2},\qquad |X_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}|=F_{m+2}-F_{m-2}
\]
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_size}).
If effective window length changes with energy or environment, the number of stable types should exhibit threshold behavior constrained by Fibonacci growth rather than arbitrary particle additions.

\paragraph{Collider as an active observer: forced zoom (interface).}
In this framework, a high-energy collider is not merely a kinetic machine but an information-focusing device.
By concentrating energy density into a small interaction region, the experiment can force the local protocol to operate at higher effective resolution and thereby make higher-$m$ structure accessible in readout.
Operationally, this corresponds to pushing the local scale across the calibrated thresholds $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(m)$ so that the deterministic selection $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu)$ jumps (Corollary~\ref{cor:resolution_selection}).
We refer to this experimental act as \emph{active renormalization}: one does not change the theorem-level folding core, but forces the protocol to render latent high-complexity modes that are otherwise inaccessible at the $m=6$ ground-state readout.

\paragraph{Fibonacci-structured thresholds.}
Since $F_{m+2}$ obeys $F_{m+2}=F_{m+1}+F_m$, any one-step resolution uplift $m\mapsto m+1$ admits only a constrained increment of stable type count.
This provides a rigid spectral-template prediction: new stable modes, if any, should enter in Fibonacci-structured batches.
At the minimal anchor $m=6$, the split is $18\oplus 3$; the next two uplifts would yield $29\oplus 5$ at $m=7$ and $47\oplus 8$ at $m=8$.

\paragraph{A minimal \texorpdfstring{$\mu\leftrightarrow m$}{mu<->m} calibration.}
To compare these discrete uplifts to energy thresholds, we use the golden resolution coordinate already employed in the mass-spectrum closure:
\[
r(\mu)=\frac{\log(\mu/m_e)}{\log\varphi}.
\]
We record a minimal calibration in which a one-step window uplift $m\mapsto m+1$ corresponds to a fixed additive depth increment
\[
r_{\mathrm{step}}:=2\pi,
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{th}}(m):=(m-6)\,r_{\mathrm{step}},
\qquad
\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(m)=m_e\,\varphi^{\,r_{\mathrm{th}}(m)}.
\]
In this calibration, the threshold for $m=10$ lies near the electroweak scale ($\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(10)\approx 91\,$GeV), providing a concrete protocol-level staircase template for where Fibonacci-structured stable-type uplifts may occur.
The resulting spectrum template is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:resolution_spectrum}.

\paragraph{Odd steps as bridges between even stability islands (interface).}
The even steps $m=6,8,10$ are already singled out by the combination of the $m=6$ coarse-lock anchor and the bounded-complexity staircase calibration: they align the minimal matter anchor (electron), a conservative hadronic-scale \MatchTag benchmark $\mu_{\mathrm{QCD}}\sim 0.2\,\mathrm{GeV}$, and the electroweak layer, respectively.
Once this scaffold is fixed, the odd steps are no longer optional: the same staircase necessarily places intermediate thresholds at $m=7,9,11$ (Table~\ref{tab:resolution_spectrum}).
This yields three concrete, falsifiable targets for where additional stable-mode structure should concentrate if it arises through resolution uplifts:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Prediction 1 (nuclear binding scale):} $m=7$ corresponds to $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(7)\sim 10\,\mathrm{MeV}$, near the characteristic nuclear binding scale (few--$10$ MeV), suggesting an intermediate ``binding'' information density that can glue $m=6$ matter-like objects into nuclei.
Here $m=8$ is treated only as a conservative hadronic-scale matching benchmark (Table~\ref{tab:resolution_spectrum} and Table~\ref{tab:resolution_calibration_multianchor}); no nonperturbative confinement or mass-gap mechanism is claimed by this staircase alone \cite{JaffeWitten2000YangMillsMassGap,Greensite2011Confinement}.
\item \textbf{Prediction 2 (bottom onset):} $m=9$ corresponds to $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(9)\sim 4.4\,\mathrm{GeV}$, close to the bottom threshold ($m_b\approx 4.18\,\mathrm{GeV}$), marking the staircase onset point for heavy-flavor physics under the fixed calibration \cite{PDGRPP2024}.
\item \textbf{Prediction 3 (BSM frontier target):} $m=11$ corresponds to $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(11)\sim 1.9\,\mathrm{TeV}$ and provides a protocol-level BSM target at the LHC/FCC frontier: any additional stable modes entering as an effective uplift to $m=11$ should be constrained by the finite topological capacity $|X_{11}|=233$ and by the inherited cyclic/boundary split constraints from the $\pi$ channel.
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}[Resolution selection by least discrepancy (interface)]\label{cor:resolution_selection}
Fix $r_{\mathrm{step}}>0$ and the threshold map $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(m)$ above.
Given an effective energy (or mass) scale $\mu$, define $r(\mu)=\log(\mu/m_e)/\log\varphi$ and select an \emph{effective window length} by the deterministic rule
\[
m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu):=6+\left\lfloor \frac{r(\mu)}{r_{\mathrm{step}}}\right\rfloor,
\]
equivalently: $m_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mu)=m$ if and only if $\mu_{\mathrm{th}}(m)\le \mu < \mu_{\mathrm{th}}(m+1)$.
This is the minimal closed selection principle compatible with CAP (Axiom~\ref{ax:cap}): window length increases only when the scale crosses a calibrated threshold, and within each band the protocol uses the smallest admissible $m$.
\end{corollary}

\subsubsection{P6: scattering delay as a measurable lapse proxy (interface)}
\label{subsec:p6_wigner_smith_delay}

Normalizing by a calibrated tick duration $\tau_0$ yields a dimensionless overhead proxy $\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}=\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}/\tau_0$ and a lapse proxy $N_{\mathrm{WS}}=\kappa_0/\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}$ (Section~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}).
The prediction is that, under any concrete platform identification that ties the measured scattering channel to protocol-local degrees of freedom, delay-derived lapse ratios should behave like redshift/clock-slowing ratios under the same identification, providing an independent falsifiability route for the overhead--lapse dictionary.

\paragraph{Data channel.}
Platforms with measurable near-unitary scattering matrices (microwave/optical cavities, mesoscopic quantum transport, cold-atom scattering, nuclear/particle scattering phase-shift analyses) provide channels where Wigner--Smith delay can be extracted and compared across controlled conditions.

\paragraph{Fail condition.}
If delay-derived lapse ratios fail to correlate with independently measured clock-rate/redshift proxies under the same platform identification (beyond stated experimental/systematic uncertainty), the overhead--lapse matching dictionary is disfavored.

\paragraph{Data channel.}
Time-delay or scattering-delay proxies together with a declared $\chi$ reconstruction (and its counterfactual sweeps) provide the primary channel.
Gravitational lensing and redshift/clock-rate proxies provide complementary channels through the same overhead dictionary.

\paragraph{Fail condition.}
If, under declared reconstruction/regularization baselines and stated uncertainties, delay-dominated regions do not correlate with elevated $\chi$ (beyond what is expected under a null shuffle/pullback control),
or if the inferred occupancy required by the budget-trigger criterion is systematically incompatible with the observed localization across comparable datasets without changing the declared protocol class,
then the budget-triggered $\chi$-horizon localization claim is disfavored as stated.

\subsubsection{P9: black-hole information recovery must appear as record-level correlations (protocol Page surrogate)}
\label{subsec:p9_bh_page_surrogate}

Black-hole information recovery is not a statement about the marginal spectrum of radiation alone, but about correlations in the externally accessible record.
In the protocol discipline of this paper, any recovery claim must be formulated on a declared record algebra $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{out}}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:bh_scope_contract}) and must supply a protocol-computable surrogate for the ``Page behavior'' of information recovery (Appendix~\ref{app:bh_page_surrogate}).
At fixed $m$, the folding fibers $P(w)=\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)$ quantify hidden micro-information under coarse stable-label readout; the BH4 information-flow table (Appendix~\ref{app:bh_evaporation_leak_channel}, Table~\ref{tab:bh4_information_flow}) makes this explicit.

\paragraph{Test strategy (within the protocol language).}
Choose a concrete platform and declare:
(i) the external record stream (stable labels, exit-channel symbols, or an explicit measurement record),
(ii) the resource ledger used to interpret long-time emission as evaporation (BH4),
and (iii) the recovery surrogate $S_{\mathrm{sur}}(R_{0:t})$ (BH5).
The falsifiability content is that the record must exhibit nontrivial higher-order correlations consistent with recovery: the surrogate should display a Page-like transition (growth to a maximum followed by a decrease toward the long-time recovery target) within the stated hypothesis class and error budget.

\paragraph{Data channel.}
Any setting that provides long record streams together with independent delay/overhead proxies is admissible:
gravitational time-delay channels (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}), K4 leakage/linewidth audits (Appendices~\ref{app:k4_pdg_leakage_audit} and \ref{app:k4_ws_linewidth_audit}), and protocol-horizon diagnostics (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_horizon_tick_trap}).

\paragraph{Fail condition.}
If, under declared baselines and stated uncertainties, the external record remains compatible with a memoryless coarse process that never develops recovery-level correlations (i.e.\ the surrogate fails to exhibit a Page-like transition without expanding the declared hypothesis class),
then the unitarity/recovery closure is disfavored as stated.
Conversely, if recovery-level correlations are claimed but require hidden knobs (undeclared kernels, untracked candidate-family expansion, or non-reproducible selection), the claim fails the audit contract.
