\section{Protocol horizon and relative black-hole criterion: a tick-trap viewpoint (extended interface module)}
\label{app:protocol_horizon_tick_trap}

\paragraph{Scope and status.}
\AuditTag This appendix defines an operational notion of a \emph{protocol horizon} (an information/observability horizon) in the tick-first language.
It is an extended interface module: it introduces no new theorem-level premise into the folding core.
The purpose is to make precise what it means for an object to appear ``black-hole-like'' \emph{relative to an observer's finite budget}, without requiring an a priori identification with a GR event horizon.

\subsection{Budgets and capacities}
\label{subsec:protocol_horizon_budgets}

\paragraph{Observer budget (bits).}
\InterfaceTag Fix an observer/measurement protocol $\mathcal O$ and let $I_{\mathrm{obs}}(\mathcal O)$ denote a finite information budget: the maximal number of bits that can be stably extracted (distinguished) about a target in the given experimental setting.
This budget is not assumed to be universal; it is an interface-level quantity.

\paragraph{Protocol screen capacity at resolution \texorpdfstring{$(m,n)$}{(m,n)}.}
\InterfaceTag At protocol resolution $(m,n)$, define the screen microstate capacity
\[
I_{\mathrm{prot}}(m,n):=\log_2\!\left(|\Omega_m|^{4^n}\right)=m\,4^n,
\]
as in Appendix~\ref{app:bh_planck_capacity_calibration}.
This is a protocol-native capacity measure; it does not presuppose continuum geometry.

\paragraph{Entropy bits vs.\ window length.}
\AuditTag To avoid overloading notation, we distinguish the window length $m$ (a protocol parameter) from an entropy/information content in bits.
When an entropy $S$ is used in physical units, the corresponding information content in bits is
\[
I:=\frac{S}{k_B\ln 2}
\]
(Assumption~\ref{ass:bh_planck_units_info}).

\subsection{Tick-trap observables and the horizon criterion}
\label{subsec:protocol_horizon_criterion}

\paragraph{Tick-trap viewpoint.}
\InterfaceTag In the tick-first stance, ``being massive'' is read as ``exhibiting systematic latency/overhead'' (Section~\ref{sec:mass_latency_coordinate}).
Operationally, a primary proxy is scattering time delay.

\begin{definition}[Protocol horizon (information/observability horizon)]
\label{def:protocol_horizon}
Fix a coarse-graining map $\mathsf C$ and an observer budget $I_{\mathrm{obs}}$.
We say that a target $X$ exhibits a \emph{protocol horizon} for $\mathcal O$ if, at the relevant resolution, (i) the externally accessible outcomes are bounded by a finite effective screen capacity, and (ii) the residual internal microstate multiplicity compatible with the observed macrostate exceeds the observer budget in the sense that the target cannot be refined (resolved) by $\mathcal O$ without changing the protocol itself.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Relative black-hole criterion (protocol)]
\label{def:relative_black_hole_protocol}
Fix $\mathcal O$ with budget $I_{\mathrm{obs}}$.
We say that $X$ is \emph{black-hole-like relative to} $\mathcal O$ if it exhibits a protocol horizon (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_horizon}) and, in addition, it is ``delay-dominated'' in the sense that its response is characterized by large dwell/lag proxies (e.g.\ large Wigner--Smith delay over the relevant band) compared to the protocol's baseline.\footnote{This second condition is intentionally stated at the interface level and is made auditable in Appendix~\ref{app:leakage_kernel} by declaring a finite leakage-kernel family and reporting delay/leakage diagnostics.}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Relation to GR horizons (specialization under \texorpdfstring{$P_{\mathrm{phys}}$}{Pphys})}
\label{subsec:protocol_horizon_gr_specialization}

\AuditTag Under the boundary/Planck information pack (Assumptions~\ref{ass:bh_planck_units_info}--\ref{ass:bh_schwarzschild_package}),
one may treat a black-hole horizon as a saturation of boundary channel capacity.
Appendix~\ref{app:bh_planck_capacity_calibration} records a finite-family calibration that links black-hole boundary information to $(m,n)$ capacities.
The present appendix does not assert that every protocol horizon is a GR event horizon; rather, it provides the operational notion that can \emph{specialize} to the standard GR horizon picture in the appropriate saturation regime.
