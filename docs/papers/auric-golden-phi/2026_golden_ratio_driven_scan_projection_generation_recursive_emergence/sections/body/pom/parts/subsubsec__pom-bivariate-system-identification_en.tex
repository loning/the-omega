\paragraph{Two-Dimensional System Identification: Finite Rectangular Data Determines the Minimal Bivariate Kernel}
Theorem \ref{thm:pom-bivariate-moment-zeta-fredholm} packages the two-dimensional moment table \(\{S_q(m)\}_{q,m\ge1}\) into a bivariate Fredholm determinant
\(
\mathfrak{Z}(z,u)=\det(I-z\mathcal{A}(u))^{-1}
\).
Under this framework, the "two-dimensional system identification" problem can be stated as follows: given only a finite rectangular window of observations \(\{S_q(m)\}_{q\le Q,\ m\le M}\), can one uniquely recover the kernel family \(\mathcal{A}(u)\) of minimal dimension (up to similarity) and extend it to the full table?
The following result establishes a standard finite-window threshold: when the two-dimensional Hankel--Krylov rank reaches the minimal rank \(R\), the finite rectangular data in principle constitutes a decidable complete invariant.

\begin{definition}[Two-Dimensional Hankel--Krylov Identification Rank]\label{def:pom-bivariate-hankel-krylov-rank}
Given a two-dimensional sequence \(\{S_q(m)\}_{q,m\ge1}\),
define its double-shift closure as
\[
\mathsf{Span}(S):=\mathrm{span}_{\QQ}\Bigl\{(m,q)\mapsto S_{q+q_0}(m+m_0):\ m_0,q_0\in\NN_0\Bigr\},
\]
and let
\[
R:=\dim_{\QQ}\mathsf{Span}(S)\ \in\ \NN\cup\{\infty\}.
\]
When \(R<\infty\), we call \(R\) the \emph{two-dimensional Hankel--Krylov minimal rank} of this table (equivalently understood as the rank of the two-dimensional Hankel operator; its finiteness is equivalent to the existence of a finite-dimensional bivariate rational representation).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Rectangular Decidability and Uniqueness (Two-Dimensional Minimal Realization)]\label{thm:pom-bivariate-system-identification-finite-window}
Suppose \(\{S_q(m)\}\) is generated by some minimal bivariate representation: there exists a family of matrices
\(\mathcal{A}(u)\in\Mat_{R\times R}(\QQ(u))\) of dimension \(R<\infty\), analytic at \(u=0\) with \(\mathcal{A}(0)=0\), satisfying
\[
\mathfrak{Z}(z,u)
:=\exp\!\Bigl(\sum_{m\ge1}\sum_{q\ge1}\frac{S_q(m)}{m}u^q z^m\Bigr)
=\det(I-z\mathcal{A}(u))^{-1}.
\]
Let \(R\) also be the minimal rank from Definition \ref{def:pom-bivariate-hankel-krylov-rank}.
Suppose a rectangular window
\[
\{S_q(m):\ 1\le m\le 2R,\ 1\le q\le 2R\}
\]
is given, and that within this window one can verify that "the two-dimensional Hankel--Krylov rank reaches \(R\)" (i.e., the linear dependence relations generated by double shifts have stably closed within the \(2R\times 2R\) window).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item There exists a unique (up to similarity transformation) minimal kernel family \(\mathcal{A}(u)\) of dimension \(R\) generating this window data;
  \item This kernel family uniquely extends to all \(\{S_q(m)\}_{q,m\ge1}\);
  \item Consequently, the two-dimensional statistics of the "fold--moment compilation kernel" can in principle be completely reconstructed from finite rectangular observation (two-dimensional system identification).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By hypothesis, \(\mathfrak{Z}(z,u)\) is a bivariate rational function, and its minimal realization dimension equals the rank \(R\) of the corresponding Hankel (double-shift) operator;
this is a standard result in minimal realization theory for finite-dimensional \(\det\)-representations (or equivalently, linear representations/recognizable rational series).
\par
When the rank is \(R\), the double-shift closure \(\mathsf{Span}(S)\) is spanned by \(R\) basis functions;
the rank stabilizing in the window \(m,q\le 2R\) means: the required linear relations (two-dimensional recurrences/elimination relations) are already observable and solvable in finitely many steps,
so that one can algebraically recover a realization of dimension \(R\) (for example, by constructing a bidirectional Krylov basis and solving for generators in the similarity class).
\par
The uniqueness (up to similarity) of the minimal realization likewise comes from the minimality of the Hankel rank: any two minimal realizations induce the same Hankel bilinear form, hence must be similar.
Once \(\mathcal{A}(u)\) is recovered, the coefficient-extraction identity
\(
\mathfrak{Z}(z,u)=\det(I-z\mathcal{A}(u))^{-1}
\)
uniquely extends all \(S_q(m)\).
\end{proof}
