
\section{Introduction: from analytic infinity to protocol geometry}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) asserts that every nontrivial zero $\rho$ of the Riemann zeta function satisfies
\[
\RePart(\rho)=\tfrac{1}{2}.
\]
In its classical analytic number theory setting, RH is embedded in the machinery of analytic continuation, functional equations, and explicit estimates for prime-counting functions \cite{Titchmarsh1986, Edwards1974, IwaniecKowalski2004}.
In the ``number theory meets physics'' tradition (e.g.\ the Hilbert--P\'olya program and trace formula perspectives), the zeros behave like a frequency spectrum dual to prime periodic data.

This paper takes a different entry point.
We treat the \emph{finite-resource scan--readout process} as a first-class object rather than a late-stage regularization of an a priori infinite analytic formalism.
Concretely, we adopt an audit-driven two-layer writing discipline:

\paragraph{Closed layer.}
Only protocol definitions, finite-resource objects, deterministic error certificates, and statements that can be proved within these notions are admitted as mathematical inputs.

\paragraph{Programmatic layer.}
Physical and teleological interpretations are allowed, but they are explicitly excluded from proofs and do not serve as theorem inputs.

Within the closed layer, our goal is a logically checkable implication chain that isolates where the nontrivial analytic difficulty resides and what is forced once a specific bridge is granted.
The bridge is a structural assumption, formulated below as a \emph{Holographic Trace Formula} (HTF), that embeds the classical explicit formula for $\zeta$ into a trace identity of a scan algebra.
Under this assumption, RH becomes a rigidity statement about \emph{Abel regularization along a canonical protocol path}.

\subsection{Summary of the closed-layer derivation}
\label{sec:intro_summary}

We aim to establish the following conditional chain.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Omega axioms (finite information + unitary scan + bounded readout).}
Selected axioms of the HPA--Omega system imply that a broad class of Abel-weighted orbit traces define holomorphic functions on the open unit disk $|r|<1$ (as power series with bounded coefficients), and that a finite-part extraction along the canonical real path $r\uparrow 1$ is an admissible operation in the closed layer (Section~\ref{sec:omega_axioms}).

\item \textbf{Holographic Trace Formula assumption (HTF).}
Assume the existence of a trace identity that is isomorphic, under the same regularization convention, to a Weil-type explicit formula: prime/periodic-orbit data form the geometric side and zeta zeros form the spectral side.
Crucially, each zero contributes as an exponential mode $e^{(\rho-\tfrac{1}{2})t}$ in the log-time variable (Section~\ref{sec:htf_rigidity}).

\item \textbf{Internal pole obstruction for off-critical modes.}
If $\rho=\beta+\iu\gamma$ with $\beta>\tfrac{1}{2}$, then the corresponding mode factor
\[
\frac{1}{1-r\,e^{(\rho-\tfrac{1}{2})}}
\]
has an interior pole at $r_\rho=e^{-(\beta-\tfrac{1}{2})}e^{-\iu\gamma}$ with $|r_\rho|<1$.
Therefore any spectral-side expression that contains this mode with nonzero coefficient cannot extend holomorphically to the full unit disk, contradicting the Omega geometric-side holomorphy (Lemma~\ref{lem:abel_obstruction}).

\item \textbf{Polar rigidity implies the critical line.}
Combining Omega's geometric-side holomorphy on $|r|<1$ with HTF and the internal-pole obstruction rules out $\RePart(\rho)\neq\tfrac{1}{2}$ and yields RH as a closed-layer consequence of (Omega + HTF) (Theorem~\ref{thm:polar_rigidity}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What this paper does and does not claim}
\label{sec:intro_scope}

The argument does \emph{not} claim an unconditional proof of RH in the standard axioms of analytic number theory.
The nontrivial content is isolated into the explicit structural bridge HTF: constructing, in classical terms, an infinite-dimensional trace/transfer operator satisfying HTF is essentially the hard part of RH.
What this paper provides is a clean protocol-level statement:
\emph{if} $\zeta$ admits an HTF embedding compatible with Omega's canonical Abel finite-part rule, \emph{then} RH is forced by an analytic rigidity on the unit disk: bounded scan traces are holomorphic for $|r|<1$, while any off-critical spectral mode would introduce an interior pole.

We emphasize that the core rigidity is analytic: once the geometric side is a holomorphic function on the unit disk (as mandated by bounded unitary scan readout) and the spectral side inserts each zero via a mode factor with pole at $r=\e^{-(\rho-\tfrac{1}{2})}$, any off-critical zero produces an interior pole and is thereby incompatible with the protocol trace identity.
