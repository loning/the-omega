\subsubsection{Simultaneous Rigorous Formulation of Addressability and Resolution: Negative Criteria and Endpoint Compression Laws in the Zero-Point Context}\label{subsubsec:xi-addressability-resolution-law}
This paragraph, under the unitary-slice visible domain constraints and the ``address precedes value'' semantics of \S\ref{subsec:xi-pw-type-safety-null} (Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null}, Definition \ref{def:multi_axis_address}), simultaneously provides rigorous formulations of two groups of objects:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Resolution/clarity:} Using verifiable readout prefixes (dyadic cylinder filtration) as the precision scale, and providing a quantitative law of ``the longer the scan, the sharper the resolution'' via endpoint compactification geometry;
  \item \textbf{Addressability:} Using ``whether a given finite prefix can form a single-valued verifiable readout'' as the definitional criterion, providing negative criteria for ``zeros mutually serve as addresses'' at the structural level, and the unique positive version that can hold (shared prefix cylinder events).
\end{enumerate}
The key insight is that in the protocol of this paper, an ``address'' is not an intrinsic pointer between zeros, but rather a prefix filtration induced by readout fibers and endpoint geometry; the only relationship that can hold between zeros is ``indistinguishability within the same cylinder at a given prefix depth,'' rather than deterministic mutual addressing.

\paragraph{Notation and Natural Spacing Scale}
Let \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) be a nontrivial zero of \(\zeta(s)\), with \(\gamma>0\) arranged in increasing order as \(\gamma_1\le\gamma_2\le\cdots\) (counted with multiplicity). Denote the counting function
\[
N_\zeta(T):=\#\{\rho:\ 0<\Im(\rho)\le T\}.
\]
Its leading term satisfies the Riemann--von Mangoldt order
\[
N_\zeta(T)=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\!\Bigl(\frac{T}{2\pi}\Bigr)-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(\log T),
\qquad(T\to\infty).
\]
This yields the classical ``natural resolution'' (mean adjacent spacing) at the height coordinate \(t\)-scale
\[
\Delta_t(T)\asymp \frac{1}{N_\zeta'(T)}\sim \frac{2\pi}{\log(T/2\pi)}\qquad(T\to\infty),
\]
which serves only as a reference scale for the subsequent ``addressable precision caliber'': the readout protocol of this paper does not treat \(\Delta_t\) itself as an address, but instantiates the address as prefix cylinder events on compactified coordinates.

\paragraph{Negative Criterion I: Incompatibility Between Finitely Generated Mechanisms and Counting Order}
The strongest version of ``one zero can serve as the address of another zero'' would compress the high-zero set into a finite union generated by finite structural data (e.g., finitely many strict arithmetic vertical lattice lines), so that a single zero could determine infinitely many successor zeros within the same vertical cluster.
\begin{proposition}[Counting Obstruction for Finitely Generated Addressability]\label{prop:xi-addressability-counting-obstruction}
Any zero/pole set generated by a single-scale Dirichlet-type finite mechanism has a height count of at most \(O(T)\) within any fixed vertical strip.
Therefore, any conception of ``finitely generated addressability'' that compresses the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta\) within the critical strip into finitely many arithmetic vertical lattice clusters is structurally incompatible with the counting growth \(N_\zeta(T)\asymp T\log T\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This conclusion is a direct application of the main text's ``single-scale Dirichlet-type strict imaginary period \(\Rightarrow\) comb lattice \(\Rightarrow\) linear counting upper bound''; see Proposition \ref{prop:single-scale-dirichlet-imag-period} and Corollary \ref{cor:single-scale-dirichlet-lattice}; the counting order of \(N_\zeta(T)\) was already used in Remark \ref{rem:single-scale-dirichlet-obstruction} to establish a density order obstruction. \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Negative Criterion II (Model Level): Decay of Long-Range ``Address Influence''}
If one compares the local statistics of zeros, after normalization by the mean spacing, with the sine kernel (or more generally the \(\operatorname{Sine}_\beta\)) limit process, then single-point conditioning (Palm) provides repulsion constraints only for local neighborhoods, while the influence on distant windows decays with distance; under this paradigm, so-called ``zeros as mutual addresses'' cannot manifest as a deterministic cross-window addressing mechanism.
\begin{remark}[Explanatory Narrative (Not a Reasoning Premise): Address Influence Radius Under Mixing/Rigidity Paradigms]\label{rem:xi-addressability-palm-decay}
Normalizing zeros by the mean spacing (i.e., using \(\Delta_t(T)\) as the scale unit near height \(T\)), Montgomery's pair correlation conjecture characterizes the two-point correlation leading type as the sine kernel type
\[
1-\Bigl(\frac{\sin(\pi u)}{\pi u}\Bigr)^2
\]
(see \cite{Montgomery1973PairCorrelation} for the pair correlation formula and its GUE-type strengthened formulation).
\par
In one standard realization of this limit model---the determinantal point process \(\operatorname{Sine}_2\) (with kernel
\(
K(x,y)=\frac{\sin(\pi(x-y))}{\pi(x-y)}
\)
)---the Palm conditional process given a point at the origin remains a determinantal process, and its first-order conditional intensity satisfies a closed-form identity (e.g., \cite[Chapter~4]{HoughKrishnapurPeresVirag2009GAFDPP})
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{Palm}}(x)=1-|K(x,0)|^2
=1-\Bigl(\frac{\sin(\pi x)}{\pi x}\Bigr)^2,
\]
with the definite asymptotic behavior as \(|x|\to\infty\):
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{Palm}}(x)=1-\frac{1}{\pi^2x^2}+O(x^{-4}).
\]
Therefore, under the mixing/rigidity limit paradigm, the influence of single-point conditioning on distant windows decays at the \(x^{-2}\) rate: it can at most provide finite neighborhood constraints induced by local repulsion, but cannot form a cross-scale, recursively generable ``address prefix.''
For the limit convergence framework of zero-point processes, see \cite{AriasDeReynaRodgers2024ConvergencePointsZetaZeros}, and for the rigidity phenomenon of \(\operatorname{Sine}_\beta\), see \cite{ChhaibiNajnudel2018RigiditySineBeta}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Positive Conclusion I: Jensen Defect Is a Strict ``Prefix Readout'' Object}
The disk-completed function \(F_\zeta\) and the Jensen defect \(D_\zeta(\varrho)\) have been defined and proved in Appendix \S\ref{subsec:unit-disk-jensen-defect}:
\[
D_{\zeta}(\varrho)
  :=
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\log\bigl|F_{\zeta}(\varrho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\bigr|\,\dd\theta
-\log|F_{\zeta}(0)|,
\qquad 0<\varrho<1,
\]
and the Jensen summation formula yields
\[
D_{\zeta}(\varrho)=\sum_{|a_k|<\varrho}\log\!\Bigl(\frac{\varrho}{|a_k|}\Bigr)\ge 0,
\qquad
D_{\zeta}(\varrho)=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\ F_\zeta\ \text{has no zeros in}\ |w|<\varrho,
\]
so that RH is equivalent to ``\(D_\zeta(\varrho)=0\) for all \(\varrho\in(0,1)\)'' (Corollary \ref{cor:app-rh-iff-jensen-defect-zero}).
Therefore, the radius scan \(\varrho\uparrow 1\) constitutes, in the strict sense, a family of \textbf{verifiable prefix readouts}: it compresses the zero-point information inside the disk into a one-dimensional prefix profile via finite circular averaging.

\paragraph{Positive Conclusion II: Endpoint Compression Squeezes High-Height Information into a Narrow Angular Window}
By the explicit parametrization of Proposition \ref{prop:app-jensen-defect-upperhalf-circle-average}, let
\[
t(\varrho,\theta):=\mathcal{C}^{-1}(\varrho e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})\in\mathbb{H},
\qquad
\Im\,t(\varrho,\theta)=\frac{1-\varrho^2}{1+\varrho^2+2\varrho\cos\theta},
\]
and as \(\varrho\uparrow 1\), the high-imaginary-part sampling is forced to concentrate at the endpoint \(\theta\approx\pi\) (i.e., \(w\approx-1\)); see Corollary \ref{cor:app-jensen-endpoint-localization}.

\begin{theorem}[Endpoint Angle-Width--Height Resolution Exchange Law]\label{thm:xi-endpoint-angle-height-exchange}
Let \(\eta:=1-\varrho\in(0,1)\) and \(\varepsilon:=\pi-\theta\).
Denote
\[
T(\eta,\varepsilon):=\Im\,t(\varrho,\theta)=\frac{1-\varrho^2}{1+\varrho^2+2\varrho\cos\theta}.
\]
As \(\eta\downarrow 0\) and \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\), there is a uniform asymptotic
\[
T(\eta,\varepsilon)=\frac{2\eta}{\eta^2+\varepsilon^2}\,(1+o(1)).
\]
Therefore, at fixed radius resolution \(\eta\), to ``reach'' height-\(T\) information in the Jensen circular average, the endpoint angular window must satisfy
\[
\varepsilon \ \lesssim\ \sqrt{\frac{2\eta}{T}}
\qquad (T\gg \eta^{-1}).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From \(\varrho=1-\eta\) and \(\theta=\pi-\varepsilon\), we have
\(
1-\varrho^2=1-(1-\eta)^2=2\eta-\eta^2
\)
and
\(
\cos\theta=\cos(\pi-\varepsilon)=-\cos\varepsilon=-(1-\varepsilon^2/2+O(\varepsilon^4)).
\)
Substituting into the closed form of \(\Im\,t(\varrho,\theta)\) and performing a uniform expansion in \(\eta,\varepsilon\), the denominator becomes
\[
1+\varrho^2+2\varrho\cos\theta
=(1-\varrho)^2+\varrho\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^4+\eta\varepsilon^2)
=\eta^2+\varepsilon^2+o(\eta^2+\varepsilon^2),
\]
whence
\(
T(\eta,\varepsilon)=\frac{2\eta+o(\eta)}{\eta^2+\varepsilon^2+o(\eta^2+\varepsilon^2)}
=\frac{2\eta}{\eta^2+\varepsilon^2}(1+o(1))
\).
The final expression follows by rearranging the inequality \(T(\eta,\varepsilon)\gtrsim 2\eta/(\eta^2+\varepsilon^2)\). \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Positive Conclusion III: Quadratic Compression of Zero Spacing in Compactified Coordinates}
Taking the phase-box compactification \(u=\upsilon(t)=\pi^{-1}\arctan t\) (Definition \ref{def:unit-circle-phase-precision-potential} and \S\ref{subsec:unit-circle-phase-precision-ledger}), we have
\(
\upsilon'(t)=\bigl(\pi(1+t^2)\bigr)^{-1}\sim (\pi t^2)^{-1}
\) (\(t\to\infty\)).
\begin{corollary}[Quadratic Resolution Compression Law in Compactified Coordinates]\label{cor:xi-compactified-spacing-quadratic}
Near height \(T\), the typical spacing of adjacent zeros in the compactified coordinate \(u\) satisfies
\[
\Delta_u(T)\sim \Delta_t(T)\cdot \upsilon'(T)\sim \frac{2}{T^2\log(T/2\pi)}
\qquad(T\to\infty).
\]
Therefore, to achieve single-zero-level resolution in the \(u\)-coordinate, the required resolution order is \(T^{-2}\log(T)^{-1}\), corresponding to a dyadic depth threshold of \(2\log_2 T+\log_2\log T+O(1)\) (of the same order as Theorem \ref{thm:xi-phase-prefix-collision-lower-bound}).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
From \(\Delta_u\approx \upsilon'(T)\Delta_t(T)\) and \(\upsilon'(T)\sim 1/(\pi T^2)\), substituting \(\Delta_t(T)\sim 2\pi/\log(T/2\pi)\) yields the first expression; the dyadic depth conversion follows immediately by taking \(-\log_2\Delta_u(T)\). \qedhere
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Verifiable Answers Regarding ``Zeros as Mutual Addresses''}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If ``address'' is understood as deterministic generation under finite rules (or finite lattice assembly), then Proposition \ref{prop:xi-addressability-counting-obstruction} provides a structural exclusion via the counting obstruction.
  \item If under the statistical limit paradigm ``address'' is understood as significant contraction of the conditional distribution across windows, then in mixing/rigidity models the single-point influence radius is finite and decays with distance (Remark \ref{rem:xi-addressability-palm-decay}), precluding recursive long-range addressing.
  \item The positive content that can hold and be verified within the protocol semantics of this paper consists of: the \textbf{endpoint compression resolution law} (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-endpoint-angle-height-exchange}) and the \textbf{quadratic compression in compactified coordinates} (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-compactified-spacing-quadratic}), as well as visible clustering in the sense of ``shared prefix cylinder events'' (see \S\ref{subsubsec:xi-phase-prefix-resolution-law}).
\end{enumerate}
