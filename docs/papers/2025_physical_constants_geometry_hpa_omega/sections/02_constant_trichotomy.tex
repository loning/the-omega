\section{A trichotomy of constants: metrology, derived quantities, and dimensionless invariants}
\label{sec:trichotomy}

\subsection{Defining constants: fixation as a metrological scale choice}
In the revised SI (2019), the unit system is anchored by fixing the numerical values of seven defining constants. In this setting, the question of ``explaining the exact decimal values'' of $c$, $h$, $e$, $k_B$, $N_A$, and $\Delta\nu_{\mathrm{Cs}}$ is not a well-posed physical prediction problem: their exact decimals encode a scale convention and interface by definition \cite{BIPMSI2019}.

\begin{remark}[Writing discipline]
\label{rem:writing_discipline}
Defining constants are excluded from geometric prediction targets. They are treated as interfaces that map geometric invariants to SI readouts.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Derived constants: interfaces plus dimensionless inputs}
In the revised SI, the vacuum permeability $\mu_0$ is no longer an exact defining quantity and becomes measurable; its uncertainty budget is controlled by the measurement of the dimensionless fine-structure constant $\alpha$ because $c,h,e$ are fixed while $\alpha$ is not \cite{NISTMu0RevisedSI,CODATA2022RMP}.

\subsection{Dimensionless invariants: where physics lives}
Dimensionless quantities (e.g., $\alpha$, mass ratios, mixing angles, $\Lambda \ell_P^2$) are invariant under unit changes and therefore serve as primary targets of geometricization. CODATA provides recommended values and uncertainties for $\alpha^{-1}$ \cite{CODATA2022RMP}, while the Particle Data Group (PDG) systematically summarizes Standard Model parameters and conventions \cite{PDGRPP2024}.
