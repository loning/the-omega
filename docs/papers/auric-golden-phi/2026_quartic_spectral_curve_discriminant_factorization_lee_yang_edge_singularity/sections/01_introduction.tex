\section{Introduction}

The geometry of partition-function zeros links statistical mechanics and analytic combinatorics.
Classical treatments often rely on numerical asymptotics, whereas finite-dimensional \emph{spectral-closure} models in this note give a fully algebraic, reproducible pipeline:
\emph{minimal recurrence and Hankel rank} \(\to\) \emph{branch locus} \(\to\) \emph{probability limit laws} \(\to\) \emph{computable verification}.

\subsection*{Main theorems}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item
  The ordinary generating function \(Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m(y)t^m\) is reduced and its denominator degree is \(4\) away from \(y\in\{-1,0,1\}\), yielding a minimal order-\(4\) recurrence and fixed Hankel rank (\(4\)) for generic parameters (Definition~\ref{def:minimal-spectral-closure}, Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-order}).
  \item
  The quartic spectral polynomial \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) has complete discriminant factorization
  \[
  \Disc_\lambda \Pi(\lambda,y)=-y(y-1)c(y),\qquad c(y)=256y^3+411y^2+165y+32,
  \]
  so the only branch values are \(y=0,1\) and \(c(y)=0\); the nontrivial real branch point \(\yLY\) is the unique real root of \(c(y)\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor}, Proposition~\ref{prop:unique-real-root}).
  \item
  On the negative real axis there is a unique non-bifurcating equimodular point \(\yEQ\approx-0.16009\) (Theorem~\ref{thm:yeq}, Definition~\ref{def:equimodular-negative}), and it appears in the zero-accumulation geometry through BKW with nonvanishing competing coefficients.
  \item
  The dominant spectral branch \(\lambda_+(y)\) controls the limiting log-mgf, giving closed-form constants \(\mu=5/18\) and \(\sigma^2=67/972\) for LLN/CLT (Theorem~\ref{thm:lambda-mgf}, Theorem~\ref{thm:lln-clt}) together with the coefficient nonnegativity foundation in Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff-nonneg}.
  \item
  The arithmetic normalization to the elliptic curve \(E:\xi^2+\xi=\lambda^3-\lambda\) (conductor \(37\)) and conductor-\(37\) corollaries are developed in Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic} to keep the core argument focused on minimality, discriminant, and zeros.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model and the quartic spectral polynomial.
Sections~\ref{sec:hankel}--\ref{sec:free} develop the minimality, discriminant, and limit-law chain in the order above.
Section~\ref{sec:zeros} states the BKW consequences; Section~\ref{sec:algo} gives a reproducible and fully certifiable numerical workflow.
Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic} collects the arithmetic normalization and conductor-\(37\) material that supports, but does not drive, the main combinatorial-analytic argument.
