\subsection{CAP closure of a continuum action: from equivalence semantics to dynamical field equations}
\label{app:cap_continuum_action_closure}

This appendix records a single purpose: to make explicit how the familiar continuum ``least-action'' machinery can be treated as a \emph{CAP-closed representative} of the tick\,+\,CAP spine, rather than as an extra ontic postulate.
The output is an \emph{action skeleton} whose terms are fixed by declared equivalence semantics (Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}) together with CAP minimality on an explicit \emph{finite} candidate family, in the audit form of Appendix~\ref{app:cap_audit_template}.

\paragraph{Status.}
\InterfaceTag The continuum closure is an interface representative: it does not alter the finite folding core.
It is introduced so that frequency/lapse/holonomy diagnostics can be translated into standard continuum field equations in a way that is auditable (no hidden knobs) and compatible with the paper's layering doctrine.

\subsubsection{Closure problem statement}
\label{subsec:cap_continuum_closure_problem}

\paragraph{Input data (from the tick+CAP spine).}
\InterfaceTag The protocol provides: (i) a tick-indexed readout stream, (ii) finite stable-sector types with fibers under projection, (iii) a locality display dictionary via addressing, and (iv) measurable overhead/delay proxies that act as clock-rate dictionaries (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}).
Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics} formalizes the semantic quotients (tick-origin, local relabelings, coarse graining, action equivalence).

\paragraph{Desired output (continuum representative).}
\InterfaceTag We seek a local covariant effective description in which:
\begin{itemize}
\item frequency/clock-rate variation is represented by a lapse-like field (or equivalently by an overhead field $\kappa$),
\item compensating transport is represented by gauge connections with curvature,
\item dynamics is specified by stationarity of a CAP-selected action within a bounded candidate family.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Candidate family: local covariant invariants under the equivalence semantics}
\label{subsec:cap_action_candidate_family}

\paragraph{Finite candidate family requirement.}
\AuditTag To keep CAP well-posed, we restrict to finite families by construction (Appendix~\ref{app:cap_audit_template}).
Concretely, we (i) restrict the list of admissible term \emph{types}, and (ii) discretize coefficient choices into a bounded rational box.

\paragraph{Term dictionary (types).}
\InterfaceTag We restrict to local scalars built from fields and at most two derivatives, consistent with minimal description complexity and with the operational meaning of locality on the addressing graph.
The candidate term types are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{gravity sector:} $\sqrt{-g}$, $\sqrt{-g}\,R$ (cosmological constant and Einstein--Hilbert);
\item \textbf{gauge sector:} $\sqrt{-g}\,\Tr(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$ for each compact gauge factor;
\item \textbf{information/overhead sector:} a scalar amplitude $\chi\ge 0$ with a quadratic gradient penalty $\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}(\nabla_\mu\chi)(\nabla_\nu\chi)$ and a local potential $\sqrt{-g}\,V(\chi^2)$;
\item \textbf{matter sector:} a placeholder $\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_m$ for additional effective degrees of freedom already closed at the protocol interface (e.g.\ the SM labeling sector).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why these terms are ``forced'' (uniqueness inputs).}
We record three standard uniqueness inputs, each aligned with an equivalence semantic already present in this paper.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Diffeomorphism covariance as representation independence.}
\InterfaceTag A continuum representative should not depend on coordinate reparametrizations of the representative manifold; this is the continuum analogue of the ``no privileged addressing coordinate'' stance (Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}).
Under the additional restriction to at most second derivatives, the lowest-complexity gravitational scalar is $R$ (Lovelock-type uniqueness in $4$D; \cite{Lovelock1971}).

\item \textbf{Probability preservation $\Rightarrow$ compact internal redundancy.}
\InterfaceTag Treating internal redundancy as probability preserving forces (projective) unitarity (Wigner; \cite{Wigner1931}), hence compactness of the connected gauge redundancy (Proposition~\ref{prop:unitary_implies_compact_redundancy}).
Given a compact gauge group, the unique gauge-invariant local quadratic kinetic term is $\Tr(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})$ (Propositions~\ref{prop:local_rephasing_connection} and \ref{prop:local_sun_connection}).

\item \textbf{Coarse-graining monotonicity $\Rightarrow$ Fisher-type quadratic form.}
\InterfaceTag Coarse graining is modeled by stochastic maps (Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}).
On finite probability simplices, C{\v{e}}ncov's theorem implies that (up to overall scale) the Fisher information metric is the unique Riemannian metric monotone under Markov morphisms \cite{Cencov1982}.
Therefore the minimal covariant quadratic penalty that measures spatial variation in statistical distinguishability is Fisher-like; we encode it by a scalar ``Fisher amplitude'' $\chi$ and a gradient term $g^{\mu\nu}(\nabla_\mu\chi)(\nabla_\nu\chi)$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Coefficient discretization and CAP selection (audit form)}
\label{subsec:cap_action_discretization}

\paragraph{Finite coefficient box.}
\AuditTag Fix a bound $B\in\NN$.
Let coefficients be chosen from a bounded rational set, e.g.\
\[
\mathcal{C}(B):=\left\{\frac{p}{q}:\ p,q\in\ZZ,\ 1\le |p|\le B,\ 1\le q\le B\right\}\cup\{0\}.
\]
This makes the candidate action family finite once the term-type dictionary is finite.

\paragraph{Complexity key (tie-break).}
\AuditTag We use a deterministic lexicographic complexity key that prefers:
(i) fewer derivatives, (ii) fewer distinct term types, (iii) smaller denominator/height in $\mathcal{C}(B)$, (iv) smaller gauge-algebra dimensions when gauge-factor choice is still open.
This mirrors the ``no hidden knobs'' contract: every refinement is an explicit enlargement of a finite box.

\subsubsection{CAP-minimal action skeleton (closure output)}
\label{subsec:cap_minimal_action_output}

\begin{proposition}[CAP-minimal covariant action skeleton (interface closure)]
\label{prop:cap_minimal_action_skeleton}
Adopt the equivalence semantics of Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics} (tick-origin shift, local redundancy/gauge, coarse-graining preorder, action equivalence).
Restrict to local covariant term types with at most two derivatives as in Section~\ref{subsec:cap_action_candidate_family}, and discretize coefficients into a finite box as in Section~\ref{subsec:cap_action_discretization}.
Then CAP selects a minimal representative action of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:cap_minimal_action_skeleton}
S_{\mathrm{eff}}
=\int \dd^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,
\left[
\frac{R-2\Lambda}{16\pi G}
-\lambda_F\, g^{\mu\nu}(\nabla_\mu\chi)(\nabla_\nu\chi)
-V(\chi^{2})
\;-\;\sum_{a}\frac{1}{4g_a^2}\Tr(F^{(a)}_{\mu\nu}F^{(a)\mu\nu})
\;+\;\mathcal{L}_{m}
\right],
\end{equation}
where $g_{\mu\nu}$ is a Lorentzian metric representative, $F^{(a)}_{\mu\nu}$ are curvature tensors for each compact gauge factor, and $\chi$ is a nonnegative scalar amplitude encoding coarse-grained distinguishability/overhead.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch (audit viewpoint)]
The term-type dictionary is constructed precisely to list the lowest-complexity local covariant invariants compatible with the equivalence semantics:
the gravitational sector contributes $\sqrt{-g}$ and $\sqrt{-g}R$ at the minimal derivative order;
the gauge sector contributes $\Tr(F^2)$ as the unique local quadratic gauge-invariant kinetic term;
the coarse-graining semantics forces a Fisher-type quadratic penalty for distinguishability variation, represented by $\chi$.
Discretizing coefficients makes the family finite; CAP then selects a canonical representative under the stated lexicographic tie-break.
Numerical values of $(G,\Lambda,\lambda_F,g_a)$ are treated as matching/calibration data unless further protocol-level closures are imposed.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Frequency-first interpretation.}
\InterfaceTag The field $\chi$ can be read as an information-density amplitude and therefore as a proxy for local clock/frequency structure once a cost-to-clock dictionary is fixed:
overhead $\kappa$ defines a lapse $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$ (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}), and frequency ratios are primary observables (Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}).
Appendix~\ref{app:variational_field_equations} records the resulting field equations obtained by varying \eqref{eq:cap_minimal_action_skeleton}.
