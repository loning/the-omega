\section{Final remarks}\label{sec:conclusion}

The structural part of the paper shows that recursive addressing stays
within the visible factor and therefore carries no hidden entropy gain.
The quantitative part identifies three sharper mechanisms.  Finite
observation is controlled by a weighted boundary automaton, whose
spectral radius yields the exact decay exponent and, for Gibbs-Markov
sources, a pressure-gap formula.  For first-entry residue events of
Markov holes, this pressure gap is exactly the escape rate of the
associated open system, so finite-observation error recovers survivor
pressure and, in the maximal-entropy case, survivor entropy.  Simultaneous
addressing exhibits a Poisson collision threshold on the R\'enyi-\(2\)
scale.  Injective collision resolution is governed by an exact
hidden-information ledger, so auxiliary memory must carry the full
conditional entropy lost by the visible address map.

Two directions remain natural.  One is to extend the weighted-boundary
formalism beyond Markov and Gibbs classes to broader visible sources
while retaining a finite-state or transfer-operator description.  The
other is to treat larger alphabets or multidimensional observation
schemes, where the factor-tower argument remains available but the
boundary automata and collision geometry become more intricate.
