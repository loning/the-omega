\section{Protocol Layer: Irrational-Orientation Cut-and-Project and Three-Dimensional Observable Set}

\subsection{Direct Sum Decomposition and Projection Operators}

Take an orthogonal direct sum decomposition
\[
\RR^{6}=E_{\parallel}\oplus E_{\perp},\qquad \dim E_{\parallel}=3,\ \dim E_{\perp}=3.
\]
Denote the orthogonal projections as
\[
\pi_{\parallel}:\RR^{6}\to E_{\parallel},\qquad \pi_{\perp}:\RR^{6}\to E_{\perp}.
\]

\subsection{Acceptance Window and Model Set}

Take a compact set $W\subset E_{\perp}$ (acceptance window) and introduce a ``phase offset'' parameter $u\in E_{\perp}$. Define the three-dimensional point set generated by cut-and-project as
\[
\Sigma(u):=\Big\{\pi_{\parallel}(n)\ :\ n\in\Lambda,\ \pi_{\perp}(n)+u\in W\Big\}\subset E_{\parallel}.
\]
When the orientation satisfies the irrationality condition
\[
E_{\parallel}\cap \Lambda = \{0\},
\]
and $\pi_{\perp}(\Lambda)$ is dense in $E_{\perp}$, $\Sigma(u)$ is a typical quasi-periodic ``model set,'' exhibiting discrete and aperiodic long-range order.

\subsection{Irrational Orientation and the ``Golden Sector''}

This paper formulates the ``golden sector'' as a choice of irrational parameter satisfying two requirements:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Anti-resonance:} Avoid readout locking caused by short-period approximations;
  \item \textbf{Encoding regularity:} Make scan iterations compatible with minimal-complexity symbolic encodings (such as Sturmian mechanical sequences) and corresponding Ostrowski/Zeckendorf representations.
\end{itemize}

In the one-dimensional prototype, scans are often modeled by circle rotation
\[
x_{t+1}=x_t+\alpha\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1,\qquad \alpha\notin \QQ
\]
The golden sector can take $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$, where $\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2$. Its continued fraction partial quotients are all $1$, making it achieve minimal resonance in the rational approximation sense, thus providing a canonical anti-locking option for finite prefix readout. This paper employs irrational orientations/scan direction choices compatible with this in the higher-dimensional case: the essence is not ``the uniquely correct choice,'' but rather ``a class of canonical candidates under finite complexity constraints.''
