\begin{proposition}[Symmetry-induced geometric quotient: explicit planar quartic model and genus three]\label{prop:sync-hatdelta-curve-quotient-plane-quartic-genus3}
Let \(\mathcal{C}\subset\mathbb{A}^2_{x,y}\) be an affine plane curve, and its equation is
\[
F(x,y)=1-y-5x+3xy+5x^2-xy^2+xy^3-6x^2y+x^2y^2-x^3=0.
\]
Then at the function domain level, \(\mathcal{X}/\langle\iota\rangle\) is represented by \(\iota\)-invariant
\[
x:=w^2,\qquad y:=sw
\]
is generated and birational isomorphism with \(\mathcal{C}\).
Its projective closure \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}\subset\mathbb{P}^2_{X:Y:Z}\) is given by the homogeneous quartic equation
\[
\begin{aligned}
0={}&Z^4-YZ^3-5XZ^3+3XYZ^2+5X^2Z^2-XY^2Z+XY^3\\
&\quad +X^2Y^2-6X^2YZ-X^3Z
\end{aligned}
\]
Characterized by, and smooth on algebraic closure.
Therefore \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}\) is a non-hyperelliptic cubic curve (equivalently: a smooth planar quartic curve), so
\[
g(\overline{\mathcal{C}})=3.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From \(\widehat\Delta(w,-s)=\widehat\Delta(-w,s)\), we get \(\widehat\Delta(-w,-s)=\widehat\Delta(w,s)\), so \(\iota\) maintains the curve.
And \(x=w^2\), \(y=sw\) are unchanged under the action of \(\iota\).
By substituting \(s=y/w\) and \(w^2=x\) on the open set of \(w\neq 0\), \(\widehat\Delta(w,s)=0\) can be equivalently rewritten as \(F(x,y)=0\), thus obtaining the explicit affine model and birational isomorphism of the quotient curve.
\par
Smoothness can be directly verified on the affine graph and the infinite point by point-wise gradient discrimination; therefore \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}\) is a smooth plane quartic curve.
The generic formula for the fourth degree of the plane gives \(g(\overline{\mathcal{C}})=(4-1)(4-2)/2=3\).
Reproducible verification script: \texttt{scripts/exp\_sync\_kernel\_hatdelta\_discriminant.py}. Certification completed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Second expansion and divergence: only two divergences and \texorpdfstring{$g(\mathcal{X})=6$}{g(X)=6}]\label{prop:sync-hatdelta-curve-double-cover-branch-genus6}
There is a second expansion in the sense of function domain
\[
\QQ(\mathcal{X})=\QQ(\overline{\mathcal{C}})\bigl(\sqrt{x}\bigr),
\]
and thus obtain standardized secondary coverage
\[
\pi:\mathcal{X}\longrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{C}},
\]
Its deck transformation is \(\iota\).
The divergence of this coverage is completely determined by the odd-order zeros and odd-order poles of \(x\) on \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}\);
For this curve, the odd-order zeros/poles are exactly two points:
\[
P_0=(x,y)=(0,1)\in \overline{\mathcal{C}}(\QQ),\qquad
P_\infty=[1:-1:0]\in \overline{\mathcal{C}}(\QQ),
\]
Among them, \(x\) is a first-order zero at \(P_0\) and a first-order pole at \(P_\infty\).
In addition, \(x\) at \([0:1:0]\) has a second-order zero point, and \(x\) at \([1:0:0]\) has a second-order pole, so there is no disagreement.
Therefore \(\pi\) diverges exactly at \(\{P_0,P_\infty\}\), \'{e}tale everywhere else, and \(\mathcal{X}^{\iota}\) consists of two points.
Obtained from Hurwitz formula
\[
g(\mathcal{X})
=2g(\overline{\mathcal{C}})-1+\frac{\#\mathrm{Branch}(\pi)}{2}
=2\cdot 3-1+\frac{2}{2}
=6.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From \(\QQ(\mathcal{X})=\QQ(w,s)\) and the invariant relations \(x=w^2\), \(y=sw\), we get \(\QQ(\overline{\mathcal{C}})=\QQ(x,y)\), and \(\QQ(\mathcal{X})=\QQ(\overline{\mathcal{C}})(w)=\QQ(\overline{\mathcal{C}})(\sqrt{x})\).
The divergence point is characterized by odd-order zeros/poles of \(x\).
\par
At the affine point \(P_0=(0,1)\), \(F_y(0,1)=-1\neq 0\), so \(x\) is a local parameter and gives a zero point.
For the infinite point \(P_\infty=[1:-1:0]\), take the graph \(X=1\) and let \((y,z)=(Y/X,Z/X)\), then \(x=X/Z=1/z\);
The partial derivative of the curve equation in this figure with respect to \(y\) is non-zero at \((y,z)=(-1,0)\), so \(z\) is a local parameter and \(x\) gives a first-order pole.
For \([1:0:0]\) and \([0:1:0]\), in the same way, the lowest-order expansion in the corresponding affine graph can obtain the second-order behavior of \(z\sim y^2\) and \(x\sim z^2\). Therefore, it is not inconsistent that the zero/pole order is an even number.
Hurwitz's formula yields a numerical conclusion. Certification completed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Triple Cover of Quotient Curves: Triple Expansion, Discriminant and \texorpdfstring{$S_3$}{S3} Closure]\label{prop:sync-hatdelta-quotient-triple-cover-s3-discriminant}
The rational function \(x\) on \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}\) defines a triple cover
\[
\varphi:\overline{\mathcal{C}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}^1_x.
\]
Its function domain expansion \(\QQ(x)\subset\QQ(\overline{\mathcal{C}})\) can be explicitly described by the cubic equation about \(y\):
\[
x\,y^3+(x^2-x)\,y^2+(3x-6x^2-1)\,y+(1-5x+5x^2-x^3)=0.
\]
The discriminant of this cubic equation with respect to \(y\) satisfies the strict identity
\[
\mathrm{Disc}_y=x\cdot P_8(x),
\qquad
P_8(x)=4x^8+85x^7+416x^6-818x^5+1196x^4-871x^3+284x^2-44x+4.
\]
Therefore, the finite divergence point of \(\varphi\) is given by \(x=0\) and \(P_8(x)=0\); another divergence occurs at infinity to satisfy the Riemann--Hurwitz constraint of \(g(\overline{\mathcal{C}})=3\).
And since \(\mathrm{Disc}_y\) is non-square in \(\QQ(x)\), the geometric/arithmetical Galois closure group of this cubic expansion is \(S_3\).
The second intermediate expansion corresponding to the square root of the discriminant determines a natural hyperelliptic curve
\[
H:\quad v^2=x\,P_8(x),
\]
Its right end is a ninth degree polynomial, so \(H\) is a four-dimensional hyperelliptic curve; it characterizes the only quadratic subfield in \(S_3\)-closure.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The affine model of \(\overline{\mathcal{C}}\) is \(F(x,y)=0\), which can be regarded as a cubic polynomial of \(y\) to obtain the cubic equation and triple coverage shown.
Taking \(\mathrm{Disc}_y\) for this polynomial and factoring it gives \(\mathrm{Disc}_y=xP_8(x)\).
The Galois group of cubic irreducible extensions is \(A_3\) if and only if the discriminant is square; here it is not square, so the closure group is \(S_3\).
The quadratic subdomain is generated by \(\sqrt{\mathrm{Disc}_y}\), thus obtaining the hyperelliptic model \(v^2=xP_8(x)\), whose genus is determined by \(\deg(xP_8)=9\).
Reproducible verification script: \texttt{scripts/exp\_sync\_kernel\_hatdelta\_discriminant.py}. Certification completed.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$\widehat\Delta(w,s)$}{hatDelta(w,s)}'s general Galois group and complete simple group property]\label{prop:sync-hatdelta-galois-s6-generic}
Consider \(\widehat\Delta(w,s)\) as a sixth degree polynomial on \(\QQ(s)\) with respect to \(w\), then its Galois group on \(\QQ(s)\) is a fully symmetric group:
\[
\mathrm{Gal}\bigl(\widehat\Delta(w,s)\ /\ \QQ(s)\bigr)\cong S_6.
\]
And the general monodromy of the natural projection \(\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{P}^1_s\) is \(S_6\), so the six-leaf cover is geometrically fully symmetric.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
When specializing \(s=3\) we get an irreducible sixth degree polynomial
\[
\widehat\Delta(w,3)=8w^6+9w^5-4w^4+9w^3-5w^2-3w+1\in\QQ[w],
\]
Its Galois group on \(\QQ\) is \(S_6\).
The Galois group in the general case is a transitive subgroup of \(S_6\), and the specialization does not increase the Galois group: for specializations except for a limited number of degenerate parameters, the specialized Galois group can be regarded as a subgroup of the general Galois group; therefore once there is a specialization that results in \(S_6\), the general Galois group can only be \(S_6\).
The simple group property is obtained by "the Galois group of the functional field is consistent with the general simple group" (for geometric fibers outside the divergence point).
Reproducible verification script: \texttt{scripts/exp\_sync\_kernel\_hatdelta\_discriminant.py}. Certification completed.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$s=\infty$}{s=infty} Puiseux branch classification and explicit expansion]\label{rem:sync-hatdelta-puiseux-at-infty}
Let \(t:=1/s\), and take \(u:=t^{1/2}\).
Then on the normalization of \(\mathcal{X}\), six analytical branches \(w=w(s)\) appear in the neighborhood of \(s=\infty\):
Two are of integer Laurent type (no divergence), and four are of half-integer Puiseux type (corresponding to two divergence points with index \(2\), each contributing two local branches).
The following standardized expansion is available (only the first few items are displayed):
\begin{enumerate}
\item Pole type branch (\(w\sim -s\)):
  \[
  w_{\mathrm{pole}}(s)= -s+\frac{4}{s}+\frac{2}{s^3}-\frac{24}{s^5}-\frac{189}{s^7}+O(s^{-9}).
  \]
\item Small branch (\(w\sim s^{-1}\)):
  \[
  w_{\mathrm{small}}(s)= \frac{1}{s}-\frac{2}{s^3}+\frac{39}{s^7}-\frac{120}{s^9}-\frac{1240}{s^{11}}+O(s^{-13}).
  \]
\item Real square root branch (two local branches merge at the same branch point):
  \[
  w_{\pm}(s)=\pm s^{-1/2}\ \mp\ \frac{3}{4}s^{-3/2}\ +\ \frac{1}{4}s^{-2}\ \pm\ \frac{65}{32}s^{-5/2}\ +\ \frac{5}{4}s^{-3}\ +\ O(s^{-7/2}).
  \]
\item Pure imaginary square root branch (another divergence point):
  \[
  w_{\pm i}(s)=\pm i\,s^{-1/2}\ \pm\ \frac{3i}{4}s^{-3/2}\ -\ \frac{1}{4}s^{-2}\ \pm\ \frac{65i}{32}s^{-5/2}\ +\ \frac{5}{4}s^{-3}\ +\ O(s^{-7/2}).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
And the effect of the involution \(\iota:(w,s)\mapsto(-w,-s)\) at this point is satisfied: the two points corresponding to the first two integer-type branches are the fixed points of \(\iota\), and the four branches of the square root type are locally exchanged in pairs (reflecting the non-trivial local effect of \(\iota\) at infinity).
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Jacobian's Prym decomposition: three-dimensional \texorpdfstring{$(1,1,2)$}{(1,1,2)}-polarization factor]\label{cor:sync-hatdelta-prym-decomposition}
Covering \(\pi:\mathcal{X}\to\overline{\mathcal{C}}\) by the quadratic divergence of the proposition \ref{prop:sync-hatdelta-curve-double-cover-branch-genus6} (the number of divergence points is \(2\)), there is a Prym algebra variety with dimension \(3\)
\(\mathrm{Prym}(\mathcal{X}/\overline{\mathcal{C}})\subset J(\mathcal{X})\), and homologous decomposition on \(\QQ\)
\[
J(\mathcal{X})\ \sim\ J(\overline{\mathcal{C}})\times \mathrm{Prym}(\mathcal{X}/\overline{\mathcal{C}}).
\]
Since the number of divergence points is \(2\), the polarization type inherited by \(\mathrm{Prym}(\mathcal{X}/\overline{\mathcal{C}})\) is \((1,1,2)\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Prym's theory for bifurcated quadratic coverage gives
\(\dim\mathrm{Prym}(\mathcal{X}/\overline{\mathcal{C}})=g(\mathcal{X})-g(\overline{\mathcal{C}})=6-3=3\)，
And the Jacobian homology decomposition holds.
When the number of divergence points is \(2\), the induced polarization type is \((1,1,2)\), which is the standard conclusion.
Certification completed.
\end{proof}
