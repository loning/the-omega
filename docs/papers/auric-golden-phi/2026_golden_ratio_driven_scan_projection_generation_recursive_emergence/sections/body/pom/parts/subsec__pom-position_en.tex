\subsection{Goal and Basic Propositions}\label{subsec:pom-goal}
The goal of this section is not to rebuild a formal system, but rather to extract an \textbf{ontological structure} from miscellaneous arithmetic phenomena: there exists a prior high-dimensional state space with local evolution laws, and all mathematical objects and operations are merely readouts of this ontology under different projections. The corresponding basic propositions can be organized as:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Ontology state space and prior fibers:} The ontology state decomposes into an observable fiber $\mathcal Z$ and three prior coordinate fibers $\mathcal S_\varphi,\mathcal T,\mathcal P$, with allowance for finite-dimensional remainder/control fields $\mathcal M$ (see subsections \ref{subsec:pom-fibers} and \ref{subsec:pom-state-evolution}).
  \item \textbf{Ontology evolution and unique primitives:} Ontology evolution $U$ exhibits locality and finite propagation; at the procedural semantics level, composition $\circ$ and fold $\Fold_\infty$ constitute the minimal primitive set (see subsection \ref{subsec:pom-primitive-free}).
  \item \textbf{Three types of projections:} Gauge projection $P_Z$ corresponds to "taking quotient and selecting section", atomic projection $P_{\mathrm{prim}}$ corresponds to "reading out primitive atomic layer from time trace", order projection $P_{\le}$ corresponds to comparison/interval constraints; these three form readout separation and complexity boundary (see subsections \ref{subsec:pom-projections} and \ref{subsec:pom-order-bottleneck}).
  \item \textbf{Space–time duality:} Readout necessarily produces spatial cost and temporal cost, coupled through the embed–evolve–project normal form (see subsection \ref{subsec:pom-resources}).
\end{enumerate}

Thus the core of arithmetic difference lies not in "different evolution", but in "coordinate selection + defect readout" combinations.

\subsubsection{No Absolute Ontology and Gauge Groupoid Calibration}\label{subsec:pom-gauge-groupoid}
We do not assume a globally fixed "absolute ontology" coordinate system, but understand objects as locally representable–convertible structure. Let \(\mathfrak{G}\) be the family of reversible rewrites/coordinate transformations acting on ontology state space \(\Omega\) (viewable as composable arrows of a gauge groupoid). Object identity is given by the equivalence relation
\[
\omega\sim g\cdot\omega,\qquad g\in\mathfrak{G}
\]
A readable observable \(O\) must satisfy gauge invariance \(O(g\cdot\omega)=O(\omega)\), otherwise undefined at the type level.

\begin{remark}[Object of symmetry: automorphism of projection system]\label{rem:symmetry-projection-automorphism}
In this paper's calibration, symmetry does not first point to some "geometric axis", but to the automorphism of the projection system (generated by ontology state space and protocol-allowed readout/certificate interfaces) in the readout sense: there exists \(g\in\mathfrak{G}\) such that for all protocol-allowed readable observables \(O\), \(O\circ g=O\);
equivalently, under the interface semantics of projection word \(2\)-category \(\mathbf{PW}\), this class of automorphisms manifests as natural isomorphism classes preserving visible output.
Thus, references to "axis/slice/orthogonal" in the text should be understood as: after fixing the visible domain or selecting a gauge section, notational descriptions of equivariant decomposition/payload components of the same object; their role is to characterize which invariants must be externalized, which components are shielded under protocol, rather than introducing additional symmetry axis assumptions.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gluing data and third register]\label{rem:pom-gauge-third-register}
Suppose objects have readouts \(x_i\) only on local coordinate patches \(U_i\), related on overlaps \(U_i\cap U_j\) by transition functions \(g_{ij}\), then
\[
x_j=g_{ij}\cdot x_i.
\]
If the cocycle condition \(g_{ij}g_{jk}g_{ki}=e\) holds on triple overlaps, local descriptions can be glued into a global object; otherwise only retained as a local family. The third register in this calibration is precisely \(\{g_{ij}\}\) or its equivalent gluing certificate: it is not "extra memory", but the necessary structure for whether the object can be globalized.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Gauge section, precision and eliminability condition]\label{rem:pom-gauge-normal-form}
Precision here is understood as: whether there exists a computable gauge section/normal form operator \(\mathrm{NF}\) such that each equivalence class selects a unique representative and \(\mathrm{NF}\) is computable in \(\mathrm{poly}(n)\) time. If this condition is met, then "third register can be found back by search" should be understood as: the register only carries pure gauge redundancy, eliminable by normal form. Conversely, once global gluing obstructions appear (e.g., non-trivial holonomy/curvature prevents selecting a global continuous section), the register is ineliminable; omitting it will confuse different local representatives of the same object, triggering \(\mathsf{NULL}\) at the type level.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Conditional expectation and fiber average}\label{subsec:pom-conditional-expectation}
Fold-induced subalgebra $\mathcal{B}_m$ and conditional expectation $E_m:\mathcal{A}_m\to\mathcal{B}_m$ (Proposition \ref{prop:fold-conditional-expectation}) give operator-algebraic interface of "averaging along fold fiber", thus making precise the information loss mechanism of $P_Z$ at the observable level.

\begin{remark}[Dual state of gauge projection]\label{rem:pom-pz-dual}

They respectively characterize "unique representative selection" and "quantification of information forgetting", together forming $P_Z$'s dual structure.
\end{remark}

In the function algebra calibration, coarse-graining along fold fibers is given by conditional expectation $E_m$: it takes uniform average over each fiber, preserving the fold-invariant subalgebra $\mathcal{B}_m$. This characterization can be derived directly from "fiber internal permutation invariance + positivity + idempotency"; related background see \cite{WaltersErgodicTheory}.

Fix $m$. Take any probability vector $\pi\in\Delta(X_m)$, and define its \textbf{fiber uniform lift} on microstate space $\Omega_m$
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-fiber-uniform-lift}
\mu(a):=\frac{\pi(\Fold_m(a))}{d_m(\Fold_m(a))},\qquad a\in\Omega_m,
\end{equation}
where $d_m(x):=|\Fold_m^{-1}(x)|$. Under $L^2(\mu)$ inner product, $E_m$ is orthogonal projection onto $\mathcal{B}_m$, thus for any $f\in\mathcal{A}_m$ we have $L^2$ decomposition and minimum error characterization:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-L2-pythagoras}
\|f\|_{2,\mu}^2=\|E_m f\|_{2,\mu}^2+\|f-E_m f\|_{2,\mu}^2,
\end{equation}
and $E_m f$ is the least-squares approximation in $\mathcal{B}_m$.

\begin{remark}[Alignment with projective metric contraction calibration]\label{rem:pom-fold-projective-metric}
Orthogonal decomposition under $L^2(\mu)$ calibration can be aligned with Hilbert projective metric or Hopf oscillation seminorm contraction coefficient characterization, yielding universal formulation of "trans-fiber uniformization/mixing"; see \cite{GaubertQu2015DobrushinCones} and related projective metric tools \cite{ReebKastoryanoWolf2011HilbertProjectiveQuantum}.
\end{remark}

Fix $m$. Let $P_m=(\Fold_m)_*:\Delta(\Omega_m)\to\Delta(X_m)$ be pushforward, $Q_m$ the fiber uniform lift (Theorem \ref{prop:fold-pushforward-lift}), and define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-Km-def}
K_m:=Q_m\circ P_m:\Delta(\Omega_m)\to\Delta(\Omega_m).
\end{equation}
Then $K_m$ is idempotent Markov projection, and for any $\mu,\nu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)$ we have visible distance equality and TV decomposition upper bound:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-tv-visible-equality}
D_{\mathrm{TV}}(K_m\mu,\ K_m\nu)=D_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_m\mu,\ P_m\nu),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-tv-decomposition-upper}
D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\nu)\le D_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_m\mu,P_m\nu)+D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,K_m\mu)+D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\nu,K_m\nu).
\end{equation}
where $D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,K_m\mu)$ quantifies residual of fiber internal non-uniformity.

Fix $m$. Let $\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)$, and denote $\pi=P_m\mu$. For any function $f:\Omega_m\to\RR$, the conditional expectation under $\sigma(\Fold_m)$ can be written as fiber conditional average
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-Emu-def}
(E_{\mu}[f\mid\Fold_m])(a)
:=
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{1}{\pi(x)}\sum_{b\in\Fold_m^{-1}(x)}\mu(b)f(b),& \pi(x)>0,\\
0,& \pi(x)=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad x=\Fold_m(a).
\end{equation}
Thus we obtain $L^2$ decomposition and variance decomposition formula:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-Emu-pythagoras}
\|f\|_{2,\mu}^2=\|E_{\mu}[f\mid\Fold_m]\|_{2,\mu}^2+\|f-E_{\mu}[f\mid\Fold_m]\|_{2,\mu}^2,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-var-decomposition}
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(f)=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(f\mid\Fold_m)]+\mathrm{Var}_{\pi}\bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[f\mid\Fold_m]\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(f\mid\Fold_m)]=\sum_{x\in X_m}\pi(x)\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_x}(f)$.

Pinsker inequality on finite alphabets bridges TV and KL:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pinsker-tv-kl}
D_{\mathrm{TV}}(p,q)\le \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p\|q)}\qquad (p,q\in\Delta(\mathcal{S})),
\end{equation}
thus for any $\mu\in\Delta(\Omega_m)$ and $\mu^e:=K_m\mu$ we have
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pom-residual-tv-by-kl}
D_{\mathrm{TV}}(\mu,\mu^e)\le \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\|\mu^e)}
\end{equation}
(see \cite{CoverThomas2006,Tsybakov2009Nonparametric}). Combined with Lemma \ref{lem:pom-fiberwise-kl}'s fiberwise KL decomposition, this gives uniform upper bound rewriting "invisible residual" as visible fiber average.

For any $m_2\ge m_1$, cylinder event extension yields $\mathcal{A}_{m_1}\subseteq \mathcal{A}_{m_2}$ and $\mathcal{B}_{m_1}\subseteq \mathcal{B}_{m_2}$; corresponding conditional expectations satisfy tower property
\[
E_{m_1}\circ E_{m_2}=E_{m_1}\qquad\text{on}\ \mathcal{A}_{m_2}.
\]

A\in\mathcal{B}_m\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad E_m(A)=A.
\]
Thus objects of "observable visible/stable readout" are equivalent to observables falling in the stable subalgebra $\mathcal{B}_m$ (limit $\mathcal{B}_\infty$).

\begin{definition}[Observable fiber $\mathcal Z$]\label{def:pom-observable-fiber}

The value map $\mathrm{Val}(c)=\sum_{k\ge 1}c_kF_{k+1}$ gives isomorphism between observable and natural numbers; its ontological meaning is "canonical section selecting unique representative on value fiber" (see Theorem \ref{thm:zeckendorf-transversal}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Scale fiber $\mathcal{S}_\varphi$]\label{def:pom-scale-fiber}
Take irrational $\alpha\in(0,1)$ with continued fraction $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\dots]$, Ostrowski representation gives
\[
N=\sum_{n\ge 1} d_n q_{n-1},
\]

\begin{remark}[Cost of changing scale slope]\label{rem:pom-scale-slope}
If using stable syntax induced by other eventually periodic continued fractions (e.g., metallic mean family), stable type size generally satisfies $|X_m|\asymp \lambda^m$. Under the premise of preserving binary readout $|\Omega_m|=2^m$, fold entropy slope becomes
\[
S_Z(m)\sim m\log\!\left(\frac{2}{\lambda}\right),
\]
thus systematically rewriting "spatial cost constant" as function of base $\lambda$.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Phase fiber $\mathcal{T}$]\label{def:pom-phase-fiber}
Let phase space be compact abelian group (e.g., $\TT^n$), with character basis
\[
\chi_k(x)=e^{2\pi i\langle k,x\rangle},\quad k\in\ZZ^n.
\]
Phase fiber $\mathcal{T}$ refers to the spectral coordinate layer induced by this character structure, used to lift additive observables to multiplicative weights and convolution diagonalization (see Section \ref{sec:zeta-finite-part} and Section \ref{sec:high-dimensional-cut-project}).
\end{definition}
