\section{Finite-order EFT representative construction (bounded candidate family)}
\label{app:eft_representative_construction}

This appendix records a theorem-facing construction of a finite-order EFT \emph{representative} within a bounded candidate family.
The intent is not to claim constructive nonperturbative QFT control, but to make the \emph{finite} choice space explicit:
field content, symmetry constraints, operator dictionary, truncation order, and coefficient discretization.
This provides a clean anchor for later theoremization steps (Ward/BRST consistency and error bounds).

\subsection{Field content and symmetries (finite input)}

\paragraph{Gauge group.}
Fix the three-factor compact gauge redundancy already closed at the interface layer:
\[
G := U(1)_Y\times SU(2)_L\times SU(3)_c
\]
(Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}), with hypercharge convention $Q=T_3+Y$.

\paragraph{Chiral fermion multiplets.}
Fix the finite fermion content induced by the closed labeling interface at the anchor scale (Section~\ref{sec:sm_labeling_closure}):
for each generation $g\in\{1,2,3\}$, the six chiral multiplets
\[
Q_L^{(g)},\ u_R^{(g)},\ d_R^{(g)},\ L_L^{(g)},\ e_R^{(g)},\ \nu_R^{(g)}.
\]
This is a finite set of representation labels under $G$ and serves as the algebraic input for EFT operator construction.

\paragraph{Scalar (Higgs) content as a bounded closure.}
For a renormalizable EFT embedding, the paper records a bounded candidate family of Higgs-doublet counts
$N_H\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and selects the CAP-minimal feasible value $N_H=1$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:minimal_higgs_doublet_count}).
In this appendix we therefore take one complex Higgs doublet
\[
H \sim (1,2)_{1/2}
\]
as the scalar field content in the EFT representative.

\paragraph{Symmetry constraints.}
All EFT terms are required to be:
\begin{itemize}
\item local and Lorentz covariant (in the continuum representative sense),
\item invariant under the gauge redundancy $G$,
\item Hermitian.
\end{itemize}
Global/discrete symmetries (e.g.\ baryon number, lepton number, CP) are treated as optional restrictions and must be declared explicitly when imposed.

\subsection{Operator dictionary and truncation}

\paragraph{Canonical mass dimensions.}
We use the standard EFT power-counting dimensions in 4D:
$[A_\mu]=1$, $[H]=1$, $[\psi]=3/2$, $[\partial_\mu]=1$.

\begin{definition}[Truncation order]\label{def:eft_truncation_order}
\MathTag Fix a maximum operator dimension $D_{\max}\in\NN$ with $D_{\max}\ge 4$.
An EFT representative at order $D_{\max}$ includes operator terms of canonical dimension $\le D_{\max}$ from a declared finite dictionary.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Finite operator dictionary (declared).}
To keep the candidate set finite and auditable, we do not quantify over all gauge-invariant polynomials.
Instead we fix an explicit finite dictionary of operator \emph{types}:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{dimension $\le 4$ (renormalizable core):}
gauge kinetic terms, fermion kinetic terms, Higgs kinetic term, Higgs potential $(H^\dagger H)$ and $(H^\dagger H)^2$, and Yukawa terms compatible with the chiral multiplets;
\item \textbf{dimension $5$ (minimal neutrino-mass representative):}
the Weinberg operator $(L H)(L H)$ as a representative lepton-number-violating completion term, when included (Appendix~\ref{app:neutrino_weinberg_operator_closure});
\item \textbf{dimension $>5$ (optional):}
an additional finite registry $\mathcal{B}_{>5}$, if declared, with each element specified as a gauge-invariant local operator type together with its canonical dimension.
\end{itemize}
The registry viewpoint keeps the work modular: enlarging the EFT hypothesis space is an explicit change in a finite list.

\begin{definition}[Declared EFT operator basis]\label{def:eft_operator_basis}
\MathTag Fix $(G,\mathcal{F},H)$ as above and fix $D_{\max}$.
Let $\mathcal{B}_{\le D_{\max}}$ be a finite set of Hermitian, gauge-invariant local operator \emph{types} with canonical dimension $\le D_{\max}$, obtained by taking:
(i) the renormalizable core dictionary (dimension $\le 4$),
(ii) optionally the Weinberg operator at dimension $5$,
and (iii) optionally a declared finite extension registry $\mathcal{B}_{>5}$ truncated by $D_{\max}$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Coefficient discretization and bounded candidate family}

\begin{definition}[Bounded rational coefficient box]\label{def:eft_coeff_box}
\MathTag Fix a bound $B\in\NN$ and define the finite coefficient set
\[
\mathcal{C}(B):=\left\{\frac{p}{q}:\ p,q\in\ZZ,\ 1\le |p|\le B,\ 1\le q\le B\right\}\cup\{0\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Finite EFT candidate family]\label{def:eft_candidate_family}
\MathTag Fix $(G,\mathcal{F},H)$, a truncation order $D_{\max}$, an operator basis $\mathcal{B}_{\le D_{\max}}$ (Definition~\ref{def:eft_operator_basis}), and a coefficient bound $B$.
Define the EFT candidate family $\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})$ as the set of Lagrangian densities of the form
\[
\mathcal{L}=\sum_{j\in \mathcal{B}_{\le D_{\max}}} c_j\,\mathcal{O}_j
\]
where each $c_j\in\mathcal{C}(B)$ and $\mathcal{O}_j$ ranges over the chosen finite operator types.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Finiteness]\label{lem:eft_candidate_family_finite}
\MathTag For fixed $(B,D_{\max},\mathcal{B}_{\le D_{\max}})$, the family $\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})$ is finite.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The basis $\mathcal{B}_{\le D_{\max}}$ is finite by construction, and each coefficient is chosen from a finite set $\mathcal{C}(B)$.
Therefore the Cartesian product of coefficient choices is finite.
\end{proof}

\subsection{CAP selection: a theorem-facing representative choice rule}

\paragraph{Why CAP is needed.}
Even after fixing the field content, symmetries, truncation order, and coefficient box, the candidate family is typically still large.
To make any choice auditable and reproducible, the paper uses bounded-complexity closure with deterministic tie-breaks (Axiom~\ref{ax:cap}; Appendix~\ref{app:cap_audit_template}).

\begin{definition}[EFT representative selection functional]\label{def:eft_selection_functional}
\AuditTag Fix a finite candidate family $\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})$.
An \emph{EFT representative selection functional} is any deterministic map
\[
J:\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})\to \RR
\]
whose value is computable from the declared protocol observables and matching dictionaries, together with a deterministic lexicographic tie-break on $\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})$.
The particular choice of $J$ (targets, weights, and scope) must be ledger-scoped.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[CAP-minimal EFT representative (finite-order)]\label{prop:cap_minimal_eft_representative}
\MathTag Fix $(B,D_{\max})$ and a finite basis $\mathcal{B}_{\le D_{\max}}$.
Given any deterministic selection functional $J$ on $\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})$ with a deterministic tie-break,
there exists a unique CAP-minimal EFT representative
\[
\mathcal{L}^{\ast}_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})\in \arg\min_{\mathcal{L}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{EFT}}(B,D_{\max})} J(\mathcal{L})
\]
selected by CAP within the declared finite family.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:eft_candidate_family_finite}, the candidate family is finite, hence the minimum of $J$ is attained.
A deterministic tie-break selects a unique minimizer when multiple minimizers have equal cost.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Renormalization/scheme boundary.}
\MatchTag The coefficients in an EFT representative are generally scheme- and scale-dependent in standard QFT.
This manuscript therefore treats renormalization conventions as matching-layer inputs unless explicitly closed within a finite candidate family; see Appendix~\ref{app:renormalization_dictionary_and_boundaries}.
The theorem-level content of the present appendix is the finiteness and determinism of the candidate family and selection rule, not constructive renormalization.
