
\begin{remark}[Computable form: explicit budget upper bound using verified \texorpdfstring{$r_q$}{rq}]\label{rem:pom-quantile-budget-computable}
Within the verified range \(S_q(m)=\Theta(r_q^m)\) (Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_moment_recursions}), Proposition \ref{prop:pom-quantile-chernoff} directly yields
$$
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[\gamma_m(X)\ge\gamma\big]\ \lesssim\
\exp\!\Bigl(m\bigl(\log r_q-\log 2-(q-1)\gamma\bigr)\Bigr).
$$
Therefore, when one takes
\(
\gamma>\frac{\log r_q-\log 2+\log(1/\varepsilon)/m}{q-1}
\),
the above expression gives an auditable tail control; substituting it into Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-quantile-budget} yields
\(
\log P_m
\gtrsim
m\gamma+\log 2
\),
a computable quota upper bound (linear in \(m\) for fixed \(q\)).
This replaces ``the spatial budget of the prime-register'' from ``worst fiber'' to ``tail quantile controlled by \(r_q\),'' and is fully aligned with the ``residual prime carrying'' semantics of Remark \ref{rem:pom-prime-carry}.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Optimization encapsulation (treating \(q\) as an adjustable knob)}
Taking the lower envelope of the above expression over \(q\ge 2\), one can define a \emph{certifiable} tail quantile upper bound (with verified \(r_q\) as input):
\[
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}_m(\varepsilon)
:=\inf_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}
\frac{\log r_q-\log 2+\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)}{q-1},
\qquad
B^{\mathrm{cert}}_m(\varepsilon):=\exp\!\bigl(m\,\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}_m(\varepsilon)\bigr).
\]
The minimizer \(q^\star\) then gives the ``most budget-efficient'' Chernoff certificate.
Furthermore, if the exact moments \(S_q(m)\) at finite \(m\) are used instead of the asymptotic leading scale \(r_q\), one obtains a completely finite-scale certificate form
\[
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert,fin}}_m(\varepsilon)
:=\inf_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}
\frac{\frac{1}{m}\log S_q(m)-\log 2+\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)}{q-1},
\]
whose correctness is directly guaranteed by Proposition \ref{prop:pom-quantile-chernoff} (no asymptotic assumption required).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Numerical example (using verified exact \texorpdfstring{$r_q$}{rq} truncated to \(q\le 17\), Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_renyi_spectrum})}
Taking \(\varepsilon=10^{-6}\) as an example:
\begin{itemize}
  \item When \(m=512\), the optimal certificate occurs at \(q^\star=8\), yielding \(\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}_{512}(10^{-6})\approx 0.224915\).
  Thus \(\log B^{\mathrm{cert}}\approx 115.156\), and from the condition \(P>2B\) one obtains a minimum budget threshold of
  \(\log_2 P\gtrsim (\log 2+\log B^{\mathrm{cert}})/\log 2\approx 167.1\) bits.
  \item When \(m=4096\), the optimal certificate occurs at \(q^\star=3\), yielding \(\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}_{4096}(10^{-6})\approx 0.218572\).
  Thus \(\log B^{\mathrm{cert}}\approx 895.27\), and one obtains \(\log_2 P\gtrsim 1292.6\) bits.
\end{itemize}
The same duality also gives a readable scale for tail rarity: e.g., taking \(\gamma=0.23\), under the discrete supremum with \(q\le 17\) one obtains
\(I(0.23)\approx 0.08059\) (optimal at \(q=17\)), so for \(m=512\) the upper bound is of order
\(\mathbb{P}_{w_m}[\gamma_m(X)\ge 0.23]\lesssim e^{-m I(0.23)}\approx 10^{-18}\).
The script module \texttt{scripts/common\_tail\_budget.py} implements the scan optimization for both types of \(\gamma\)-certificates;
the script \texttt{scripts/exp\_fold\_tail\_budget\_gamma\_cert.py} further verifies the true tail mass under \(w_m\) using a residue-DP histogram and outputs a JSON certificate.
\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Unimodal selection (why the optimal \(q^\star\) is auditably ``searchable'')}
By Proposition \ref{prop:pom-pressure-convexity}, \(q\mapsto \log r_q\) is discretely convex at integer points.
Thus for fixed \((m,\varepsilon)\), the certificate objective
\[
q\ \longmapsto\ \gamma^{(q)}_{m}(\varepsilon)
:=\frac{\log r_q-\log 2+\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)}{q-1}
\]
is a discrete quasi-convex function: for any threshold \(\Gamma\), the inequality \(\gamma^{(q)}_m(\varepsilon)\le \Gamma\) is equivalent to
\(
\log r_q \le (q-1)\Gamma+\log 2-\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)
\),
whose right-hand side is affine in \(q\); the sublevel set of ``discretely convex function minus affine function'' on the integers is necessarily an interval.
Therefore the optimal set \(q^\star\) is a (short) contiguous integer interval, which can be found either by full-table scan or by unimodal search in \(O(\log q_{\max})\) evaluations.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Explicit closed form for the total prime-register budget (optimizable over \(q\))}
From the \(B_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)\) form of Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-q-quota-family} and the CRT side-condition \(P_m>2B\), for any \(q\ge 2\) one obtains a fully explicit, auditable sufficient budget:
\[
\boxed{\;
\log P_m\ \gtrsim\ \log 2\ +\ \frac{m}{q-1}\bigl(\log r_q-\log 2\bigr)\ +\ \frac{1}{q-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)\;}
\]
Therefore, over a given auditable moment-order set \(\mathcal{Q}\subseteq\ZZ_{\ge 2}\), the ``moment-order selection'' can be written directly as
\[
q^\star(m,\varepsilon)=\arg\min_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}\left(\beta_q+\frac{1}{m(q-1)}\log(1/\varepsilon)\right),
\qquad
\beta_q=\frac{\log r_q-\log 2}{q-1}.
\]

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Phase diagram for the optimal moment-order selection (exact breakpoint form in Remark \ref{rem:pom-qstar-phase-diagram-breakpoints})}
The breakpoint closed form and the explicit phase-diagram table for \(\varepsilon=10^{-6}\) are given in Remark \ref{rem:pom-qstar-phase-diagram-breakpoints} and Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_qstar_breakpoints_eps1e6_q2_20}; they convert ``selecting \(q\)'' from a heuristic into a deterministic selection law uniquely determined by the audit table \(\{r_q\}\).
Let \(\alpha:=\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)\).

More strongly, by the discrete convexity of Proposition \ref{prop:pom-pressure-convexity} and the endpoint identity \(r_1=2\) (i.e., \(P(1)=\log 2\)), the secant slope
\(
\beta_q=\frac{P(q)-P(1)}{q-1}
\)
is non-decreasing for \(q\ge 2\): taking \((q_0,q_1,q_2)=(1,q,q+1)\) in the discrete convexity condition yields
\[
P(q)\ \le\ \frac{1}{q}P(1)+\frac{q-1}{q}P(q+1)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\beta_q\le \beta_{q+1}.
\]
Therefore, when \(\log(1/\varepsilon)=o(m)\) (e.g., \(\varepsilon\) is fixed and \(m\to\infty\)),
\[
\gamma_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)=\beta_q+\frac{\alpha}{q-1}=\beta_q+o(1),
\]
and the leading exponential term is minimized at \(q=2\); the benefit of increasing \(q\) primarily comes from the reliability term \(\alpha/(q-1)\) at finite \(m\) or extremely small \(\varepsilon\).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{(Reliability scaling phase transition)}
Treating \(\varepsilon\) as a risk budget varying with \(m\): if \(\varepsilon_m=e^{-c m}\) (i.e., \(\alpha_m\to c>0\)), then under the \(r_q\) certificate calibration the optimal exponent is given by
\[
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}(c)
=\inf_{q\ge 2}\left(\beta_q+\frac{c}{q-1}\right).
\]
When \(c\downarrow 0\), this recovers the ``constant failure rate'' phase with \(\beta_2=\log(r_2/2)\);
when \(c\to\infty\), in order to suppress \(c/(q-1)\) the optimal \(q^\star\) is pushed toward large \(q\), and by \(\lim_{q\to\infty}\beta_q=\log\sqrt{\varphi}\) one obtains
\[
\lim_{c\to\infty}\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}(c)=\log\sqrt{\varphi},
\]
equivalent to asymptotically approaching the endpoint exponent of ``provisioning by the maximum fiber'': as the error tolerance tends to 0, the benefit of replacing worst-case provisioning with quantile-based provisioning inevitably vanishes.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Auditable example (\(m=30,\ \varepsilon=10^{-6}\))}
Within the exact fingerprint range \(q\le 17\) (Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_renyi_spectrum}), a full-table scan yields the optimal moment order \(q^\star(30,10^{-6})=17\), corresponding to
\[
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}_{30}(10^{-6})\approx 0.253745,\qquad
B^{\mathrm{cert}}_{30}(10^{-6})\approx 2.02\times 10^3,
\]
so the prime-register product budget need only satisfy \(P>2B\approx 4.05\times 10^3\).
The output of script \texttt{scripts/exp\_fold\_tail\_budget\_gamma\_cert.py} simultaneously provides a directly usable set of coprime modulus recommendations: for example, taking \((2,3,5,7,11,13)\) gives product \(30030>2B\).
\par
If the auditable range is extended to the DP-extended fingerprints \(q\le 20\) (Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_renyi_spectrum_mod_dp_q20}), then the optimal moment order at the same operating point shifts to \(q^\star=20\), further compressing the budget to \(B\approx 1.81\times 10^3\) (the same set of primes still suffices).
For comparison, using only the second-order certificate \(q=2\) yields \(B\sim 6\times 10^8\), a budget on the order of $10^8$, clearly demonstrating the structural conclusion that ``higher-order moments primarily improve the reliability term for extremely small \(\varepsilon\).''

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Pareto frontier of the space/time duality (auditable definition)}
The ``time cost'' on the certificate side is quantified using an auditable realization-size proxy: for example, take
\[
T(q):=\text{realization/recursion order proxy}\ d_q
\]
(in the Fold case, \(d_q\) can be read from the verified integer-coefficient recursion order; see Tables \ref{tab:fold_collision_moment_spectrum_k2_8} and \ref{tab:fold_collision_moment_spectrum_k9_17}).
The space-side cost is taken as the lower bound of
\(
S(q):=\log_2 P_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)
\)
(read directly from the formula \(\log P_m\gtrsim \log2+m\beta_q+\frac{1}{q-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)\)).
Then the non-dominated subset of the discrete point set
\(
\{(T(q),S(q)):\ q\in\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{cert}}\}
\)
gives a verifiable Pareto frontier: it quantifies ``higher-order moment certificate (larger \(T\)) \(\Rightarrow\) smaller prime-register (smaller \(S\))'' as a discrete frontier curve.
For the example \((m,\varepsilon)=(30,10^{-6})\) with \(\mathcal{Q}_{\mathrm{cert}}=\{2,\dots,17\}\), the scan yields frontier points at
\[
q\in\{3,5,9,11,15,17\},
\]
with \((d_q,\log_2 P)\) approximately
\[
(3,20.35),\ (5,15.46),\ (7,13.08),\ (9,12.63),\ (11,12.13),\ (13,11.98),
\]
This Pareto frontier characterizes the auditable exchange rate between ``time/implementation complexity'' and ``space/prime-register budget.''
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Phase diagram of breakpoints for the Chernoff quantile certificate \(q^\star\) (design law; final conclusion)]\label{rem:pom-qstar-phase-diagram-breakpoints}
Under the Chernoff quantile certificate definition of Remark \ref{rem:pom-quantile-budget-computable}, for each integer \(q\ge 2\) write
\[
\gamma^{(q)}_m(\varepsilon)
:=
\frac{\log r_q-\log 2+\frac{1}{m}\log(1/\varepsilon)}{q-1},
\qquad
\gamma^{\mathrm{cert}}_m(\varepsilon)=\inf_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}\gamma^{(q)}_m(\varepsilon),
\qquad
q^\star(m,\varepsilon)\in\arg\min_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}\gamma^{(q)}_m(\varepsilon).

  \boxed{\ \gamma_\infty=\log(r_2/2)=0.2155928379\ldots\ }.

\end{enumerate}

\begin{proposition}[Quantile-CRT compilation law: \texorpdfstring{$\varepsilon$}{eps}-sound order projection elimination]\label{prop:pom-rcrt-epsilon}
Fix resolution \(m\) and error budget \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), and let \(X\sim w_m\) under the fold readout calibration.
Suppose the order projection gate \(P_{\le}\) acts on an internal integer label that is a random variable \(Y\) satisfying the crude bound
\[
|Y|\ \le\ d_m(X)
\qquad(\text{e.g., the fiber-index carried by the prime-register, or any register value bounded by fiber size}).
\]
Take any \(B\ge 1\) such that
\[
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[d_m(X)>B\big]\ \le\ \varepsilon,
\]
and choose pairwise coprime moduli \(p_1,\dots,p_r\) with product \(P=\prod_i p_i\) satisfying \(P>2B\).
Then under the error-enriched calibration (Definition \ref{def:pom-eps-sound-rewrite}) there exists an \(\varepsilon\)-sound rewrite certificate
\[
(\mathrm{RCRT}_\varepsilon)\qquad
P_{\le}\ \Longrightarrow_{\varepsilon}\ \mathrm{CRT}_{\{p_i\}}\circ P_{\chi,\{p_i\}}.
\]
Here the right-hand side is precisely the spatialized implementation of the hard-threshold version (RCRT) (Definition \ref{def:pom-order-elimination-rewrite}): first read out residues in parallel along the modular axes (\(P_{\chi,\{p_i\}}\)), then reconstruct via CRT (\(\mathrm{CRT}_{\{p_i\}}\)).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let the bad event be \(E:=\{|Y|>B\}\).
By the assumption \(|Y|\le d_m(X)\), we have \(E\subseteq\{d_m(X)>B\}\), hence
\(
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}[E]\le \varepsilon
\).
On the good event \(E^c\), \(Y\in[-B,B]\), so the order/interval judgment of \(P_{\le}\) can be viewed as the restricted gate \(P_{\le,[-B,B]}\).
Under the side condition \(P>2B\), the hard-threshold version (RCRT) preserves correct integer readout on \([-B,B]\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-order-elimination-correct}), hence on \(E^c\) one has
\(
P_{\le}= \mathrm{CRT}_{\{p_i\}}\circ P_{\chi,\{p_i\}}
\)
(as visible readout).
Therefore by Definition \ref{def:pom-eps-sound-rewrite}, the above rewrite holds with failure probability at most \(\varepsilon\). \qed
\end{proof}
\begin{remark}[Two hardening paths and \texorpdfstring{$(u,q,\varepsilon)$}{(u,q,eps)} scheduling]\label{rem:pom-hardening-u-vs-q}
The ``hardening'' of order gates admits at least two executable paths within the interface of this paper:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Temperature path:} Lowering \(u\) (Remark \ref{rem:pom-order-as-zero-temp}) causes the soft order gate \(\mathrm{PROJ}_u\) to harden to \(P_{\le}\); the time-side cost is primarily reflected in the twisted spectral gap/mixing scale \(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(u)\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-rewrite-audit-loop}).
  \item \textbf{Moment-quantile path:} Fixing (or not drastically lowering) \(u\), one raises \(q\) and uses the moment-kernel (\(\mathrm{MOM}_q\)) to produce a tail certificate, thereby triggering \((\mathrm{RCRT}_\varepsilon)\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-rcrt-epsilon}) to compile the order gate into a spatialized residue+CRT readout; the space-side cost is primarily reflected in the prime-register quota \(\log P\) (Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-quantile-budget} and the \(\gamma\)-certificate of Remark \ref{rem:pom-quantile-budget-computable}).
\end{itemize}
Thus the compiler can treat \((u,q,\varepsilon)\) as three knobs: \(u\) primarily controls the time scale, \(q\) primarily controls tail amplification and spatial quota, and \(\varepsilon\) primarily controls the risk budget; the additive law of Proposition \ref{prop:pom-eps-sound-compose} is used to account for the overall failure probability across multiple approximate order-gate eliminations.
The two-dimensional pressure surface \(P(q,u)\) provides a unified coordinate system placing both paths into a single ``spectral phase diagram'' (\S\ref{subsec:pom-pressure-surface-qu}).
\end{remark}

\(
\Delta_m:=-\log r_m
\)
(i.e., the step-count mixing scale \(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(m):=1/\Delta_m\)).
Then provided that
\[
n_{\mathrm{eff}}\ \gtrsim\ \frac{\log|G_m|+\log(1/\delta)}{\Delta_m},
\]
the relative error can be suppressed to \(O(\delta)\) (absorbing constants into \(\gtrsim\)).
Consequently, ``using more congruence/character axes (space) to replace longer primitive lengths (time)'' is not free: each additional layer simultaneously changes
\(|G_m|\) and \(r_m\), and the net effect can be directly audited via the workload fingerprint
\[
W(m):=\frac{\log|G_m|}{\Delta_m}=\log|G_m|\cdot\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(m)
\]
: if adding an axis increases \(|G_m|\) but drives \(r_m\to 1\) (spectral gap shrinks, \(\Delta_m\downarrow\)), then \(W(m)\) may increase, resulting in ``slower'' performance.
The scan table for the real-input \(40\)-state kernel on discrete prime modulus axes (Table \ref{tab:real-input-40-spectral-tax-workload}) provides a reproducible direct illustration (e.g., the comparison between \(p=3\) and \(p=5\)).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Maximum modulus control law: multiplicative space, quadratic time (near-coboundary + falsifiable selection law)]\label{rem:pom-max-modulus-control-law}
This paragraph further splices the ``multiplicative space budget'' and ``time spectral-tax'' of Table \ref{tab:pom-space-time-duality-prime-register} into a single testable engineering law: on a separable direct-product residue axis, the \emph{spatial capacity} of the prime-register is determined by the product \(P=|G|\), while the \emph{temporal diffusion} at leading order is determined by the ``worst small angle/maximum modulus,'' not by \(P\) itself.
\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{Setup (direct-product residue axis)}
Take pairwise coprime moduli \(\{p_1,\dots,p_k\}\) and let
\[
G:=\prod_{i=1}^k\ZZ/p_i\ZZ,
\qquad
P:=|G|=\prod_{i=1}^k p_i,
\qquad
L:=\max_i p_i.
\]
Here by CRT one has \(G\cong \ZZ/P\ZZ\), but the direct-product coordinates make the audit of ``minimal nontrivial angle \(2\pi/p_i\) on each axis'' and the formulation of the ``single-axis slow-mode selection law'' more direct.
Denote the worst-case twisted ratio for ``cylinder congruence equidistribution'' by
\(
r_G:=\max_{\chi\neq\chi_0}\rho(M_\chi)/\lambda
\)
and let the spectral-tax rate be
\(
\Delta_G:=-\log r_G
\)
(consistent with the notation of Remark \ref{rem:pom-spectral-tax-workload}).
\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{(Small-angle quadratic law)}
In the near-coboundary small-angle regime, the twisted gap is controlled by the variance density: for any one-dimensional direction \(F\) with minimal nontrivial angle \(2\pi/m\),
\[
\log\frac{\rho(M_{it_mF})}{\lambda}
=-\frac{\sigma_F^2}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{m}\right)^2+O(m^{-4})
\]
(Corollary \ref{cor:dirichlet-modulus-diffusion-barrier}; where \(\sigma_F^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a\), \(\Sigma=\nabla^2P(0)\)).
Thus, the leading-order spectral gap for the ``pure-axis twist'' along the \(i\)-th axis (nontrivial only on \(\ZZ/p_i\), with \(j=\pm1\)) is
\[
\Delta_i\ \approx\ 2\pi^2\,\sigma_i^2/p_i^2
=4\pi^2\,\kappa_i/p_i^2,
\qquad \kappa_i:=\sigma_i^2/2.
\]
\par
\medskip\noindent\textbf{(Falsifiable selection law \(\Rightarrow\) maximum modulus control)}
If the direct-product axis satisfies the ``single-axis slow-mode selection law'':
\[
\arg\max_{\chi\neq\chi_0}\frac{\rho(M_\chi)}{\lambda}
\ \text{is attained by a pure-axis character (only one axis nontrivial), and occurs at the largest modulus }p_{i^\star}=L,
\]
then the leading order of \(\Delta_G\) is locked to this worst small angle, and consequently
\[
n\ \gtrsim\ \frac{\log|G|+\log(1/\delta)}{\Delta_G}
\ \approx\
\frac{L^2}{4\pi^2\,\kappa_{i^\star}}\Bigl(\log P+\log\tfrac{1}{\delta}\Bigr).
\]
This shows that, under the above auditable premise, \textbf{the spatial budget of the prime-register is multiplicative \(P\), but the leading-order time budget is quadratic \(L^2\), entering only through \(\log P\)}.
\par
The ``single-axis slow-mode selection law'' itself is falsifiable/auditable: for instance, in the \(((3,3,p))\) pure-collision-axis scan of the real-input 40-state kernel, the worst character index is stably \((0,0,\pm1)\) (Table \ref{tab:real_input_40_arity_335_n2_selection_law_primes}), consistent with the near-coboundary small-angle mechanism; the more general selection law under the quadratic approximation is equivalent to a metric shortest vector problem (Theorem \ref{thm:real-input-40-near-coboundary-svp}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Primorial modulus selection and \texorpdfstring{$\mathrm{poly}(\log P)$}{poly(log P)} time closure (external number-theoretic fact)]\label{rem:pom-primorial-polylog-time}
Under the spatial constraint given by Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-quantile-budget},
\(
P>2B_m(\varepsilon)=2\exp(m\gamma_m(\varepsilon))
\),
the capacity's leading scale satisfies \(\log P\asymp m\gamma_m(\varepsilon)\).
\par
If one wishes to minimize the largest modulus \(L=\max_i p_i\) while satisfying \(\prod_i p_i\ge P\), number theory (the linear-order growth of the Chebyshev \(\vartheta\) function) shows that for a given cutoff \(L\), the product \(\prod_{p\le L}p\) is maximized; moreover
\[
\log\!\left(\prod_{p\le L}p\right)=\vartheta(L)=\Theta(L).
\]
Therefore, when adopting the primorial scheme of ``taking all primes \(\le L\)'', one has
\[
L\ =\ \Theta(\log P)\ =\ \Theta(m\gamma_m(\varepsilon)).
\]
Substituting this back into the time budget of Remark \ref{rem:pom-max-modulus-control-law} (and treating \(\kappa_{i^\star}\) as a constant extractable via the spectral scan/Poisson equation interface; see Remark \ref{rem:arity-335-kappa-poisson}), one obtains the leading-order complexity law
\[
n\ \gtrsim\ \frac{(\log P)^2}{4\pi^2\kappa_{i^\star}}\Bigl(\log P+\log\tfrac{1}{\delta}\Bigr).
\]
In particular, when \(\delta\) is a constant-level (or polynomial-level) target precision, the time cost exhibits an explicit \(\,O\big((\log P)^3\big)\,\) scaling: \textbf{exponential in space (Euler product), but only polynomial in \(\log P\) for time}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Variance-matched modulus allocation: turning \texorpdfstring{$\Sigma$}{Sigma} into a register design law]\label{rem:pom-variance-matched-moduli}
The near-coboundary quadratic law converts the question ``which moduli to choose'' into a metric discrete geometry problem.
In the notation of Corollary \ref{cor:dirichlet-modulus-diffusion-barrier}, for each independently twistable axis (or readout direction) \(F_i\), denote its variance density by \(\sigma_i^2=\mathrm{Var}_\infty(F_i)\) (more generally \(\sigma_F^2=a^{\mathsf T}\Sigma a\), where \(\Sigma=\nabla^2P(0)\)).
Then in the pure-axis small-angle regime, the gap for the \(i\)-th axis modulus \(p_i\) has leading order \(\Delta_i\approx 2\pi^2\sigma_i^2/p_i^2\).
\par
Therefore, if the overall slow mode is dominated by the ``worst axis'' (single-axis selection law holds), then the dominant factor in the mixing time is
\[
\max_i\frac{p_i^2}{\sigma_i^2}.
\]
Under the continuous approximation with the capacity constraint \(\prod_i p_i\ge P\), a minimax impedance matching principle is to equalize the gap across all axes:
\[
\frac{\sigma_1^2}{p_1^2}\ \approx\ \frac{\sigma_2^2}{p_2^2}\ \approx\ \cdots\ \approx\ \frac{\sigma_k^2}{p_k^2},
\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad
p_i\ \propto\ \sigma_i.
\]
This allocation law directly couples the pressure Hessian/covariance density \(\Sigma\) into the discrete modulus-selection problem for the prime-register: allocate larger moduli to ``harder'' (higher variance density) directions, in order to suppress the diffusion-time upper bound imposed by the worst axis.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Multi-axis budget barrier: geometry-of-numbers lower bound via the shortest vector mechanism}\label{subsubsec:pom-multi-axis-budget-barrier}
Remark \ref{rem:pom-variance-matched-moduli} provides an impedance-matching design law: if the slow mode is indeed dominated by a single ``worst axis,'' then equalizing the gap across all axes can suppress the upper bound.
However, when \(d\ge 1\) independently twistable axes coexist, a more fundamental constraint arises from the geometry-of-numbers structure of the angle lattice itself: in the regime where the near-coboundary small-angle quadratic law is effective, the worst-case twist gap is approximately equivalent to a metric shortest vector problem;
the Minkowski convex body theorem yields a budget-exponent barrier (with exponent \(2/d\)) that no modulus-selection strategy can avoid, and this barrier can be translated via Dirichlet normalization into a near-\(1\) leading-pole edge-hugging law; combining with the space--time conversion law, one can lock the edge-hugging and finite-part drift linearly at second-order precision.
Finally, if one allows unimodular reparametrization (\(GL_d(\ZZ)\) transformations) on the residue axis and optimizes the angle lattice shape within the same covolume class, the limiting constant is controlled by the Hermite constant; simultaneously, a leading-order counting law for the edge-hugging poles can be obtained.

\paragraph{Multi-axis twist and near-coboundary quadratic law}
Let \(d\ge 1\).
Consider a family of unit-circle twisted matrices \(M_{i\vartheta}\) (\(\vartheta\in\RR^d\)), and denote
\[
P(\vartheta):=\log\rho(M_{i\vartheta}),\qquad \lambda:=\rho(M_{0}),\qquad \Sigma:=\nabla^2P(0)\succ 0.
\]
In the three-dimensional observations of the real-input \(40\)-state kernel, the above \(\Sigma\) and small-angle quadratic law are provided in ``boxed'' form by Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:real-input-40-near-coboundary-svp}; on a general finite-dimensional primitive kernel, the analogous form can be obtained via analytic perturbation of the Perron branch and taking the real part (same proof framework, see the proof of \ref{thm:real-input-40-near-coboundary-svp}).
This paper works within this small-angle regime: assume there exists \(\delta_0>0\) such that for \(\|\vartheta\|\le \delta_0\) the following holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-near-coboundary}
\log\frac{\rho(M_{i\vartheta})}{\lambda}
\;=\;
-\frac12\,\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\,\vartheta\ +\ O(\|\vartheta\|^4).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Angle lattice and budget}
Given a modulus tuple \(m=(m_1,\dots,m_d)\in(\ZZ_{\ge 2})^d\), introduce the unwrapped angle lattice
\[
L(m):=\frac{2\pi}{m_1}\ZZ\times\cdots\times\frac{2\pi}{m_d}\ZZ\subset\RR^d,
\qquad
\det L(m)=\frac{(2\pi)^d}{B},
\qquad
B:=\prod_{k=1}^d m_k.
\]
Here \(\det L(m)\) denotes the covolume of the lattice, and \(B\) is the multiplicative budget (modulus product) of the prime-register.
Wrapping the angle lattice by \(2\pi\) (applying coordinate-wise \(\mathrm{wrap}(\cdot)\) to \([-\pi,\pi]\)) yields the finite wrapped angle lattice
\[
\Lambda(m):=\Bigl\{\vartheta(j)
=\bigl(\mathrm{wrap}(2\pi j_1/m_1),\dots,\mathrm{wrap}(2\pi j_d/m_d)\bigr)\in[-\pi,\pi]^d:\ j\in(\ZZ/m_1)\times\cdots\times(\ZZ/m_d)\Bigr\}.
\]
(Below we abbreviate \(\ZZ/m_k\ZZ\) as \(\ZZ/m_k\)).
Thus \(\Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}\) corresponds exactly to all nontrivial character channels' reachable small-angle twists (\(\vartheta=0\) corresponds to the trivial character).
Under the quadratic approximation, the worst-case twist corresponds to minimizing the quadratic form \(\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\vartheta\) over \(\Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}\).
Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-qm}
q(m):=\min_{\vartheta\in\Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}}\ \vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\,\vartheta.
\end{equation}
When \(B\) is large enough that the shortest vector realizing the minimum lies within the effective domain \(\|\vartheta\|\le\delta_0\), equation \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near-coboundary} applies directly to that shortest vector.

\begin{theorem}[Minkowski budget barrier: universal upper bound on the shortest energy]\label{thm:pom-minkowski-budget-barrier}
Let \(V_d:=\mathrm{vol}\{x\in\RR^d:\ |x|\le 1\}=\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2+1)\), and let
\[
C_d:=\Bigl(\frac{2^{d}}{V_d}\Bigr)^{2/d}.
\]
Then for any \(m\in(\ZZ_{\ge 2})^d\), when \(B=\prod_k m_k\) is large enough to ensure that the shortest vector of \(q(m)\) lies in the small-angle effective domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-minkowski-qm-upper}
q(m)\ \le\ (2\pi)^2\,C_d\,(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}\,B^{-2/d}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let \(E_r:=\{x\in\RR^d:\ x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x\le r^2\}\).
This set is centrally symmetric and convex, with volume
\[
\mathrm{vol}(E_r)=\frac{V_d\,r^d}{\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}.
\]
By Minkowski's convex body theorem (see, e.g., \cite{Cassels1957}), when \(\mathrm{vol}(E_r)>2^d\det L(m)\), there exists a nonzero vector \(x\in L(m)\cap E_r\), so that \(x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x\le r^2\).
When \(B\) is large enough, the above \(r=r(B)\to 0\), so the obtained \(x\) lies in the wrapped angle domain and represents some nontrivial character channel, i.e., \(x\in\Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}\);
thus \(q(m)\le x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x\le r^2\).
Setting \(r\) so that
\(
V_d r^d/\sqrt{\det\Sigma}=2^d\det L(m)
\)
and taking a value strictly greater yields
\[
q(m)
\le r^2
=\Bigl(\frac{2^d\,\det L(m)\,\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}{V_d}\Bigr)^{2/d}
=(2\pi)^2\Bigl(\frac{2^d}{V_d}\Bigr)^{2/d}(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}B^{-2/d},
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:pom-minkowski-qm-upper}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Budget-exponent barrier for spectral gap and mixing time]\label{cor:pom-multi-axis-mixing-budget-barrier}
Under the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pom-minkowski-budget-barrier}, define the worst-case twisted spectral ratio and gap
\[
r_{\max}(m):=\max_{\vartheta\in \Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\rho(M_{i\vartheta})}{\lambda},
\qquad
\Delta_{\mathrm{spec}}(m):=-\log r_{\max}(m),
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(m):=\frac{1}{\Delta_{\mathrm{spec}}(m)}
\]
(the calibration of \(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\) is consistent with \S\ref{subsec:xi-near1-diffusive}).
Then as \(B\to\infty\) within the small-angle effective domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-gap-qm}
\Delta_{\mathrm{spec}}(m)=\frac12\,q(m)\ +\ O\!\bigl(q(m)^2\bigr),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-taumix-lb}
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(m)\ \ge\ \frac{B^{2/d}}{2\pi^2\,C_d\,(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}}\,(1+o(1)).
\end{equation}
Consequently: \emph{under any modulus-selection strategy, the optimal budget exponent for the time cost must be \(2/d\)}; if one wishes to suppress \(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}\) to constant order, then the budget \(B\) must satisfy at least \(B=\Omega(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{d/2})\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the small-angle domain, by \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near-coboundary},
\(
-\log(\rho(M_{i\vartheta})/\lambda)=\frac12\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\vartheta+O(\|\vartheta\|^4)
\).
Taking the maximum spectral ratio (equivalently, the minimum gap) at the shortest vector where \(\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\vartheta=q(m)\) yields \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-gap-qm}, and substituting \eqref{eq:pom-minkowski-qm-upper} from Theorem \ref{thm:pom-minkowski-budget-barrier} yields \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-taumix-lb}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Multi-dimensional Siegel-like barrier: near-\texorpdfstring{$1$}{1} leading-pole edge-hugging law]\label{cor:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-barrier}
Under the Dirichlet normalization \(z=\lambda^{-s}\), define
\[
s(\vartheta):=\frac{P(\vartheta)}{\log\lambda}
=\frac{\log\rho(M_{i\vartheta})}{\log\lambda},
\qquad
\Delta(\vartheta):=-\log\frac{\rho(M_{i\vartheta})}{\lambda}.
\]
Then in the small-angle domain,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-translate}
1-\Re s(\vartheta)=\frac{\Delta(\vartheta)}{\log\lambda}
=\frac{1}{2\log\lambda}\,\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\vartheta+O(\|\vartheta\|^4).
\end{equation}
Furthermore, for any modulus tuple \(m\) (with \(B\) sufficiently large), there must exist some nontrivial twist \(\vartheta_\star\in \Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-budget}
1-\Re s(\vartheta_\star)
\ \le\
\frac{(2\pi)^2\,C_d}{2\log\lambda}\,(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}\,B^{-2/d}\ +\ o(B^{-2/d}).
\end{equation}
That is: when the number of independently twistable axes is \(d\), the optimal edge-hugging rate for near-\(1\) poles is characterized by \(B^{-2/d}\), and cannot be surpassed by any modulus-selection strategy.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-translate} is a direct rewriting of \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near-coboundary}.
Taking \(\vartheta_\star\) as the shortest vector realizing \(q(m)\) and controlling its quadratic form via \eqref{eq:pom-minkowski-qm-upper} yields \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-budget}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Pole--drift budget binding: edge-hugging poles carry same-order finite-part drift]\label{cor:pom-multi-axis-pole-drift-binding}
Under the setting of Corollary \ref{cor:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-barrier}, further assume that the Abel finite-part constant \(\log\mathfrak{M}(\theta)\) is real-analytic in a neighborhood of \(\theta=0\) (Remark \ref{prop:logM-analytic}), and let
\(
H:=\nabla^2\log\mathfrak{M}(0)
\).
For any direction \(v\neq 0\), define the directional coupling ratio
\[
\mathcal{W}(v):=\frac{v^{\mathsf T}Hv}{v^{\mathsf T}\Sigma v},
\]
and for \(\vartheta\neq 0\) write \(v(\vartheta):=\vartheta/\|\vartheta\|\), \(\Delta_{\mathfrak{M}}(\vartheta):=\log\mathfrak{M}(0)-\Re\log\mathfrak{M}(i\vartheta)\).
Then by the space--time conversion law (Appendix Proposition \ref{prop:real-input-40-space-time-conversion-law}, read in analogous form), as \(\|\vartheta\|\to 0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-pole-drift-conversion}
\Delta_{\mathfrak{M}}(\vartheta)
=(\log\lambda)\,\mathcal{W}\bigl(v(\vartheta)\bigr)\,\bigl(1-\Re s(\vartheta)\bigr)\ +\ O(\|\vartheta\|^4).
\end{equation}
In particular, for the unavoidable \(\vartheta_\star\) of Corollary \ref{cor:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-barrier},
\[
|\Delta_{\mathfrak{M}}(\vartheta_\star)|=O(B^{-2/d}),
\]
i.e., the budget edge-hugging of a near-\(1\) pole must simultaneously carry a same-order finite-part drift at the constant level.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-pole-drift-conversion} is a notational substitution of Proposition \ref{prop:real-input-40-space-time-conversion-law}: at second-order approximation,
\(
\Delta_{\mathfrak{M}}(\vartheta)=\frac12\vartheta^{\mathsf T}H\vartheta+O(\|\vartheta\|^4)
\)
and
\(
1-\Re s(\vartheta)=\frac{1}{2\log\lambda}\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\vartheta+O(\|\vartheta\|^4)
\);
dividing yields the stated conversion law.
Substituting \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-budget} and noting the boundedness of \(\mathcal{W}(v)\) on the unit sphere (e.g., controlled by \(\mu_{\max}:=\|\Sigma^{-1/2}H\Sigma^{-1/2}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\)) yields \(|\Delta_{\mathfrak{M}}(\vartheta_\star)|=O(B^{-2/d})\).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Limiting constant: Hermite constant characterizes optimal lattice packing density}
The above three corollaries establish the insurmountability of the budget exponent \(2/d\).
Furthermore, if one allows invertible integer-coefficient reparametrization (\(GL_d(\ZZ)\) transformations) on the residue axis and views the angle lattice within the same covolume class as a realizable general lattice, then ``maximizing the worst-case twist energy'' (and hence achieving the fastest mixing) can be promoted to the lattice packing density extremal problem in geometry of numbers.

\begin{theorem}[Hermite constant characterization: optimal constant within the same covolume class]\label{thm:pom-hermite-constant-characterization}
Let \(\mathcal{L}(V)\) denote the set of all lattices in \(\RR^d\) with covolume \(V\).
For \(\Sigma\succ 0\) and \(\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(V)\), define the first minimum
\[
\lambda_1(\Lambda,\Sigma):=\min_{0\neq x\in\Lambda}x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x.
\]
Denote the Euclidean Hermite constant by
\[
\gamma_d:=\sup_{\Lambda}\ \frac{\min_{0\neq x\in\Lambda}|x|^{2}}{(\det\Lambda)^{2/d}}\quad(<\infty).
\]
Then for any \(\Sigma\succ 0\) and \(V>0\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-hermite-sup}
\sup_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}(V)}\ \lambda_1(\Lambda,\Sigma)
\ =\ \gamma_d\,(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}\,V^{2/d}.
\end{equation}
In particular, when \(\det L(m)=(2\pi)^d/B\), if one allows optimizing the lattice shape within the same covolume class, the optimal spectral gap constant satisfies
\[
\Delta_{\mathrm{spec}}^{\mathrm{opt}}(B)\ \sim\ \frac12(2\pi)^2\gamma_d(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}B^{-2/d},
\qquad
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}^{\mathrm{opt}}(B)\ \sim\ \frac{B^{2/d}}{2\pi^2\gamma_d(\det\Sigma)^{1/d}}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply the linear transformation \(y=\Sigma^{1/2}x\), so that \(x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x=|y|^2\), and the lattice covolume satisfies
\(
\det(\Sigma^{1/2}\Lambda)=(\det\Sigma)^{1/2}\det\Lambda
\).
Therefore
\[
\lambda_1(\Lambda,\Sigma)
=\min_{0\neq y\in\Sigma^{1/2}\Lambda}|y|^2.
\]
Applying the definition and scale invariance of the Hermite constant to the Euclidean case, for any covolume \(W\),
\(
\sup_{\Omega:\det\Omega=W}\min_{0\neq y\in\Omega}|y|^2=\gamma_d\,W^{2/d}
\).
Taking \(W=(\det\Sigma)^{1/2}V\) yields \eqref{eq:pom-hermite-sup}.
Substituting \(V=(2\pi)^d/B\) and combining with \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-gap-qm} yields the final two expressions.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Minkowski constant and Hermite constant]\label{rem:pom-minkowski-vs-hermite}
The constant \(C_d\) of Theorem \ref{thm:pom-minkowski-budget-barrier} provides an unconditional upper bound on the Hermite constant: \(\gamma_d\le C_d\).
Therefore \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-taumix-lb} provides a universal budget lower bound independent of lattice optimization assumptions; Theorem \ref{thm:pom-hermite-constant-characterization} then characterizes the optimal constant when lattice shape optimization is permitted.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Density law for edge-hugging poles: volume-ratio leading term}
Theorem \ref{thm:pom-minkowski-budget-barrier} and Corollary \ref{cor:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-barrier} establish that ``at least one'' edge-hugging pole channel exists.
Within the small-angle effective domain, one can also obtain a leading-order counting law for the number of edge-hugging poles: for \(\varepsilon>0\), define
\[
N_\varepsilon(m):=\#\Bigl\{\vartheta\in \Lambda(m)\setminus\{0\}:\ 1-\Re s(\vartheta)\le \varepsilon\Bigr\}.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Leading term for the edge-hugging pole count]\label{prop:pom-multi-axis-near1-density}
Within the small-angle effective domain, if \(\varepsilon\downarrow 0\), \(B\to\infty\), and \(B\,\varepsilon^{d/2}\to\infty\) (to ensure the volume-ratio approximation is stable), the following asymptotic holds:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-density}
N_{\varepsilon}(m)
\ =\
\frac{B}{(2\pi)^{d}}\cdot\frac{V_{d}}{\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}\cdot(2\log\lambda\cdot\varepsilon)^{d/2}
\ +\ o\!\bigl(B\varepsilon^{d/2}\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, at the natural edge-hugging scale \(\varepsilon\asymp B^{-2/d}\), \(N_\varepsilon(m)=O(1)\): the pole family closest to \(1\) remains sparse under budget amplification, rather than exhibiting macroscopic-level multiple edge-hugging.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-translate}, ignoring higher-order terms,
\(
1-\Re s(\vartheta)\le \varepsilon
\)
is equivalent to
\(
\vartheta^{\mathsf T}\Sigma\vartheta\le 2\log\lambda\cdot\varepsilon
\).
Let \(R:=\sqrt{2\log\lambda\cdot\varepsilon}\) and denote the ellipsoid \(E_R=\{x:\ x^{\mathsf T}\Sigma x\le R^2\}\).
When the angle lattice is sufficiently fine (guaranteed by \(B\,\varepsilon^{d/2}\to\infty\)), the lattice point count is approximated by the volume ratio:
\[
N_\varepsilon(m)
\sim
\frac{\mathrm{vol}(E_R)}{\det L(m)}
=\frac{V_d R^d}{\sqrt{\det\Sigma}}\cdot\frac{B}{(2\pi)^d},
\]
and substituting \(R^d=(2\log\lambda\cdot\varepsilon)^{d/2}\) yields \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near1-density}.
The final statement follows by substituting \(\varepsilon\asymp B^{-2/d}\) back into the leading term.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Closed deduction chain (resource--spectral--pole--finite-part)}
On the foundation of the near-coboundary quadratic law \eqref{eq:pom-multi-axis-near-coboundary} and the space--time conversion law (Proposition \ref{prop:real-input-40-space-time-conversion-law}), one obtains a closed auditable deduction chain:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Budget \(\Rightarrow\) spectral:} The Minkowski budget barrier gives \(q(m)=O(B^{-2/d})\) (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-minkowski-budget-barrier}), so the worst spectral gap satisfies \(\Delta_{\mathrm{spec}}(m)=O(B^{-2/d})\) and forces \(\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}(m)=\Omega(B^{2/d})\) (Corollary \ref{cor:pom-multi-axis-mixing-budget-barrier}).
  \item \textbf{Spectral \(\Rightarrow\) pole:} Dirichlet normalization translates the same gap into a near-\(1\) leading-pole edge-hugging law \(1-\Re s=O(B^{-2/d})\) (Corollary \ref{cor:pom-multi-axis-near1-pole-barrier}).
  \item \textbf{Pole \(\Rightarrow\) finite-part:} The space--time conversion locks the edge-hugging and finite-part drift linearly at second-order precision, so the unavoidable edge-hugging channel must carry a same-order finite-part drift (Corollary \ref{cor:pom-multi-axis-pole-drift-binding}).
  \item \textbf{Constant optimization and density:} When lattice shape optimization within the same covolume class is permitted, the limiting constant is controlled by the Hermite constant (Theorem \ref{thm:pom-hermite-constant-characterization}); simultaneously, the edge-hugging pole count satisfies a volume-ratio leading term \(N_\varepsilon\sim B\varepsilon^{d/2}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-multi-axis-near1-density}).
\end{itemize}
