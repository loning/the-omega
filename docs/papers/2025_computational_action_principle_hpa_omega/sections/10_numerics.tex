\section{Numerical toy experiments: reproducible checks of key CAP links}
\label{sec:numerics}

This section provides two minimal simulations that check intermediate CAP links: (i) discrepancy accumulation across scan slopes; (ii) defect source $\to$ Poisson phase potential $\to$ approximate $1/r$ field.
The corresponding reference implementations are included in Appendix~\ref{app:repro_code}.

\subsection{Experiment A: star discrepancy for Kronecker sequences}
\label{subsec:expA}

We consider the Kronecker orbit
\begin{equation}
  x_n = (x_0 + n\alpha)\bmod 1,
  \qquad x_0=0.12345,
\end{equation}
and compute the one-dimensional star discrepancy $D_N^\ast$ and the accumulated mismatch $E_N=ND_N^\ast$.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:ostrowski_dk_cap} provides a worst-case upper bound on $E_N$ in terms of the continued-fraction proxy $C_m(\alpha)$, and Corollary~\ref{cor:golden_proxy_cap} shows that the golden branch minimizes this proxy at every finite depth. The empirical $E_N$ at a fixed $x_0$ can still fluctuate among bounded-type slopes; the relevant CAP statement is the uniform stability of the bound that governs sustainable readout.

\paragraph{Log-fit diagnostic.}
To quantify the ``slow growth'' trend, we fit a simple finite-range model
\begin{equation}
  E_N \approx A\log N + B
\end{equation}
by least squares on the sampled $N$ values (Table~\ref{tab:EN}).
This fit is not a theorem; it is an operational diagnostic consistent with the $E_N=O(\log N)$ bound for bounded-type slopes (Remark after Corollary~\ref{cor:golden_proxy_cap}).

\subsection{Experiment B: FFT solution of Poisson equation and an approximate \texorpdfstring{$1/r$}{1/r} phase potential}
\label{subsec:expB}

We solve the periodic-grid Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\Phi = 4\pi\rho
\end{equation}
in a three-dimensional periodic box using FFT, with $\rho$ a single lattice-site point source. After solving, we compute radial shell averages of $\Phi$ about the source center.

For a representative run with $n=64$ grid, the near-field behavior is well-approximated by $\Phi(r)\sim -\mathrm{const}/r$ (so $r\langle\Phi\rangle$ is approximately constant) before periodic-boundary effects dominate at larger radii. The script also supports a least-squares fit of the form $\langle\Phi\rangle \approx C_0 - M/r$ over a chosen radius range and reports RMS residuals as a quantitative goodness-of-fit.

\paragraph{$1/r$ fit (representative).}
Using the values in Table~\ref{tab:poisson} and fitting over $r\in\{2,3,4,5,6\}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \langle\Phi\rangle(r) \approx C_0 - \frac{M}{r},
  \qquad
  C_0 \approx 0.016236,\quad M\approx 0.854953,
\end{equation}
with RMS residual $\approx 6.09\times 10^{-3}$ and $R^2\approx 0.9964$ on that fit window. This provides a quantitative confirmation of the near-field $1/r$ behavior expected from the Poisson closure.

In the continuum normalization $\Delta\Phi=4\pi\rho$ with a unit point source $\rho=\delta$, one expects $M=1$ (Green function normalization). The fitted $M\approx 0.855$ is therefore within $\sim 15\%$ of the continuum value, consistent with finite-grid and periodic-image effects at $n=64$.
