\section{Quantitative closure: standard theorems and bounds}
\label{sec:verification_route}

As a closed constitution, the present framework is only as strong as its \emph{quantitative} consequences. Here we record standard theorems (with explicit constants where possible) and the constant-bearing bounds used throughout the paper.

\subsection{From unitary scan to induced measures: equidistribution}
\label{sec:quant_equidistribution}

Under O5/O6 the scan orbit is an irrational rotation. Weyl's equidistribution theorem implies that the orbit induces the Lebesgue measure on $\RR/\ZZ$ \cite{Weyl1916,KuipersNiederreiter1974}; in particular, for any interval window $W$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_W(x_t)\to |W|.
\end{equation}
Thus the limiting ``probabilities'' of window readout are not postulated but induced by the scan dynamics and the chosen finite-resolution window.

\paragraph{Digit statistics from the modular-geodesic coding.}
Section~\ref{sec:modular_geodesic_cf} identifies continued-fraction digits as return-symbols of modular geodesics. The Gauss map admits a canonical invariant measure and a closed-form digit law (Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss_digit_law}), so digit frequencies are quantitatively constrained for typical orbits. Beyond almost-everywhere digit frequencies, the Gauss--Kuzmin theorem provides a finite-time relaxation guarantee: for broad absolutely continuous initial laws, the distribution of $G^n$ converges exponentially fast to the Gauss invariant measure (Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss_kuzmin_convergence}). Metrical continued-fraction theory also provides sharp scaling constants (Section~\ref{sec:ostrowski}).

\paragraph{Finite-$N$ error control.}
For any finite set of scan samples $P_N=\{x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\}$ and any interval window $W$, the Koksma inequality gives an explicit finite-$N$ bound
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_W(x_t)-|W|\right|\le 2\,D_N^\ast(P_N),
\end{equation}
see Appendix~\ref{app:koksma_inequality}. In particular, on convergent lengths the closed-form expressions in Proposition~\ref{prop:convergent_star_discrepancy} turn induced probability into a constant-controlled estimate.
More generally, the Ostrowski/Zeckendorf coding of the tick count $N$ controls discrepancy via digit sums (Appendix~\ref{app:ostrowski_discrepancy}), providing a direct quantitative bridge from canonical coding to finite-$N$ readout accuracy.
More precisely, if $p_n/q_n$ are convergents of $\alpha$ and
\begin{equation}
N=\sum_{n=0}^{m} b_n q_n
\end{equation}
is the Ostrowski expansion of $N$, then Proposition~\ref{prop:ostrowski_birkhoff_bound} gives, for every interval window $W$,
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_W(x_t)-|W|\right|
\le \frac{2}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{m} b_n,
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{equation}
D_N^\ast\!\left(P_N(\alpha)\right)\le \frac{2}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{m} b_n.
\label{eq:ostrowski_discrepancy_main}
\end{equation}
This bound holds for \emph{all} $N\ge 1$ (not only at convergent lengths) and makes the constant explicit.
For the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ at Fibonacci lengths $N=F_n$, Appendix~\ref{app:golden_constant} yields explicit constants: for odd $n$, $D_{F_n}^\ast=1/F_n$ so the window-frequency error is bounded by $2/F_n$; for even $n$,
\begin{equation}
D_{F_n}^\ast=\frac{1}{F_n}+\left(1-\frac{1}{F_n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}\,F_n},
\end{equation}
so the window-frequency error is bounded by $2\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right)\frac{1}{F_n}$ up to the vanishing factor $(1-1/F_n)$.
More generally, choosing $\alpha$ with bounded continued-fraction coefficients (constant type) yields an explicit uniform guarantee:
if $a_n\le A$ for all $n$, then Appendix~\ref{app:ostrowski_discrepancy} gives
\begin{equation}
D_N^\ast\!\left(P_N(\alpha)\right)\le \frac{2A(2+\log_\varphi N)}{N},
\label{eq:bounded_type_discrepancy_main}
\end{equation}
recovering the classical $O((\log N)/N)$ rate with explicit constants. This provides a principled scan-slope selection rule: \emph{bounded partial quotients} give uniformly low discrepancy, and the golden branch ($A=1$) is the canonical extremal choice compatible with Zeckendorf coding.

\subsection{Quantitative discrepancy control and a closed form on convergent lengths}
\label{sec:quant_discrepancy}

The same irrational-rotation model admits quantitative, non-asymptotic control. The Denjoy--Koksma inequality yields uniform bounds at convergent lengths $q_n$ \cite{Denjoy1932,Koksma1935,KatokHasselblatt1995}. More sharply, Proposition~\ref{prop:convergent_star_discrepancy} gives a closed form for the one-dimensional star discrepancy at lengths $q$ satisfying $|\alpha-p/q|<1/q^2$.

For the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$, where the convergents are Fibonacci ratios, this implies an explicit constant:
\begin{equation}
F_n\,D_{F_n}^\ast \to 1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}
\qquad (n\ \text{even}),
\end{equation}
while $F_n\,D_{F_n}^\ast=1$ for odd $n$.

\subsection{Coefficient recovery by scan quadrature: variation bounds and parameter choices}
\label{sec:coeff_recovery_bounds}

The constitution repeatedly uses the cusp interface as a continuous-to-discrete bridge. To make this quantitative at finite $N$, it is useful to treat coefficient extraction as a deterministic quadrature problem along a scan orbit, with a discrepancy-controlled error.

\paragraph{Coefficient recovery at height \texorpdfstring{$y$}{y}.}
Let $f$ be holomorphic on $\HH$ and $T$-periodic, so it admits a $q$-expansion
\begin{equation}
f(\tau)=\sum_{m\ge 0} a_m q^m,\qquad q=\e^{2\pi\iu\tau}.
\end{equation}
For any $y>0$, Fourier inversion at height $y$ gives the exact coefficient formula
\begin{equation}
a_n
=
\e^{2\pi n y}\int_0^1 f(x+\iu y)\,\e^{-2\pi\iu n x}\,\dd x
\qquad (n\ge 0).
\label{eq:coeff_recovery_integral}
\end{equation}
Define the integrand
\begin{equation}
g_{n,y}(x):=f(x+\iu y)\,\e^{-2\pi\iu n x}\qquad (x\in\RR/\ZZ),
\label{eq:def_gny}
\end{equation}
so that $a_n=\e^{2\pi n y}\int_{\RR/\ZZ} g_{n,y}\,\dd x$.

\paragraph{Scan quadrature estimator and a deterministic finite-\texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} bound.}
Given a scan orbit $x_t=x_0+t\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)$, define the scan-quadrature estimator
\begin{equation}
\widehat a_n^{(N)}(y)
:=
\e^{2\pi n y}\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1} g_{n,y}(x_t).
\label{eq:scan_quadrature_estimator}
\end{equation}
If $g_{n,y}$ has bounded variation, then Koksma's inequality (Appendix~\ref{app:koksma_inequality}) yields
\begin{equation}
\left|\widehat a_n^{(N)}(y)-a_n\right|
\le
\e^{2\pi n y}\,\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})\,D_N^\ast\!\left(P_N(\alpha)\right).
\label{eq:coeff_recovery_koksma_bound}
\end{equation}
Thus, for coefficient recovery the quantitative problem reduces to (i) bounding $\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})$ as a function of $y$ and $n$, and (ii) choosing $\alpha,N$ to control $D_N^\ast$.

\begin{proposition}[Uniform variation bound from the \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-expansion]
\label{prop:var_gny_qexp}
Let $f(\tau)=\sum_{m\ge 0} a_m q^m$ and $g_{n,y}$ be defined by \eqref{eq:def_gny}. Then for any $y>0$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})
\le
2\pi\sum_{m\ge 0} |m-n|\,|a_m|\,\e^{-2\pi m y}.
\label{eq:var_gny_series_bound}
\end{equation}
In particular, using $|m-n|\le m+n$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})
\le
2\pi\left(
n\sum_{m\ge 0} |a_m|\,\e^{-2\pi m y}
\;+\;
\sum_{m\ge 0} m\,|a_m|\,\e^{-2\pi m y}
\right).
\label{eq:var_gny_mplusn_bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Writing $\tau=x+\iu y$ gives
\begin{equation}
g_{n,y}(x)=\sum_{m\ge 0} a_m\,\e^{-2\pi m y}\,\e^{2\pi\iu(m-n)x}.
\end{equation}
Termwise differentiation (justified by absolute convergence for fixed $y>0$) yields
\begin{equation}
g'_{n,y}(x)=2\pi\iu\sum_{m\ge 0} (m-n)\,a_m\,\e^{-2\pi m y}\,\e^{2\pi\iu(m-n)x}.
\end{equation}
For absolutely continuous $1$-periodic functions, $\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})\le\int_0^1 |g'_{n,y}(x)|\,\dd x$, and the triangle inequality gives \eqref{eq:var_gny_series_bound}. The bound \eqref{eq:var_gny_mplusn_bound} follows from $|m-n|\le m+n$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{An explicit elementary bound for weighted power sums.}
For integer $s\ge 0$ and $q\in(0,1)$, one has
\begin{equation}
\sum_{m\ge 1} m^s q^m = \frac{q\,P_s(q)}{(1-q)^{s+1}},
\end{equation}
where $P_s$ is the Eulerian polynomial of degree $s$ with nonnegative coefficients and $P_s(1)=s!$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\sum_{m\ge 1} m^s q^m \le \frac{s!\,q}{(1-q)^{s+1}}.
\label{eq:power_sum_bound}
\end{equation}
See, e.g., \cite{GrahamKnuthPatashnik1994} for standard generating-function identities and Eulerian-number properties.
Below we use \eqref{eq:power_sum_bound} with $q=\e^{-2\pi y}$ to turn \eqref{eq:var_gny_series_bound} into explicit $y$- and $n$-dependent bounds.

\paragraph{Eisenstein series (level 1).}
For even $k\ge 4$, the normalized Eisenstein series satisfies
\begin{equation}
E_k(\tau)=1-\frac{2k}{B_k}\sum_{m\ge 1}\sigma_{k-1}(m)\,q^m,
\end{equation}
so $|a_m|\le C_k\,m^{k-1}$ for $m\ge 1$ with
\begin{equation}
C_k:=\left|\frac{2k}{B_k}\right|\zeta(k-1),
\end{equation}
using $\sigma_{k-1}(m)\le \zeta(k-1)\,m^{k-1}$. Applying Proposition~\ref{prop:var_gny_qexp} and \eqref{eq:power_sum_bound} with $q=\e^{-2\pi y}$ gives the uniform bound
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y}^{(E_k)})
\le
2\pi n
+
2\pi C_k\left(
n\frac{(k-1)!\,q}{(1-q)^{k}}
\;+\;
\frac{k!\,q}{(1-q)^{k+1}}
\right),
\qquad q=\e^{-2\pi y}.
\label{eq:var_bound_eisenstein}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Hecke eigen cusp forms (coarse uniform bound).}
Let $f(\tau)=\sum_{m\ge 1} a_m q^m$ be a normalized Hecke eigen cusp form of even weight $k$ (level $1$ for concreteness). Deligne's bound implies $|a_m|\le d(m)\,m^{(k-1)/2}$. Using the trivial divisor bound $d(m)\le m+1\le 2m$ for $m\ge 1$ yields $|a_m|\le 2\,m^{(k+1)/2}$, hence Proposition~\ref{prop:var_gny_qexp} and \eqref{eq:power_sum_bound} give
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y}^{(f)})
\le
4\pi\left(
n\frac{s_1!\,q}{(1-q)^{s_1+1}}
\;+\;
\frac{s_2!\,q}{(1-q)^{s_2+1}}
\right),
\qquad
s_1:=\left\lceil\frac{k+1}{2}\right\rceil,\ \ s_2:=\left\lceil\frac{k+3}{2}\right\rceil,
\label{eq:var_bound_cusp_coarse}
\end{equation}
with $q=\e^{-2\pi y}$. For the discriminant form $\Delta$ of weight $12$, the same bound applies with $s_1=7$, $s_2=8$.

\paragraph{Choosing \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha} and \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} (discrepancy control).}
Given \eqref{eq:coeff_recovery_koksma_bound}, a principled scan-slope choice is to enforce uniformly small $D_N^\ast$. The explicit constant-type bound \eqref{eq:bounded_type_discrepancy_main} shows that bounded partial quotients ($a_n\le A$) guarantee $D_N^\ast=O((\log N)/N)$ with an explicit constant; the golden branch ($A=1$) yields the canonical smallest bound among constant-type choices and also admits exact discrepancy constants on Fibonacci lengths (Appendix~\ref{app:golden_constant}).
For generic $\alpha$ with unbounded partial quotients there is no comparable uniform constant, and $D_N^\ast$ can exhibit spikes at $N$ tied to unusually large continued-fraction digits.

\begin{corollary}[Constant-type design inequality for coefficient recovery]
\label{cor:const_type_design_inequality}
Assume the scan slope $\alpha$ has bounded continued-fraction coefficients $a_j\le A$ for all $j$ (constant type), so that the discrepancy bound \eqref{eq:bounded_type_discrepancy_main} holds.
Then for any $T$-periodic holomorphic $f$ with $q$-expansion and for any $y>0$, the scan-quadrature estimator \eqref{eq:scan_quadrature_estimator} satisfies
\begin{equation}
\left|\widehat a_n^{(N)}(y)-a_n\right|
\le
\e^{2\pi n y}\,\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})\,\frac{2A(2+\log_\varphi N)}{N},
\label{eq:const_type_design_bound}
\end{equation}
where $g_{n,y}$ is defined in \eqref{eq:def_gny}.
In particular, for $f=E_k$ one may upper-bound $\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})$ by \eqref{eq:var_bound_eisenstein}, and for a normalized Hecke eigen cusp form by \eqref{eq:var_bound_cusp_coarse}.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Joint choice of \texorpdfstring{$y$}{y} and \texorpdfstring{$N$}{N} for a target coefficient index \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}.}
The finite-$N$ recovery bound \eqref{eq:coeff_recovery_koksma_bound} combines (i) an amplification factor $\e^{2\pi n y}$ and (ii) a decay factor in $\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})$ driven by $q=\e^{-2\pi y}$. For the polynomial-growth classes above, the small-$y$ asymptotic $q/(1-q)^{s+1}\sim (2\pi y)^{-(s+1)}$ suggests minimizing a proxy of the form $\e^{2\pi n y}\,y^{-p}$, whose optimizer is
\begin{lemma}[exponential--power tradeoff optimizer]
\label{lem:exp_power_tradeoff}
Let $c>0$ and $p>0$. The function $h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)$ given by $h(y)=\e^{c y}\,y^{-p}$ attains its unique minimum at $y=p/c$, and
\begin{equation}
\min_{y>0}\e^{c y}\,y^{-p}=\e^{p}\left(\frac{c}{p}\right)^{p}.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate $\log h(y)=c y-p\log y$ to obtain $(\log h)'(y)=c-p/y$, which vanishes if and only if $y=p/c$. Since $(\log h)''(y)=p/y^2>0$, this critical point is the unique minimizer. Evaluating at $y=p/c$ gives the stated minimum.
\end{proof}
\begin{equation}
y_\star = \frac{p}{2\pi n}.
\label{eq:y_star_rule}
\end{equation}
For Eisenstein series $E_k$, the dominant term in \eqref{eq:var_bound_eisenstein} corresponds to $p=k$; for cusp eigenforms of weight $k$ with the coarse bound \eqref{eq:var_bound_cusp_coarse}, the dominant term corresponds to $p\approx s_1+1\approx \frac{k+3}{2}$.
Once $y$ is fixed, one selects $N$ so that $D_N^\ast(P_N(\alpha))\le \varepsilon/\bigl(\e^{2\pi n y}\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})\bigr)$ for a target tolerance $\varepsilon$, using \eqref{eq:bounded_type_discrepancy_main} (or the exact convergent-length formulas) as an explicit design inequality.

\paragraph{Where R1 enters beyond \texorpdfstring{$|q|$}{|q|} analogy.}
At the operator level, coefficient recovery and discrepancy control both involve regulated orbit sums of the form $\sum_{t\ge 0} U_{\mathrm{scan}}^{\,t}$ applied to mean-zero observables (the cohomological equation for a rotation). The canonical way to make these sums well-defined is precisely Abel/resolvent regularization:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{t\ge 0} r^t\,U_{\mathrm{scan}}^{\,t}=(1-r\,U_{\mathrm{scan}})^{-1},\qquad 0<r<1,
\end{equation}
followed by the controlled limit $r\uparrow 1$ selecting a finite part (R1; Appendix~\ref{app:abel_regularization}). In protocol terms, this is the minimal finite-coherence response kernel used to turn formally divergent infinite-time expressions into auditable finite quantities.

\paragraph{Noise terms (explicit).}
If samples of $g_{n,y}(x_t)$ are corrupted by an additive deterministic error $\eta_t$ with $|\eta_t|\le \delta$ for all $t$, then
\begin{equation}
\left|\widehat a_n^{(N)}(y)-a_n\right|
\le
\e^{2\pi n y}\,\mathrm{Var}(g_{n,y})\,D_N^\ast\!\left(P_N(\alpha)\right)
\;+\;\e^{2\pi n y}\,\delta,
\label{eq:coeff_recovery_with_noise}
\end{equation}
where the first term is the discrepancy-controlled quadrature error and the second is the amplified measurement perturbation.

\paragraph{Wasserstein bounds versus star discrepancy (why we use discrepancy here).}
For Lipschitz test functions $h$ on $\RR/\ZZ$, Kantorovich--Rubinstein duality implies $|\frac{1}{N}\sum h(x_t)-\int h|\le \mathrm{Lip}(h)\,W_1(\mu_N,\lambda)$. In one dimension, $W_1(\mu_N,\lambda)=\int_0^1 |F_N(t)-t|\,\dd t\le D_N^\ast(P_N)$, so a Wasserstein-$1$ bound cannot improve the order of the deterministic discrepancy control unless one has an independent sharp bound on $W_1$ for the specific orbit. Moreover, for discontinuous window readout $\mathbf{1}_W$ the Lipschitz constant is infinite while $\mathrm{Var}(\mathbf{1}_W)=2$, so bounded-variation/discrepancy estimates are the natural quantitative language for the scan--projection protocol.

\subsection{Hecke rigidity in coefficients: recursion, multiplicativity, and Deligne bounds}
\label{sec:quant_hecke_tau}

For a normalized Hecke eigenform, Hecke commutativity and eigen-structure force strong arithmetic constraints on $q$-coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:eigenforms}). For the discriminant cusp form $\Delta$ of weight $12$, the coefficients $\tau(n)$ are Hecke eigenvalues and satisfy multiplicativity on coprime indices and prime-power recursions (standard; see \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}). In addition, Deligne's theorem implies the sharp Ramanujan--Petersson bound
\begin{equation}
|\tau(p)|\le 2p^{11/2}\qquad (p\ \text{prime}),
\end{equation}
see Appendix~\ref{app:deligne_bound}.
For completeness, Appendix~\ref{app:tau_global_bound} derives a standard global growth bound for all integers $n$ from Deligne's prime bound and the prime-power Hecke recursion:
\begin{equation}
|\tau(n)|\le d(n)\,n^{11/2}.
\end{equation}
\par
Finally, the Euler-product identity for $L(\Delta,s)$ and its absolute convergence for $\Re(s)>13/2$ are standard consequences of Hecke theory (see, e.g., \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}). Beyond pointwise bounds, mature distribution results constrain normalized prime coefficients. In the non-CM case, Sato--Tate predicts a semicircle law for the normalized values $a_p/(2p^{(k-1)/2})$; see \cite{Clozel2006SatoTate,Harris2014SatoTate,BarnetLambGeeGeraghty2011} for background and broad theorem statements.
\par
Beyond coefficient statistics, there are also deep equidistribution theorems for \emph{Hecke eigenfunctions} on the modular surface (quantum unique ergodicity, and effective variants). Such results are directly relevant whenever one appeals to ``stability under Hecke dynamics'' or to induced measures associated with Hecke eigenbases; see, e.g., \cite{BisainHumphriesMandelstamWalshWang2024EffectiveQUE} for an effective QUE statement for Hecke--Maa\ss\ cusp forms on $\SL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\SL_2(\RR)$.

\paragraph{How many coefficients determine an eigenform? Sturm bounds and prime reduction.}
A practical quantitative consequence of Hecke rigidity is that \emph{finitely many} coefficients determine a modular form. Sturm's theorem gives an explicit cutoff: if $f,g\in M_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ satisfy $a_n(f)=a_n(g)$ for all $1\le n\le B_{k,N}$, where
\begin{equation}
B_{k,N}:=\left\lfloor \frac{k}{12}\,[\SL_2(\ZZ):\Gamma_0(N)]\right\rfloor,
\end{equation}
then $f=g$ \cite{Sturm1987,DiamondShurman2005}. In particular, for level~$1$ one has $[\SL_2(\ZZ):\Gamma_0(1)]=1$, so matching coefficients up to $\lfloor k/12\rfloor$ is enough to identify a form.
\par
For normalized Hecke eigenforms, the Hecke relations further reduce the amount of \emph{independent} data needed: knowing the prime eigenvalues $a_p$ for primes $p\le B_{k,N}$ determines $a_{p^r}$ by the prime-power recursion and then determines all $a_n$ with $n\le B_{k,N}$ by multiplicativity. Thus one can, in principle, reconstruct (or validate) an eigenform up to the Sturm bound using only as many prime-indexed constraints as there are primes $p\le B_{k,N}$, providing a concrete quantitative meaning of the ``prime skeleton'' as a compression mechanism.

\subsection{Modular invariance of \texorpdfstring{$j$}{j} and truncation error control}
\label{sec:quant_j_invariant}

The $j$-invariant is $\PSL_2(\ZZ)$-invariant by construction; in particular, $j(\tau)=j(-1/\tau)$ \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990}. When $E_4,E_6$ are evaluated via truncated $q$-series (absolutely convergent for $|q|<1$), truncation errors admit explicit tail bounds that decay essentially geometrically in the truncation depth $N$ at fixed $\tau$ (Appendix~\ref{app:eisenstein_tail_bounds}). Moreover, Appendix~\ref{app:j_truncation_bounds} gives a certified truncation-only error propagation bound for the induced $j$-invariant error. Since
\begin{equation}
j(\tau)=1728\,\frac{E_4(\tau)^3}{E_4(\tau)^3-E_6(\tau)^2}=\frac{E_4(\tau)^3}{\Delta(\tau)},
\end{equation}
numerical sensitivity is amplified near the cusp where $|\Delta(\tau)|$ is small, so any finite truncation or finite-precision evaluation should be interpreted through explicit tail bounds and stability estimates.

\subsection{A worked end-to-end instance: scan, window readout, cusp evaluation, Hecke checks, and coding}
\label{sec:worked_end_to_end_instance}

This subsection records one explicit ``toy instance'' that runs through the full constitution chain with no interpretive steps: a concrete scan model, a concrete finite-resolution instrument, an explicit cusp evaluation of standard modular objects with certified truncation control, prime-skeleton checks on coefficient data, and a canonical tick-time coding.

\paragraph{Step 1: a covariant scan model and an effective observer state.}
Use the canonical covariant model of O6 on $L^2(\RR/\ZZ)$ (Section~\ref{sec:axioms_O5_O6}): $(U_{\mathrm{scan}}\psi)(x)=\psi(x+\alpha)$, $(V\psi)(x)=\e^{2\pi\iu x}\psi(x)$ with $\alpha\notin\QQ$.
For the commutative subalgebra generated by functions of the phase $x$, fix an effective observer state given by Lebesgue integration,
\begin{equation}
\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(g)=\int_{\RR/\ZZ} g(x)\,\dd x,
\end{equation}
which is the invariant state naturally induced by unique ergodicity of irrational rotation.

\paragraph{Step 2: an explicit finite-resolution instrument (POVM by interval windows).}
Fix an integer $K\ge 1$ (resolution $\varepsilon:=1/K$) and partition $\RR/\ZZ$ into $K$ disjoint intervals
\begin{equation}
I_k^{(\varepsilon)}=[k\varepsilon,(k+1)\varepsilon)\qquad (k=0,\ldots,K-1),
\end{equation}
and define effects $E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\mathbf{1}_{I_k^{(\varepsilon)}}$ (multiplication operators in the $x$-representation). Then
\begin{equation}
P_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\left(E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\right)=\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
so the protocol-level limiting probabilities are fixed by the geometry of the window family, not postulated.
For a finite sample of $N$ scan ticks, the empirical frequencies satisfy the constant-controlled bound
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_{I_k^{(\varepsilon)}}(x_t)-\varepsilon\right|
\le 2\,D_N^\ast(P_N),
\end{equation}
by Koksma (Appendix~\ref{app:koksma_inequality}), with explicit closed forms at convergent lengths (Proposition~\ref{prop:convergent_star_discrepancy}).

\paragraph{Step 3: a canonical tick length and coding choice.}
Specialize to the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ and take a Fibonacci length $N=F_n$.
Then Appendix~\ref{app:golden_constant} supplies explicit constants for $D_{F_n}^\ast$, turning the scan--projection statistics into an auditable finite-$N$ estimate.
The same choice aligns with Zeckendorf coding (Section~\ref{sec:zeckendorf}): $N$ admits a unique Fibonacci-digit representation with a locally checkable admissibility rule (no adjacent $1$'s), and the corresponding digit sums control discrepancy via Appendix~\ref{app:ostrowski_discrepancy}.

\paragraph{Step 4: cusp evaluation with certified truncation control.}
Fix an imaginary part $y>0$ and define a modular coordinate along the scan by
\begin{equation}
\tau_t := x_t+\iu y,\qquad q_t := \e^{2\pi\iu\tau_t}=\e^{-2\pi y}\,\e^{2\pi\iu x_t}.
\end{equation}
Evaluate $E_4(\tau_t),E_6(\tau_t)$ by truncated $q$-series (truncation depth $M$) and form the induced approximation $j^{(M)}(\tau_t)$.
Appendix~\ref{app:eisenstein_tail_bounds} and Appendix~\ref{app:j_truncation_bounds} provide certified truncation-only error bounds, so modular invariance checks such as
\begin{equation}
j(\tau_t)=j(-1/\tau_t)
\end{equation}
can be turned into finite-truncation numerical audits with explicit stability budgets (Section~\ref{sec:quant_j_invariant}).

\paragraph{Step 5: prime-skeleton checks on coefficient data.}
For the discriminant form $\Delta(q)=\sum_{n\ge 1}\tau(n)\,q^n$, Hecke eigen-structure implies exact arithmetic constraints:
multiplicativity on coprime indices and the prime-power recursion
\begin{equation}
\tau(p^{r+1})=\tau(p)\,\tau(p^r)-p^{11}\tau(p^{r-1})\qquad (r\ge 1),
\end{equation}
see Section~\ref{sec:quant_hecke_tau}.
As concrete consistency checks (all exact integer identities),
\begin{equation}
\tau(6)=\tau(2)\tau(3)=(-24)(252)=-6048,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\tau(4)=\tau(2)^2-2^{11}=576-2048=-1472,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}
In any model where discrete readout is hypothesized to expose coefficient data of a Hecke eigenform (even after coarse-graining), such relations supply sharp, prime-indexed cross-scale constraints that can be audited at finite depth.

\subsection{Discrimination tasks, noise models, and explicit error budgets (conditional falsifiability)}
\label{sec:discrimination_tasks}

This paper is a constitution rather than a complete laboratory theory, so ``falsifiable'' must be read in the conditional sense: once one posits a concrete lab-to-protocol mapping (what plays the role of the pointer phase $x$, what instrument family realizes $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$, and how coarse-graining is implemented), the protocol makes checkable quantitative claims that can fail on data.

\paragraph{A minimal set of discrimination tasks.}
The following tests are designed to discriminate structured scan--projection readout from generic stochastic readout models:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Window statistics with constant-controlled finite-$N$ error.}
  For chosen windows $W$ (or a window partition), compare empirical frequencies to the induced limiting probabilities, using discrepancy bounds (Section~\ref{sec:quant_equidistribution}--\ref{sec:quant_discrepancy}). The point is not merely convergence but the availability of explicit constants at special lengths (e.g.\ Fibonacci lengths on the golden branch).

  \item \textbf{Sturmian/low-complexity signatures for binary thresholds.}
  For a binary readout $s_t=\mathbf{1}_W(x_t)$, the irrational-rotation protocol yields a Sturmian mechanical word (Section~\ref{sec:sturmian_words}) with rigid combinatorial constraints (balance, minimal complexity). Generic i.i.d.\ noise does not reproduce these constraints except in degenerate limits.

  \item \textbf{Cross-resolution consistency under canonical coarse-graining.}
  If an experimental knob changes resolution by an integer factor (e.g.\ aperture/threshold coarsening), then protocol-level coarse-graining predicts compatibility relations between fine and coarse statistics. On the boundary, a canonical model is the fiber-average operator $\mathcal{C}_n$ of \eqref{eq:boundary_coarse_grain}; on the modular stage, the corresponding symmetry-preserving cross-scale family is organized by Hecke operators (Section~\ref{sec:hecke_protocol_origin}).

  \item \textbf{Prime-skeleton constraints when coefficient data is claimed.}
  In any model that identifies readout with coefficient data of a Hecke eigenform (even approximately), multiplicativity and prime-power recursions supply stringent internal consistency checks (Section~\ref{sec:quant_hecke_tau}). These constraints are sharply discriminative: they are not generic properties of arbitrary integer sequences.

  \item \textbf{Protocol fitting and audit-ready parameter extraction.}
  Once a concrete lab-to-protocol mapping is specified (what is $x$, what family realizes the windows), one can fit protocol parameters with deterministic finite-$N$ guarantees and test Sturmian/three-gap/Hecke signatures as falsifiers; see Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_fitting}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{A quantitative noise/decoherence interface.}
At Layer~1, noise enters by deforming either the effective state $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}$ or the effects $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$. In the Heisenberg picture, one may represent an instrument/noise channel by a completely positive unital map $\mathcal{N}$ on the observer algebra and write
\begin{equation}
P_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\left(\mathcal{N}\!\left(E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\right)\right).
\label{eq:noise_channel_effects}
\end{equation}
Two canonical toy deformations capture common laboratory imperfections:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Finite aperture / window smoothing.}
  Replace a sharp window $\mathbf{1}_W$ by a smoothed kernel $w_{W,\sigma}=\kappa_\sigma * \mathbf{1}_W$ (convolution on $\RR/\ZZ$). This models finite spatial/phase resolution. In discrepancy bounds, the variation $V(\mathbf{1}_W)=2$ is replaced by $V(w_{W,\sigma})\le 2$, improving finite-$N$ constants when the aperture is smooth.

  \item \textbf{Finite coherence / exponential memory.}
  Replace uniform time-averages by Abel-weighted averages $(1-r)\sum_{t\ge 0} r^t(\cdot)$, which is the canonical resolvent regularization of the scan dynamics (R1 and Appendix~\ref{app:abel_regularization}). This models a causal finite-memory response with exponential forgetting. The limit $r\uparrow 1$ is an idealized infinite-coherence limit.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Error-budget template.}
In a commutative phase-window model, an auditable total error budget takes the additive form
\begin{equation}
\Bigl|\widehat P_W - P_W\Bigr|
\;\le\;
V(w_{W,\sigma})\,D_N^\ast(P_N)
\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{instr}}(\sigma)
\;+\;\delta_{\mathrm{num}}(M,\tau),
\label{eq:error_budget_template}
\end{equation}
where the first term is the deterministic finite-$N$ discrepancy contribution, $\delta_{\mathrm{instr}}$ collects instrument-model mismatch (e.g.\ calibration error beyond the smoothing kernel), and $\delta_{\mathrm{num}}$ is a certified truncation/precision term when modular objects are evaluated via finite $q$-series depth $M$ (Appendix~\ref{app:eisenstein_tail_bounds} and Appendix~\ref{app:j_truncation_bounds}). The key point is methodological: each contribution can be made explicit and independently audited.
