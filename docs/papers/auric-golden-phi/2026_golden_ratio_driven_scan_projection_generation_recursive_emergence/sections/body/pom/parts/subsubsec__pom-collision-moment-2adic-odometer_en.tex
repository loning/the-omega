\begin{conclusion}[The \(2\)-adic Odometer Law for \texorpdfstring{$q=4$}{q=4}: From Constant Modulo \texorpdfstring{$2^6$}{64} to Exact Doubling Periods]\label{con:pom-s4-2adic-odometer}
Let \(S_4(m):=\sum_{x\in X_m} d_m(x)^4\).
Then \(S_4\) exhibits the following completely rigid \(2\)-adic behavior:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For all \(m\ge 16\), one has \(S_4(m)\equiv 2^5\pmod{2^6}\), i.e., \(v_2(S_4(m))=5\) and \(S_4(m)\equiv 32\pmod{64}\).
  \item For any integer \(k\ge 7\), the sequence \(m\mapsto S_4(m)\bmod 2^k\) enters a pure-periodic phase after the threshold \(m\ge 3k-2\); its minimal period is \(2^{\max(1,k-7)}\).
  In particular, when \(k\ge 9\) the minimal period is exactly \(2^{k-7}\), and the number of distinct residue classes in this periodic orbit is also \(2^{k-7}\).
  \item Consequently, in the doubling-stable regime \(k\ge 9\), the \(2\)-adic fingerprint for \(q=4\) forms a strict additive odometer: each increment of \(1\) in \(2\)-adic precision (from \(2^k\) to \(2^{k+1}\)) exactly doubles the period in the stable phase (from \(2^{k-7}\) to \(2^{k-6}\)), and the length increment required to enter the stable phase is constant at \(3\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}

\end{proof}
