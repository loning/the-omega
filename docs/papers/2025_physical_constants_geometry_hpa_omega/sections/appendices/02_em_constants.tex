\section{Relations among electromagnetic constants (revised SI)}
\label{app:em_constants}

Starting from
\begin{equation}
\alpha=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\epsilon_0\hbar c},
\end{equation}
and using
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_0=\frac{1}{\mu_0 c^2},\qquad \hbar=\frac{h}{2\pi},
\end{equation}
we obtain
\begin{align}
\alpha
&=\frac{e^2}{4\pi\,(1/(\mu_0 c^2))\,(h/(2\pi))\,c}
=\frac{\mu_0 e^2 c}{2h}.
\end{align}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\mu_0=\frac{2\alpha h}{e^2 c}.
\end{equation}
In the revised SI, $c,h,e$ are defining constants while $\alpha$ is experimentally determined, so the uncertainty of $\mu_0$ is controlled by that of $\alpha$ \cite{BIPMSI2019,NISTMu0RevisedSI,CODATA2022RMP}.

We also record
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_0=\frac{1}{\mu_0 c^2},\qquad Z_0=\sqrt{\frac{\mu_0}{\epsilon_0}}=\mu_0 c.
\end{equation}
