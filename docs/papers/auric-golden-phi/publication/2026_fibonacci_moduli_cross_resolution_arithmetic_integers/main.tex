\begin{abstract}
For each $n\ge 2$, let $B_n$ be the set of divisors $q$ of $F_n$ with
rank of apparition $\alpha(q)=n$. We study $B_n$ as a fiber of the
order-of-apparition map inside the divisor lattice of $F_n$. We show that each
birth layer is an upper fiber obtained by removing the covering ideals
associated with the maximal proper divisors of $n$, so it is controlled by its
minimal generators. Our main theorem classifies those minimal generators by
pairwise coprime witness covers built from atomic prime-power birth moduli.
From this we deduce the bound $\omega(m)\le \omega(n)$ for every minimal
generator $m$, a primitive-versus-ladder dichotomy for effective atomic
factors, an exact description of the case $\omega(n)=2$, and an explicit list
of the admissible witness-cover types when $\omega(n)=3$. As secondary
consequences we obtain canonical principal intervals and Boolean subposets in
nonexceptional birth layers, together with the counting identities furnished by
Stroinski's $\alpha$-contraction formalism and a supplementary quotient-ring
interpretation.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Fibonacci numbers, rank of apparition, divisor

\section{Introduction}

Let $\alpha(q)$ denote the rank of apparition of an integer $q\ge 2$ in the
Fibonacci sequence, that is, the least index $n$ for which $q\mid F_n$. For
each $n\ge 2$, write
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\qquad
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\}.
\]
We study $B_n$ as an ordered subset of $D_n$. The point of view is simple:
rather than asking only how many moduli are born for the first time at level
$n$, we ask how the whole birth layer sits inside the divisor lattice of
$F_n$, how it is generated as an upper set, and what kind of internal
combinatorics it carries.

Three classical facts drive the geometry. First, Wall and Vinson show that
\[
q\mid F_m\iff \alpha(q)\mid m
\]
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm,Vinson1963RankApparition}. Second, the Fibonacci
sequence is a strong divisibility sequence:
\[
\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}.
\]
Third, ranks of apparition respect least common multiples:
\[
\alpha(\lcmop(u,v))=\lcmop(\alpha(u),\alpha(v)).
\]
Primitive divisor theorems then guarantee genuinely new primes at every
nonexceptional index $n\ge 3$, $n\notin\{6,12\}$
\cite{Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas},
while the classical prime-power formulas of Fulton--Morris, Jarden, and
Lengyel control the $p$-primary side
\cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions,Jarden1966RecurringSequences,Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}.

This point of view is different from several nearby strands in the literature.
Stroinski introduced the $\alpha$-contraction of an arithmetic function and
proved a Dirichlet-product identity that already subsumes the usual M\"obius
counting formulas for first appearances \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}.
Accordingly, Section~3 treats those formulas as corollaries rather than as
primary new results. Luca and Pomerance study the local behavior of the order
of appearance under additive shifts \cite{LucaPomerance2014LocalBehavior};
Trojovsk\'a and FitzGibbons--Javaheri--Miller--Verga study periodic points,
fixed points, and iteration dynamics of the self-map $z:\NN\to\NN$
\cite{Trojovska2020PeriodicPoints,FitzGibbonsJavaheriMillerVerga2025Dynamics};
and Cameron, Counts, Lund, Miller, Piechnik, and Wong study fibotomic
polynomials and related algebraic factorizations
\cite{CameronCountsLundMillerPiechnikWong2022Fibotomic}. The present paper
instead fixes a single fiber $B_n$ and studies its divisibility geometry inside
$D_n$. We are not aware of earlier work that formulates these fibers as upper
sets in divisor lattices or that gives the two-support and three-support
classifications proved here.

A quotient-ring model in terms of Fibonacci words is recalled in
Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}; it is useful for the auxiliary threshold and
readout questions, but the main line of the paper is lattice-theoretic rather
than automata-theoretic.

\subsection*{Main results}

Our first result identifies each birth layer as an upper fiber in the divisor
lattice. Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber-cover-ideal} shows that
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus \bigcup_{p\mid n} D_{n/p},
\]
so $B_n$ is obtained by removing the covering ideals corresponding to the
maximal proper divisors of $n$. It follows in
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators} that $B_n$ is an upper set, determined by
its minimal generators $\mathcal M_n$ through the interval decomposition
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}[m,F_n].
\]

The main structural theorem is
Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification}. It classifies the minimal
generators in terms of pairwise coprime witness covers built from atomic
prime-power birth moduli. As a first consequence we obtain the general support
bound
\[
\omega(m)\le \omega(n)
\qquad
(m\in\mathcal M_n),
\]
recorded in Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound}.

Section~\ref{sec:atomic-ladders} makes this classification effective by
combining the witness-cover formalism with the classical prime-power formulas.
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy} shows that every effective atomic
factor is of exactly one of two types: either it is primitive, in the sense
that lowering the prime-power exponent destroys the apparition index entirely,
or it lies on a genuine prime-power ladder and changes exactly one prime
coordinate. This dichotomy is the bridge from the local $p$-primary formulas
to the global witness-cover geometry.

The same section sharpens the general witness-cover theorem in the first
nontrivial support cases. Theorem~\ref{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
describes the extremal case $\omega(m)=\omega(n)$, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification} gives an exact
if-and-only-if classification of the layers with $\omega(n)=2$. For
$\omega(n)=3$, Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-coordinate-types} gives the
complete abstract type table, Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}
shows that exactly one abstract type is excluded in the Fibonacci setting, and
Corollary~\ref{cor:three-coordinate-realization} reduces the realization
problem to finitely many primitive and ladder atomic families.

Several secondary consequences are included as well. Primitive divisors yield
canonical principal intervals inside nonexceptional birth layers
(Corollary~\ref{thm:canonical-principal-interval}), and the same input produces
large Boolean subposets and quantitative lower bounds for the size and width of
$B_n$ (Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton} and
Theorem~\ref{thm:birth-layer-maximal-order}). On the more classical side,
Section~3 records the counting identities furnished by Stroinski's
$\alpha$-contraction formalism, while Section~6 gives a brief quotient-ring
interpretation of the same structures.

\subsection*{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} collects the background on Fibonacci
divisibility and recalls the quotient-ring model based on Fibonacci words.
Section~3 records the quotient-threshold criterion and the counting corollaries
coming from $\alpha$-contraction. Section~4 develops the upper-fiber and
witness-cover theory in general. Section~\ref{sec:atomic-ladders} treats the
prime-power input and the effective classifications for $\omega(n)=2$ and
$\omega(n)=3$. Section~6 contains a short supplementary quotient-ring
perspective, and Section~7 concludes with a few further questions.

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

For each $m\ge 1$, we continue to write
\[
X_m=\{(w_1,\dots,w_m)\in\{0,1\}^m:\ w_iw_{i+1}=0\text{ for }1\le i<m\}
\]
and
\[
V_m(w_1,\dots,w_m)=\sum_{k=1}^m w_kF_{k+1}.
\]

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection}
For every $m\ge 1$, the map $V_m$ is a bijection from $X_m$ onto
$\{0,1,\dots,F_{m+2}-1\}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
This is the finite-range form of Zeckendorf's theorem \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.
Every integer $N<F_{m+2}$ has a unique Zeckendorf expansion whose largest
nonzero digit occurs at a position at most $m$, and this expansion is exactly a
word in $X_m$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:finite-resolution-arithmetic}
For $w,v\in X_m$, define
\[
w\boxplus_m v:=w_m\bigl((V_m(w)+V_m(v))\bmod F_{m+2}\bigr),
\]
\[
w\boxtimes_m v:=w_m\bigl((V_m(w)V_m(v))\bmod F_{m+2}\bigr),
\]
where $w_m(N)$ denotes the unique word in $X_m$ with value
$N\in\{0,1,\dots,F_{m+2}-1\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{thm:finite-resolution-mod}
For every $m\ge 1$, the value map gives a ring isomorphism
\[
V_m:(X_m,\boxplus_m,\boxtimes_m)\longrightarrow \ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection}, $V_m$ is a bijection between
$X_m$ and a complete residue system modulo $F_{m+2}$. By construction,
\[
V_m(w\boxplus_m v)\equiv V_m(w)+V_m(v)\pmod{F_{m+2}}
\]
and
\[
V_m(w\boxtimes_m v)\equiv V_m(w)V_m(v)\pmod{F_{m+2}}.
\]
Thus $V_m$ is a bijective ring homomorphism.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:phase-trichotomy}
Let $N:=F_{m+2}$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item If $N$ is prime, then $X_m$ is a field.
\item If $N$ is a prime power of exponent at least $2$, then $X_m$ is a local
ring.
\item If $N$ has at least two distinct prime factors, then $X_m$ splits as a
nontrivial product of residue rings by the Chinese remainder theorem.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is immediate from Proposition~\ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod} and the
corresponding structure theory of $\ZZ/(N\ZZ)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:rank-of-apparition}
For an integer $q\ge 2$, the \emph{rank of apparition} $\alpha(q)$ is the least
positive integer $n$ such that $q\mid F_n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}
Let $q\ge 2$ and $n\ge 1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item One has $q\mid F_n$ if and only if $\alpha(q)\mid n$.
\item For any finite family $q_1,\dots,q_s\ge 2$,
\[
\alpha(\lcmop(q_1,\dots,q_s))=\lcmop(\alpha(q_1),\dots,\alpha(q_s)).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Part (1) is classical; see Wall \cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm} and Vinson
\cite{Vinson1963RankApparition}. For part (2), write $Q=\lcmop(q_1,\dots,q_s)$.
Then for every $n\ge 1$,
\[
Q\mid F_n
\iff
q_i\mid F_n\ \text{for all }i
\iff
\alpha(q_i)\mid n\ \text{for all }i
\iff
\lcmop(\alpha(q_1),\dots,\alpha(q_s))\mid n.
\]
Applying the minimality characterization in part (1) gives the formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:fibonacci-strong-divisibility}
For all integers $a,b\ge 1$,
\[
\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}.
\]
Consequently,
\[
F_a\mid F_b \iff a\mid b.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The strong divisibility identity is classical; see, for example, Wall
\cite{Wall1960FibonacciModm}. If $a\mid b$, then
$\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_a$, so $F_a\mid F_b$. Conversely, if $F_a\mid F_b$, then
\[
F_a=\gcd(F_a,F_b)=F_{\gcd(a,b)}.
\]
Since the Fibonacci sequence is strictly increasing for indices at least $2$,
one gets $a=\gcd(a,b)$ whenever $a\ge 2$, hence $a\mid b$. The case $a=1$ is
trivial.
\end{proof}

\section{Quotient thresholds and counting corollaries}

We record here the quotient-threshold criterion and the standard counting
identities that come from Stroinski's $\alpha$-contraction formalism. They form
part of the arithmetic background for the later sections.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:quotient-observable-threshold}
Let $m\ge 1$ and $q\ge 2$. The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item There exists a unit-preserving quotient
\[
X_m\twoheadrightarrow \ZZ/(q\ZZ).
\]
\item $q\mid F_{m+2}$.
\item $\alpha(q)\mid (m+2)$.
\end{enumerate}
When these conditions hold, the quotient is unique. In particular,
\[
m_{\min}(q)=\alpha(q)-2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}, giving a unit-preserving
quotient on $X_m$ is equivalent to giving a unit-preserving surjective ring
homomorphism
\[
\widetilde{\pi}:\ZZ/(F_{m+2}\ZZ)\twoheadrightarrow \ZZ/(q\ZZ).
\]
Such a map, if it exists, is necessarily the reduction map
$\overline{t}\mapsto [t]_q$, and it is well defined and surjective exactly when
$q\mid F_{m+2}$. This proves (1) $\Leftrightarrow$ (2). The equivalence of
(2) and (3) is Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1) with
$n=m+2$. The minimality statement is the case $n=\alpha(q)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:birth-spectra}
For each $n\ge 2$, define
\[
A(n):=\#\{q\ge 2:\ \alpha(q)=n\},
\]
\[
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(n):=\#\{p\text{ prime}:\ \alpha(p)=n\},
\]
\[
A_{\mathrm{pp}}(n):=\#\{p^r:\ p\text{ prime},\ r\ge 1,\ \alpha(p^r)=n\}.
\]
These symbols always denote cardinalities. To avoid any collision with this
counting notation, the atomic prime-power sets introduced in Section~4 are
written as $\Atom(d)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:alpha-contraction}
Let $f:\NN\to \mathbb{C}$ be an arithmetic function with $f(1)=0$. Its
\emph{$\alpha$-contraction} is the arithmetic function
\[
f_\alpha(n):=\sum_{\alpha(m)=n} f(m).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:stroinski-alpha-contraction}
Let $f:\NN\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $f(1)=0$. Then for every $n\ge 1$,
\[
\sum_{d\mid F_n} f(d)=\sum_{d\mid n} f_\alpha(d).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
(1*f)(F_n)=(1*f_\alpha)(n).
\]
This is the Fibonacci-specialized form of Stroinski's $\alpha$-contraction
identity \cite{Stroinski2016AlphaContraction}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Using Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1), one has
\[
\sum_{d\mid F_n} f(d)
=
\sum_{\substack{m\ge 2\\ \alpha(m)\mid n}} f(m)
=
\sum_{d\mid n}\ \sum_{\alpha(m)=d} f(m)
=
\sum_{d\mid n} f_\alpha(d).
\]
The Dirichlet-convolution form is merely a rephrasing of the same identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{thm:birth-spectrum-mobius}
For every $n\ge 2$,
\[
A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\tau(F_d),
\]
\[
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\omega(F_d),
\]
\[
A_{\mathrm{pp}}(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\Omega(F_d).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Take $f$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:stroinski-alpha-contraction} to be the
indicator of the set of integers at least $2$. Then
\[
\tau(F_n)-1=\sum_{d\mid n} A(d),
\]
because $f_\alpha(d)=A(d)$. M\"obius inversion yields
\[
A(n)=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu\!\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\bigl(\tau(F_d)-1\bigr).
\]
Since $\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)=0$ for $n\ge 2$, this simplifies to the first
displayed formula.

If $f$ is the indicator of the primes, then
\[
\omega(F_n)=\sum_{d\mid n} A_{\mathrm{pr}}(d),
\]
and if $f$ is the indicator of the prime powers, then
\[
\Omega(F_n)=\sum_{d\mid n} A_{\mathrm{pp}}(d).
\]
Applying M\"obius inversion to these two identities gives the remaining
formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:birth-spectrum-exceptional}
The exceptional indices $6$ and $12$ satisfy
\[
A_{\mathrm{pr}}(6)=A_{\mathrm{pr}}(12)=0,
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{pp}}(6)=A_{\mathrm{pp}}(12)=2.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $F_6=8$, the only prime divisor is $2$, and $\alpha(2)=3$, so no new
prime field is born at $6$. On the other hand, $\alpha(4)=\alpha(8)=6$, hence
$A_{\mathrm{pp}}(6)=2$.

Likewise $F_{12}=144=2^4\cdot 3^2$. Its prime divisors are $2$ and $3$, with
$\alpha(2)=3$ and $\alpha(3)=4$, so $A_{\mathrm{pr}}(12)=0$. The new prime
powers are $9$ and $16$, because $\alpha(9)=12$ and $\alpha(16)=12$, whereas
$\alpha(4)=\alpha(8)=6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:primitive-field-quotients}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, there exists a prime $p_n$ such
that $\alpha(p_n)=n$. Equivalently, for every $m\ge 1$ with $m\notin\{4,10\}$,
the ring $X_m$ admits a quotient onto a prime field that does not factor
through any smaller depth.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $n=3,4,5$ one may take $p_3=2$, $p_4=3$, and $p_5=5$. For $n\ge 7$ with
$n\neq 12$, the primitive divisor theorem for Fibonacci numbers
\cite{Yabuta2001PrimitiveDivisorFibonacci,BiluHanrotVoutier2001PrimitiveDivisorsLucas}
gives a prime $p_n$ dividing $F_n$ but no earlier Fibonacci number $F_d$ with
$1\le d<n$. By definition of rank of apparition, $\alpha(p_n)$ is the least
index at which $p_n$ appears. Since $\alpha(p_n)\mid n$ and $p_n$ divides no
earlier $F_d$, necessarily $\alpha(p_n)=n$.

The equivalent statement for resolutions follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient-observable-threshold}: $X_{n-2}$ admits a quotient
onto $\ZZ/(p_n\ZZ)$, and any factorization through $X_k$ with $k<n-2$ would
force $p_n\mid F_{k+2}$, hence $\alpha(p_n)\le k+2<n$, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\section{Upper fibers and witness covers}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:birth-layer-poset}
For each $n\ge 2$, let
\[
D_n:=\operatorname{Div}(F_n),
\]
and define the \emph{birth layer}
\[
B_n:=\{q\in D_n:\ \alpha(q)=n\}.
\]
Ordered by divisibility, $B_n$ is a finite poset. Its width, namely the
cardinality of a largest antichain, is denoted by $\widthop(B_n)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:upper-fiber-cover-ideal}
For every $n\ge 2$,
\[
B_n=D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n} D_{n/p}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $q\in B_n$, then $\alpha(q)=n$, so $q\nmid F_{n/p}$ for every prime
$p\mid n$; otherwise Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1) would
give $n=\alpha(q)\mid n/p<n$, a contradiction. Hence
$q\in D_n\setminus\bigcup_{p\mid n} D_{n/p}$.

Conversely, let $q\in D_n$ and assume that $q\nmid F_{n/p}$ for every prime
$p\mid n$. Since $q\mid F_n$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1)
gives $d:=\alpha(q)\mid n$. If $d<n$, then $n/d>1$, so choose a prime
$p\mid n/d$. Then $d\mid n/p$, hence $q\mid F_d\mid F_{n/p}$, contradicting
the assumption. Therefore $d=n$ and $q\in B_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:upper-fiber-inclusion-exclusion}
Let $\mathcal P(n)$ be the set of prime divisors of $n$, and let
$f:\NN\to\mathbb{C}$ satisfy $f(1)=0$. Then
\[
\sum_{q\in B_n} f(q)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq \mathcal P(n)}
(-1)^{|S|}
\sum_{q\mid F_{\,n/\prod_{p\in S}p}} f(q).
\]
In particular, Corollary~\ref{thm:birth-spectrum-mobius} is an
inclusion--exclusion specialization of the upper-fiber decomposition.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:upper-fiber-cover-ideal}, the set $B_n$ is the complement
in $D_n$ of the finite union $\bigcup_{p\mid n} D_{n/p}$. Applying
inclusion--exclusion to this union yields the stated formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:minimal-generators}
Let $\mathcal M_n:=\min(B_n)$ be the set of minimal elements of $B_n$ under
divisibility. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$;
\item $F_n$ is the unique maximal element of $B_n$;
\item one has
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}[m,F_n],
\qquad
[m,F_n]:=\{d\in D_n:\ m\mid d\};
\]
\item for $q\in D_n$,
\[
q\in\mathcal M_n
\iff
\alpha(q)=n\ \text{and}\ \alpha(q/p)<n\ \text{for every prime }p\mid q.
\]
\end{enumerate}
If $q=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{e_i}$ and we write
\[
\lambda_i:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i}),
\qquad
\lambda_i^-:=\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})
\]
with the convention $\alpha(1)=1$, then $q\in\mathcal M_n$ if and only if
\[
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r)=n
\]
and, for every $i$,
\[
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\dots,\lambda_r)<n.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If $q\in B_n$ and $q\mid d\mid F_n$, then $d\mid F_n$ gives $\alpha(d)\mid n$,
while $q\mid d$ and $d\mid F_{\alpha(d)}$ imply
$\alpha(q)\mid \alpha(d)$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1).
Since $\alpha(q)=n$, one gets $n\mid \alpha(d)\mid n$, hence $\alpha(d)=n$.
Thus $d\in B_n$, proving that $B_n$ is an upper set.

By Proposition~\ref{prop:fibonacci-strong-divisibility},
$F_n\mid F_m$ holds if and only if $n\mid m$. Therefore the least index at
which $F_n$ appears is exactly $n$, so $\alpha(F_n)=n$ and $F_n\in B_n$. Since
$F_n$ is the largest element of $D_n$, it is the unique maximal element of
$B_n$.

Because $D_n$ is finite and $B_n$ is an upper set, every element of $B_n$ lies
above at least one minimal element of $B_n$. This proves the interval
decomposition.

Now let $q\in D_n$. If $q\in\mathcal M_n$, then certainly $\alpha(q)=n$.
Moreover, for every prime $p\mid q$, the divisor $q/p$ lies in $D_n$ but must
lie outside $B_n$ by minimality of $q$, so $\alpha(q/p)<n$.

Conversely, assume $\alpha(q)=n$ and $\alpha(q/p)<n$ for every prime $p\mid q$.
Let $d\mid q$ with $d\in B_n$. If $d<q$, choose a prime $p\mid q/d$. Then
$d\mid q/p$. Since $B_n$ is an upper set in $D_n$, this would force
$q/p\in B_n$, contradicting $\alpha(q/p)<n$. Hence $d=q$, so $q$ is minimal in
$B_n$.

For the lcm criterion, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(2)
gives
\[
\alpha(q)=\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_r)
\]
and
\[
\alpha(q/p_i)=
\lcmop(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_i^-,
\lambda_{i+1},\dots,\lambda_r)
\]
for each $i$. The displayed characterization of $\mathcal M_n$ is therefore
exactly the prime-power reformulation of part~(4).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Atomic generators and witness covers}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:atomic-birth-families}
For each $d\ge 2$, define the \emph{atomic birth family}
\[
\Atom(d):=\{p^e:\ \alpha(p^e)=d,\ \alpha(p^{e-1})<d\}.
\]
Thus $\Atom(d)$ consists of the prime-power moduli whose apparition index
first reaches the value $d$ at that exponent.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:witness-cover}
Let
\[
n=\prod_{\ell\in\mathcal P(n)}\ell^{a_\ell}.
\]
For $\theta=p^e\in \Atom(d)$ with $d\mid n$, write
\[
d(\theta):=\alpha(\theta)=d,
\qquad
d^{-}(\theta):=\alpha(p^{e-1}),
\]
and define
\[
T_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\ \nu_\ell(d(\theta))=a_\ell\},
\]
\[
E_n(\theta):=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\ \nu_\ell(d(\theta))=a_\ell>
\nu_\ell(d^{-}(\theta))\}.
\]
A finite set
\[
\Theta\subseteq \bigcup_{d\mid n}\Atom(d)
\]
is an \emph{$n$-witness cover} if its elements are pairwise coprime,
\[
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n),
\]
and, for every $\theta\in\Theta$,
\[
E_n(\theta)\nsubseteq
\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:atomic-witness-classification}
For every $n\ge 2$,
\[
\mathcal M_n=
\left\{
\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta:\ \Theta\text{ is an $n$-witness cover}
\right\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$, and write its prime-power factorization as
\[
m=\prod_{i=1}^r \theta_i,
\qquad
\theta_i=p_i^{e_i}.
\]
The prime powers $\theta_i$ are pairwise coprime. We first show that each
$\theta_i$ is atomic. If
\[
\alpha(p_i^{e_i-1})=\alpha(p_i^{e_i}),
\]
then replacing $\theta_i$ by $p_i^{e_i-1}$ does not change the least common
multiple of the prime-power apparition values, so
$\alpha(m/p_i)=\alpha(m)=n$. This contradicts the local minimality criterion in
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}. Hence every $\theta_i$ belongs to
$\Atom(d(\theta_i))$.

Since
\[
n=\alpha(m)=\lcmop\bigl(d(\theta_1),\dots,d(\theta_r)\bigr)
\]
and every $d(\theta_i)$ divides $n$, each prime coordinate of $n$ must be
realized at full exponent by at least one factor $\theta_i$. Equivalently,
\[
\bigcup_{i=1}^r T_n(\theta_i)=\mathcal P(n).
\]

Now fix $\theta_i$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators},
$\alpha(m/p_i)<n$. Write
\[
\alpha(m/p_i)=\lcmop\bigl(d(\theta_1),\dots,d(\theta_{i-1}),
d^{-}(\theta_i),d(\theta_{i+1}),\dots,d(\theta_r)\bigr).
\]
If one had
\[
E_n(\theta_i)\subseteq \bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j),
\]
then every prime coordinate of $n$ lost by lowering $\theta_i$ would still be
supplied at full exponent by one of the other factors, while the coordinates
outside $E_n(\theta_i)$ would remain full already in $d^{-}(\theta_i)$. The
displayed least common multiple would then still equal $n$, contradicting
$\alpha(m/p_i)<n$. Therefore
\[
E_n(\theta_i)\nsubseteq \bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j).
\]
So
\[
\Theta(m):=\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_r\}
\]
is an $n$-witness cover.

Conversely, let $\Theta$ be an $n$-witness cover and set
\[
m_\Theta:=\prod_{\theta\in\Theta}\theta.
\]
Because the factors are pairwise coprime, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(2)
gives
\[
\alpha(m_\Theta)=\lcmop\bigl(d(\theta):\theta\in\Theta\bigr).
\]
The cover condition
\[
\bigcup_{\theta\in\Theta}T_n(\theta)=\mathcal P(n)
\]
implies that this least common multiple attains every prime coordinate of $n$
at full exponent. Since each $d(\theta)$ divides $n$, one gets
$\alpha(m_\Theta)=n$.

Fix $\theta=p^e\in\Theta$. Then
\[
\alpha(m_\Theta/p)=
\lcmop\bigl(d^{-}(\theta),d(\eta):\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}\bigr).
\]
Choose
\[
\ell\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta),
\]
which exists by the witness condition. For this coordinate $\ell$, the
exponent in $d^{-}(\theta)$ is strictly below $a_\ell$, and none of the other
factors attains the full exponent $a_\ell$. Hence
$\nu_\ell(\alpha(m_\Theta/p))<a_\ell$, so $\alpha(m_\Theta/p)<n$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}, this proves that $m_\Theta\in\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound}
Every $m\in\mathcal M_n$ satisfies
\[
\omega(m)\le \omega(n).
\]
More precisely, $m$ is a product of at most $\omega(n)$ pairwise coprime atomic
birth moduli.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification}, $m$ comes from an
$n$-witness cover $\Theta$. For each $\theta\in\Theta$, choose
\[
\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta).
\]
If $\theta\neq \eta$, then $\ell(\theta)\neq \ell(\eta)$, because
$\ell(\theta)\notin T_n(\eta)$. Thus the map $\theta\mapsto \ell(\theta)$ is
injective from $\Theta$ into $\mathcal P(n)$. Hence
\[
\#\Theta\le \#\mathcal P(n)=\omega(n).
\]
Since the elements of $\Theta$ are pairwise coprime prime powers, one has
$\omega(m)=\#\Theta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:two-support-binary}
If $\omega(n)=2$, then every element of $\mathcal M_n$ is either atomic or the
product of two pairwise coprime atomic birth moduli.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is immediate from Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:witness-cover-finite-type}
Fix $k\ge 1$. Up to relabeling the prime coordinates by $[k]$, the witness
patterns that occur in layers with $\omega(n)=k$ form a finite collection.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound}, every witness cover for
a layer with $\omega(n)=k$ has at most $k$ elements. For a fixed coordinate set
$[k]$, each atomic factor contributes a pair
\[
(E,T)
\qquad\text{with}\qquad
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq T\subseteq [k].
\]
There are only finitely many such pairs. Hence there are only finitely many
families of at most $k$ such pairs, up to permutation, and therefore only
finitely many witness-cover types.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{thm:canonical-principal-interval}
Let $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, and choose a primitive prime divisor
$\pi_n$ with $\alpha(\pi_n)=n$. Then $\pi_n\in \mathcal M_n$, and therefore
\[
[\pi_n,F_n]\subseteq B_n.
\]
Hence the map
\[
\operatorname{Div}(F_n/\pi_n)\hookrightarrow B_n,
\qquad
d\longmapsto \pi_n d,
\]
is an order embedding, and
\[
A(n)\ge \tau(F_n/\pi_n)
=\tau(F_n)\frac{\nu_{\pi_n}(F_n)}{\nu_{\pi_n}(F_n)+1}
\ge \frac12\tau(F_n).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-field-quotients}, such a prime $\pi_n$ exists.
Since $\pi_n$ is prime, one has
\[
\pi_n\in \Atom(n),
\qquad
T_n(\pi_n)=\mathcal P(n),
\qquad
E_n(\pi_n)=\mathcal P(n).
\]
Thus $\{\pi_n\}$ is an $n$-witness cover. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification}, this gives
$\pi_n\in\mathcal M_n$. The interval inclusion now follows from the upper-set
property in Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}. The rest is the same divisor
count as before.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}
\label{ex:witness-cover-n20}
For $n=20$, one has
\[
\mathcal M_{20}=\{15,33,41\}.
\]
The relevant atomic factors are
\[
3\in \Atom(4),\qquad
5\in \Atom(5),\qquad
11\in \Atom(10),\qquad
41\in \Atom(20).
\]
The corresponding witness covers are
\[
\{41\},\qquad \{3,5\},\qquad \{3,11\},
\]
which yield the three minimal generators above.
\end{example}

\begin{example}
\label{ex:witness-cover-n30}
For $n=30$, one has
\[
\mathcal M_{30}=\{20,22,31,244,671\}.
\]
The relevant atomic factors are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&2\in \Atom(3),\quad
4\in \Atom(6),\quad
5\in \Atom(5),\\
&11\in \Atom(10),\quad
31\in \Atom(30),\quad
61\in \Atom(15).
\end{aligned}
\]
The witness covers are
\[
\{31\},\qquad
\{4,5\},\qquad
\{2,11\},\qquad
\{4,61\},\qquad
\{11,61\},
\]
and their products are exactly the five minimal generators listed above.
\end{example}

\subsection*{Primitive-prime subgeometry}

\begin{corollary}
\label{thm:divisor-lattice-lift}
Let $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$ and set
\[
\mathcal D^\sharp(n):=\{d\mid n:\ d\notin\{1,2,6,12\}\}.
\]
For each $d\in \mathcal D^\sharp(n)$, choose a prime $\pi_d$ such that
$\alpha(\pi_d)=d$. For every $t\in \mathcal D^\sharp(n)$, define
\[
M_t:=\prod_{d\in \mathcal D^\sharp(t)} \pi_d.
\]
Then
\[
\alpha(M_t)=t,
\qquad
s\mid t \Longleftrightarrow M_s\mid M_t
\quad (s,t\in \mathcal D^\sharp(n)).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $d\in \mathcal D^\sharp(t)$, then $\alpha(\pi_d)=d\mid t$, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1) gives $\pi_d\mid F_t$ and
hence $M_t\mid F_t$. Distinct indices $d$ yield distinct primes $\pi_d$,
because the rank of apparition of a prime is unique. Therefore
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(2) yields
\[
\alpha(M_t)
=
\lcmop\bigl(\alpha(\pi_d):d\in \mathcal D^\sharp(t)\bigr)
=
\lcmop\bigl(d:d\in \mathcal D^\sharp(t)\bigr)
=t,
\]
since $t\in \mathcal D^\sharp(t)$.

If $s\mid t$, then $\mathcal D^\sharp(s)\subseteq \mathcal D^\sharp(t)$, so
$M_s\mid M_t$. Conversely, if $M_s\mid M_t$, then $\pi_s\mid M_t$, and the
pairwise distinctness of the $\pi_d$ forces $s\in \mathcal D^\sharp(t)$, hence
$s\mid t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{thm:boolean-skeleton}
Let $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$, and retain the primes $\pi_d$ from
Corollary~\ref{thm:divisor-lattice-lift}. Set
\[
r(n):=\#\bigl(\mathcal D^\sharp(n)\setminus\{n\}\bigr)
=\tau(n)-2-\mathbf{1}_{2\mid n}-\mathbf{1}_{6\mid n}-\mathbf{1}_{12\mid n}.
\]
For each subset $S\subseteq \mathcal D^\sharp(n)\setminus\{n\}$, define
\[
Q_S:=\pi_n\prod_{d\in S}\pi_d.
\]
Then the map
\[
S\longmapsto Q_S
\]
is an order embedding from the Boolean lattice $B_{r(n)}$ into the birth layer
$B_n$. In particular,
\[
Q_S\mid Q_T \Longleftrightarrow S\subseteq T,
\qquad
A(n)=\#B_n\ge 2^{r(n)}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For each $d\in S\cup\{n\}$ one has $\alpha(\pi_d)=d\mid n$, so
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1) yields $\pi_d\mid F_n$.
Hence $Q_S\mid F_n$. Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(2) then
gives
\[
\alpha(Q_S)
=\lcmop\bigl(\alpha(\pi_n),\alpha(\pi_d):d\in S\bigr)
=\lcmop\bigl(n,d:d\in S\bigr)=n.
\]
Therefore $Q_S\in B_n$.

The primes $\pi_d$ are pairwise distinct by the proof of
Corollary~\ref{thm:divisor-lattice-lift}. Thus $Q_S\mid Q_T$ holds exactly when
the prime support of $Q_S$ is contained in that of $Q_T$, namely when
$S\subseteq T$. The image is therefore a Boolean subposet of rank $r(n)$
inside $B_n$, and it contributes $2^{r(n)}$ distinct elements.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:birth-layer-width}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\ge \binom{r(n)}{\lfloor r(n)/2\rfloor}.
\]
In particular,
\[
\widthop(B_n)\gg \frac{2^{r(n)}}{\sqrt{r(n)+1}}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton}, the poset $B_n$ contains a copy of the
Boolean lattice $B_{r(n)}$. The largest antichain in $B_{r(n)}$ has cardinality
$\binom{r(n)}{\lfloor r(n)/2\rfloor}$ by Sperner's theorem
\cite{Sperner1928Subsets}. The displayed lower bound then follows from the
standard central-binomial estimate.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:birth-layer-maximal-order}
One has
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log A(n)\,\log\log n}{\log n}\ge \log 2,
\]
and
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\frac{\log\log \widthop(B_n)\,\log\log n}{\log n}\ge \log 2.
\]
Equivalently, there exist infinitely many integers $n$ such that
\[
\log_2 A(n)\ge
\exp\!\left((\log 2+o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right)
\]
and
\[
\log_2 \widthop(B_n)\ge
\exp\!\left((\log 2+o(1))\frac{\log n}{\log\log n}\right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For every $n\ge 3$ with $n\notin\{6,12\}$,
Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton} gives $\log_2 A(n)\ge r(n)$, while
Corollary~\ref{cor:birth-layer-width} gives
\[
\log_2 \widthop(B_n)\ge r(n)-O(\log r(n)).
\]
Since
\[
r(n)=\tau(n)-2-\mathbf{1}_{2\mid n}-\mathbf{1}_{6\mid n}-\mathbf{1}_{12\mid n}
=\tau(n)+O(1),
\]
it follows that
\[
\log r(n)=\log \tau(n)+o(1)
\]
along any sequence for which $\tau(n)\to\infty$.

Wigert's theorem \cite{Wigert1907Divisors} states that
\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log \tau(n)\,\log\log n}{\log n}=\log 2.
\]
Combining this with the previous inequalities yields the two limsup bounds.
The equivalent exponential formulation follows immediately.
\end{proof}

\section{Atomic ladders and low-support classifications}
\label{sec:atomic-ladders}

This section recasts the classical prime-power apparition formulas as the local
input for the atomic witness-cover classification. In particular, it identifies
which prime powers are genuinely atomic on each $p$-primary ladder and which
ones are only nonminimal thickenings of an already existing atomic layer, then
uses that local input to make the witness-cover theory effective in the
two-support and three-support cases.

\begin{proposition}
\label{thm:prime-power-apparition-ladders}
Let $p$ be prime and $r\ge 1$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item For $p=5$,
\[
\alpha(5^r)=5^r.
\]
\item One has
\[
\alpha(2)=3,
\qquad
\alpha(2^r)=3\cdot 2^{\max(1,r-2)}\quad (r\ge 2).
\]
\item If $p\neq 2,5$ and $e_p:=\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})$, then
\[
\alpha(p^r)=\alpha(p)\,p^{\max(0,r-e_p)}.
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
These formulas are classical. For odd primes $p\neq 5$, part (3) goes back to
Fulton and Morris \cite{FultonMorris1969ArithmeticalFunctions}. The special
formula for powers of $2$ is already present in Jarden's treatment of
$z(10^d)$ \cite{Jarden1966RecurringSequences}; we include the short valuation
derivation because the notation is used repeatedly in what follows.

For $p\neq 2,5$, Lengyel's valuation formula \cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas}
states that
\[
\nu_p(F_n)=
\begin{cases}
\nu_p(n)+\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)}), & \alpha(p)\mid n,\\
0, & \alpha(p)\nmid n.
\end{cases}
\]
Since $\alpha(p)\mid p\pm 1$, one has $p\nmid \alpha(p)$. Writing
$n=\alpha(p)u$, the condition $p^r\mid F_n$ is equivalent to
\[
\nu_p(u)+e_p\ge r.
\]
The least such $u$ is $p^{\max(0,r-e_p)}$, which proves part (3).

The case $p=5$ follows from the explicit valuation formula
$\nu_5(F_n)=\nu_5(n)$, so the least $n$ with $5^r\mid F_n$ is $5^r$. For
$p=2$, the classical valuation formula gives the stated values
\cite{Lengyel1995OrderFibonacciLucas,Jarden1966RecurringSequences}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:atomic-prime-power-inputs}
Let $p$ be prime.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Every power $5^r$ belongs to the atomic family $\Atom(5^r)$.
\item One has
\[
2\in \Atom(3),\qquad 4\in \Atom(6),
\qquad
2^r\in \Atom(3\cdot 2^{r-2})\quad (r\ge 4),
\]
and no other power of $2$ is atomic.
\item If $p\neq 2,5$, write
\[
z_p:=\alpha(p),
\qquad
e_p:=\nu_p(F_{z_p}).
\]
Then
\[
p\in \Atom(z_p),
\qquad
p^r\in \Atom(z_p\,p^{r-e_p})\quad (r\ge e_p+1),
\]
and no power $p^r$ with $2\le r\le e_p$ is atomic.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Part~(1) is immediate from Proposition~\ref{thm:prime-power-apparition-ladders}(1),
since $\alpha(5^{r-1})=5^{r-1}<5^r$.

For part~(2), Proposition~\ref{thm:prime-power-apparition-ladders}(2) gives
\[
\alpha(2)=3,\qquad \alpha(4)=6,\qquad \alpha(8)=6,
\]
and for $r\ge 4$,
\[
\alpha(2^r)=3\cdot 2^{r-2}>3\cdot 2^{r-3}=\alpha(2^{r-1}).
\]
This is exactly the atomicity criterion.

For part~(3), Proposition~\ref{thm:prime-power-apparition-ladders}(3) shows that
\[
\alpha(p^r)=z_p \quad (1\le r\le e_p),
\qquad
\alpha(p^r)=z_p\,p^{r-e_p}\quad (r\ge e_p).
\]
Thus only $p$ is atomic on the initial plateau, while every step strictly above
the plateau is atomic.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}
Let $n\ge 2$, and let $\theta=p^e$ be an atomic birth modulus that occurs in
some $n$-witness cover. Then exactly one of the following holds:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item $d^{-}(\theta)=1$, in which case
\[
E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta);
\]
\item $d^{-}(\theta)>1$, in which case
\[
E_n(\theta)=\{\lambda\}
\]
for a unique prime coordinate $\lambda\in\mathcal P(n)$.
\end{enumerate}
Equivalently, every effective atomic factor is either a \emph{primitive atom},
characterized by $E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$, or a \emph{ladder atom},
characterized by $\#E_n(\theta)=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write
\[
d(\theta)=\alpha(p^e),
\qquad
d^{-}(\theta)=\alpha(p^{e-1}).
\]
By Corollary~\ref{cor:atomic-prime-power-inputs}, every odd prime
$p\neq 2,5$ contributes atomic factors in exactly two ways. Either $e=1$, in
which case $d^{-}(\theta)=1$, or $e\ge e_p+1$, in which case
\[
\alpha(p^e)=z_p\,p^{e-e_p},
\qquad
\alpha(p^{e-1})=z_p\,p^{e-1-e_p},
\]
so lowering $\theta$ changes only the $p$-coordinate. For $p=5$, one has
$\alpha(5^r)=5^r$, so each atomic step changes only the $5$-coordinate. For
$p=2$, the only atomic powers are $2$, $4$, and $2^r$ for $r\ge 4$; among
these, $\theta=2$ has $d^{-}(\theta)=1$, while $4$ and $2^r$ for $r\ge 4$
change only the $2$-coordinate.

If $d^{-}(\theta)=1$, then by definition
\[
E_n(\theta)=\{\ell\in\mathcal P(n):\ \nu_\ell(d(\theta))=a_\ell>0\}=T_n(\theta).
\]
If $d^{-}(\theta)>1$, the preceding case-by-case analysis shows that exactly
one prime coordinate drops when $\theta$ is lowered, so $E_n(\theta)$ is a
singleton. This proves the dichotomy.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation}
Let $n\ge 2$, write $k:=\omega(n)$, and let $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Then
\[
\omega(m)=k
\]
if and only if, after indexing the prime coordinates as
\[
\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\dots,\ell_k\},
\]
one can write
\[
m=\theta_1\cdots \theta_k
\]
with pairwise coprime atomic birth moduli $\theta_i$ such that
\[
T_n(\theta_i)=\{\ell_i\}
\qquad
(1\le i\le k).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Theta$ be the $n$-witness cover attached to $m$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification}. The proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound} constructs an injection
\[
\Theta\longrightarrow\mathcal P(n),
\qquad
\theta\longmapsto \ell(\theta),
\]
where
\[
\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\setminus
\bigcup_{\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}}T_n(\eta).
\]
Because the factors in a witness cover are pairwise coprime prime powers, one
has
\[
\#\Theta=\omega(m).
\]
If $\omega(m)=k=\omega(n)$, then
\[
\#\Theta=\omega(m)=\omega(n)=\#\mathcal P(n),
\]
so the injection is a bijection.

Fix $\theta\in\Theta$. Since $\ell(\theta)\in E_n(\theta)\subseteq T_n(\theta)$,
it remains to show that no other coordinate belongs to $T_n(\theta)$. Let
$\mu\in \mathcal P(n)\setminus\{\ell(\theta)\}$. Because the map
$\eta\mapsto \ell(\eta)$ is bijective, there exists
$\eta\in\Theta\setminus\{\theta\}$ with $\mu=\ell(\eta)$. By construction,
\[
\mu\notin \bigcup_{\xi\in\Theta\setminus\{\eta\}}T_n(\xi),
\]
and $\theta\in \Theta\setminus\{\eta\}$, so $\mu\notin T_n(\theta)$. Therefore
$T_n(\theta)=\{\ell(\theta)\}$ for every $\theta\in\Theta$, proving the forward
implication.

Conversely, if
\[
m=\theta_1\cdots\theta_k
\]
with pairwise coprime atomic birth moduli satisfying
$T_n(\theta_i)=\{\ell_i\}$, then
\[
\bigcup_{i=1}^k T_n(\theta_i)=\mathcal P(n).
\]
Moreover, each $E_n(\theta_i)$ is a nonempty subset of the singleton
$T_n(\theta_i)$, so necessarily
\[
E_n(\theta_i)=\{\ell_i\}.
\]
Hence
\[
E_n(\theta_i)\cap \bigcup_{j\ne i}T_n(\theta_j)=\varnothing
\qquad
(1\le i\le k),
\]
and therefore $\{\theta_1,\dots,\theta_k\}$ is an $n$-witness cover. Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification}
then gives $m\in\mathcal M_n$. Since the factors are pairwise coprime prime
powers, one has $\omega(m)=k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:two-support-complete-classification}
Assume
\[
\mathcal P(n)=\{\ell_1,\ell_2\}.
\]
Define
\[
\Atom_{\ell_i}(n):=
\left\{
\theta\in \bigcup_{d\mid n}\Atom(d):\ T_n(\theta)=\{\ell_i\}
\right\}
\qquad
(i=1,2).
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal M_n
=
\Atom(n)
\sqcup
\{xy:\ x\in\Atom_{\ell_1}(n),\ y\in\Atom_{\ell_2}(n),\ \gcd(x,y)=1\}.
\]
In particular, every non-atomic minimal generator in a two-support layer is
obtained by taking one singleton-coordinate atom on each coordinate, and this
criterion is both necessary and sufficient.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $m\in\mathcal M_n$ be non-atomic. By
Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound}, one has $\omega(m)\le 2$.
Since $m$ is not atomic, its witness cover has at least two factors, so
$\omega(m)=2=\omega(n)$. Theorem~\ref{thm:extremal-coordinate-separation} then
shows that
\[
m=xy
\]
with $x$ and $y$ pairwise coprime atomic birth moduli satisfying
$T_n(x)=\{\ell_1\}$ and $T_n(y)=\{\ell_2\}$ after relabeling if necessary.
Thus
\[
x\in\Atom_{\ell_1}(n),
\qquad
y\in\Atom_{\ell_2}(n).
\]

Conversely, if
\[
x\in\Atom_{\ell_1}(n),
\qquad
y\in\Atom_{\ell_2}(n),
\qquad
\gcd(x,y)=1,
\]
then $E_n(x)$ and $E_n(y)$ are nonempty subsets of singleton sets, so
\[
E_n(x)=\{\ell_1\},
\qquad
E_n(y)=\{\ell_2\}.
\]
Therefore $\{x,y\}$ is an $n$-witness cover, and
Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification} gives $xy\in\mathcal M_n$.
The atomic part $\Atom(n)\subseteq \mathcal M_n$ is immediate from the local
minimality criterion in Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:abstract-three-coordinate-types}
Let $\Theta$ be an abstract witness family on $[3]$, meaning a finite family
of pairs $(E,T)$ with
\[
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq T\subseteq [3],
\qquad
\bigcup_{(E,T)\in\Theta}T=[3],
\]
such that for every $(E,T)\in\Theta$,
\[
E\not\subseteq
\bigcup_{(E',T')\in\Theta,\ (E',T')\neq (E,T)} T'.
\]
For brevity, write $123$ for $\{1,2,3\}$, $12$ for $\{1,2\}$, and similarly
for the other subsets of $[3]$. Then, up to relabeling of $[3]$ and
reordering of the factors, exactly nine abstract types occur:
\[
\Gamma_1=\{(123,123)\},
\qquad
\Gamma_2=\{(12,123)\},
\qquad
\Gamma_3=\{(1,123)\},
\]
\[
\Gamma_4=\{(12,12),(23,23)\},
\qquad
\Gamma_5=\{(1,12),(23,23)\},
\qquad
\Gamma_6=\{(1,12),(3,23)\},
\]
\[
\Gamma_7=\{(1,1),(23,23)\},
\qquad
\Gamma_8=\{(1,1),(2,23)\},
\qquad
\Gamma_9=\{(1,1),(2,2),(3,3)\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each $(E,T)\in\Theta$, choose
\[
\ell(E,T)\in E\setminus
\bigcup_{(E',T')\in\Theta,\ (E',T')\neq (E,T)} T'.
\]
The witness condition guarantees that such a choice exists. If
$(E,T)\neq (E',T')$, then $\ell(E,T)\notin T'$, so
$\ell(E,T)\neq \ell(E',T')$. Hence the map
\[
(E,T)\longmapsto \ell(E,T)
\]
is injective from $\Theta$ into $[3]$, and therefore $\#\Theta\le 3$.

If $\#\Theta=1$, then necessarily $T=[3]$. Up to relabeling, the set $E$ has
size $3$, $2$, or $1$, giving $\Gamma_1$, $\Gamma_2$, or $\Gamma_3$.

If $\#\Theta=3$, the preceding injection is bijective. Fix $(E,T)\in\Theta$.
The chosen coordinate $\ell(E,T)$ belongs to $E\subseteq T$. Every other
coordinate in $[3]\setminus\{\ell(E,T)\}$ is equal to $\ell(E',T')$ for some
other factor $(E',T')$, and therefore lies outside $T$. Thus $T$ is a
singleton for every factor, which yields $\Gamma_9$.

It remains to consider $\#\Theta=2$. Write
\[
\Theta=\{(E_1,T_1),(E_2,T_2)\}.
\]
Neither $T_1$ nor $T_2$ can equal $[3]$, because if $T_1=[3]$, then
$E_2\subseteq T_2\subseteq T_1$, contradicting the witness condition for the
second factor. Hence each $T_i$ has size $1$ or $2$. Since $T_1\cup T_2=[3]$,
there are only two support patterns up to relabeling:

If
\[
T_1=\{1\},
\qquad
T_2=\{2,3\},
\]
then $E_1=\{1\}$, while $E_2$ is, up to swapping the coordinates $2$ and $3$,
either $\{2,3\}$ or $\{2\}$. This gives $\Gamma_7$ and $\Gamma_8$.

If
\[
T_1=\{1,2\},
\qquad
T_2=\{2,3\},
\]
then the witness conditions force $1\in E_1$ and $3\in E_2$, so
\[
E_1\in\bigl\{\{1,2\},\{1\}\bigr\},
\qquad
E_2\in\bigl\{\{2,3\},\{3\}\bigr\}.
\]
Up to interchanging the two factors, this yields exactly three cases:
primitive/primitive, mixed, and ladder/ladder, namely
$\Gamma_4$, $\Gamma_5$, and $\Gamma_6$.

These exhaust all possibilities.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types}
Assume $\omega(n)=3$, and relabel the prime coordinates of $n$ by $[3]$. Then
the witness cover attached to any element of $\mathcal M_n$ is, up to
relabeling of the coordinates and reordering of the factors, of one of the
eight types
\[
\Gamma_1,\ \Gamma_3,\ \Gamma_4,\ \Gamma_5,\ \Gamma_6,\ \Gamma_7,\ \Gamma_8,\ \Gamma_9.
\]
Equivalently, among the nine abstract types of
Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-coordinate-types}, the unique type
$\Gamma_2=\{(12,123)\}$ is the only one excluded in Fibonacci upper fibers.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:abstract-three-coordinate-types}, every witness cover on
three coordinates belongs to one of the nine abstract types
$\Gamma_1,\dots,\Gamma_9$. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:primitive-ladder-dichotomy}, every atomic factor that occurs
in a Fibonacci witness cover satisfies either $E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)$ or
$\#E_n(\theta)=1$. The type $\Gamma_2$ is the unique abstract type containing
a factor with
\[
1<\#E<\#T=3.
\]
Hence $\Gamma_2$ cannot occur in Fibonacci upper fibers, while every actual
Fibonacci witness cover must belong to one of the remaining eight types.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:three-coordinate-realization}
Assume $\omega(n)=3$, and relabel the prime coordinates by $[3]$. For
nonempty $S\subseteq [3]$ and $i\in S$, define
\[
\mathcal P_S(n):=\{\theta:\ E_n(\theta)=T_n(\theta)=S\},
\]
\[
\mathcal L_{i,S}(n):=\{\theta:\ E_n(\theta)=\{i\},\ T_n(\theta)=S\},
\]
and
\[
\mathcal S_i(n):=\mathcal P_{\{i\}}(n).
\]
Then every element of $\mathcal M_n$ is obtained by making pairwise coprime
selections from one of the following families, together with a relabeling of
the three coordinates:
\[
\mathcal P_{[3]}(n),
\qquad
\mathcal L_{i,[3]}(n),
\qquad
\mathcal P_{\{i,j\}}(n)\mathcal P_{\{j,k\}}(n),
\qquad
\mathcal L_{i,\{i,j\}}(n)\mathcal P_{\{j,k\}}(n),
\]
\[
\mathcal L_{i,\{i,j\}}(n)\mathcal L_{k,\{j,k\}}(n),
\qquad
\mathcal S_i(n)\mathcal P_{\{j,k\}}(n),
\qquad
\mathcal S_i(n)\mathcal L_{j,\{j,k\}}(n),
\qquad
\mathcal S_i(n)\mathcal S_j(n)\mathcal S_k(n),
\]
where $\{i,j,k\}=[3]$ and each product family denotes the set of products of
pairwise coprime choices from the displayed factors. Conversely, every such
pairwise coprime selection yields an element of $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:fibonacci-three-coordinate-types} lists the eight admissible
abstract witness-cover types. By definition, each type corresponds exactly to
one of the displayed primitive or ladder families. Conversely, if one chooses
pairwise coprime atoms from any displayed family, then the resulting set of
factors has precisely the corresponding $(E,T)$-pattern, hence is an
$n$-witness cover. Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification} then gives
the corresponding element of $\mathcal M_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{thm:prime-power-minimal-generators}
If $n=\ell^a\ge 3$ is a prime power, then
\[
\mathcal M_n=\Atom(n),
\qquad
B_n=\bigcup_{\theta\in \Atom(n)}[\theta,F_n].
\]
If
\[
F_n=\prod_p p^{c_p}
\]
and
\[
\Atom(n)=\{p^{e_p(n)}:\ \alpha(p^{e_p(n)})=n\},
\]
then
\[
A(n)=\#B_n
=
\tau(F_n)\left(
1-\prod_{p^{e_p(n)}\in\Atom(n)}
\frac{e_p(n)}{c_p+1}
\right).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since $\omega(n)=1$, Corollary~\ref{cor:minimal-generator-support-bound} shows
that every minimal generator has only one prime factor. Theorem~\ref{thm:atomic-witness-classification}
therefore gives $\mathcal M_n=\Atom(n)$. The interval decomposition follows
from Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-generators}, and the counting formula is the same
divisor-complement count used in the support-one case of the previous version.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{thm:odd-semiprime-minimal-generators}
If $n=pq$ with $p$ and $q$ distinct odd primes, then
\[
\mathcal M_{pq}
=
\Atom(pq)
\sqcup
\{xy:\ x\in\Atom(p),\ y\in\Atom(q)\}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Since the prime coordinates of $pq$ are exactly $\{p,q\}$,
Corollary~\ref{cor:two-support-complete-classification} gives
\[
\mathcal M_{pq}
=
\Atom(pq)
\sqcup
\{xy:\ x\in\Atom_p(pq),\ y\in\Atom_q(pq),\ \gcd(x,y)=1\}.
\]
If $x\in \Atom_p(pq)$, then $T_{pq}(x)=\{p\}$, so $\alpha(x)$ is a divisor of
$pq$ divisible by $p$ but not by $q$; hence $\alpha(x)=p$, which means
$x\in\Atom(p)$. Similarly, $\Atom_q(pq)=\Atom(q)$. Substituting these
identities proves the claimed formula.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:prime-power-geometric-recursion}
Let $p\neq 2,5$ be prime and write $e_p=\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})$. If $r\ge e_p$,
then
\[
\alpha(p^{r+1})=p\,\alpha(p^r).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
m_{\min}(p^{r+1})+2=p\bigl(m_{\min}(p^r)+2\bigr).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
This is immediate from Proposition~\ref{thm:prime-power-apparition-ladders}(3).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:wss-thickening-criterion}
Let $p\neq 2,5$ be prime. The following are equivalent.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $\alpha(p^2)=\alpha(p)$.
\item The primitive $p$-birth layer already carries a quotient
\[
X_{\alpha(p)-2}\twoheadrightarrow \ZZ/(p^2\ZZ).
\]
\item $\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2$.
\item $p$ is a Wall--Sun--Sun prime.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{thm:prime-power-apparition-ladders}(3),
\[
\alpha(p^2)=\alpha(p)
\iff
\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2.
\]
The equivalence of (i) and (ii) is exactly
Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient-observable-threshold}. For $p\neq 2,5$, the
Wall--Sun--Sun condition is equivalent to $\nu_p(F_{\alpha(p)})\ge 2$; see
Sun and Sun \cite{SunSun1992FibonacciFLT}.
\end{proof}

\section{A quotient-ring perspective}

We close the main body of the paper with a short quotient-ring interpretation
of the birth-layer constructions. It complements the divisor-lattice point of
view developed in the earlier sections.

\begin{definition}
\label{def:joint-readout}
Let $S=\{q_1,\dots,q_s\}$ be a finite family of integers at least $2$, and let
$m\ge 1$ satisfy $q_i\mid F_{m+2}$ for all $i$. The \emph{joint readout} at
depth $m$ is
\[
\rho_{m;S}:X_m\longrightarrow \prod_{i=1}^s \ZZ/(q_i\ZZ),
\qquad
x\longmapsto \bigl([V_m(x)]_{q_1},\dots,[V_m(x)]_{q_s}\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:finite-signature-unified-resolution-minimal}
Let $S=\{q_1,\dots,q_s\}$ and write $Q=\lcmop(q_1,\dots,q_s)$. The least depth at
which all moduli in $S$ occur simultaneously is
\[
L(S)=\alpha(Q)-2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:quotient-observable-threshold}, all $q_i$ occur at depth
$m$ if and only if $\alpha(q_i)\mid m+2$ for all $i$. By
Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(2), this is equivalent to
$\alpha(Q)\mid m+2$. The minimal such $m$ is therefore $\alpha(Q)-2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:finite-signature-joint-readout-image-fiber}
Let $m\ge 1$, let $S=\{q_1,\dots,q_s\}$ with $q_i\mid F_{m+2}$ for all $i$, and
write
\[
M:=F_{m+2},
\qquad
Q:=\lcmop(q_1,\dots,q_s).
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item the image of $\rho_{m;S}$ is the compatibility locus
\[
\left\{(a_1,\dots,a_s)\in\prod_i \ZZ/(q_i\ZZ):
a_i\equiv a_j\pmod{\gcd(q_i,q_j)}\text{ for all }i,j\right\};
\]
\item the image has cardinality $Q$;
\item every fiber has cardinality $M/Q$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Proposition~\ref{thm:finite-resolution-mod}, the map $\rho_{m;S}$
identifies with
\[
\ZZ/(M\ZZ)\longrightarrow \prod_{i=1}^s \ZZ/(q_i\ZZ),
\qquad
\overline{t}\longmapsto ([t]_{q_1},\dots,[t]_{q_s}).
\]
Its image is the usual compatibility locus from the noncoprime Chinese
remainder theorem, proving (1). Two residue classes $\overline{t}$ and
$\overline{u}$ have the same image if and only if $q_i\mid (t-u)$ for every
$i$, equivalently $Q\mid (t-u)$. Thus the kernel has size $M/Q$, which proves
(3). Since the domain has size $M$, the image has size $Q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:joint-readout-pairwise-coprime}
If $q_1,\dots,q_s$ are pairwise coprime, then $\rho_{m;S}$ is surjective and
every fiber has cardinality
\[
\frac{F_{m+2}}{q_1\cdots q_s}.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If the $q_i$ are pairwise coprime, then $Q=q_1\cdots q_s$ and the compatibility
conditions in Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-signature-joint-readout-image-fiber}(1)
are vacuous.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:proper-shadow-complexity}
A \emph{proper shadow family} at depth $m$ is a finite family of proper
divisors $q_1,\dots,q_s$ of $F_{m+2}$. Define $\sigma(m)$ to be the least $s$
for which the corresponding joint readout is injective. If no such family
exists, set $\sigma(m)=\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:proper-shadow-compressibility}
Let
\[
F_{m+2}=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item a proper shadow family $\{q_1,\dots,q_s\}$ is injective if and only if
$\lcmop(q_1,\dots,q_s)=F_{m+2}$;
\item $\sigma(m)=\infty$ if $r=1$;
\item $\sigma(m)=2$ if $r\ge 2$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Part (1) follows from
Theorem~\ref{thm:finite-signature-joint-readout-image-fiber}(3): the joint
readout is injective exactly when its fiber size is $1$, that is, when the
least common multiple of the moduli equals $F_{m+2}$.

If $r=1$, then $F_{m+2}$ is a prime power and every proper divisor misses the
full exponent, so the least common multiple of proper divisors is always still
proper. By part (1), no proper shadow family is injective.

If $r\ge 2$, no single proper divisor can be injective. On the other hand,
\[
q:=\frac{F_{m+2}}{p_1^{a_1}},
\qquad
q':=\frac{F_{m+2}}{p_2^{a_2}}
\]
are proper divisors with $\lcmop(q,q')=F_{m+2}$, so part (1) gives
$\sigma(m)=2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}
\label{def:prime-power-shadow-complexity}
A proper shadow family at depth $m$ is \emph{prime-power} if every modulus is a
proper prime-power divisor of $F_{m+2}$. Define $\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)$ to be
the least size of an injective prime-power shadow family. If no such family
exists, set $\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)=\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:prime-power-shadow-complexity}
Write
\[
F_{m+2}=\prod_{i=1}^r p_i^{a_i}.
\]
Then
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)=
\begin{cases}
\infty, & r=1,\\
r, & r\ge 2.
\end{cases}
\]
In particular, whenever $X_m$ is CRT-split,
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)=\omega(F_{m+2}).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume first that an injective prime-power shadow family exists. By
Theorem~\ref{thm:proper-shadow-compressibility}(1), its least common multiple
must equal $F_{m+2}$. Since every allowed modulus is a prime power divisor of
$F_{m+2}$, the family must contain the full factor $p_i^{a_i}$ for each $i$.
Thus every injective prime-power family has size at least $r$.

If $r=1$, the only prime power carrying the full exponent is $F_{m+2}$ itself,
which is not proper, so no such family exists. If $r\ge 2$, the family
$\{p_1^{a_1},\dots,p_r^{a_r}\}$ consists of proper divisors and has least
common multiple $F_{m+2}$, hence it is injective by
Theorem~\ref{thm:proper-shadow-compressibility}(1).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:prime-power-shadow-divisor-lower-bound}
Let $n:=m+2$. Then
\[
\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)\ge \tau(n)-4.
\]
In particular, prime-power shadow complexity is unbounded along the Fibonacci
modulus chain.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)=\infty$, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, by
Theorem~\ref{thm:prime-power-shadow-complexity},
$\sigma_{\mathrm{pp}}(m)=\omega(F_n)$. For each divisor
$d\mid n$ with $d\notin\{1,2,6,12\}$, Theorem
~\ref{thm:primitive-field-quotients} gives a prime $p_d$ with $\alpha(p_d)=d$.
Since $d\mid n$, Proposition~\ref{prop:rank-of-apparition-basic}(1) implies
$p_d\mid F_n$. Distinct divisors $d$ give distinct primes $p_d$, because the
rank of apparition of a prime is unique. Hence
$\omega(F_n)\ge \tau(n)-4$.
\end{proof}

\section{Further questions}

The witness-cover theorem, together with the primitive-ladder dichotomy and the
explicit support-two and support-three classifications, shifts the remaining
questions away from low-support combinatorics and toward arithmetic realization
and asymptotic complexity. The interval decomposition
\[
B_n=\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal M_n}[m,F_n].
\]
is now controlled abstractly by atomic witness covers, and Section~5 makes the
$p$-primary atomic inputs explicit. Several natural questions remain.

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item Turn the support-three classification into sharp arithmetic nonemptiness
criteria for the families in Corollary~\ref{cor:three-coordinate-realization},
and then extend the same effective program to $k=\omega(n)\ge 4$. The
remaining obstacle is no longer combinatorial typing but determining which of
the finitely many primitive and ladder atomic families are arithmetically
nonempty and pairwise coprime in a given fiber.
\item Determine how witness-cover decompositions control global invariants of
$B_n$, such as $A(n)$, $\widthop(B_n)$, maximal-chain length, and overlap
patterns among the intervals $[m,F_n]$. Corollary~\ref{thm:boolean-skeleton}
gives one universal lower-bound mechanism, but the witness-cover theorem
suggests that sharper estimates should come from counting atomic cover types
rather than from one distinguished squarefree subposet.
\item Extend the atomic witness-cover formalism to Lucas sequences and, more
generally, to strong divisibility sequences equipped with a rank-of-apparition
map and a primitive-divisor theorem. It is natural to ask which parts of the
upper-fiber and atomic-generator theory persist in that wider setting.
\end{enumerate}

These questions indicate that first-appearance spectra are best viewed as a
family of divisor-lattice fibers governed by atomic witness systems rather than
by isolated counting functions.
