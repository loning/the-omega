\subsubsection{``Read Reflector'' and Decision Completeness under the Single-Slice Protocol}\label{subsubsec:xi-read-reflector-completeness}
Following the protocol setting of Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null}, where the readout \(\mathrm{read}:\Omega\to X_{\mathrm{vis}}\cup\{\mathsf{NULL}\}\) is uniquely determined by ``partial observation \(\operatorname{obs}\) + visible domain filter \(X_{\mathrm{vis}}\) + checker \(\mathrm{Chk}\),'' and in the unitary-slice framework of this chapter takes \(X_{\mathrm{vis}}=\mathsf{Phase}\) (see \S\ref{subsec:xi-pw-type-safety-null}).
The key point of this protocol is not to force objects to be identified, but to restrict \emph{auditable decisions} to typed operations that are insensitive to the readout fibers: any additional degrees of freedom that are not explicitly externalized as orthogonal recording axes can only collapse to \(\mathsf{NULL}\) at the type level (see Proposition \ref{prop:xi-null-three-way} and Definition \ref{def:multi_axis_address}).
\par
The following factorization law expresses this ``closure-type identification'' as a rigorous reflection/quotient space property.

\begin{theorem}[Factorization Law of the Read Reflector]\label{thm:xi-read-reflector-factorization}
Let
\(
R_{\mathrm{read}}:=\mathrm{read}(\Omega)\subseteq X_{\mathrm{vis}}\cup\{\mathsf{NULL}\}
\)
be the image of the readout, and define the equivalence relation
\[
\omega\sim_{\mathrm{read}}\omega'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{read}(\omega)=\mathrm{read}(\omega').
\]
For any predicate \(P:\Omega\to\{0,1\}\), if \(P\) satisfies protocol invariance
\[
\omega\sim_{\mathrm{read}}\omega'\ \Longrightarrow\ P(\omega)=P(\omega'),
\]
then there exists a unique function \(p:R_{\mathrm{read}}\to\{0,1\}\) such that
\[
P=p\circ \mathrm{read}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For any \(y\in R_{\mathrm{read}}\), choose \(\omega\in\Omega\) with \(\mathrm{read}(\omega)=y\), and define \(p(y):=P(\omega)\).
If \(\omega'\) also satisfies \(\mathrm{read}(\omega')=y\), then \(\omega\sim_{\mathrm{read}}\omega'\), and by protocol invariance \(P(\omega)=P(\omega')\), so \(p\) is well-defined.
Thus for any \(\omega\in\Omega\), \(p(\mathrm{read}(\omega))=P(\omega)\), i.e., \(P=p\circ\mathrm{read}\).
If \(p_1\circ\mathrm{read}=p_2\circ\mathrm{read}=P\), then for any \(y\in R_{\mathrm{read}}\), choosing \(\omega\) with \(\mathrm{read}(\omega)=y\) gives \(p_1(y)=P(\omega)=p_2(y)\), so \(p_1=p_2\).
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Decision Completeness as ``Closure on Visible Fibers'']\label{rem:xi-read-reflector-completeness}
Theorem \ref{thm:xi-read-reflector-factorization} shows that under the protocol-fixed read reflector, all \emph{auditable} decisions on \(\Omega\) are forced to close on the same interface \(R_{\mathrm{read}}\).
Therefore, the completeness gap discussed in this chapter (Remark \ref{rem:xi-soundness-vs-completeness}) can be precisely located as: whether the event to be decided can be compiled into a single-valued verifiable function on \(R_{\mathrm{read}}\) under this reflector.
If not, the discrepancy can only enter the auditable semantics through externalized orthogonal recording axes: typically, disk-interior point data appear with the minimal dimensionality forced by Blaschke/point spectrum defects (Definition \ref{def:blaschke-defect-dimension}; Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-point-spectrum-correspondence}), while off-slice amplitude information appears via explicit registerization of the endpoint/radial channel (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-null-second-order-radial-channel}).
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[RH Typing Principle for the Single-Slice Protocol]\label{cor:xi-single-slice-rh-typing-principle}
Under the single-slice protocol with fixed visible domain \(X_{\mathrm{vis}}=\mathsf{Phase}\) (Definition \ref{def:xi-visible-read-null}), any auditable decision satisfying protocol invariance must factorize through the read reflector (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-read-reflector-factorization}).
If the readout carrier is further restricted to bounded-type analytic objects \(F\) in the unit disk, then the addressability condition ``no externalized discrete orthogonal residual axes'' is equivalent on the analytic side to the Blaschke defect being zero (Definition \ref{def:blaschke-defect-dimension}; Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-defect-zero-fixed-slice-support}), and can be equivalently compressed on the horizon ledger side into the Jensen defect horizon limit criterion (Definition \ref{def:app-jensen-defect}; Proposition \ref{prop:app-jensen-single-zero-lower-bound}).
The special case \(F=F_{\zeta}\) yields the RH single-scalar translation stated in Theorem \ref{thm:xi-horizon-purity-criterion}.
\par
The same ``disk-interior zero exclusion'' condition can also be equivalently rewritten as a positivity constraint on the horizon logarithmic derivative: RH \(\Longleftrightarrow\) the horizon Carath\'eodory logarithmic derivative function \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) (Appendix equation \eqref{eq:app-horizon-carath-def}) is analytic on \(\mathbb{D}\) and satisfies \(\Re\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\ge 0\), which is further equivalent to the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy \(\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0\) (Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}).
In certificate semantics, this provides a failure witness complementary to the Jensen defect chain: finite-level PSD failure can be directly located by negative eigenvalues in finite dimensions (Corollary \ref{cor:app-horizon-toeplitz-z2-cert-split}).
\par
When the completion bridge kernel arises from a determinantal bridge kernel with \(B(u)\) being a first-order family, the single-slice constraint can also be equivalently stated on the spectral side as the horizon circle constraint of the quadratic eigenvalue pencil \(\mathcal{P}_L(z)\): the zeros of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) are strictly equivalent to quadratic eigenvalues (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-qep-zero-equivalence}), and the critical line corresponds to the horizon circle \(|z|=L^{-1/2}\) (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-critical-line-horizon-circle}).
Therefore, off-axis degrees of freedom in this framework are compressed into the ``leakage spectrum'' (\S\ref{subsubsec:xi-quadratic-pencil-leakage-spectrum}), and when horizon circle reciprocal symmetry holds, they are forced to appear in pairs (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-L-reciprocal-implies-critical-pairing}).
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Quantitative Interface for Endpoint Certification: A Group of Independently Citable Conclusions}
The following group of conclusions compresses the interface consequences of ``single-slice decision closure \(\Rightarrow\) endpoint certification task'' into verifiable invariants and resource lower bounds; their proofs and verification entry points are respectively given by the Toeplitz--PSD detection threshold (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-detection-threshold}), the comoving scan spectral decomposition (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}), and the endpoint tomography chain (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-endpoint-absorption-coefficient}, Theorem \ref{thm:xi-endpoint-flux-pointmode-coupling}).

\begin{proposition}[Quantitative Duality between Toeplitz Certificate Length and Boundary Layer Depth]\label{con:xi-toeplitz-length-depth-duality}
Suppose the diskified completion readout \(F_{\zeta}\) has an inner factor containing a finite Blaschke product
\(
B=\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}b_{a_j}^{m_j}
\)
(counted with multiplicities), and let
\[
h_j:=1-|a_j|
\qquad(j=1,\dots,\kappa)
\]
denote the depth of zeros to the unit circle boundary (note \(1-|a_j|^2\asymp h_j\)).
Let \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) be the corresponding horizon Carath\'eodory logarithmic derivative interface (\eqref{eq:app-horizon-carath-def}), and let \(\mathbf{T}_N\) be its Toeplitz principal submatrix (Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}).
Then for any off-slice zero \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) (\(\beta<\tfrac12\)) inducing the disk-interior pole \(w_\rho\) (i.e., a zero of \(B\)) with endpoint layer remainder
\(
h(\rho):=1-|w_\rho|^2
\)
(Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}), the endpoint-focused detection threshold for finite-level Toeplitz--PSD verification satisfies
\[
N\ \gtrsim\ \frac{1}{h(\rho)}\,\log\!\bigl(1+\delta\,h(\rho)^{-1}\bigr)
\qquad(\delta:=\tfrac12-\beta>0),
\]
and in particular has the coarse lower bound \(N\gtrsim 1/h(\rho)\) (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-detection-threshold}; see also Proposition \ref{prop:xi-extreme-resonance-toeplitz-threshold} and Corollary \ref{cor:xi-toeplitz-height-phi-calibration} for the height-window form).
Therefore: the thinner the endpoint boundary layer (\(h(\rho)\downarrow 0\)), the finite-order Toeplitz--PSD certificate length unavoidably diverges as \(1/h(\rho)\) in the complexity sense (up to a logarithmic factor).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Geometric Lower Bound on Negative Spectral Mass: Minimal Negative Eigenvalue Controls Visible Depth]\label{con:xi-toeplitz-negative-eigenvalue-controls-depth}
Continuing with the setting of Proposition \ref{con:xi-toeplitz-length-depth-duality}.
Denote \(\lambda_{\min}(\mathbf{T}_N)=-\eta_N<0\) as the minimal eigenvalue of the Toeplitz principal submatrix.
Then there exists a disk-interior zero \(a\in\mathbb{D}\) (belonging to the zero set of \(B\)) whose depth \(h:=1-|a|\) satisfies
\[
h\ \ge\ c\,\frac{\eta_N}{N},
\]
where \(c>0\) is an absolute constant.
Therefore, the ``negative magnitude'' of the finite-order Toeplitz certificate not only provides a binary falsifier for ``point defect existence,'' but also yields an auditable lower bound on the defect depth; in the regime semantics, this is equivalent to a minimum visible scale estimate for ``off-slice leakage'' in the endpoint boundary layer.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Scan Hankel Rank \(=\) Defect Degree: Identification of Tomographic Samples with the Blaschke Index]\label{con:xi-scan-hankel-rank-equals-defect}
Let the comoving scan profile \(H(x_0)\) be as defined in Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}, and fix a sampling step \(\Delta>0\).
Take the one-sided exponential spectral samples \(u_n:=\widehat H(n\Delta)\) (\(n\ge 0\)), and for window order \(K\ge 0\) form the Hankel matrix

\mathsf{H}^{(K)}:=(u_{p+q})_{0\le p,q\le K}.
\]
If the exponential parameters \(\{(1+\delta_j)+\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\}\) in the spectral decomposition \eqref{eq:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier} are pairwise distinct, then there exists \(K_0\) such that for all \(K\ge K_0\),
\[
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathsf{H}^{(K)}=M,
\]
where \(M\) is the number of atoms in the finite defect multiset (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}), also equal to the minimal linear realization dimension of the sequence \((u_n)\) (Theorem \ref{thm:hankel-rank-minimal}).
In particular, under the simple zero assumption (\(m_j\equiv 1\), so \(M=\deg(B)\)), the equivalent characterization
\[
\deg(B)<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sup_{K}\mathrm{rank}\,\mathsf{H}^{(K)}<\infty,
\qquad
\sup_{K}\mathrm{rank}\,\mathsf{H}^{(K)}=\deg(B)
\]
holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Exponential Spectral Separation of Single-Scale Profiles: Auditable Three-Decomposition of Height--Offset--Multiplicity]\label{con:xi-scan-exponential-spectrum-separation}
Under the finite defect setting of Proposition \ref{con:xi-scan-hankel-rank-equals-defect}, \(\widehat H\) has the explicit exponential spectral decomposition (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}):
\[
\widehat H(\xi)
=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{M} m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}\,
e^{-(1+\delta_j)|\xi|}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\xi}.
\]
Therefore \( \xi\mapsto \widehat H(\xi)\,e^{|\xi|}\) (restricted to either side \(\xi\ge 0\) or \(\xi\le 0\)) is a linear combination of finitely many exponentials \(e^{-\delta_j|\xi|}\) and phase factors \(e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\xi}\), and this representation is unique when the exponential parameters are distinct.
Consequently, the full parameters of the off-line zeros admit a three-step decomposition auditable within the regime: first, uniquely recover \(\{\delta_j\}\) and the corresponding coefficients \(\{m_j\delta_j/(1+\delta_j)\}\) from the decay spectrum; next, uniquely recover \(\{\gamma_j\}\) from the phase spectrum (e.g., via Prony/matrix pencil/ESPRIT); and the multiplicities \(m_j\) are determined by coefficient ratios.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Computability of Endpoint Flux Reflection Coefficient: Exponential Approximation of \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_{-1}$}{Phi-(-1)} by Finite CMV Certificates]\label{con:xi-endpoint-flux-computable-by-finite-cmv}

\bigl|\Phi_{-1}(B)-\mathcal{F}_N\bigr|\ \le\ C\,q^N.

Moreover, \(\mathcal{F}_N\) depends only on the finite certificate parameters \((\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{N-1})\) and is realizable in pure multiply-add rational arithmetic.
Therefore, the endpoint flux is not merely an existential invariant but possesses an effectively approximable finite certificate expression: if the defect exists, its endpoint strength can be recovered by finite CMV certificates with exponential precision.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Unified Lower Bound for Two Types of Certificates: Unavoidable Growth of Toeplitz Order and Scan Coverage Number]\label{con:xi-toeplitz-vs-scan-complexity-lower-bounds}
Fix the offset lower bound \(\delta\ge\delta_0>0\) and height window \(|\gamma|\le T\).

N_{\mathrm{Toep}}\ \gtrsim\ Q_{\max}(T,\delta_0)\,\log\!\bigl(1+\delta_0 Q_{\max}(T,\delta_0)\bigr)

\paragraph{Toeplitz--Hankel Mutual Certification and Regime Consequences of Lattice Scanning}
The following conclusions further connect the ``comoving scan spectral fingerprint'' to the Toeplitz negative subspace and the finite-dimensional falsifier generation mechanism, and provide auditable thresholds induced by the global mass, local peaks, and lattice step size of \(H\).

\begin{proposition}[Isomorphic Splitting of Toeplitz--Scan Hankel: Negative Subspace is Spanned by Tomographic Spectral Fingerprints]\label{con:xi-toeplitz-hankel-isomorphic-split}
Under the finite point defect and no singular inner factor assumption, let \(F_{\zeta}=B\,O\) with \(B\) a finite Blaschke product (each factor expanded by multiplicity to degree one),
\[
B=\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}b_{a_j},
\qquad
a_j=\mathcal{C}(\gamma_j+\mathrm{i}\delta_j)\in\mathbb{D},
\qquad
\delta_j>0,
\]
where \(\kappa=\deg(B)=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F_{\zeta})\) (Definition \ref{def:blaschke-defect-dimension}).
Let the comoving scan profile \(H(x_0)\) and its Fourier spectrum \(\widehat H\) be as in Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}, and take integer sampling
\[
u_n:=\widehat H(n)
=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}\,e^{-(1+\delta_j)|n|}e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j n},
\qquad n\in\ZZ.
\]
Denote the Hankel window and Toeplitz certificate matrices respectively as
\[
\mathsf H_N:=(u_{p+q})_{0\le p,q\le N},
\qquad
\mathbf{T}_N:=(c_{j-k})_{0\le j,k\le N},
\]
where \(\mathbf{T}_N\) is generated by the moment data of the horizon Carath\'eodory interface \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}).
If there exists \(N_0\) such that \(\mathrm{rank}(\mathsf H_N)=\kappa\) for all \(N\ge N_0\) (equivalently: \(\mathsf H_{\kappa-1}\) has full rank), then for all sufficiently large \(N\),
\[
\nu_-\!\bigl(\mathbf{T}_N\bigr)=\kappa
\]
(Theorem \ref{thm:xi-toeplitz-negative-squares-equals-blaschke-defect}), and there exists an invertible matrix family \(R_N\) such that the Toeplitz quadratic form splits under congruence into a positive block and a negative block generated by the tomographic spectral fingerprint:
\[
R_N^{\ast}\mathbf{T}_N R_N
=
\begin{pmatrix}
P_N & 0\\
0 & -\,\mathbf{G}_{\kappa-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
P_N\succeq 0,
\qquad
\mathbf{G}_{\kappa-1}:=\mathsf H_{\kappa-1}^{\ast}\mathsf H_{\kappa-1}\succ 0.
\]
Therefore, all negative directions of the Toeplitz hierarchy are not diffusely generated in the regime semantics, but can be completely spanned and determined by finitely many scan spectral samples \(\{u_n\}_{0\le n\le 2\kappa-2}\) through the Hankel fingerprint; within the stable interval, both equivalently encode the same set of point defect degrees of freedom.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[\(L^1\)--\(L^2\)--\(L^\infty\) Unified Lower Bound for Cauchy--Poisson Profiles: Defects Necessarily Produce Detectable Peaks]\label{con:xi-poisson-profile-l1l2linfty-lower}
Remaining in the finite defect framework, write the comoving depth profile (expanded by multiplicity) as (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-basepoint-depth-closed-form})
\[
H(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\frac{4\delta_j}{(x-\gamma_j)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}.
\]
Then its \(L^1\) mass is a strict closed form (Corollary \ref{cor:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-mass}):
\[
\|H\|_{L^1(\RR)}=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}.
\]
Moreover, by non-negative cross terms and the kernel square integral formula
\(
\int_{\RR}\frac{\dd x}{(x^2+a^2)^2}=\frac{\pi}{2a^3}
\),
the orthogonal lower bound
\[
\|H\|_{L^2(\RR)}^2
\ge
\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}
\int_{\RR}\left(\frac{4\delta_j}{(x-\gamma_j)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}\right)^2\dd x
=
\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}8\pi\,\frac{\delta_j^2}{(1+\delta_j)^3}
\]
holds. Therefore there exists \(x^{\ast}\in\RR\) such that (\(\|H\|_2^2\le \|H\|_\infty\|H\|_1\))
\[
\|H\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\ge
\frac{\|H\|_{L^2(\RR)}^2}{\|H\|_{L^1(\RR)}}
\ge
2\cdot
\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\dfrac{\delta_j^2}{(1+\delta_j)^3}}
{\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\dfrac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}}.
\]
This inequality provides an irrecoverable peak witness: as long as point defects are nonempty, the scan profile necessarily exhibits a strictly detectable peak lower bound, whose magnitude is controlled by the weighted second moment of \(\{(\gamma_j,\delta_j)\}\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Auditable Squeeze for Defect Counting: Two-Sided Bounds from Mass and Peak]\label{con:xi-defect-count-mass-peak-sandwich}
Let \(\delta_{\min}:=\min_j\delta_j\), \(\delta_{\max}:=\max_j\delta_j\), and denote the total defect multiplicity (Blaschke degree) as \(\kappa=\deg(B)\).
Then from the \(L^1\) closed form of Proposition \ref{con:xi-poisson-profile-l1l2linfty-lower} and the monotonicity of \(x\mapsto \frac{x}{1+x}\),
\[
4\pi\,\frac{\delta_{\min}}{1+\delta_{\max}}\,\kappa
\le
\|H\|_{L^1(\RR)}
\le
4\pi\,\frac{\delta_{\max}}{1+\delta_{\min}}\,\kappa,
\]
so the mass statistic provides a two-sided auditable bound on the defect count:
\[
\frac{1+\delta_{\min}}{4\pi\,\delta_{\max}}\|H\|_{L^1(\RR)}
\le
\kappa
\le
\frac{1+\delta_{\max}}{4\pi\,\delta_{\min}}\|H\|_{L^1(\RR)}.
\]
On the other hand, from the termwise pointwise upper bound
\(
\frac{4\delta_j}{(x-\gamma_j)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}\le \frac{4\delta_j}{(1+\delta_j)^2}
\le \frac{4\delta_{\max}}{(1+\delta_{\min})^2}
\),
\[
\|H\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}
\le
\frac{4\delta_{\max}}{(1+\delta_{\min})^2}\,\kappa,
\qquad\text{i.e.,}\qquad
\kappa\ \ge\ \frac{(1+\delta_{\min})^2}{4\delta_{\max}}\,\|H\|_{L^\infty(\RR)}.
\]
Therefore, within the scan channels permitted by the regime, the defect count can be simultaneously squeezed by mass and peak statistics: mass provides upper and lower bounds, while the peak provides an unavoidable lower bound threshold.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Inversion Stability from Minimal Separation: Local Bi-Lipschitz Property of Finite-Sample Spectral Fingerprints]\label{con:xi-min-separation-bilip-prony}
Under the finite defect framework, let \(u_n:=\widehat H(n)\) as in Proposition \ref{con:xi-toeplitz-hankel-isomorphic-split}, and suppose there exists a separation constant \(s>0\) such that
\(
|\gamma_j-\gamma_k|\ge s
\)
for all \(j\neq k\), and \(\delta_j\in[\delta_{\min},\delta_{\max}]\subset(0,\infty)\).
Then the map from parameters to finite spectral fingerprints
\[
\{(\gamma_j,\delta_j)\}_{j=1}^{\kappa}\ \longmapsto\ \{u_n\}_{n=0}^{2\kappa-1}
\]
is locally bi-Lipschitz on the parameter manifold satisfying this separation constraint:
there exist constants \(C_1,C_2>0\) (depending only on \(\kappa,s,\delta_{\min},\delta_{\max}\)) such that for any two parameter multisets \(\Theta,\widetilde\Theta\) satisfying the same separation constraint,
\[
C_1\,d_{\mathrm{match}}(\Theta,\widetilde\Theta)
\le
\max_{0\le n\le 2\kappa-1}|u_n(\Theta)-u_n(\widetilde\Theta)|
\le
C_2\,d_{\mathrm{match}}(\Theta,\widetilde\Theta),
\]
where \(d_{\mathrm{match}}\) can be taken as the symmetrized matching distance (computing \(|\Delta\gamma|+|\Delta\delta|\) under optimal pairing).
Therefore, when minimal separation holds, the finite-sample scan spectral fingerprint not only uniquely determines the defect parameters (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}), but also possesses quantitative stability under noise perturbation; this stability is compatible with the standard inversion framework of Prony/matrix pencil/ESPRIT (see the Nyquist sampling paragraph of \S\ref{subsec:discrete-abel-weil-polar-rigidity}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Far-Field Moment Expansion: Tail Coefficients of the Scan Profile Yield Algebraic Moments of \texorpdfstring{$(\gamma,\delta)$}{(gamma,delta)}]\label{con:xi-farfield-moment-chain}
Let \(H\) be as in Proposition \ref{con:xi-poisson-profile-l1l2linfty-lower}.
Denote its complex pole set as
\(
x=\gamma_j\pm \mathrm{i}(1+\delta_j)
\)
(counted with multiplicity), and take
\(
R:=\max_j\sqrt{\gamma_j^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}.
\)
Then \(H\) has a convergent Laurent expansion at infinity for \(|x|>R\):
\[
H(x)=\sum_{n\ge 0}\frac{M_n}{x^{n+2}},
\qquad (|x|>R),
\]
where the coefficients \(M_n\) are explicit algebraic moments of the defect parameters.
In particular, the first two coefficients satisfy
\[
M_0=4\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\delta_j,
\qquad
M_1=8\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}\delta_j\gamma_j,
\]
and more generally, \(M_n\) is a linear combination of \(\sum_j \delta_j\,\gamma_j^{k}(1+\delta_j)^{\ell}\) (\(k+\ell\le n\)).
Therefore, the far-field tail provides a moment chain certificate in the purely one-dimensional visible channel: if one can estimate \((M_0,\dots,M_N)\) with auditable precision on some far-field interval, then the low-order moments of the defect measure in the \(\gamma\) direction and the mixed moments of \(\delta\) can be recovered, thereby compressing the two-dimensional off-line parameters into a sequence of verifiable algebraic moment invariants.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Aliasing Threshold of Address Discretization: Lattice Scanning Can Only Determine the Congruence Class of \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}]\label{con:xi-grid-scan-aliasing-threshold}
If the regime only permits reading the comoving scan profile \(H(x_0)\) at lattice points \(x_0=n\Delta\) (\(n\in\ZZ\)), then from the phase factor of the spectral fingerprint
\(
e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j n\Delta}
\),
one sees that lattice data alone cannot distinguish \(\gamma_j\) from \(\gamma_j+\frac{2\pi k}{\Delta}\) (\(k\in\ZZ\)) producing the same congruence class.
In particular, if the prior height window is \(\gamma_j\in[-T,T]\), then achieving alias-free unique determination requires the necessary condition
\[
\Delta<\frac{\pi}{T}.
\]
This threshold provides a regime-level lower bound on address resolution: when addresses are discretized to a fixed-step lattice, height information automatically folds into the congruence class modulo \(2\pi/\Delta\); to avoid this recovery effect, the step size must shrink linearly with the target height window (compatible with the Nyquist sampling scale of \S\ref{subsec:discrete-abel-weil-polar-rigidity}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Double-Step De-Aliasing Theorem: Phase Carriers from Two Address Grids Compress Height Congruence Classes to the Minimal Intersection]\label{con:xi-grid-scan-two-step-dealiasing}
Suppose the height phase can only enter the visible spectral fingerprint through the characters \(e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma n\Delta}\) (\(n\in\ZZ\)) of lattice scanning step \(\Delta>0\); then under a single \(\Delta\), the height can only be determined up to the congruence group
\[
\Gamma_{\Delta}:=\frac{2\pi}{\Delta}\ZZ.
\]
If a second step size \(\Delta'>0\) is additionally introduced, the visible invariance group contracts to the intersection
\[
\Gamma_{\Delta}\cap \Gamma_{\Delta'}.
\]
Moreover:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(\Delta/\Delta'\notin\QQ\), then \(\Gamma_{\Delta}\cap \Gamma_{\Delta'}=\{0\}\), so the height parameter has no global congruence aliasing in theory;
  \item If \(\Delta/\Delta'=p/q\in\QQ\) (in lowest terms), then there exists a unique \(d>0\) such that \(\Delta=pd,\ \Delta'=qd\), and
  \[
  \Gamma_{\Delta}\cap \Gamma_{\Delta'}=\frac{2\pi}{d}\ZZ,
  \]
  i.e., the dual grid contracts the aliasing period to the least common congruence period \(2\pi/d\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Local Peak Rigidity, Phase Spectral Shift, and Finite-Sample Ill-Posedness}
The following conclusions further compress the point defect profile of the comoving scan into a group of mutually aligned auditable interfaces of ``local peak geometry--phase flow density--finite-sample inversion'': on the one hand, the single-atom Lorentzian shape possesses an indestructible amplitude--half-width algebraic constraint; on the other hand, a finite defect family induces an absolutely continuous phase derivative density on the upper half-plane inner function, thereby normalizing the profile \(H\) as a weighted spectral shift; finally, the identifiability and unavoidable ill-posedness explosion of finite sampling can be jointly characterized by the minimum sample threshold and spectral point spacing lower bound of the Hankel/Prony fingerprint.

\begin{proposition}[Lorentzian Rigidity: Amplitude--Half-Width Algebraic Constraint and Consistency Test for Defect Peaks]\label{con:xi-lorentzian-amplitude-width-rigidity}
For a single point defect atom \((\gamma,\delta,m)\), the comoving profile kernel is
\[
H_{(\gamma,\delta,m)}(x)
:=
m\,\frac{4\delta}{(x-\gamma)^2+(1+\delta)^2}\qquad(x\in\RR),
\]
with peak value
\(
A:=H_{(\gamma,\delta,m)}(\gamma)
\)
and half-width at half-maximum \(w>0\) (the smallest positive number satisfying \(H_{(\gamma,\delta,m)}(\gamma\pm w)=A/2\)).
Then the strict rigidity relation
\[
\boxed{\;
w=1+\delta,\qquad
\frac{A}{m}=\frac{4(w-1)}{w^2}\; }
\]
holds. In particular, in the off-critical-line offset regime \(\delta\in(0,1)\) (so \(w\in(1,2)\)), letting \(a:=A/m\in(0,1)\), the quadratic equation \(aw^2-4w+4=0\) yields the closed-form inversion
\[
\boxed{\;
w=\frac{2\bigl(1-\sqrt{1-a}\bigr)}{a},\qquad
\delta=w-1\;}
\]
(taking the branch with \(w\in(1,2)\)).
Therefore: in the finite defect mixture case, the local structure of any peak identified as ``single-atom dominated isolated'' must satisfy the above amplitude--half-width consistency; this consistency provides a regime-internal geometric falsification criterion, and under this branch framework enables closed-form recovery of \(\delta\) from local geometric quantities of the peak.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral Shift Expression of Scan Density: Absolutely Continuous Phase Derivative of a Weighted Inner Function]\label{con:xi-spectral-shift-phase-density}
Let the finite point defect family have parameters \(\{(\gamma_j,\delta_j,m_j)\}_{j=1}^{\kappa}\), and define the inner function in the upper half-plane
\[
\mathcal{S}(z)
:=
\prod_{j=1}^{\kappa}
\left(
\frac{z-\gamma_j-\mathrm{i}(1+\delta_j)}{z-\gamma_j+\mathrm{i}(1+\delta_j)}
\right)^{m_j},
\qquad \Im z>0.
\]
Then its non-tangential boundary values satisfy \(|\mathcal{S}(x)|=1\) (a.e.\ \(x\in\RR\)), with the phase derivative formula
\[
\frac{\dd}{\dd x}\arg \mathcal{S}(x)
=
2\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{1+\delta_j}{(x-\gamma_j)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}
\qquad\text{(a.e.\ \(x\in\RR\))}.
\]
On the other hand, the comoving scan profile (point defect density)
\[
H(x)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{4\delta_j}{(x-\gamma_j)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}
\]
satisfies the strict weighted spectral shift representation
\[
\boxed{\;
H(x)
=
\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{2\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}\,
\frac{\dd}{\dd x}\arg\!\left(
\frac{x-\gamma_j-\mathrm{i}(1+\delta_j)}{x-\gamma_j+\mathrm{i}(1+\delta_j)}
\right)
\;}
\]
so that point defects in the protocol-visible domain are equivalent to a ``weighted spectral shift density'' (the absolutely continuous part of the phase flow).
Its total mass is
\[
\int_{\RR}H(x)\,\dd x
=
4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}
\]
(consistent with the mass formula of Corollary \ref{cor:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-mass}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Minimal Completeness of Finite Sampling: Necessity and Generic Sufficiency of \texorpdfstring{$2\kappa$}{2kappa}-Order Spectral Fingerprints]\label{con:xi-2kappa-minimal-complete-samples}
Fix step size \(\Delta>0\), and take the scan spectral fingerprint
\(
u_n:=\widehat H(n\Delta)
\)
(\(n\in\ZZ\)), where \(\widehat H\) is given by \eqref{eq:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier}.
Then for \(n\ge 0\) there exist complex parameters
\[
r_j:=e^{-(1+\delta_j)\Delta}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\Delta}\in\mathbb{D},
\qquad
c_j:=4\pi\,m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}>0,
\]
such that
\[
u_n=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}c_j\,r_j^{n}\qquad(n=0,1,2,\dots),
\qquad
u_{-n}=\overline{u_n}.
\]
Under the ``general position'' assumption (\(r_j\) pairwise distinct, and the Hankel principal block \((u_{i+j})_{0\le i,j\le \kappa-1}\) invertible), the consecutive samples
\(
(u_0,u_1,\dots,u_{2\kappa-1})
\)
alone suffice to uniquely recover the multiset \(\{(r_j,c_j)\}_{j=1}^{\kappa}\), and thereby uniquely determine \(\{(\gamma_j,\delta_j,m_j)\}\) (combined with the aliasing constraint of Proposition \ref{con:xi-grid-scan-aliasing-threshold}).
Moreover, this length is incompressible in general position: if the sample length \(N+1\le 2\kappa-1\), there exist two distinct parameter sets producing the same length-\((N+1)\) sample prefix.
This ``\(2\kappa\)-point lock'' is consistent with the minimal realization characterization of Hankel rank (Theorem \ref{thm:hankel-rank-minimal}) and the \(2d\)-point finite identification principle (Theorem \ref{thm:dephys-hankel-finite-audit}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Collision Ill-Posedness: Defect Merging Causes Inevitable Blow-Up of Inversion Condition Number]\label{con:xi-collision-illposedness-sep-blowup}
Continuing with the parametrization of Proposition \ref{con:xi-2kappa-minimal-complete-samples}, let the spectral point spacing be
\[
\mathrm{sep}:=\min_{j\neq k}|r_j-r_k|.
\]
Then for any inversion based on finite samples \((u_n)_{n=0}^{2\kappa-1}\) (including Prony/Hankel null space methods and their equivalent variants), the local error amplification factor (local Lipschitz constant of the inverse map) cannot remain uniformly bounded as \(\mathrm{sep}\downarrow 0\); more quantitatively, there exists a constant \(C_\kappa>0\) (depending only on \(\kappa\) and the sampling norm) such that within a general position neighborhood, the following blow-up lower bound holds:
\[
L\ \ge\ C_\kappa\,\mathrm{sep}^{-(\kappa-1)}.
\]
In particular, when two defects satisfy \(\delta_j=\delta_k\) and \(|\gamma_j-\gamma_k|\to 0\), one has \(|r_j-r_k|\asymp \Delta|\gamma_j-\gamma_k|\), so the error amplification factor of any stable inversion diverges at least as \(|\gamma_j-\gamma_k|^{-(\kappa-1)}\).
This conclusion characterizes the ``conceptual collision implies recovery'' ill-posedness in the regime semantics: defects approaching each other in visible coordinates inevitably trigger inversion ill-posedness, manifesting as the resource-theoretic consequence of merging/indistinguishability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Double Residue Ratio Law: \texorpdfstring{$\delta$}{delta} Identification Mechanism without Separation Assumption]\label{con:xi-double-residue-ratio-delta}
Under the finite defect framework, define the Cauchy transform (\(\Im z>0\))
\[
M_H(z):=\int_{\RR}\frac{H(x)}{x-z}\,\dd x,
\]
and the ``phase flow generator'' induced by the upper half-plane inner function \(\mathcal{S}\) from Proposition \ref{con:xi-spectral-shift-phase-density}:
\[
M_{\mathcal{S}}(z):=-\mathrm{i}\,\frac{\mathcal{S}'(z)}{\mathcal{S}(z)}.
\]
Both admit meromorphic continuation to \(\CC\setminus\RR\) and share the same pole set in the lower half-plane:
\[
z_j^{\downarrow}:=\gamma_j-\mathrm{i}(1+\delta_j)\qquad(1\le j\le \kappa),
\]
with residues given by the explicit closed forms
\[
\operatorname*{Res}_{z=z_j^{\downarrow}}M_H(z)
=-4\pi\,m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j},
\qquad
\operatorname*{Res}_{z=z_j^{\downarrow}}M_{\mathcal{S}}(z)
=\mathrm{i}\,m_j.
\]
Hence the strict ``residue ratio'' identification law
\[
\boxed{\;
\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}
=
\frac{1}{4\pi}\left|
\frac{\operatorname*{Res}_{z=z_j^{\downarrow}}M_H(z)}
{\operatorname*{Res}_{z=z_j^{\downarrow}}M_{\mathcal{S}}(z)}
\right|\;}
\]
holds. This formula does not depend on minimal separation of \(\{\gamma_j\}\): as long as the poles remain distinct (i.e., \((\gamma,\delta)\) are not all identical), \(\delta_j\) can be directly recovered from analytic residues within the protocol-visible domain; separation affects only numerical stability (see Proposition \ref{con:xi-collision-illposedness-sep-blowup}), not analytic identifiability.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[No-Hair Limit of Poisson Coarse-Graining: Only Finitely Many Moment Invariants Remain at Large Scales]\label{con:xi-poisson-coarsegraining-nohair}
Let the low-order moments of the defect measure be
\[
M_0:=\sum_{j}m_j\delta_j,
\qquad
M_1:=\sum_{j}m_j\delta_j\,\gamma_j,
\qquad
\bar\gamma:=\frac{M_1}{M_0}\ \ (M_0>0),
\]
and let the Poisson semigroup coarse-grained profile \(G_t\) be as defined in \eqref{eq:xi-offcritical-comoving-poisson-field}.
Then \(M_0=\int_{\RR}G_t\) and \(M_1=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{\RR}xG_t\) are strict conservation quantities (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-poisson-conservation-dissipation}).
Moreover, as \(t\to\infty\), there exists a uniform asymptotic expansion
\[
G_t(x)
=
\frac{M_0}{\pi}\frac{t}{(x-\bar\gamma)^2+t^2}
\;+\;O(t^{-2}),
\]
where the remainder is uniform in \(x\in\RR\) and can be controlled by the second central moment of the defects.
Therefore, when the observation scale \(t\) is sufficiently large, all point defects in the protocol-visible domain inevitably degenerate into a single ``effective Lorentzian'': the distinguishable parameters are only the total mass \(M_0\) and the centroid \(\bar\gamma\); higher-order geometric information is strictly suppressed at the \(t^{-2}\) level and decays under the coarse-graining flow (consistent with the energy dissipation formula \eqref{eq:xi-offcritical-poisson-energy-dissipation}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Second-Order Normal Form of Poisson Coarse-Graining: Universal Correction Term Controlled Solely by the Second Central Moment]\label{con:xi-poisson-coarsegraining-second-order-normal-form}
Under the notation of Proposition \ref{con:xi-poisson-coarsegraining-nohair}, let the normalized weight measure be
\[
\tilde\nu:=\frac{1}{M_0}\sum_{j}m_j\delta_j\,\delta_{\gamma_j},
\qquad
\bar\gamma:=\int_{\RR}\gamma\,\dd\tilde\nu(\gamma),
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}:=\int_{\RR}(\gamma-\bar\gamma)^2\,\dd\tilde\nu(\gamma),
\]
and define the large-scale Poisson coarse-grained density
\[
h_t(x):=\int_{\RR}\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{t}{(x-\gamma)^2+t^2}\,\dd\tilde\nu(\gamma)
\qquad (t>0).
\]
Then as \(t\to\infty\), there exists a second-order uniform asymptotic expansion (valid uniformly in \(x\in\RR\)):
\[
\boxed{\;
h_t(x)
=
\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{t}{(x-\bar\gamma)^2+t^2}
\;+\;
\frac{\mathrm{Var}}{\pi}\,
\frac{t\bigl(3(x-\bar\gamma)^2-t^2\bigr)}{\bigl((x-\bar\gamma)^2+t^2\bigr)^3}
\;+\;O(t^{-4})\; }.
\]
The remainder \(O(t^{-4})\) can be controlled by the third central moment \(\int|\gamma-\bar\gamma|^3\,\dd\tilde\nu(\gamma)\).
Therefore, under large-scale horizon observation, the visible contribution of all geometric degrees of freedom collapses strictly to a two-parameter family \((\bar\gamma,\mathrm{Var})\) before second order: higher-order shape information is regime-suppressed at progressively higher powers and enters the irrecoverable zone.
\end{proposition}
