\section{Introduction}

The geometry of partition-function zeros provides a unifying description of critical phenomena in statistical physics and analytic combinatorics.
Classical approaches often rely on large-state numerical approximations, whereas finite-dimensional \emph{spectral-closure} models allow one to turn the localization of a ``critical boundary'' into exact algebraic and spectral computations.

In this note we study a family of partition functions \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) controlled by a quartic spectral curve.
The main features are:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the sequence is fully determined by a reduced rational generating function, hence satisfies a linear recurrence of fixed order and has fixed Hankel rank;
  \item the algebraic structure of the branch locus is given by a complete factorization of the discriminant of a quartic spectral polynomial; the only nontrivial real branch point \(\yLY<0\) marks a collision and real-to-complex transition among subdominant spectral roots (Proposition~\ref{prop:yLY-collision});
  \item a limiting log-moment generating function induced by the dominant spectral branch yields closed-form fluctuation constants, producing auditable limit laws;
  \item all key quantities can be computed (and certified) by finitely many algebraic operations together with an order-\(4\) recurrence, giving a parameter-size-independent verification pipeline.
\end{enumerate}

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model and the associated quartic spectral polynomial.
Section~\ref{sec:hankel} establishes minimality of the order-\(4\) spectral closure and the fixed Hankel rank.
Section~\ref{sec:disc} computes and factors the discriminant, characterizing the branch locus and the spectral-root collision at~\(\yLY\).
Section~\ref{sec:free} derives the free energy and the limiting log-moment generating function, together with a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem with explicit constants.
Sections~\ref{sec:zeros}--\ref{sec:algo} discuss zero accumulation, and give an auditable computational workflow.
