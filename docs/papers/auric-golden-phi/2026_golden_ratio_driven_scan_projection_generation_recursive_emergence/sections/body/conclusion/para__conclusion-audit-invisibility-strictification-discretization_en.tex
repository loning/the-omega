\paragraph{Integer audit blindness, sparsification flows, and constant-level reduction: from cyclotomic invisibility to the discretization regime of phase closure}
Within the ``finite certificate + offline verification'' protocol caliber of this paper, the \(\bmod p^k\) integrality guardrails, constant-level strictification, and unitary-slice phase closure verification jointly form a discrete constraint chain orthogonal to Archimedean spectral-radius/pole data; the key consequences can be summarized as:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Cyclotomic invisibility: \texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-power-level integer audits are blind to root-of-unity twists.}
  If the trace ghost sequence satisfies \(p\)-power Dwork congruences, then for any odd prime \(p\), all \(u^p=1\), and all \(k\ge 1\),
  \[
  a_{p^k}(u)\equiv a_{p^k}(1)\pmod{p^k}
  \]
  (Corollary~\ref{cor:witt-cyclotomic-invisibility}).
  Thus all \(p\)-power length-level \(\bmod p^k\) checks cannot distinguish ``twisted'' from ``untwisted''; the twist discrepancy manifests only in Archimedean spectral radius and constant-drift layers.

  \item \textbf{Frobenius sparsification renormalization flow: high-level information compresses to low-level Frobenius pushforward at fixed precision.}
  Dwork congruence coefficient-by-coefficient forces \(p^k\)-sparsification: polynomials modulo \(p^k\) land in \((\ZZ/p^k\ZZ)[u^p]\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:witt-pk-sparsification});
  furthermore, for any \(1\le \ell\le k\) there exists an iterated descent
  \(a_{p^k}(u)\equiv a_{p^{\ell-1}}(u^{p^{k-\ell+1}})\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p^\ell)\)
  (Proposition~\ref{prop:witt-frobenius-iterated-descent}), so at fixed precision \(p^\ell\) all information from ``high level \(p^k\)'' pushes down to lower levels solely via power substitution.

  \item \textbf{Reducibility of constant-level anomaly: drift vectors form an \texorpdfstring{$\mathcal{A}_G$}{AG}-torsor.}
  Over a finite abelian lift group \(G\), the pure constant counterterm gate \(\mathrm{CT}_\eta\) preserves pole data and shifts only finite-part drifts additively (Definition~\ref{def:pom-finite-part-counterterm-gate}; Proposition~\ref{prop:pom-counterterm-anom-cancel}),
  so the family of admissible drift vectors under fixed pole data forms an affine fiber with structure group \(\mathcal{A}_G\) (Remark~\ref{rem:xi-finite-part-drift-torsor}).

  \item \textbf{Strong discretization of phase-closure verification: Tate obstruction degenerates to a single Maslov index and regime-level exclusion of continuous residual axes.}
  For self-dual bounded-type functions in the Hardy/Smirnov model, the equivariant Tate obstruction splits into a point-wise integer part and a boundary continuous part; if the regime requires unitary-slice phase-closure verification, then necessarily the singular inner factor \(S_F\equiv 1\), so the obstruction degenerates to a single integer \(\deg(B_F)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:selfdual-maslov-singular-dichotomy}; see also Theorem~\ref{thm:xi-tate-maslov-pontryagin-unification}).

  \item \textbf{Bartholdi fugacity infinite-root cluster mechanism: \texorpdfstring{$13$}{13} necessarily escaping branches emerge at \texorpdfstring{$t=1$}{t=1}.}
  In the Bartholdi two-variable determinant closed form for the real-input kernel, \(F(z,t)\) undergoes a \(z\)-degree collapse \(20\to 7\) as \(t\to 1\), so in the projective sense there must be \(13\) root branches escaping to infinity (Remark~\ref{rem:real-input-40-bartholdi-infinite-root-cluster}; Theorem~\ref{thm:real-input-40-bartholdi-det}).
  More precisely, the \((t-1)\)-adic Newton--Puiseux polygon exhibits three negative-slope edges at \(t=1\), yielding the escape exponent spectrum \(\{1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13\}\) and leading-term initial forms, thereby elevating the escape multiplicity and leading-term algebraic fields to auditable pure-algebraic outputs (Propositions~\ref{prop:real-input-40-bartholdi-newton-puiseux-spectrum},~\ref{prop:real-input-40-bartholdi-escape-initial-forms}; Remark~\ref{rem:real-input-40-bartholdi-graded-spectrum}).

  \item \textbf{Quadratic discriminant law for worst character: selection-law discriminant \texorpdfstring{$g(p)$}{g(p)} limit constant given solely by \texorpdfstring{$\Sigma_{33}$}{Sigma33}.}
  In discrete-character scanning, let \(\Sigma=\nabla^2P(0)\) be the equilibrium Fisher quadratic form; then
  \[
  g(p):=\left(1-\frac{\rho_{3,3,p}}{\lambda}\right)p^2
  \xrightarrow[p\to\infty]{}\ 2\pi^2\,\Sigma_{33}
  \]
  (Remark~\ref{rem:arity-335-selection-law-gp}), compressing the ``worst-character'' large-modulus limit scaling constant into a falsifiable law given by a single covariance matrix element.

  \item \textbf{Integer-lattice rigidity of the Green kernel: spectral \texorpdfstring{$4\sin^2$}{4 sin^2} coefficient is a non-scalable arithmetic normalization constant.}
  The sawtooth Green kernel \(K_k(i,j)=\min(i,j)\) has an integer Gram factorization with \(\det(K_k)=1\) (Lemma~\ref{lem:pom-Kk-gram-det}), forcing
  \(\prod_{p=1}^k 4\sin^2(\varphi_p)=1\)
  and corresponding sum identities (Corollary~\ref{cor:pom-Kk-sine-product-sum});
  the coefficient \(4\) is pinned by integer-lattice normalization rigidity and cannot be absorbed by arbitrary rescaling.
\end{enumerate}
