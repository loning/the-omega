\section{Emergent gauge fields: compensating local phase errors under Weyl complementarity}
\label{sec:gauge_fields}

\subsection{Weyl-pair complementarity and unavoidable local phase jitter}
\label{subsec:weyl_jitter}

In the scan--phase algebra, the Weyl relation
\begin{equation}
  UV = \e^{2\pi \iu \alpha} VU
\end{equation}
implies that scan localization (readout tick/position) and phase-mode localization (frequency/energy) cannot be simultaneously sharpened. In CAP language, finite-resolution readout of phase necessarily introduces local phase errors $\delta\theta(x)$, which can accumulate into macroscopic structure.

This is the field-theoretic translation of the layer tension. The readout layer cannot simultaneously enforce sharp time ticks (scan localization) and sharp phase coherence (mode localization) because the underlying operators do not commute. Therefore, any effective continuum description that treats phase as a local degree of freedom must include a mechanism that tracks and compensates unavoidable phase jitter.

\subsection{From local rephasing to a connection field}
\label{subsec:gauge_connection}

If readout errors are interpreted as uncontrolled local rephasings
\begin{equation}
  \psi(x) \mapsto \e^{\iu \lambda(x)}\psi(x),
\end{equation}
then keeping the effective action invariant under this redundancy requires a compensating connection $A_{\mu}$ and the replacement of derivatives by covariant derivatives:
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_{\mu} \;\to\; D_{\mu} = \nabla_{\mu} - \iu q A_{\mu},
  \qquad
  A_{\mu} \mapsto A_{\mu} + \partial_{\mu}\lambda .
\end{equation}
This is the standard minimal-coupling construction for local $U(1)$ redundancy \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,Nakahara2003}.
At second order in derivatives, the minimal local gauge kinetic term is the Yang--Mills quadratic form $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ \cite{YangMills1954,WuYang1975}, which can be included in $\mathcal{L}_m$ in \eqref{eq:omega_action}.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic gauge rigidity]
\label{prop:gauge_rigidity}
Assume an effective local action contains a gauge sector for a $U(1)$ connection $A_\mu$ that is (i) invariant under $A_\mu\mapsto A_\mu+\partial_\mu\lambda$, (ii) local and Lorentz covariant, (iii) built using at most first derivatives of $A_\mu$, and (iv) quadratic in $A_\mu$ and its derivatives in the bulk. Then, up to an overall normalization and boundary terms, the unique bulk quadratic kinetic term is $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$, where $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Gauge invariance forbids dependence on $A_\mu$ without derivatives in the pure gauge sector, and the only gauge-invariant tensor built from first derivatives of $A_\mu$ is the antisymmetric combination $F_{\mu\nu}$. Any local Lorentz scalar quadratic in first derivatives therefore reduces (up to integration by parts) to a linear combination of $F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$ and $\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F_{\mu\nu}F_{\rho\sigma}$; the latter is a total derivative in four dimensions and does not affect the bulk Euler--Lagrange equations \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,Nakahara2003}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Nonabelian extension.}
If the effective phase redundancy is promoted from a $U(1)$ rephasing to a local internal symmetry group $G$, the same compensating logic yields a nonabelian connection $A_\mu=A_\mu^a T^a$ and curvature $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu+[A_\mu,A_\nu]$ \cite{Nakahara2003}. CAP does not fix $G$ at the present level; it fixes that some compensating connection is required once local phase alignment is demanded.

\noindent The key point is structural: gauge fields need not be postulated as external symmetries; they arise as the minimal compensating structure demanded by \emph{local phase consistency} under Weyl complementarity and finite readout resolution. In this sense, gauge dynamics is the variationally cheapest ``patch'' that keeps local phase descriptions compatible with finite-resolution readout.
