\section{Resolution of the Information Paradox}
\label{sec:information_paradox}

\subsection{Unitary Scanning and Modular Flow}
The central claim of Paper~I \cite{Ma2025HPA} is that the \textbf{Omega Operator} $\Theta$ is fundamentally unitary (scan time is an honest unitary evolution). A natural bridge to continuum QFT is provided by Tomita--Takesaki theory: for a von Neumann algebra $\mathcal{A}$ acting on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and a cyclic separating vector $|\Omega\rangle$, there exists a modular operator $\Delta_\Omega$ and modular conjugation $J_\Omega$ such that the modular flow is implemented by the unitary one-parameter group $\Delta_\Omega^{it}$ \cite{Takesaki2002, Borchers2000, Witten2018}.
\par
In algebraic QFT, modular data are not merely formal: for the vacuum state and a Rindler wedge algebra, the modular automorphism group is precisely the Lorentz-boost flow and the state satisfies a KMS condition at the Unruh temperature; moreover, modular conjugation implements a wedge reflection (TCP) and exchanges the wedge algebra with its commutant. These statements are the content of the Bisognano--Wichmann theorem and its standard expositions \cite{BisognanoWichmann1975, BisognanoWichmann1976, Haag1996}.

\begin{assumption}[Modular-scan identification]
\label{assump:modular_scan}
Omega scanning is modeled as a \emph{discretization} of modular flow: for some choice of $(\mathcal{A},|\Omega\rangle)$ and a fixed scan step $\delta>0$,
\begin{equation}
  \Theta \;\equiv\; \Delta_\Omega^{i\delta}.
\end{equation}
This assumption is motivated by the thermal-time interpretation of modular flow \cite{ConnesRovelli1994}.
\end{assumption}

The second modular object, $J_\Omega$, is an \emph{antiunitary} involution satisfying $J_\Omega \mathcal{A} J_\Omega = \mathcal{A}'$ (the commutant) \cite{Takesaki2002}. We use this structure to represent the holographic inversion channel as an antiunitary map exchanging bulk/interior and boundary readout algebras:
\begin{equation}
  |\Psi_{\mathrm{out}}\rangle \;=\; J_{\mathrm{inv}}\,|\Psi_{\mathrm{in}}\rangle ,
\end{equation}
with $J_{\mathrm{inv}}$ playing the role of modular conjugation in the scan Hilbert space. This is compatible with the \textbf{Black Holes as Mirrors} picture \cite{HaydenPreskill2007}: once the dynamics is unitary and sufficiently scrambling \cite{SekinoSusskind2008}, information is not destroyed but redistributed into subtle correlations.

\begin{axiom}[Algebraic inversion channel]
\label{ax:algebraic_inversion}
\mbox{}\par
There exist von Neumann algebras $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}$ (infalling/bulk) and $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bdry}}$ (boundary readout) acting on $\mathcal{H}$.
\par
There exists an antiunitary involution $J_{\mathrm{inv}}$ such that
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{inv}}\,\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}\,J_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bdry}},
  \qquad J_{\mathrm{inv}}^2=\ind.
\end{equation}
In the Tomita--Takesaki template, one may take $\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bdry}}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{bulk}}'$ and $J_{\mathrm{inv}}=J_\Omega$ for a suitable cyclic separating $|\Omega\rangle$ \cite{Takesaki2002}.
\end{axiom}

\subsection{Hawking Radiation as Decrypted Phase}
Hawking radiation \cite{Hawking1975} is traditionally modeled as approximately thermal, leading to the information loss paradox if the evaporation map is taken to be fundamentally non-unitary.
In Omega Dynamics, the outgoing data are modeled as a \textbf{coarse-grained readout channel} acting on the boundary phase sequence produced by unitary scanning together with the inversion channel (Axiom~\ref{ax:algebraic_inversion}). In this paper we do not attempt to derive the detailed semiclassical spectrum; instead we exhibit a minimal deterministic coding mechanism in which marginal statistics can look featureless while correlations remain structured.

\begin{definition}[A minimal phase-to-radiation readout channel]
Let $x_n:=\vartheta_n/(2\pi)\pmod{1}\in[0,1)$ be the normalized scan phase. Define a binary radiation symbol by a two-interval coarse-graining,
\begin{equation}
  s_n :=
  \begin{cases}
    0,& x_n\in[0,1-\alpha),\\
    1,& x_n\in[1-\alpha,1),
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is the irrational scan slope (Paper~I). This is the standard mechanical-word coding of an irrational rotation \cite{Lothaire2002, LindMarcus1995}.
We refer to Paper~I for the concrete choice of $\alpha$ (e.g.\ the golden slope) and the associated Koopman-unitary model \cite{Ma2025HPA}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Sturmian structure of the readout]
If $\alpha\notin\QQ$ then $(s_n)$ is Sturmian (equivalently, it has minimal subword complexity among aperiodic binary sequences) \cite{MorseHedlund1940, Lothaire2002}; see Appendix~\ref{app:sturmian}.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Thermality in Marginals vs.\ Information in Correlations}
The symbolic readout model cleanly separates coarse one-point statistics and fine multi-time correlations.

\begin{proposition}[Single-symbol statistics from equidistribution]
In the irrational-rotation model $x_n=\{n\alpha+\beta\}$ with $\alpha\notin\QQ$, the binary readout defined by the window $W=[1-\alpha,1)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} s_n
  =\mu(W)
  =\alpha,
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is Lebesgue measure on $[0,1)$. Equivalently, the frequency of the symbol $1$ converges to $\alpha$ \cite{Weyl1916}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Low complexity and vanishing entropy rate of Sturmian readouts]
If $(s_n)$ is Sturmian, its factor complexity satisfies $p(m)=m+1$ \cite{MorseHedlund1940, Lothaire2002}. Moreover, Sturmian subshifts are minimal and uniquely ergodic, hence admit a unique shift-invariant probability measure $\mu$ \cite{LindMarcus1995, KatokHasselblatt1995}. Let $P_m(w)$ denote the $\mu$-frequency of length-$m$ words $w\in\{0,1\}^m$. Then the block Shannon entropy
\begin{equation}
  H_m:=-\sum_{w\in\{0,1\}^m} P_m(w)\,\log P_m(w)
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  H_m \le \log(m+1),
\end{equation}
and therefore the entropy rate $\limsup_{m\to\infty}H_m/m$ vanishes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At most $p(m)=m+1$ distinct words of length $m$ occur in a Sturmian sequence, hence at most $m+1$ words have positive $\mu$-frequency. Any probability distribution supported on at most $m+1$ points has Shannon entropy bounded by $\log(m+1)$, which proves the stated inequality. Dividing by $m$ yields zero entropy rate.
\end{proof}

The first proposition supplies a simple mechanism for thermal-looking \emph{marginals} under coarse-graining. The second shows that the readout remains globally constrained: while the one-point Shannon entropy $H_1=-(1-\alpha)\log(1-\alpha)-\alpha\log\alpha$ is positive, the entropy \emph{rate} is zero, so information can be stored in long-range correlations rather than in one-point statistics.

\begin{remark}[Deterministic coding vs.\ stochastic readout]
The mechanical-word coding is a deterministic factor of an irrational rotation and therefore has zero entropy rate, even though its one-point entropy is positive. In Paper~I \cite{Ma2025HPA}, the observable layer involves a lossy projection readout (instrument/POVM structure) that can introduce genuine stochasticity under coarse-graining; the present subsection isolates the logical point that thermal-looking marginals do not imply information loss when information is stored in correlations.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Decoding Template: Canonical Coding from Paper I}
Paper~I \cite{Ma2025HPA} develops a self-contained scan--projection model together with canonical integer-time coordinates (Ostrowski/Zeckendorf in the golden case) and orbit-calculus tools (orbit traces and finite-part prescriptions). In the present context, these constructions provide an explicit arithmetic template for how information can reside in the correlation structure of an apparently featureless readout: decoding is not performed from single-symbol frequencies but from the constrained combinatorics of long words and their canonical block decompositions.

Finally, in random-unitary/scrambling models of evaporation, Page's theorem implies an entropy turnover at the Page time for typical pure states \cite{Page1993, HaydenPreskill2007}. In Omega Dynamics, the corresponding turnover is attributed to the emergence of long-range phase correlations induced by the inversion channel $J_{\mathrm{inv}}$, rather than to fundamental non-unitarity.
In this sense, the black hole acts as a \textbf{holographic storage channel}: coarse-grained observables are approximately thermal, while fine-grained correlations carry the information.
