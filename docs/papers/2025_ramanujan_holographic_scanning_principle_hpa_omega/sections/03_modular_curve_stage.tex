\section{The arithmetic-geometric stage: the modular curve, hyperbolic fundamental domain, and cusps}
\label{sec:modular_curve_stage}

This paper's key structural move is to place the minimal scan model of Section~\ref{sec:axioms_O5_O6} on an arithmetic-geometric mother space: the modular curve $X(1)$. This section fixes standard definitions and the single geometric fact we will repeatedly exploit: \emph{cusps are identified in the modular quotient, producing a canonical endpoint equivalence}.

\subsection{The upper half-plane and negative curvature}
\label{sec:upper_half_plane}

Let
\begin{equation}
\HH = \{\tau\in\CC : \Im \tau > 0\}
\end{equation}
be the Poincar\'e upper half-plane equipped with the hyperbolic metric
\begin{equation}
\dd s^2 = \frac{\dd\tau\,\dd\overline{\tau}}{(\Im \tau)^2}.
\end{equation}
One reason hyperbolic geometry is a natural stage under holographic constraints is the exponential growth of boundary ``capacity'' with hyperbolic radius. While O2 does not uniquely force hyperbolic geometry, the combination ``maximal boundary encoding efficiency + strong discrete symmetry'' makes $\HH$ a canonical candidate arena.

\subsection{The modular group, fundamental domain, and cusps}
\label{sec:modular_group}

The modular group $\PSL_2(\ZZ)$ acts on $\HH$ by fractional linear transformations:
\begin{equation}
\tau \mapsto \gamma\cdot\tau = \frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d},\qquad
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}\in \PSL_2(\ZZ).
\end{equation}
It is generated by
\begin{equation}
T:\tau\mapsto \tau+1,\qquad
S:\tau\mapsto -\frac{1}{\tau}.
\end{equation}
The standard fundamental domain $\mathcal{F}\subset\HH$ is bounded by $|\tau|=1$ and $\Re\tau=\pm \tfrac12$, with the ``infinite end'' corresponding to the cusp $\tau = \iu\infty$.

By adjoining the cusps and taking the quotient, one obtains the modular curve
\begin{equation}
X(1) = \PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash \left(\HH \cup \{\text{cusps}\}\right).
\end{equation}
There is a single cusp on $X(1)$: in the quotient, $0$ and $\infty$ lie in the same $\PSL_2(\ZZ)$-orbit (indeed, $S$ exchanges $0$ and $\infty$), so they define the same cusp class.

\begin{remark}[interpretation-layer hint: UV/IR endpoint identification]
The identification $0\sim\infty$ is an arithmetic-topological statement about the modular quotient. Any physical reading---for example, interpreting $S$ as a scale inversion template---belongs to Layer~2 and is not used as a premise.
\end{remark}
