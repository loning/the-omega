\section{An explicit HTF model: Frobenius trace and Weil zeta for curves over finite fields}
\label{app:htf_function_field}

This appendix records a fully explicit instance of an HTF-style trace bridge in a setting where the analogue of RH is known: zeta functions of smooth projective curves over finite fields.
The purpose is not to re-prove the Weil conjectures, but to exhibit a concrete trace identity in which spectral modes appear through resolvent factors and the pole-barrier mechanism becomes an elementary spectral-radius statement.

\subsection{Weil zeta and Frobenius eigenmodes}
Let $C$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve of genus $g$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$.
Define the point counts $N_n:=\#C(\mathbb{F}_{q^n})$ and the Weil zeta function
\[
Z_C(u):=\exp\!\left(\sum_{n\ge 1} \frac{N_n}{n}\,u^n\right).
\]
It is a rational function of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:weil_zeta_rational}
Z_C(u)=\frac{P_C(u)}{(1-u)(1-qu)},
\qquad
P_C(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{2g}\left(1-\alpha_j u\right),
\end{equation}
where the $\alpha_j$ are algebraic numbers encoding the Frobenius spectrum on the first cohomology of $C$ (see, e.g., \cite{Rosen2002}).
The nontrivial zeros of $Z_C$ occur at $u=\alpha_j^{-1}$.
The Weil RH statement for curves is the modulus condition
\[
\abs{\alpha_j}=q^{1/2}\qquad (1\le j\le 2g),
\]
equivalently $\abs{\alpha_j^{-1}}=q^{-1/2}$.

\subsection{A resolvent trace generating function and interior poles}
Define the normalized eigenvalues
\[
\lambda_j:=\frac{\alpha_j}{q^{1/2}},
\qquad 1\le j\le 2g.
\]
Consider the formal power series
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:function_field_trace_series}
G(r):=\sum_{n\ge 0}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{2g}\lambda_j^n\right) r^n,
\qquad r\in\CC.
\end{equation}
Whenever the sequence $\sum_j\lambda_j^n$ is bounded, the series \eqref{eq:function_field_trace_series} is holomorphic on the unit disk $|r|<1$ (cf.\ Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded_trace_holomorphy}).
On the other hand, for $|r|<1$ one has the resolvent identity
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:function_field_resolvent}
G(r)=\sum_{j=1}^{2g}\frac{1}{1-r\lambda_j},
\end{equation}
which is meromorphic with poles at $r=\lambda_j^{-1}$.
Thus:
\begin{itemize}
\item if $\abs{\lambda_j}>1$ for some $j$, then $r=\lambda_j^{-1}$ is a pole strictly inside $|r|<1$;
\item if $G(r)$ is holomorphic on $|r|<1$, then necessarily $\abs{\lambda_j}\le 1$ for all $j$;
\item applying the same argument to the dual spectrum (or using the functional equation symmetry in \eqref{eq:weil_zeta_rational}) forces $\abs{\lambda_j}=1$ for all $j$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relation to the pole-barrier mechanism}
The identity \eqref{eq:function_field_resolvent} is a concrete trace bridge in the same structural form as Assumption~\ref{ass:HTF}:
the geometric-side holomorphy of an Abel-type trace on $|r|<1$ is incompatible with interior poles created by exponential-growth spectral modes.
In the function-field setting, the spectral data are explicit (Frobenius eigenvalues), and the ``critical line'' statement becomes the unit-modulus constraint $\abs{\lambda_j}=1$, i.e.\ $\abs{\alpha_j}=q^{1/2}$.
This provides a fully worked model demonstrating that an HTF package can be realized as a genuine trace identity rather than as a purely heuristic slogan:
the resolvent identity gives the mode decomposition (HTF-Modes), the unit-disk holomorphy criterion is explicit, and interior poles are genuine unless the corresponding mode coefficient vanishes (HTF-NonCancel).

\subsection{A concrete genus-one example (explicit poles and noncancellation)}
\label{sec:htf_function_field_genus_one}

We now instantiate the discussion on an explicit genus-one curve, where all objects can be written down concretely.

\paragraph{Elliptic curve over \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{F}_5$}{F5}.}
Let $q=5$ and consider the elliptic curve
\[
E:\ y^2=x^3+x+1
\qquad \text{over }\mathbb{F}_5.
\]
Direct point counting gives $\#E(\mathbb{F}_5)=9$, hence the Frobenius trace is
\[
a:=q+1-\#E(\mathbb{F}_5)=6-9=-3.
\]
The Weil zeta numerator is therefore
\[
P_E(u)=1-a u+q u^2=1+3u+5u^2.
\]
The Frobenius eigenvalues $\alpha_\pm$ are the roots of $T^2-aT+q=0$, i.e.
\[
\alpha_\pm=\frac{a\pm\sqrt{a^2-4q}}{2}
=\frac{-3\pm \iu\sqrt{11}}{2},
\qquad
\abs{\alpha_\pm}=\sqrt{5}.
\]
The normalized eigenvalues are
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:genus_one_lambdas}
\lambda_\pm:=\frac{\alpha_\pm}{\sqrt{q}}=\frac{-3\pm \iu\sqrt{11}}{2\sqrt{5}},
\qquad
\abs{\lambda_\pm}=1,
\qquad
\lambda_-=\overline{\lambda_+}=\lambda_+^{-1}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Geometric-side boundedness and unit-disk holomorphy.}
In genus $g=1$ one has $2g=2$ modes, and the coefficient sequence in \eqref{eq:function_field_trace_series} becomes
\[
b_n:=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\lambda_j^n=\lambda_+^n+\lambda_-^n=2\RePart(\lambda_+^n),
\qquad n\ge 0.
\]
Since $\abs{\lambda_\pm}=1$, the coefficients are bounded by $\abs{b_n}\le 2$ for all $n$.
Therefore the Abel series $G(r)=\sum_{n\ge 0} b_n r^n$ is holomorphic on $|r|<1$ (cf.\ Lemma~\ref{lem:bounded_trace_holomorphy}).

\paragraph{Spectral-side resolvent decomposition and explicit poles.}
The resolvent identity \eqref{eq:function_field_resolvent} is completely explicit here:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:genus_one_resolvent_explicit}
G(r)=\frac{1}{1-r\lambda_+}+\frac{1}{1-r\lambda_-}.
\end{equation}
Hence $G$ is meromorphic with simple poles at
\[
r_\pm=\lambda_\pm^{-1}=\overline{\lambda_\pm},
\qquad
|r_\pm|=1.
\]
In particular, there are \emph{no} poles in the open unit disk, matching the geometric-side holomorphy.

\paragraph{Noncancellation is explicit.}
The principal part of $(1-r\lambda_\pm)^{-1}$ at $r=r_\pm$ is
\[
\frac{1}{1-r\lambda_\pm}
=-\frac{1}{\lambda_\pm}\cdot \frac{1}{r-r_\pm}+(\text{holomorphic}),
\]
so the residue is $-1/\lambda_\pm\neq 0$.
Because $r_+\neq r_-$, the poles are at distinct points and cannot cancel.
Thus HTF-NonCancel holds in this explicit model in the strongest possible sense: each off-unit-circle eigenmode would force a genuine interior pole.

\paragraph{Pole barrier as a spectral-radius statement.}
If one replaces \eqref{eq:genus_one_lambdas} by a hypothetical mode with $\abs{\lambda}>1$, then $r=\lambda^{-1}$ lies strictly inside $|r|<1$ and \eqref{eq:genus_one_resolvent_explicit} would contain an interior pole.
Equivalently, the coefficient sequence would grow like $\abs{b_n}\sim \abs{\lambda}^n$, and the Abel series would have radius of convergence $1/|\lambda|<1$.
Therefore, demanding unit-disk holomorphy forces $\abs{\lambda}\le 1$, and applying the same argument to the dual mode (or using the functional equation symmetry) forces $\abs{\lambda}=1$.
This is exactly the pole-barrier mechanism in its simplest fully explicit form.
