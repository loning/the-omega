\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Finite codings of measure-preserving systems provide a standard meeting
point for ergodic theory, symbolic dynamics, and information theory.
Given a measurable set \(W\) in a measure-preserving system
\((X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)\), the binary itinerary
\[
  x \longmapsto \bigl(\mathbf{1}_W(T^t x)\bigr)_{t\geq 0}
\]
records the part of the dynamics visible through the partition
\(\{W,X\setminus W\}\).  The present paper studies recursive address
constructions in which new observables are defined by finite cylinder
conditions in previously constructed visible processes.

The first objective is structural.  Recursive addressing remains inside
ordinary factor theory: each refinement level is produced from the
preceding level by a sliding-block code, so the visible
\(\sigma\)-algebras decrease and factor entropy cannot increase.  The
second objective is quantitative.  We ask how accurately a visible
event can be recovered from depth-\(m\) data, how collisions behave when
many objects are addressed simultaneously at resolution \(m\), and how
much auxiliary memory is required to remove such collisions exactly.

To state the results, let \(\pi\) denote the base coding map associated
to \(W\), let \(\mathcal{F}_m\) be the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by
length-\(m\) cylinders in the visible factor, and let \(\pi^{(L)}\) be
the level-\(L\) process produced by a recursive addressing scheme.  The
precise definitions are given in
Sections~\ref{sec:preliminaries} and~\ref{sec:recursive-addressing}.

\begin{theorem}[Main structural theorem]\label{thm:main-structure}
Let \((X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)\) be a measure-preserving system with a
binary readout set \(W\).  Consider a recursive addressing scheme in
which every level-\(L+1\) observable is obtained from finitely many
cylinder events in the level-\(L\) process.  Write
\[
  \mathcal{G}^{(L)}
  := \sigma\bigl(\mathbf{1}_{C}(T^t\cdot):
      C\in\mathcal{C}^{(L)},\ t\geq 0\bigr)
\]
for the visible \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by the level-\(L\) concept
family, and let \(\nu^{(L)}\) be the law of the coded process
\(\pi^{(L)}\).

Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item For every \(L\geq 0\) there exists a measurable sliding-block
    code \(\Phi_L\) such that
    \[
      \pi^{(L+1)} = \Phi_L \circ \pi^{(L)}
      \qquad \mu\text{-almost everywhere}.
    \]
  \item The visible \(\sigma\)-algebras are non-expanding:
    \[
      \mathcal{G}^{(L+1)} \subseteq \mathcal{G}^{(L)}
      \qquad\text{for every } L\geq 0.
    \]
    Equivalently, each level-\(L+1\) process is a factor of the
    level-\(L\) process.
  \item The factor entropies are non-increasing:
    \[
      h_{\nu^{(L+1)}}(\sigma) \leq h_{\nu^{(L)}}(\sigma)
      \qquad\text{for every } L\geq 0.
    \]
  \item If \(s_q\) denotes the maximal cylinder length used in the
    passage from level \(q-1\) to level \(q\), and
    \[
      R_L := 1+\sum_{q=1}^{L}(s_q-1),
    \]
    then every depth-\(m\) observable of the level-\(L\) process is
    measurable with respect to the depth-\((m+R_L-1)\) observation
    \(\sigma\)-algebra of the level-\(0\) process.
  \item If the scheme admits an address model over the visible factor in
    the sense of Definition~\ref{def:address-model}, then the inverse
    limit of compatible addresses is a compact ultrametric space, every
    compatible address chain determines a unique visible symbolic point,
    and the resulting address map to the visible factor is
    \(1\)-Lipschitz.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The first four parts place recursive addressing inside ordinary factor
theory and identify the exact finite-depth tax incurred by iteration.
Section~\ref{sec:recursive-addressing} also proves a reconstruction
theorem: under a completeness hypothesis on the address model, the
inverse-limit address space is homeomorphic to the visible factor, and
compatible refinement data transport the shift to the address side.

The quantitative core begins with finite observation.  Given an event
\(\mathsf{P}\) visible in the full factor, let
\(\varepsilon_m(\mathsf{P};\mu)\) be the smallest possible error among
all \(\{0,1\}\)-valued \(\mathcal{F}_m\)-measurable classifiers of
\(\mathsf{P}\).

\begin{theorem}[Finite observation and weighted boundary spectra]
\label{thm:main-clarity}
Let \(\mathsf{P}\) belong to the visible \(\sigma\)-algebra.  The error
profile \(m\mapsto \varepsilon_m(\mathsf{P};\mu)\) admits an exact
cylinder decomposition and a Tanaka-type martingale identity.  If there
exist constants \(K<\infty\) and \(\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}>1\) such that
every non-empty \(m\)-cylinder \(C\) satisfies
\(\mu(C)\leq K\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}^{-m}\), then
\[
  \varepsilon_m(\mathsf{P};\mu)
  \leq \frac{K}{2}\lambda_{\mathrm{cyl}}^{-m}
     N_m(\partial\mathsf{P}),
\]
where \(N_m(\partial\mathsf{P})\) denotes the number of boundary
cylinders at depth \(m\).

Assume next that the visible factor law
\(\nu:=\mu\circ\omega_\infty^{-1}\) is a fully supported stationary
Markov measure on a topologically mixing one-step subshift of finite
type, and that the boundary language of \(\mathsf{P}\) is regular.
Then the weighted boundary masses
\[
  Z_m^\mu(\partial\mathsf{P})
  := \sum_{a\in\partial_m\mathsf{P}}\mu(C_a)
\]
are given by a finite weighted automaton:
\[
  Z_m^\mu(\partial\mathsf{P}) = u^{\mathsf T}B^{m-1}v
  \qquad (m\geq 1).
\]
Consequently the weighted boundary zeta function
\[
  \zeta_{\partial\mathsf{P},\mu}(s)
  := \sum_{m\geq 1}
      Z_m^\mu(\partial\mathsf{P})e^{-sm}
\]
is a resolvent of \(B\) and extends meromorphically as a rational
function of \(e^{-s}\).  If \(B\) is primitive and the boundary
conditional probabilities stay uniformly away from \(0\) and \(1\),
then
\[
  \lim_{m\to\infty}
  -\frac{1}{m}\log \varepsilon_m(\mathsf{P};\mu)
  = -\log\rho(B).
\]
For one-step Gibbs-Markov sources this exponent is the pressure gap
\(P_Y(\varphi)-P_\partial(\varphi)\) between the ambient visible
dynamics and the boundary automaton.  For first-entry residue events of
Markov holes, the same quantity is the escape rate
\(P_Y(\varphi)-P_{Y_H}(\varphi)\) of the corresponding open system.
\end{theorem}

Thus the finite-observation exponent is not fundamentally a counting
invariant.  In the regular Markov regime it is determined by a weighted
spectral radius, and the earlier boundary-counting law appears only as
the zero-potential, near-uniform special case.  Section~\ref{sec:clarity}
shows, in particular, that the Bayes error profile for first-entry
residue events recovers survivor pressure, and that for the doubling
hole \(H=[3/4,1)\) one has the exact formula
\[
  \varepsilon_m=\frac{F_{m+3}}{5\,2^m}.
\]

The second quantitative question concerns simultaneous addressing of
many objects at fixed resolution \(m\).  The natural scale is the
collision count
\[
  W_m := \sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N_m}
    \mathbf{1}_{\{X_m^{(i)}=X_m^{(j)}\}},
\]
whose mean is \(\binom{N_m}{2}S_2(m)\), where
\(S_r(m):=\sum_{a}\lambda_m(a)^r\) are the power sums of the visible
address law \(\lambda_m\).

\begin{theorem}[Poisson collision threshold]
\label{thm:main-capacity}
Let \((T_m,X_m)\) be the synchronization pair at resolution \(m\), let
\(\lambda_m\) be the law of \(X_m\), and let
\((T_m^{(i)},X_m^{(i)})_{1\leq i\leq N_m}\) be independent copies.
Given time budgets \(n_m\), let \(\mathsf{Fail}_{m,n_m,N_m}\) be the
event that some object has not synchronized by time \(n_m\) or that two
objects that have synchronized by time \(n_m\) receive the same visible
\(m\)-address.  Set
\[
  q_m:=\PP(T_m>n_m),
  \qquad
  \Lambda_m:=\binom{N_m}{2}S_2(m).
\]
Then the following hold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item If \(N_m q_m\to 0\) and \(\Lambda_m\to 0\), then
    \(\PP(\mathsf{Fail}_{m,n_m,N_m})\to 0\).
  \item If \(N_m q_m\to 0\), \(\Lambda_m\to\tau\in(0,\infty)\), and
    \(N_m^3S_3(m)\to 0\), then
    \[
      W_m \Rightarrow \mathrm{Poisson}(\tau),
      \qquad
      \PP(\mathsf{Fail}_{m,n_m,N_m})\to 1-e^{-\tau}.
    \]
  \item If \(N_m q_m\to 0\), \(\Lambda_m\to\infty\), and
    \(N_m^3S_3(m)=o(\Lambda_m^2)\), then
    \(\PP(\mathsf{Fail}_{m,n_m,N_m})\to 1\).
\end{enumerate}
In particular, the collision threshold lies on the scale
\[
  \binom{N_m}{2}S_2(m)\asymp 1,
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  2\log N_m \asymp -\log S_2(m).
\]
\end{theorem}

The old union-bound capacity estimate survives as the subcritical part
of this theorem, but the main phenomenon is a sharp Poisson threshold
along the R\'enyi-\(2\) line.

The third quantitative question concerns the amount of hidden
information lost when finite addresses are collapsed.

\begin{theorem}[Entropy ledger for injective refinements]
\label{thm:main-register}
Let \(f_m:\Sigma^m\to \mathcal{Y}_m\) be a finite-resolution address map, let
\(U_m\) be any \(\Sigma^m\)-valued random variable with law \(u_m\), and
set \(Y_m:=f_m(U_m)\).  For \(y\in \mathcal{Y}_m\), write
\(d_m(y):=\card{f_m^{-1}(y)}\), and define the fibre-uniform lift
\[
  \widetilde u_m(\omega)
  := \frac{\PP(Y_m=f_m(\omega))}
           {d_m(f_m(\omega))}.
\]
Then
\[
  H(U_m\mid Y_m)
  = \EE[\log d_m(Y_m)] - D(u_m\Vert \widetilde u_m).
\]
If \(r_m:\Sigma^m\to R_m\) is any auxiliary register such that
\(\omega\mapsto(f_m(\omega),r_m(\omega))\) is injective, then
\[
  H(r_m(U_m)\mid Y_m)=H(U_m\mid Y_m).
\]
Hence every injectivizing register must carry the entire hidden
conditional entropy.  In particular,
\[
  \log\card{R_m}\geq H(U_m\mid Y_m).
\]
For the uniform source on \(\Sigma^m\), this becomes
\[
  \log_2\card{R_m}
  \geq m-H_2(Y_m)\geq m-\log_2\card{\mathcal{Y}_m}.
\]
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:main-register} upgrades the earlier worst-fibre lower
bound to an exact information balance: the auxiliary register is not
merely large enough to separate the largest fibre, but must, on
average, encode precisely the full hidden conditional entropy.

Thermodynamic formalism for subshifts and equilibrium states is
classical; we use standard material from
\citet{Bowen2008,Ruelle1978,Walters1975GMeasures,WaltersErgodicTheory}.
For the open-system interpretation of pressure differences and escape
rates, compare \citet{DemersWrightYoung2012}.
The Poisson approximation input is the Chen--Stein method in the form
of \citet{ArratiaGoldsteinGordon1989}.  The information-theoretic
notation follows \citet{CoverThomas2006}.  What is specific here is the
way recursive addressing, weighted boundary automata, open-system escape
rates, collision thresholds, and hidden information costs are assembled
into a single visible-factor framework.

The paper is organized as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries}
introduces the visible symbolic factor and the finite observation
\(\sigma\)-algebras.  Section~\ref{sec:spg} records visible word maps
and a Sturmian example.  Section~\ref{sec:recursive-addressing} proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-structure}.  Section~\ref{sec:clarity} proves
Theorem~\ref{thm:main-clarity}.  Section~\ref{sec:double-budget}
contains the Poisson threshold theorem and the entropy ledger from
Theorems~\ref{thm:main-capacity} and \ref{thm:main-register}.  The
paper ends with brief final remarks.
