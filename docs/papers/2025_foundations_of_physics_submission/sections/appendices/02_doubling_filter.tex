\section{Projection filtering and a doubler gap bound (1D Fibonacci case)}
\label{app:doubling_filter}
This appendix spells out the acceptance-window Fourier transform and a simple mass-gap bound for the projection-filter mechanism discussed in Section~\ref{sec:doubling_filter}, specializing to the standard 1D Fibonacci cut-and-project construction from $\mathbb{Z}^2$.\par

\subsection{Interval window and explicit Fourier transform}
For the Fibonacci chain, the internal space is one-dimensional and the acceptance window $W\subset\mathbb{R}$ can be taken as an interval of length $R>0$ (up to an affine change of coordinates). Let $W=[-R/2,R/2]$ and define the normalized Fourier transform
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{W}(q)\equiv \frac{1}{R}\int_{-R/2}^{R/2} e^{-iqy}\,dy
    = e^{-iq\cdot 0}\,\frac{\sin(qR/2)}{qR/2}
    \equiv \mathrm{sinc}\!\left(\frac{qR}{2}\right),
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{sinc}(x)\equiv \sin(x)/x$ and $\widehat{W}(0)=1$.
The elementary bound $|\sin x|\le 1$ implies
\begin{equation}
    |\widehat{W}(q)| \le \min\left\{1,\ \frac{2}{|q|R}\right\}.
\label{eq:window_sinc_bound}
\end{equation}

\subsection{A concrete lower bound for would-be doubler momenta}
In the cut-and-project embedding picture one decomposes embedding momentum $k\in(\mathbb{R}/(2\pi/\varepsilon)\mathbb{Z})^2$ into components $(k_\parallel,k_\perp)$ along the physical and internal directions. For a ``mirror'' shift by $\pi/\varepsilon$ in one embedding coordinate, Diophantine irrationality of the slope implies that the induced internal component is not arbitrarily small; in the noble (golden) case one can take a bound of the form
\begin{equation}
    |q_{\mathrm{doubler}}|\equiv |k_\perp|\ge \frac{c_0}{\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
for some constant $c_0>0$ that depends on the embedding convention and on the chosen family of mirror shifts. For golden slopes, such lower bounds are a manifestation of bad Diophantine approximability (Appendix~\ref{app:homogenization}).\par

\subsection{Mass gap from the window bound}
With the projection-filter mass term $\mathcal{M}(q)=\mu(1-\widehat{W}(q))$ from Eq.~(\ref{eq:proj_filter_mass}), the sinc bound (\ref{eq:window_sinc_bound}) implies that for $|q|\ge c_0/\varepsilon$ one has $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le 2\varepsilon/(c_0R)$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{M}(q_{\mathrm{doubler}})| \ge \mu\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon}{c_0R}\right).
\end{equation}
Choosing the UV normalization $\mu=\mu_0/\varepsilon$ yields
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{M}(q_{\mathrm{doubler}})| \ge \frac{\mu_0}{\varepsilon}\left(1-\frac{2\varepsilon}{c_0R}\right),
\end{equation}
which is of order $1/\varepsilon$ for small $\varepsilon$, as claimed.\par

\subsection{Numerical cross-check on periodic approximants}
For the periodic approximants used in Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca}, a would-be doubler would appear as a near-zero quasienergy branch at Brillouin-edge momenta. The fixed-parameter script \texttt{scripts/generate\_fib\_dqca\_figures.py} regenerates the dispersion figures and can be extended to directly evaluate the smallest quasienergy magnitude at $k=\pi$ as a diagnostic for the doubler sector.
