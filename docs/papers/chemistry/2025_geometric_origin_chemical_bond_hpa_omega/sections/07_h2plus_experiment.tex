\section{A textbook anchor: $\mathrm{H}_2^+$ and the \texorpdfstring{$(\alpha,\mu)$}{(alpha,mu)} control of scale and hierarchy}
\label{sec:h2plus}

To keep the chemistry discussion anchored to a concrete system without introducing unnecessary numerical artifacts, we use the simplest bound molecule as a textbook reference: the hydrogen molecular ion $\mathrm{H}_2^+$ (two protons and one electron). Its BO potential curve is a classical worked example in molecular quantum mechanics and can be treated analytically (or numerically to essentially arbitrary precision) in standard references \cite{PaulingWilson1935,AtkinsFriedman2011,SzaboOstlund1996}.
Our purpose here is not to improve accuracy, but to isolate two structural consequences of $(\alpha_{\mathrm{em}},\mu)$ that are already visible in this minimal system:
\begin{itemize}
\item the \textbf{absolute scale} of $R_{\mathrm{eq}}$ and $D_e$ is fixed once $a_0$ and $E_h$ are fixed, hence by $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$;
\item the \textbf{vibrational hierarchy} is controlled by $\mu^{-1/2}$ through nuclear reduced mass.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Born--Oppenheimer setup in atomic units}
Fix the internuclear distance $R$ (in Bohr units). The electronic Hamiltonian is
\begin{equation}
\hat H_e(R)=-\frac12\nabla^2-\frac{1}{r_A}-\frac{1}{r_B},
\end{equation}
with nuclei placed at $\mathbf{R}_A=(0,0,-R/2)$ and $\mathbf{R}_B=(0,0,R/2)$. Let $E_e(R)$ be the electronic ground-state energy. The BO potential curve for nuclear motion is
\begin{equation}
E(R)=E_e(R)+\frac{1}{R}.
\end{equation}
Bonding corresponds to a local minimum of $E(R)$ below the dissociation limit $E(\infty)=-1/2$ (in Hartree).

\subsection{Scale statement: dimensionless geometry is \texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}-independent}
In the nonrelativistic Coulomb model, after rescaling to atomic units, $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ disappears from the dimensionless Hamiltonian (Section~\ref{sec:chemistry_scaling}). Consequently, dimensionless molecular geometry (e.g.\ $R_{\mathrm{eq}}$ expressed in Bohr) is determined by the dimensionless Coulombic problem and does not depend on $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$. The \emph{absolute} bond length is then
\[
R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(\mathrm{SI})}=R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(\mathrm{Bohr})}\,a_0,
\qquad
a_0=\frac{\hbar}{m_e c\,\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}},
\]
so $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ fixes the overall length scale.
This statement is understood within the nonrelativistic Coulomb model; small relativistic and QED corrections reintroduce mild $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$-dependence at high precision \cite{AtkinsFriedman2011}.

\subsection{Textbook quantitative anchor}
For $\mathrm{H}_2^+$, standard treatments find a bound minimum at an equilibrium separation $R_{\mathrm{eq}}$ of order $2\,a_0$, with a well depth of order $10^{-1}\,E_h$ (relative to the $\mathrm{H}+p$ limit) \cite{PaulingWilson1935,AtkinsFriedman2011}. Numerically, representative values are
\[
R_{\mathrm{eq}}\approx 2.0\,a_0,
\qquad
E(R_{\mathrm{eq}})\approx -0.60\,E_h,
\qquad
D_e:=E(\infty)-E(R_{\mathrm{eq}})\approx 0.10\,E_h,
\]
consistent across standard references. These are exactly the kind of scale data emphasized here: order-unity dimensionless geometry in Bohr units and sub-Hartree well depth in Hartree units.
Using $a_0\approx 0.529\,\text{\AA}$ and $E_h\approx 27.21\,\mathrm{eV}$ \cite{CODATA2022RMP}, this corresponds to
\[
R_{\mathrm{eq}}\approx 1.06\,\text{\AA},
\qquad
D_e\approx 2.7\,\mathrm{eV},
\]
illustrating the chemically natural length and energy scales as direct consequences of atomic units.

\subsection{Nuclear zero-point motion and the \texorpdfstring{$\mu$}{mu} control}
Near $R_{\mathrm{eq}}$, approximate the BO curve by a harmonic potential:
\[
E(R)\approx E(R_{\mathrm{eq}})+\tfrac12 k (R-R_{\mathrm{eq}})^2,
\qquad
k=E''(R_{\mathrm{eq}}).
\]
For two identical nuclei of mass ratio $\mu=M/m_e$, the reduced mass (in electron-mass units) is $\mu_{\mathrm{red}}=\mu/2$. The vibrational frequency in atomic units is
\begin{equation}
\omega\approx \sqrt{\frac{k}{\mu_{\mathrm{red}}}},
\end{equation}
and the zero-point energy is $E_{\mathrm{ZPE}}\approx \omega/2$. Therefore
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{ZPE}}\propto \mu^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
which is the quantitative statement behind the qualitative BO ``frozen geometry'' picture. In particular, the large closed-theory value $\mu_{\mathrm{geo}}=6\pi^5$ implies $\mu^{-1/2}\approx 2.3\times 10^{-2}$ and therefore a strong separation between electronic and vibrational scales.
