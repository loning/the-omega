\section{Anatomy of a Holographic Black Hole}

\subsection{The Event Horizon: Phase Locking Radius}
In the classical picture, the event horizon at $r_s = 2M$ is a null surface from which light cannot escape. In Omega Dynamics, we interpret this via the scanning operator $\Theta$.
In the isotropic holographic radius $\rho$ (Section~\ref{sec:omega_metric}), the horizon corresponds to the finite \textbf{Phase Locking Radius} $\rho_{lock}:=\rho_h=M/2$, characterized by the vanishing of the redshift (Cayley) factor $\mathcal{J}(\rho)$:
\begin{equation}
  \rho=\rho_{lock}\quad \Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathcal{J}(\rho)=0 \quad \Longleftrightarrow\quad g_{tt}=0 .
\end{equation}
At this locking surface, the local proper-time tick rate of a static observer satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \frac{d\tau}{dt}=\sqrt{-g_{tt}}=|\mathcal{J}(\rho)| \to 0 \qquad (\rho\to \rho_{lock}),
\end{equation}
so external time $t$ corresponds to vanishing local proper time $d\tau$: signals emitted from increasingly near the horizon are increasingly redshifted as seen by a static observer at infinity \cite{Wald1984}. The thermal side of the same barrier is consistent with the \textbf{Unruh Effect} \cite{Unruh1976} and its black-hole specialization (Hawking temperature), with temperature proportional to surface gravity \cite{Hawking1975}. Independently, black holes saturate the Bekenstein entropy bound / holographic scaling \cite{Bekenstein1973, tHooft1993, Susskind1995}.
\begin{equation}
  T_H = \frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{sg}}}{2\pi}=\frac{1}{8\pi M}\qquad(\text{Schwarzschild}),
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\mathrm{sg}}$ is the surface gravity (for Schwarzschild, $\kappa_{\mathrm{sg}}=1/(4M)$) \cite{Wald1984}.
The corresponding Bekenstein--Hawking entropy is \cite{Bekenstein1973, Hawking1975, Bousso2002}
\begin{equation}
  S_{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac{A}{4},\qquad A=4\pi r_s^2=16\pi M^2 .
\end{equation}

\subsection{The Interior: A Topological Inverter}
In classical GR, extending through the horizon requires a coordinate choice adapted to the null surface (e.g.\ Kruskal coordinates). In Omega Dynamics we instead \emph{define} the continuation through the locking surface $\rho=\rho_{lock}$ by the inversion map of the isotropic exterior geometry (Axiom~\ref{ax:omega_inversion}). In this sense, the ``interior channel'' is represented by the inverted chart $\rho<\rho_{lock}$ (the second asymptotic end of the Einstein--Rosen completion on the time-symmetric slice). The coordinate $\rho$ does not simply count down to a point; instead it inverts by $\mathcal{I}$:
\begin{equation}
	    \mathcal{I}: \rho \to \frac{\rho_{lock}^2}{\rho}
\end{equation}
This is precisely the isotropic inversion symmetry of the Schwarzschild exterior (Section~\ref{sec:omega_metric}). The geometry is conformal to the exterior but with inverted polarity: $\rho\to 0$ corresponds to a second asymptotically flat boundary rather than a curvature blow-up. Omega Dynamics interprets this inversion as the dynamical continuation of infalling degrees of freedom into a boundary readout channel, realizing the holographic principle \cite{Susskind1995, Maldacena1998} not just as a boundary condition but as an explicit map.
This is an axiom-level modeling step rather than a statement about classical GR.

\subsection{The Throat and the Second Asymptotic Boundary}
In the isotropic completion, the minimal surface at $\rho=\rho_h$ is the \textbf{Topological Throat} (the Einstein--Rosen bridge) connecting two asymptotic ends $\rho\to\infty$ and $\rho\to 0$ \cite{EinsteinRosen1935, Kruskal1960}. In Omega Dynamics, this throat mediates the map between bulk infall and boundary degrees of freedom. This geometric channel is compatible with the \textbf{ER=EPR} conjecture \cite{SusskindMaldacena2013}, in which entanglement between bulk interior and boundary radiation is encoded by wormhole-like connectivity.
\begin{remark}
We do not claim to derive ER=EPR from the Omega axioms; we only note that the ``throat + boundary channel'' structure provides a suggestive template for encoding correlations across the bulk/readout split.
\end{remark}
