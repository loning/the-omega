\subsection{Phase gate and absolute calibration of compact Lie groups: rank-one volume constants and gauge normalization}\label{subsec:unit-circle-phase-compact-lie-normalization}
The phase coordinate gate identifies \(\mathbb{U}=\RR/\ZZ\) as the phase box, and under the angular variable \(\vartheta=2\pi u\), identifies the Lebesgue measure \(\dd u\) with the normalized Haar measure \(\dd\vartheta/(2\pi)\) of \(U(1)\) (Remark \ref{rem:unit-circle-phase-haar-pullback-weight}).
When this gate is juxtaposed with the finite-window audit interface and orbit-Levi rigidity without introducing additional axioms (see \cite{Internal2025ResolutionFoldingPhiPi}; and refer to the minimal anchor calibration in \S\ref{subsubsec:window6-terminal-propositions}), several continuous degrees of freedom that are usually regarded as "gauge choices" can be reduced to absolute calibration problems endogenously determined by rigidity.
This subsection provides a set of directly referenceable structural corollaries: they provide the compact Lie group Haar calibration forced by the phase gate, and lock the discrete entropy scale \(\log(2/\pi)\) and the compact Lie group Weyl pushforward constants on the same non-adjustable scale.
\par

\begin{proposition}[Rank-one factor scale rigidity forced by phase gate: absolute Haar volume]\label{prop:phase-gate-rank1-volume-rigidity}
Let \(\vartheta\in[0,2\pi)\) be the phase angle and view it as the canonical angular coordinate of \(U(1)\).
For rank-one compact Lie group factors \(G\in\{U(1),SU(2),SO(3)\}\), take a maximal torus embedding \(U(1)\hookrightarrow G\).
Call a Riemannian metric on \(G\) a \emph{phase-consistent bi-invariant metric} if the metric is bi-invariant and the geodesic parameter on the above \(U(1)\) subgroup is consistent with \(\vartheta\) (thus the fundamental closed orbit length is \(2\pi\)).
Then this metric is uniquely determined on each \(G\), and the induced Riemannian volume (equivalent to the absolute calibration of Haar measure) satisfies
\[
\mathrm{Vol}(U(1))=2\pi,\qquad
\mathrm{Vol}(SU(2))=2\pi^{2},\qquad
\mathrm{Vol}(SO(3))=\pi^{2}.
\]
Moreover, for the projectivized phase fiber \(\mathbb{RP}^{1}=U(1)/\{\pm 1\}\), we have \(\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{RP}^{1})=\pi\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Any bi-invariant metric on \(U(1)\) must be a constant-curvature circle metric, and phase consistency requires its fundamental closed orbit length to be \(2\pi\), thus \(\mathrm{Vol}(U(1))=2\pi\).
\par
For \(SU(2)\), bi-invariant metrics in the compact simple case differ only by an overall scaling; and under the identification \(SU(2)\cong S^{3}\), the bi-invariant metric corresponds to the round sphere metric of radius \(r\), whose volume is \(\mathrm{Vol}(S^{3}_{r})=2\pi^{2}r^{3}\).
The maximal torus closed orbit in this metric is a geodesic great circle with length \(2\pi r\); phase consistency forces this length to be \(2\pi\), thus \(r=1\), yielding \(\mathrm{Vol}(SU(2))=2\pi^{2}\).
\par
Further, from \(SO(3)\cong SU(2)/\{\pm I\}\) being a free isometric double quotient, the volume is halved to \(\mathrm{Vol}(SO(3))=\pi^{2}\); similarly, \(\mathbb{RP}^{1}=U(1)/\{\pm 1\}\) is an isometric double quotient, thus \(\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{RP}^{1})=\pi\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$u(1)$}{u(1)} direction selected by phase gate eliminates scale freedom of compact simple factors]\label{prop:phase-gate-u1-fixes-simple-scale}
Let \(\mathfrak g\) be a compact simple Lie algebra, and given a one-parameter subgroup transported by the phase gate \(u\mapsto \exp(uH)\) (where \(H\in\mathfrak g\) is unique up to sign under conjugation), whose fundamental period under phase angle \(\vartheta=2\pi u\) is \(2\pi\).
Then there exists a unique \(\mathrm{Ad}\)-invariant inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\) such that this \(U(1)\) direction satisfies phase-consistent normalization (equivalently: the corresponding closed orbit length is \(2\pi\)).
Thus the bi-invariant metric scale of \(\mathfrak g\) is completely fixed; in particular, the absolute calibration of the Haar measure compatible with this scale becomes a computable rigid object without continuous adjustable factors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The space of \(\mathrm{Ad}\)-invariant inner products on compact simple \(\mathfrak g\) is a one-dimensional cone: taking any inner product \(\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{0}\), any other inner product must be \(c\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{0}\) (\(c>0\)).
The generator \(H\neq 0\) specified by the phase gate determines a fixed \(U(1)\) direction; phase-consistent normalization requires the fundamental closed orbit length in this direction to be \(2\pi\), which is equivalent to imposing a definite norm constraint \(\|H\|=\mathrm{const}\) on a fixed \(H\).
Since the scaling \(c\) only changes the overall scale of the norm, there exists a unique \(c>0\) such that \(c\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{0}\) satisfies this constraint, thus eliminating the scale freedom. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Absolute calibration of Levi Haar volume under orbit-Levi rigidity]\label{cor:window6-levi-haar-volume-rigidity}
Under the orbit-Levi calibration of window-\(6\), the discrete dimensional skeleton rigidly locks
\(
\mathfrak g^{\mathrm{Levi}}_6\cong \mathfrak{su}(4)\oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)_L\oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)_R
\)
(Corollary \ref{cor:window6-levi-rigidity-pati-salam}), and the relative position of \(\mathfrak{su}(4)\supset \mathfrak{su}(3)\oplus\mathfrak u(1)\) is unique up to conjugation under automorphisms (Corollary \ref{cor:window6-su4-embedding-rigidity}), and the right-hand block endogenously selects a one-dimensional fixed-point direction \(\mathfrak u(1)_{\mathrm{eff}}\subset \mathfrak{su}(2)_R\) (Corollary \ref{cor:window6-levi-rigidity-sm-residual}).
Viewing these \(u(1)\) directions locked by discrete certificate chains as phase-gate-transportable objects, and applying the phase-consistent normalization of Proposition \ref{prop:phase-gate-u1-fixes-simple-scale} to each compact simple ideal,
then the Haar volume form of the entire Levi group is completely determined by discrete data.
Therefore, any dimension-independent function taking Haar volume (or its logarithm/ratio) as input becomes an algorithmically computable object on this window, without any continuous fitting parameters.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For \(\mathfrak{su}(2)_L\) and \(\mathfrak{su}(2)_R\), their \(\mathrm{Ad}\)-invariant inner products differ only by a scaling, and the phase-consistent closed orbit condition fixes this scaling; similarly for \(\mathfrak{su}(4)\), its scaling is uniquely determined under Proposition \ref{prop:phase-gate-u1-fixes-simple-scale} by the \(\mathfrak u(1)\) direction locked by Corollary \ref{cor:window6-su4-embedding-rigidity}.
Thus the absolute calibration of each ideal on the Levi direct sum is fixed, and the Haar volume form is determined accordingly. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Identity of \texorpdfstring{\(\log(2/\pi)\)}{log(2/pi)} constant and \(SU(2)\) Weyl pushforward normalization]\label{cor:zigzag-entropy-su2-weyl-constant}
Denote by \(E_n\) the Euler zigzag numbers, and define the discrete entropy constant
\[
c_{\mathrm{zz}}:=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1n\log\frac{E_n}{n!}.
\]
Then by Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fence-volume-log2pi}, we have \(c_{\mathrm{zz}}=\log(2/\pi)\).
\par
On the other hand, let \(G=SU(2)\) take the phase-consistent bi-invariant metric of Proposition \ref{prop:phase-gate-rank1-volume-rigidity}, and parameterize the eigenvalues \(e^{\pm i\phi}\) by the conjugacy class angle parameter \(\phi\in[0,\pi]\).
Let \(\nu\) be the pushforward of the normalized Haar probability measure under the map \(SU(2)\to[0,\pi]\).
Then \(\nu\) has density
\[
\dd\nu(\phi)=\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\phi\,\dd\phi,
\]
whose normalization constant is \(Z_{SU(2)}=2/\pi\).
Thus we obtain the strict identity
\[
c_{\mathrm{zz}}=\log\Bigl(\frac{2}{\pi}\Bigr)=\log Z_{SU(2)}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The first part is precisely Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fence-volume-log2pi}.
\par
For the second part, viewing \(SU(2)\cong S^{3}\) as unit quaternions, take the polar decomposition
\(
S^{3}\ni g=(\cos\phi,\ \sin\phi\cdot n)
\)
(where \(n\in S^{2}\)), then \(\phi\in[0,\pi]\) is precisely the conjugacy class angle parameter.
Under the phase-consistent calibration of Proposition \ref{prop:phase-gate-rank1-volume-rigidity}, the Haar probability measure corresponds to the uniform volume measure on \(S^{3}\) divided by \(\mathrm{Vol}(S^{3})=2\pi^{2}\).
The volume element of \(S^{3}\) in this coordinate decomposes as
\(
\dd V_{S^{3}}=\sin^{2}\phi\,\dd\phi\,\dd A_{S^{2}}
\), where \(\mathrm{Area}(S^{2})=4\pi\).
Integrating over \(n\in S^{2}\) and normalizing yields
\[
\dd\nu(\phi)=\frac{4\pi}{2\pi^{2}}\sin^{2}\phi\,\dd\phi=\frac{2}{\pi}\sin^{2}\phi\,\dd\phi.
\]
Thus the normalization constant is \(2/\pi\), and the conclusion holds. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Volume constant closure under phase-consistent calibration: transcendental structure arises only from \texorpdfstring{\(\pi\)}{pi} and \texorpdfstring{\(2\)}{2}]\label{rem:phase-gate-volume-constants-closure}
By Proposition \ref{prop:phase-gate-rank1-volume-rigidity}, the rank-one primitive set
\(
\{U(1),SU(2),SO(3),\mathbb{RP}^{1}\}
\)
under phase-consistent calibration all have volumes of the form \(q\pi^{k}\) (\(q\in\QQ_{>0}\), \(k\in\{1,2\}\)).
Therefore:
\begin{itemize}
  \item If only finite direct products and finite coverings/finite quotients (e.g., \(\{\pm 1\}\) double quotients) are allowed to generate new volume constants, then volumes still fall within a finitely generated subring of \(\QQ\cdot\pi^{\ZZ_{\ge 0}}\); their logarithms fall within the finite-dimensional linear span of \(\QQ\log\pi+\QQ\log 2\).
  \item If further allowing finite additive combinations of volumes (e.g., disjoint unions under volume-additive calibrations), then volumes still fall in \(\QQ[\pi]\); but if logarithms act on additive combinations, they generally introduce \(\log(1+\cdots)\)-type nonlinear terms, so additional constraints are needed to remain within the two-dimensional span.
\end{itemize}
In other words, under the phase-consistent calibration and finite algebraic operation closure adopted in this work, the transcendental source points appearing are compressed to the pair of rigid constants \(\pi\) (and \(2\) introduced by its double quotient).
\end{remark}
