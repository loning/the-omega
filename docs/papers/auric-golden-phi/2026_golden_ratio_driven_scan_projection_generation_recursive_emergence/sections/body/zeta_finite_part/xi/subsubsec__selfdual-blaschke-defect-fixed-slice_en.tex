\subsubsection{Self-Dual Scattering Symmetry's "Index Vanishing" Principle: Krein Spectral Shift Function, Inner-Functionalization, and Fixed Slice Support}\label{subsubsec:selfdual-blaschke-defect-fixed-slice}
Without introducing new empirical assumptions, this section indexes "the discrete orthogonal residual axis deviating from the fixed slice" as an integer invariant, and under Hardy/Smirnov factorization caliber equivalently identifies it as Blaschke defect: the minimal externalized dimension required for disk point zeros (or point spectral residuals). In particular, in a scattering realization, this integer invariant can be given by the scattering phase winding induced by Krein spectral shift function; self-dual (antilinear) symmetry forces this winding to zero, thereby deriving Blaschke part vanishing and compressing point singularities rigidly to boundary support of the fixed slice.
This caliber is consistent with Remark \ref{rem:symmetry-projection-automorphism}: the ontology of symmetry is not "axis," but the automorphism of projection system in the readout sense and its fixed slice; the so-called "axis/orthogonality" is merely a notational description for the equivariant decomposition components induced by automorphism.

\paragraph{Structural Chain (Indexing Summary)}
In realizations satisfying standard regularity and boundary unitarization (inner-functionalization) conditions, the following chain provides a verifiable criterion caliber for "fixed slice support":
\[
\boxed{\;
\text{Self-dual (antilinear) symmetry}
\Rightarrow
\text{Spectral shift parity}
\Rightarrow
\mathrm{Wind}(\det\mathcal S)=0
\Rightarrow
\deg(B_F)=0
\Rightarrow
\text{Point singularities can only be boundary-supported}\;}
\]
where \(\mathcal S\) is the scattering matrix, \(\mathrm{Wind}\) is the winding index of its determinant, and \(\deg(B_F)\) is the total multiplicity of the Blaschke factor within the disk (finite defect caliber). Below we will rigorously align this chain with the "Blaschke defect dimension \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}\)" and minimal realization point spectral residual already established in this paper.

\paragraph{Equivariant Translation: Maslov–Singular Dichotomy of Tate Obstruction and "Residual Axis" Classification}\label{par:selfdual-tate-maslov-splitting}
To lift the above "winding/Blaschke index chain" to reusable equivariant semantics, one can understand self-dual antilinear symmetry as a \(\ZZ/2\)-equivariant descent problem.
Let \(\sigma\) be an involution (typically \(\sigma(w)=1/\overline w\) in our disk compactification caliber), and let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a commutative "readout unit group" (e.g., the inner factor group of bounded type invertible functions in the unit disk, modulo constant phase).
For such \((\mathcal{A},\sigma)\), define the first-level obstruction group of \(\ZZ/2\)-equivariant Tate cohomology as
\[
\widehat H^1_{\sigma}(\mathcal{A})
:=
\frac{\{a\in\mathcal{A}:\ a\cdot\sigma(a)=1\}}{\{b/\sigma(b):\ b\in\mathcal{A}\}}.
\]
Its elements can be interpreted as: whether "readout reductions" satisfying self-dual constraints at local level can be globally made consistent (denominator being reduction transformations), and the equivalence classes remaining when inconsistent.
In Hardy/Smirnov models (\(F\) being bounded type and boundary-unitarized thus admitting inner-outer decomposition), this obstruction naturally splits into point-like and continuous parts: the Blaschke part yields integer-type Maslov (Toeplitz/Fredholm) index, while singular inner factor yields data of boundary continuous residual axis.

\begin{proposition}[Maslov–Singular Dichotomy: Point Defect and Continuous Residual Axis]\label{prop:selfdual-maslov-singular-dichotomy}
Let \(F\) be bounded type in \(\mathbb{D}\) and satisfy self-dual \(F=F^\sharp\).
Write its canonical decomposition \(F=B_F S_F O_F\) (see the review below in this section).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Point/Integer Part)} All disk point zeros are carried by \(B_F\), and its total multiplicity \(\deg(B_F)\) equals the Blaschke defect dimension \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)\) in finite defect cases (Remark \ref{rem:blaschke-defect-finite}), and can equivalently be viewed as a Maslov/Toeplitz index (e.g., appearing in Toeplitz/Fredholm caliber with symbol \(B_F\)).
  \item \textbf{(Boundary/Continuous Part)} The singular inner factor \(S_F\) corresponds one-to-one to singular measure classes on the unit circle; its existence means there still exists a data channel that cannot be discretized into disk zeros, namely "continuous residual axis" (boundary support).
  \item \textbf{(Phase Closure Verification \(\Rightarrow\) Exclude Continuous Residual)} If the readout regime requires phase closure verification of unitary-slice (no externalizing radial/amplitude registers, see semantics of Theorem \ref{thm:phase-lift-fixed-slice}), then boundary continuous residual cannot be carried as auditable certificate within regime; under this verification caliber one necessarily requires \(S_F\equiv 1\), thus the obstruction degenerates to a single integer \(\deg(B_F)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Quaternionic Real Type (\(J^2=-I\)) Even Multiplicity and \texorpdfstring{$\ZZ_2$}{Z2} Indexing]\label{rem:selfdual-quaternionic-z2}
If self-dual antilinear structure is given by \(J:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H\) satisfying \(J^2=-I\) (quaternionic real/symplectic real structure), then Kramers degeneracy appears: under compatible bounded type/inner-functionalization caliber, point spectral defects and disk zeros can only appear with even multiplicity.
Correspondingly, the point Maslov/Toeplitz index \(\deg(B_F)\) can only take even values in this symmetry class, and its "residual axis" classification can be stated as a \(\ZZ_2\) invariant on the quotient group \(\ZZ/2\).
Under phase closure verification regime (excluding continuous residual \(S_F\)), only this \(\ZZ_2\) class remains as possible off-slice defect certificate.
If resonance/zero anomaly is further encoded in determinant caliber (e.g., the Fuglede–Kadison determinant interface in Appendix \S\ref{subsec:op-algebra-modular-zk-index-fkdet}, Definition \ref{def:op-algebra-fk-determinant-zeta}), Kramers degeneracy similarly forces algebraic multiplicity of isolated resonance spectral points (e.g., \(1\in\mathrm{Spec}(K_{s_0})\)) to be even; therefore in matrix degeneration cases, the zero order of \(\det(I-K_s)\) at \(s_0\) (equivalently the pole order of corresponding \(\zeta\)-type object) can only be even.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Involutive Automorphism and Fixed Slice (Self-Dual Readout)}

On disk readout variable \(w\in\CC^\times\), take inversion involution
\[
\boxed{\;\sigma(w):=\frac{1}{\overline{w}}\;}
\]
whose fixed slice is the unit circle \(\TT=\{w:|w|=1\}\) (can also be viewed as an equivalent statement of unitary-slice, see Remark \ref{rem:xi-critical-line-unit-circle} and Appendix \ref{subsec:unit-circle-phase-lift}).
Given a class of verifiable readout functions \(F\) (typically from auditable transfer/determinant expressions in our context), define reflection operator
\[
F^\sharp(w):=\overline{F(\sigma(w))}.
\]
We say \(F\) satisfies \textbf{self-dual constraint} if \(F=F^\sharp\) holds in its analytic continuation sense.
This constraint forces zeros/poles divisor to close under \(\sigma\): if \(w_0\) is a zero (or pole), then \(\sigma(w_0)\) is also a zero (or pole) of the same order.
Therefore, any discrete singularity deviating from fixed slice \(|w|=1\) must appear in pairs as two-point orbits; this precisely corresponds to the minimal divergence structure of "fixed-slice visible readout insufficient to distinguish two branches of realization" (and see Proposition \ref{prop:xi-read-fiber-orbits} for general caliber of "readout fiber = visible symmetry orbit").

\paragraph{Krein Spectral Shift Function and Scattering Winding: Integer Index of Point Off-Slice Defect}\label{par:selfdual-krein-wind}

To give an indexing source for "Blaschke defect," consider the caliber of standard trace-class perturbation scattering theory: let \(\mathcal H\) be a complex Hilbert space, \((H_0,H)\) be a self-adjoint operator pair, and assume perturbation \(H-H_0\in\mathfrak S_1\) is trace-class so that Krein spectral shift function \(\xi(\lambda)\) (defined up to additive constant ambiguity) exists. If we further assume wave operator completeness so scattering matrix \(\mathcal S(\lambda)\) exists almost everywhere on absolute continuous spectrum, then its determinant satisfies Birman–Krein formula (see \cite{Simon2005TraceIdeals,Yafaev1992ScatteringGeneralTheory})
\begin{equation}\label{eq:birman-krein-detS-ssf}
\boxed{\;\det\mathcal S(\lambda)=\exp\!\bigl(-2\pi\mathrm{i}\,\xi(\lambda)\bigr)\qquad(\text{a.e. }\lambda\in\RR)\;}
\end{equation}
and accordingly one can choose scattering phase branch \(\vartheta(\lambda)\) such that
\(\vartheta(\lambda)=-2\pi\xi(\lambda)\) (modulo \(2\pi\)).
\par
Under this caliber, point defect is measured by "winding/spectral flow" index: if there exists continuous branch \(\widetilde\vartheta\) such that for any \(R>0\) define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scattering-wind-def}
\mathrm{Wind}_R(\det\mathcal S)
:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\bigl(\widetilde\vartheta(R)-\widetilde\vartheta(-R)\bigr),
\end{equation}
and the limit \(\mathrm{Wind}(\det\mathcal S):=\lim_{R\to\infty}\mathrm{Wind}_R(\det\mathcal S)\) exists, then under standard regularity conditions this limit is an integer and equivalent to Levinson-type point spectral counting/spectral flow (also see \cite{Yafaev1992ScatteringGeneralTheory,Simon2005TraceIdeals} for trace-class scattering and determinant theory).

\begin{definition}[Self-Dual (Antilinear) Scattering Symmetry: Semantic Axiom of Spectral Shift Parity]\label{def:selfdual-scattering-symmetry-ssf}
Under the above scattering caliber, we say \(\det\mathcal S\) satisfies \textbf{self-dual (antilinear) scattering symmetry} if there exists an antilinear isometry \(C:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H\) (\(C^2=I\)) such that for almost all \(\lambda\in\RR\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selfdual-scattering-det-sym}
\boxed{\;\det\mathcal S(-\lambda)=\overline{\det\mathcal S(\lambda)}\;}
\end{equation}
and high-energy normalization can be chosen so \(\lim_{|\lambda|\to\infty}\det\mathcal S(\lambda)=1\) (equivalently \(\vartheta(\lambda)\to 0\)).
This assumption expresses: scattering data mirror-matches under antilinear involution by \(\lambda\mapsto-\lambda\), thereby compressing allowed global phase freedom to "odd function phase."
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral Shift Parity Enforcement]\label{prop:selfdual-ssf-odd}
Under assumptions of Definition \ref{def:selfdual-scattering-symmetry-ssf}, one can gauge-choose spectral shift function so
\[
\boxed{\;\xi(-\lambda)=-\xi(\lambda)\qquad(\text{a.e. }\lambda\in\RR)\;}
\]
equivalently, one can take scattering phase satisfying \(\vartheta(-\lambda)=-\vartheta(\lambda)\) (modulo consistent \(2\pi\) gauge).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:birman-krein-detS-ssf} and \eqref{eq:selfdual-scattering-det-sym} we have
\(
e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi(-\lambda)}=\overline{e^{-2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi(\lambda)}}=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\xi(\lambda)}
\)
almost everywhere, so \(\xi(-\lambda)\equiv-\xi(\lambda)\pmod{\ZZ}\).
Using spectral shift function's "defined up to additive constant" ambiguity and combining high-energy normalization (making \(\xi(\lambda)\to 0\) at infinity), one can choose consistent gauge eliminating this integer-valued ambiguity, thereby obtaining stated parity. QED.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Parity \(\Rightarrow\) Winding Vanish]\label{prop:selfdual-wind-vanish}
Under gauge of Proposition \ref{prop:selfdual-ssf-odd}, if winding index \(\mathrm{Wind}(\det\mathcal S)\) exists, then necessarily
\[
\boxed{\;\mathrm{Wind}(\det\mathcal S)=0\;}
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:scattering-wind-def} and \(\widetilde\vartheta(-\lambda)=-\widetilde\vartheta(\lambda)\) we have \(\mathrm{Wind}_R(\det\mathcal S)=\widetilde\vartheta(R)/\pi\).
By high-energy normalization \(\widetilde\vartheta(R)\to 0\) (\(R\to\infty\)) we immediately get \(\mathrm{Wind}(\det\mathcal S)=0\). QED.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Hardy/Smirnov Factorization and Blaschke Defect Dimension}
Under Hardy/Smirnov condition caliber, zero-point information of disk compactification object can be strictly separated by inner-outer factorization (Appendix \S\ref{subsec:unit-disk-inner-outer}):
if \(F\) is bounded type in unit disk \(\mathbb{D}=\{w:|w|<1\}\) (e.g., \(F\in N^+(\mathbb{D})\) or some \(H^p\) quotient), then there exists canonical decomposition
\[
\boxed{\;F(w)=B_F(w)\,S_F(w)\,O_F(w)\;}
\]
where \(B_F\) is Blaschke factor (encoding zeros in \(\mathbb{D}\)), \(S_F\) is singular inner factor, \(O_F\) is outer factor; and boundary modulus \(|F(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})|\) determines only \(O_F\), while disk zero data is completely carried by \(B_F\) \cite{Duren1970TheoryHp,Garnett2007BoundedAnalyticFunctions}.
\par
If further satisfying boundary unitarization (e.g., boundary condition \(|\det\mathcal S(\lambda)|=1\) in scattering realization rewrites to \(|F(e^{\mathrm{i}\theta})|=1\) after Cayley disk transformation), then \(F\) is inner function and outer factor \(O_F\) degenerates to constant phase; disk point defects are therefore completely carried by Blaschke data. At this point "point defect index" can be aligned with winding and Blaschke degree as a pure topological invariant.

\begin{proposition}[Winding=\texorpdfstring{$\deg(B_F)$}{deg(B)} (Finite Blaschke/No Singular Inner Factor Caliber)]\label{prop:winding-equals-blaschke-degree}
Let \(F\) be inner function on \(\mathbb{D}\) with no singular inner factor (\(S_F\equiv 1\)), and its Blaschke factor \(B_F\) is finite Blaschke product.
Define boundary winding
\[
\mathrm{Wind}(F):=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|w|=1}\frac{F'(w)}{F(w)}\,dw\in\ZZ.
\]
Then the identity holds
\[
\boxed{\;\mathrm{Wind}(F)=\deg(B_F)=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)\;}
\]
where the last step uses Remark \ref{rem:blaschke-defect-finite}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(F\) is inner function and \(S_F\equiv 1\), its outer factor is constant phase and \(F=c\,B_F\) (\(|c|=1\)).
Constant phase does not affect \(\mathrm{Wind}(F)\), thus
\[
\mathrm{Wind}(F)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{|w|=1}\frac{B_F'(w)}{B_F(w)}\,dw.
\]
By argument principle, right side equals total count of disk zeros counted with multiplicity, namely \(\deg(B_F)\).
Finally by Remark \ref{rem:blaschke-defect-finite} we get \(\deg(B_F)=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)\). QED.
\end{proof}
\par
To rewrite "discrete orthogonal residual axis needing externalization" as a pure analytic invariant, introduce model space
\[
K_B:=H^2\ominus BH^2
\]
and accordingly define Blaschke defect dimension.

\begin{definition}[Blaschke Defect Dimension (Disk Point Spectral Residual)]\label{def:blaschke-defect-dimension}
Let \(F\) be bounded type in \(\mathbb{D}\) and denote its Blaschke factor as \(B_F\).
Define the \textbf{Blaschke defect dimension} of \(F\) as
\[
\boxed{\;\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F):=\dim K_{B_F}\in\{0,1,2,\dots,\infty\}\;}
\]
where \(K_{B_F}:=H^2\ominus B_FH^2\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Finite Blaschke Case: Defect Dimension Equals Total Zero Multiplicity]\label{rem:blaschke-defect-finite}
If \(B_F\) is finite Blaschke product, then \(K_{B_F}\) is finite-dimensional and \(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)\) equals total count of zeros in \(\mathbb{D}\) with multiplicity.
Therefore under auditable finite-dimensional/rational realization caliber, \(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)\) can be directly verified by root counting/algebraic certificate.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Index Vanish \(\Rightarrow\) Blaschke Defect Zero]\label{cor:selfdual-index-vanish-implies-dbl0}
When scattering caliber of Proposition \ref{prop:selfdual-wind-vanish} and finite inner function caliber of Proposition \ref{prop:winding-equals-blaschke-degree} both hold, we have
\[
\boxed{\;\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)=0\qquad(\text{equivalent to }B_F\equiv 1)\;}
\]
thus no point zero data in disk.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition \ref{prop:selfdual-wind-vanish} we get \(\mathrm{Wind}(F)=0\) (identifying disk readout of scattering determinant as \(F\)), then by Proposition \ref{prop:winding-equals-blaschke-degree} we get \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)=\mathrm{Wind}(F)=0\). QED.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Blaschke Defect \texorpdfstring{$\Leftrightarrow$}{<->} Point Spectral Residual: Minimal Realization of Functional Model}
Blaschke data is not just "zero counting," it is equivalent in functional model to point spectral residual of minimal realization: disk zeros correspond to discrete eigenmodes, while singular support on fixed slice corresponds to continuous/boundary spectral load.
In verifiable semantics of this section, this gives a stronger interpretive statement: the so-called "orthogonal residual axis" is not arbitrarily added but forced out as minimal point spectral defect by Blaschke factor of readout function itself.

\begin{theorem}[Blaschke–Point Spectrum Correspondence (Finite Defect Caliber)]\label{thm:blaschke-point-spectrum-correspondence}
Let \(B\) be finite Blaschke product and \(K_B:=H^2\ominus BH^2\).
Denote \(S\) as unilateral shift on \(H^2\) \((Sf)(w)=wf(w)\), and let compressed shift
\[
S_B:=P_{K_B}\,S|_{K_B},
\qquad
A_B:=S_B^\ast.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(A_B\) is contraction, and its point spectrum (by algebraic multiplicity) coincides with zeros of \(B\) (by multiplicity);
  \item Dimension of point spectral generating subspace \(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{pp}}(A_B)\) satisfies
  \[
  \boxed{\;\dim\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{pp}}(A_B)=\dim K_B=\deg B\;}
  \]
  thus \(\deg B\) gives minimal orthogonal dimension of "point spectral residual axis."
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Alignment with Characteristic Function/Minimal Realization]\label{rem:blaschke-characteristic-function-alignment}
Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-point-spectrum-correspondence} is a verifiable instance of Sz.-Nagy–Foia\c{s} functional model in scalar finite inner function case: \(B\) can be viewed as characteristic function of some minimal contraction realization, and its disk zeros precisely correspond to discrete eigenmodes of realization; corresponding general theory and de Branges–Rovnyak/model space framework can be seen in \cite{SzNagyFoiasBercoviciKerchy2010HarmonicAnalysis,Sarason1994SubHardyHilbertSpaces}.
\par
In our semantics, meaning of this alignment is: if readout object is required on fixed slice to have "no extra orthogonal residual axis," then its realization does not allow above point spectral residual to appear; this constraint on analytic side is precisely \(B_F\equiv 1\).
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[CMV Limit Point Spectrum Characterization: Blaschke Defect Degree Equals CMV Unitary Operator's Point Spectral Dimension (Finite Defect Caliber)]\label{cor:xi-cmv-point-spectrum-dimension-equals-blaschke-degree}
Let \(B\) be finite Blaschke product, denote its Schur/Verblunsky parameters as \((\alpha_n)_{n\ge 0}\).
Let \(U_\infty(B)\) be CMV unitary object determined by \((\alpha_n)\) (in finite Blaschke case its Schur recursion terminates in finite steps, so \(U_\infty(B)\) degenerates to finite-dimensional block; see \cite{Simon2005OPUC1}).
Then point spectral generating subspace dimension satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;\dim\mathcal H_{\mathrm{pp}}\!\bigl(U_\infty(B)\bigr)=\deg(B)=\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(B).\;}
\]
In particular, \(\deg(B)=0\) if and only if this CMV model has no point spectrum (equivalently: spectral measure has no atomic part).
\par
For general bounded type readout \(F\), if its inner factor satisfies finite defect caliber of "finite Blaschke and no singular inner factor," then above formula gives operator characterization fully isomorphic to Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-point-spectrum-correspondence}: \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)\) equals minimal point spectral residual axis dimension.
For disk compactification object \(F_\zeta\), by Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-rh-iff-disk-zero-free} (RH \(\Longleftrightarrow\) disk zero forbidden) we obtain
\[
\mathrm{RH}
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F_\zeta)=0
\ \Longleftrightarrow\
\dim\mathcal H_{\mathrm{pp}}\!\bigl(U_\infty(B_{F_\zeta})\bigr)=0.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Finite Blaschke product's Schur recursion terminates in finite steps: if \(\deg(B)=\kappa\), then \(B\) can be completely recovered by finite segment \((\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{\kappa-1})\), and corresponding CMV model contains only \(\kappa\) discrete modes (see \cite{Simon2005OPUC1}).
Therefore point spectral subspace dimension is \(\kappa\).
Then by \(\mathfrak d_{\mathrm{Bl}}(B)=\deg(B)\) (Remark \ref{rem:blaschke-defect-finite}) we get shown equality; remaining equivalence directly follows from \(\kappa=0\iff B\equiv 1\) and Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-rh-iff-disk-zero-free}. QED.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Fixed Slice Support Principle: Blaschke Defect Zero Forces Boundary Support}
\begin{theorem}[Blaschke Defect Zero \(\Rightarrow\) Singularity Supported on Fixed Slice]\label{thm:blaschke-defect-zero-fixed-slice-support}
Let \(F\) be bounded type in \(\mathbb{D}\) and satisfy self-dual \(F=F^\sharp\).
If \(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)=0\) (equivalent to \(B_F\equiv 1\)), then \(F\) has no zeros in \(\mathbb{D}\).
Furthermore, under self-dual constraint, zeros of \(F\) in exterior domain \(|w|>1\) are also excluded; therefore all zeros/poles of \(F\) can only appear on fixed slice \(|w|=1\) in form of boundary singular support (if existing then determined by \(S_F\) and boundary measure, see Appendix \S\ref{subsec:unit-disk-inner-outer}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{Bl}}(F)=0\) equivalent to \(K_{B_F}=\{0\}\), thus \(B_F\equiv 1\); so \(F\) has no zeros in \(\mathbb{D}\).
Self-dual \(F=F^\sharp\) forces disk interior-exterior zeros to pair correspond under \(\sigma\): if exterior zero \(w_0\) exists (\(|w_0|>1\)), then \(\sigma(w_0)=1/\overline{w_0}\in\mathbb{D}\) must be disk zero, contradiction.
Therefore both disk interior and exterior have no zeros.
Under bounded type caliber, remaining possible singularities can only be supported on \(|w|=1\) as boundary singular inner factor \(S_F\) or boundary limit.
QED.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Scattering Determinant–Blaschke Defect–Negative Square Index: Mutually Exclusive Invariant Chain of True Resonance and Threshold Resonance}\label{par:selfdual-scattering-blaschke-neg-square-chain}
This section already has two complementary decompositions strictly dichotomizing "extreme resonance" into two mutually exclusive supports:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{True resonance (point singularity in stable domain)} is carried by disk point data and completely encoded by Blaschke factor \(B_F\) in bounded type factorization (Proposition \ref{prop:selfdual-maslov-singular-dichotomy});
  \item \textbf{Threshold resonance (boundary support)} is carried by singular inner factor \(S_F\) and boundary singular measure (same Proposition \ref{prop:selfdual-maslov-singular-dichotomy}).
\end{itemize}
If further in caliber of "finite-type point resonance, and no point pole on boundary," then true resonance counting can be compressed into a set of mutually equivalent, realization-independent, auditable invariants: scattering determinant winding, Blaschke degree, model space dimension, and generalized Carathéodory/Schur negative square index (Pontryagin index).

\paragraph{Birman–Krein–Blaschke Identity: Fourfold Equivalence of Resonance Counting}
Let \(F\) be bounded type readout function on \(\mathbb{D}\), and its canonical decomposition satisfies \(F=B_FO_F\) (omitting singular inner factor to highlight point resonance; and assume \(B_F\) is finite Blaschke product).
Let \(\det\mathcal S(\lambda)\) be unitary-slice scattering determinant compatible with this readout (boundary value of \(\lambda\in\RR\)), and let \(\xi_F(\lambda)\) be Krein spectral shift function making Birman–Krein relation \eqref{eq:birman-krein-detS-ssf} hold.
\par
To align "negative square index" with point resonance on same object, introduce logarithmic derivative induced by \(F\) (horizon impedance/generalized Carathéodory logarithmic derivative)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selfdual-CF-log-derivative}
\boxed{\;
\mathscr{C}_F(w):=-\frac{F'(w)}{F(w)}\qquad(w\in\mathbb{D}\setminus F^{-1}(0))\;}
\end{equation}
which has poles at disk zeros; in finite-type case of "finite disk zeros and no boundary zeros," \(\mathscr{C}_F\) is meromorphic function on \(\mathbb{D}\) with only finitely many disk poles.
Denote total algebraic multiplicity of these disk poles as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:selfdual-kappaF-polecount}
\boxed{\;
\kappa(F):=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{D}}\operatorname{ord}_{a}\!\bigl(\mathscr{C}_F\bigr)\ \in\ \NN\cup\{0\},\;}
