\section{Golden Step Scan and Space-filling: Uniformity Certificates}

\subsection{One dimension: Kronecker scan on the circle}

On $\RR/\ZZ$, the sequence $x_t=x_0+t\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)$ is equidistributed for any irrational $\alpha$. Finite-sample uniformity is characterized by discrepancy:
\[
D_N^*(\alpha):=\sup_{0\le u\le 1}\left|\frac{1}{N}\card{\{0\le t<N:\ x_t\in[0,u)\}}-u\right|.
\]
When $\alpha$ is badly approximable, $D_N^*(\alpha)$ has an explicit upper bound of type $O((\log N)/N)$; the golden branch $\alpha=\varph^{-1}$ has optimal constant-type properties within this class, and is therefore more stable under finite resource constraints.

\subsection{Two dimensions: From torus uniformity to disk uniformity}

Take
\[
(u_t,v_t)=(t\alpha\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1,\ t\beta\ \mathrm{mod}\ 1)\in[0,1)^2,
\]
where $(1,\alpha,\beta)$ is rationally independent; then $(u_t,v_t)$ is equidistributed in the unit square. Via the area-preserving map
\[
r=\sqrt{u},\quad \theta=2\pi v,
\]
we obtain disk points $p_t=(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)$. When $(u_t,v_t)$ is equidistributed, $p_t$ has asymptotic uniform distribution in the disk. Choosing $\alpha=\varph^{-1}$ and $\beta=\varph^{-2}$ gives a ``golden angle'' type construction, whose finite-sample uniformity is inherited from the torus discrepancy certificate.

\subsection{Higher dimensions: Kronecker orbits and space-filling map composition}

For arbitrary dimension $d$, take $\boldsymbol\alpha\in\RR^d$ such that $(1,\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_d)$ is rationally independent, and set
\[
\boldsymbol x_t=\boldsymbol x_0+t\boldsymbol\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\in[0,1)^d.
\]
This sequence is equidistributed in the unit hypercube. If the target space $\mathcal M$ (a compact manifold or bounded domain) is parametrized by a measure-preserving surjection $H:[0,1)\to\mathcal M$ (such as a high-dimensional generalization of Hilbert-type space-filling maps), then the composite sequence $H(\pi_1(\boldsymbol x_t))$ (or more general encodings) yields a deterministic scan on $\mathcal M$. The role of the golden branch is to provide stronger finite-sample uniformity bounds for $\boldsymbol x_t$, thereby enhancing readout stability.
