\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

At the conceptual level, HPA treats unitarity as ontology: the microscopic object is a unit-norm state, and information conservation is norm conservation.
Observable data arise only after readout, i.e.\ an orthogonal cut into distinguishable channels (Section~\ref{sec:ontological_premise} and Subsection~\ref{subsec:orthogonality_price_of_readout}).
Concretely, for a unit state $|\Psi\rangle$ and an orthonormal basis $\{|e_i\rangle\}$, the coefficients $c_i:=\langle e_i|\Psi\rangle$ satisfy the Hilbert-space Pythagorean law
$$
\|\Psi\|^2=\sum_i |c_i|^2,
$$
so conservation of the whole is expressed as a sum of orthogonal components.
For unit vectors, $|c_i|=|\langle e_i|\Psi\rangle|=\cos(\theta_i)$ for the readout angle $\theta_i$ between $|\Psi\rangle$ and $|e_i\rangle$, so squared projection amplitudes are literally cosine-square losses in readout geometry.
In standard quantum kinematics this geometry underlies the Born identification of probabilities with squared projection amplitudes; in HPA, probability and uncertainty are likewise read as unavoidable projection costs when a rotational monolith is forced into a discrete, low-dimensional coordinate grammar (Section~\ref{sec:interference}, Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_holographic_slice}, and Theorem~\ref{thm:weyl_uncertainty}).
The projection kernel formalism (Definition~\ref{def:projection_kernel}) provides one concrete way to implement such probabilistic readout as a squared-mismatch weighting on the discrete lattice image.
\par

We introduced Holographic Polar Arithmetic (HPA) as a self-contained toolkit.

\begin{itemize}
    \item From the $\mathbb{F}_1$-inspired multiplicative skeleton we defined a radial character $\rho$ and a multiplicative phase $\theta_\times$, yielding an embedding $\mathcal{Z}(n)=\rho(n)\e^{\iu\theta_\times(n)}$ that linearizes multiplication.
    \item We proved a no-go theorem: no nontrivial holographic embedding into $\CC^*$ can preserve linear addition together with multiplication. Additive readout therefore appears as a lossy projection from vector synthesis in $\CC$ back to a discrete coordinate lattice, with an intrinsic quantum gap.
    \item We introduced a genuine unitary scan operator $\Theta$ (irrational-rotation Koopman operator), defined time as iteration count, and constructed an orbit-wise cyclic calculus (difference, trace, finite part).
    \item Via binary window readout we obtained Sturmian/Fibonacci structures and, in the golden branch, a canonical coordinate system given by Zeckendorf decompositions (Ostrowski degeneration).
    \item We packaged three reusable modules: singularity compactification, finite-part extraction, and Zeckendorf-coded lattice path sums.
\end{itemize}

Under the minimal-complexity criteria, the golden branch is singled out not by numerology but by two concrete extremal features: Hurwitz anti-resonance and binary-minimal canonical encoding (Proposition~\ref{prop:golden_binary_minimal}). Future work can focus on robustness bounds, explicit error control for finite parts, and interfaces to multiplicative/spectral statistics.
\par
In the same projection viewpoint, probabilistic uncertainty can be read as the effective shadow of higher-dimensional structure when encoded on a one-dimensional time/readout axis.
However, this dimensional reduction is not a defect but a generative constraint. By compressing a static, high-dimensional archive into a low-dimensional scan-and-readout protocol, the model trades the ``total clarity'' of a static description for the ``dynamic meaning'' of a causal sequence. In this sense, the quantum gap (Section~\ref{sec:interference}) can be read as the necessary breathing room in which algebraic agency operates (Subsection~\ref{subsec:algebraic_agency}).
In particular, the scan-rate viewpoint naturally interfaces with kinematic time measures such as Fubini--Study arc-length and the associated scattering phenomenology; see~\cite{Ma2025FSTime}.
