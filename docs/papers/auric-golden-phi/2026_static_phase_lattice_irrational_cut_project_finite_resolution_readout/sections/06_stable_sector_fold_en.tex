\section{Stable Sectors, Type Folding, and Protocol-Layer Characterization}

\subsection{Local Window and Microstate Space}

For any time step $t$, consider a local readout window on the three-dimensional observable set $\Sigma_t$ (such as a finite neighborhood or fixed-length bit window). Abstract it as a binary microstate word of length $m$:
\[
\Omega_m:=\{0,1\}^{m},\qquad |\Omega_m|=2^{m}.
\]
This $\Omega_m$ should not be understood as true particle states, but rather as ``local symbolic microstates recordable after finite-resolution readout.''

\subsection{Constraint Channel and Stable Type Set}

Introduce a group of verifiable ``stable constraint channels'' to select type sets that maintain syntactic stability under protocol readout. To provide a computable prototype, this paper adopts the classical ``forbidden-word grammar'' paradigm: define the stable type set as
\[
X_m^{\mathrm Z}:=\Big\{w\in\{0,1\}^{m}:\ \text{does not contain substring }11\Big\}.
\]
This is a prototype finite-type subshift in symbolic dynamical systems. Its cardinality satisfies Fibonacci recursion:
\[
|X_m^{\mathrm Z}|=F_{m+2},
\]
where $F_{n}$ is the Fibonacci sequence ($F_1=F_2=1$). This cardinality can serve as a quantitative index of ``the capacity of types to sustain long-term stability under local constraints.''

\subsection{Folding Map: From Microstates to Stable Types}

Define a folding operator from microstates to stable types as
\[
\Fold_m:\Omega_m \twoheadrightarrow X_m^{\mathrm Z},
\]
whose action maps microstates that do not satisfy stable syntax to the nearest stable type via some standardized normalization rule. To ensure auditability, this paper provides in the appendix a canonical folding scheme based on Ostrowski/Zeckendorf representations, making $\Fold_m$ computable with explicit degenerate structure (different microstates can fold to the same stable type). The degenerate structure can be understood as ``equivalence classes arising from insufficient readout resolution.''

\subsection{Multi-Scale Consistency (Inverse Systems/Inverse Limits): Resolution-Independent Unified Object}

This subsection formalizes the meaning of ``the ontology and resolution $m$ are independent; results at different $m$ are consistent, only differing in precision'': $m$ does not change the ontological-layer object (the ontology exists on $\Sigma$), but only changes the coarsening degree of protocol-layer readout/folding; the ``sameness'' across $m$ should be understood as \textbf{compatibility/factorization}, rather than that output spaces for different $m$ are completely identical.

\begin{definition}[Scale projection (truncation)]\label{def:static-pi}
For any $m_2\ge m_1$, define the truncation map
\[
\pi_{m_2\to m_1}:X_{m_2}^{\mathrm Z}\to X_{m_1}^{\mathrm Z},\qquad
\pi_{m_2\to m_1}(x_1,\dots,x_{m_2})=(x_1,\dots,x_{m_1}).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Well-definedness and Consistency]\label{lem:static-pi-basic}
The map $\pi_{m_2\to m_1}$ is well-defined and surjective, satisfying
\[
\pi_{m_3\to m_1}=\pi_{m_2\to m_1}\circ \pi_{m_3\to m_2}\qquad (m_3\ge m_2\ge m_1).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The forbidden-word constraint is local, so truncation preserves legality and is well-defined; any element of $X_{m_1}^{\mathrm Z}$ can be extended to an element of $X_{m_2}^{\mathrm Z}$ by padding zeros at the tail, hence surjectivity; consistency follows directly from the truncation definition.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Infinite Zeckendorf Subshift]\label{def:static-XinfZ}
Define the resolution-independent object
\[
X_\infty^{\mathrm Z}:=\{(x_i)_{i\ge 1}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}:\ x_ix_{i+1}=0\ \text{for all}\ i\ge 1\},
\]
and define the natural projection $\pi_m^\infty:X_\infty^{\mathrm Z}\to X_m^{\mathrm Z}$ by $\pi_m^\infty((x_i)_{i\ge 1})=(x_1,\dots,x_m)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Inverse Limit Isomorphism (Scale Consistency)]\label{thm:static-inverse-limit}
There exists a natural isomorphism
\[
X_\infty^{\mathrm Z}\ \cong\ \varprojlim X_m^{\mathrm Z},
\]
and satisfies the commutation relation $\pi_{m_2\to m_1}\circ \pi_{m_2}^\infty=\pi_{m_1}^\infty$ (for $m_2\ge m_1$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Infinite legal sequences yield a consistent family via their prefix families; consistent families are uniquely pasted together to infinite sequences by prefix compatibility; both constructions are mutually inverse and natural.
\end{proof}

\begin{assumption}[Readout Factorization (Scale Compatibility)]\label{assump:static-readout-factorization}
When comparing folded outputs at different $m$, require the folding maps to be compatible under truncation: for original window readout $\omega^{(m)}$, taking the truncation $\omega^{(m_1)}$ when $m_2\ge m_1$, require
\[
\pi_{m_2\to m_1}\bigl(\Fold_{m_2}(\omega^{(m_2)})\bigr)=\Fold_{m_1}(\omega^{(m_1)}).
\]
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Stable Types, Preimage Fibers, and Protocol-Layer Characterization}

Under given resolution and constraint channel, a stable type $x\in X_m^{\mathrm Z}$ is the visible output; its preimage fiber
\[
\mathcal{F}_m(x)=\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\subseteq\Omega_m
\]
objectifies the residual indistinguishable differences under this resolution. The protocol-layer characterization of ``persistence/re-identifiability'' can be expressed through statistical stability of stable types under scan iterations (such as occurrence frequency, adjacency statistics, or defect budget control). Correspondingly:
\begin{itemize}
  \item ``Materiality'' in this paper is not stated as fundamental ontological-layer entity, but as protocol-layer \textbf{persistence and reproducibility of stable type statistics};
  \item ``Interaction'' in this paper appears only as a protocol-layer testable interface: compensatory rules or connection structures needed to maintain cross-location/cross-scale consistency (such as constraint consistency conditions).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Objectifying the ``Future'': Conditional Uncertainty and Posterior Contraction}

For the stabilized visible process $(x_t)$, strictly objectify ``future'' as the uncertainty magnitude of the conditional distribution, such as conditional entropy
\[
H(x_{t+1}\mid x_{\le t}),
\]
or more generally the contraction degree of posterior cylinder set measures. This calibration places ``future'' within the observational-theory construction (conditioning/refinement) rather than reifying the unrecoverable remainder as a new ontological object.
