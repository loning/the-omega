\section{Introduction: from ``negative entropy'' to phase friction, from fitness to predictive efficiency}
\label{sec:introduction}

Schr\"odinger famously characterized life as ``feeding on negative entropy'' \cite{Schrodinger1944}. The statement is operationally correct---living systems are open and must dissipate---but it does not by itself answer a sharper question: \emph{what is life, as a physical mechanism, in a theory where the ontic microdynamics is unitary and therefore reversible?}
Standard thermodynamics can quantify entropy production, yet it treats irreversibility at the operational level via coarse graining and macroscopic state variables \cite{Callen1985}.
The HPA--$\Omega$ program instead locates irreversibility at a specific interface: finite-resolution scan--projection readout \cite{Ma2025HPA,Ma2025FoP,Ma2025HPOT}.
In this view, ``temperature,'' ``probability,'' and entropy increase are not ontic primitives but operational consequences of the readout channel.

\paragraph{From readout mismatch to phase friction.}
Arithmetic statistical mechanics (ASM) makes this interface explicit by identifying a computable mismatch certificate between a continuous phase orbit and discrete readout statistics \cite{Ma2025HPT}.
For a Weyl scan with slope $\alpha$, the readout induces a finite point set in $[0,1)$; the star discrepancy $D_N^\ast$ quantifies the worst-case deviation from uniformity over a length-$N$ window, and the accumulated mismatch $E_N := N D_N^\ast$ defines the operational phase-friction entropy certificate $S_{\mathrm{pf}}(N):=\kB E_N$.
The key structural bifurcation is arithmetic: rational slopes lock into finite periodic orbits with linear mismatch growth, while badly-approximable irrational slopes admit logarithmic mismatch growth and are maximally anti-locking in the Hurwitz/Markov sense \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974,Cassels1957}.

\paragraph{Stable sectors and correction as two faces of the same interface.}
Finite-resolution readout induces symbolic words and therefore admits a complementary viewpoint in which explicit protocol constraints select a stable visible sector (a compressed type set) from a larger microstate alphabet.
The present manuscript emphasizes the control-theoretic dual: rather than assuming stability as a primitive, an agent maintains stability by predictive modeling and feedback that reduce mismatch relative to a passive baseline.
Appendix~\ref{app:isomorphism_dictionary} records a protocol-level dictionary connecting these two languages (stability selection and active correction) within the same layered audit rule \cite{Ma2025RF,Ma2025Z128SM}.

\paragraph{Life as predictive active error correction (AEC).}
Once irreversibility is tied to a concrete mismatch mechanism, a minimal engineering fact becomes unavoidable: long-lived low-entropy structure requires \emph{continuous repair}.
This paper proposes a strict operational definition:
\emph{life is a predictive AEC subsystem that reduces its own phase-friction entropy production rate relative to a passive baseline by acquiring information, maintaining an internal model, and applying feedback, while paying the required dissipation cost.}
This definition does not invoke metaphysical teleology; rather, it derives an \emph{engineering teleology} from readout constraints and information thermodynamics: a living system must keep a positive margin between predictive free-energy gain and dissipation required for repair.

\paragraph{Main contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item We formulate an auditable interface definition of life/agency in HPA--$\Omega$ as predictive active error correction against readout-induced phase friction (Section~\ref{sec:life_aec}).
  \item We derive necessary survival inequalities from a geometric Landauer principle and information-thermodynamic bounds, and introduce predictive efficiency as an evolutionary performance metric (Section~\ref{sec:bounds}).
  \item We propose a falsifiable control-law hypothesis for biological rhythms: adaptive coupling ratios should be biased toward badly-approximable irrationals, with the golden branch as the extremal anti-locking candidate (Section~\ref{sec:golden_control}).
  \item We give concrete experimental/data-analysis interfaces (Section~\ref{sec:tests}) and provide minimal reference implementations for computing the core certificates and statistics (Appendix~\ref{app:repro_code}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Scope and posture.}
This manuscript does not claim to derive terrestrial biochemistry from first principles.
Instead, it provides a \emph{layered interface language} that constrains what any sustainable ``life-like'' subsystem must do in a scan--projection universe: it must implement predictive control at a sufficient information rate and at a sufficient dissipation budget.
Biological chemistry is then a particular high-performance implementation of that control problem.
