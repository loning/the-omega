\subsubsection{Single-Layer Torsion near-RH Criterion: Vandermonde Inversion, Hankel Certificates, and Finite-Step Decidability}\label{subsubsec:finite-part-single-layer-torsion-near-rh}
The constant-layer microscope of cyclic lifts compresses the non-Perron normalized spectrum \(\{\lambda_j\}_{j=2}^d\) into two classes of discrete certificates: torsion-point values and cyclotomic components (\(\mathcal{C}_r\)) (\S\ref{subsec:finite-part-cyclic-lift-cyclotomic-microscope}).
On this basis, this section records a purely algebraic near-RH criterion framework: only a single \(q\)-layer torsion table (\(q\ge d\)) is needed to recover the reduced spectrum polynomial and verify the square-root spectral bound; equivalently, the same criterion can be given in finitely many steps via the Hankel/recurrence structure of the power-sum sequence.

\begin{theorem}[Single-Layer Torsion Criterion for Square-Root Spectral Bound]\label{thm:finite-part-single-layer-torsion-near-rh}
Let \(M\in\ZZ_{\ge0}^{d\times d}\) be primitive, \(\rho:=\rho(M)>1\), and denote the normalized non-Perron spectrum \(\lambda_j:=\mu_j/\rho\ (j=2,\dots,d)\).
For any integer \(q\ge d\), given single-layer torsion data \(\{F_M(\omega_q^j)\}_{j=0}^{q-1}\) (or equivalently, given \(C(M)\) and \(\{\zeta_M(\omega_q^j/\rho)\}_{1\le j\le q-1}\), see Corollary \ref{cor:finite-part-cyclic-lift-zeta-torsion-determines-spectrum}).
Then the following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{kernel-RH:} \(\max_{j\ge2}|\mu_j|\le \sqrt{\rho}\), equivalently \(\max_{j\ge2}|\lambda_j|\le \rho^{-1/2}\);
  \item \textbf{Spectral-disk framework:} The polynomial
  \[
  F_M(t)=\prod_{j=2}^{d}(1-\lambda_j t)
  \]
  reconstructed via Theorem \ref{thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-single-q-torsion-reconstruction} has reciprocal zeros \(\{\lambda_j\}\) satisfying \(\max_{j\ge2}|\lambda_j|\le \rho^{-1/2}\) (equivalently all zeros \(t=\lambda_j^{-1}\) of \(F_M\) satisfy \(|t|\ge \sqrt{\rho}\));
  \item \textbf{Hankel/exponential-rate framework:} Let \(u_n(M):=\sum_{j=2}^{d}\lambda_j^{n}\) (see the power-sum notation in \S\ref{subsec:finite-part-cyclic-lift-cyclotomic-microscope}), then
  \[
  \limsup_{n\to\infty}|u_n(M)|^{1/n}\le \rho^{-1/2}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, the above criterion is \emph{decidable in finitely many steps} in principle: because \(\{u_n(M)\}\) is a finite exponential sum, it satisfies a finite-order linear recurrence (Theorem \ref{thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-spectrum-identifiability}), and its exponential rate \(\limsup |u_n|^{1/n}\) is completely determined in finitely many steps by the moduli of the roots of this recurrence's characteristic polynomial.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{(1)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(2).}
By definition \(\lambda_j=\mu_j/\rho\), the condition \(\max_{j\ge2}|\mu_j|\le \sqrt{\rho}\) is equivalent to \(\max_{j\ge2}|\lambda_j|\le \rho^{-1/2}\).
Theorem \ref{thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-single-q-torsion-reconstruction} states: when \(q\ge d\) the single-layer torsion data uniquely determines \(F_M\) (hence uniquely determines its reciprocal zeros \(\{\lambda_j\}\)), so (2) is a purely algebraic restatement of (1).
\par
\textbf{(2)\(\Leftrightarrow\)(3).}
Since \(u_n(M)=\sum_{j=2}^{d}\lambda_j^n\) is a finite exponential sum, the exponential rate satisfies
\(
\limsup_{n\to\infty}|u_n(M)|^{1/n}=\max_{j\ge2}|\lambda_j|
\),
so (3) is equivalent to \(\max|\lambda_j|\le\rho^{-1/2}\).
\par
Finally, "finite-step decidability" comes from the Prony--Hankel identifiability recorded in Theorem \ref{thm:finite-part-cyclic-lift-spectrum-identifiability}: once the minimal recurrence (or equivalently, the minimal polynomial) is obtained, its root-modulus set can be recovered in finitely many steps and used to verify the inequality.
\end{proof}
