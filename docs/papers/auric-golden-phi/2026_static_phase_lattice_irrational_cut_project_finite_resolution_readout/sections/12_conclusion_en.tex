\section{Conclusion}

This paper presents a unified framework with six-dimensional integer lattice phase backbone as ontology and irrational-orientation cut-and-project with scan readout as protocol, to explain how three-dimensional spatial representation, temporal sequence, and material stability emerge as products of finite-resolution readout. The key contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Defining time as discrete scan iterations of complementary phase parameters;
  \item Defining protocol-layer characterization as persistence and reproducibility of stable type and preimage fiber statistics;
  \item Objectifying future as the uncertainty magnitude of conditional distributions (such as conditional entropy) and posterior contraction calibration;
  \item Formulating observer agency as controllable variational degrees of freedom of phase shift and projection parameters, and characterizing self-referential structure via closed-loop update laws;
  \item Formulating readout input domain restriction by causal reachability as the ``light-cone boundary readout'' causal structure interface, and clarifying that it does not constitute replacement of the ontological layer.
\end{itemize}

The next step of this framework is to complete the identification-layer mapping in concrete experimental and data contexts, verify the degeneracy fingerprints, quasi-periodic spectra, and closed-loop stability propositions proposed in this paper, thereby transforming the abstract protocol structure into competitive physical interpretations and predictive systems.
