\subsubsection{Discretization and comoving tomography of horizon spectral measure: redshift weights, Lorentzian windows, and unitary spectral reconstruction interface}\label{subsubsec:xi-horizon-spectral-measure-tomography}
This subsection advances the "horizon Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz certificate" to a precise level usable for spectral reconstruction and tomographic scanning: under the RH premise, the horizon spectral measure not only exists but can be taken as pure point atoms; their atomic weights are structurally forced to be \((1+\gamma^{2})^{-1}\) (redshift weights) by the Jacobian of Cayley compactification.
Furthermore, by introducing the comoving translation parameter \(x_0\), we obtain a family of Lorentzian window (Cauchy kernel) weighted discrete spectral measures, which organize the "horizon hair (multipole moments)" into a smoothly scannable sequence family of moments, and are fully compatible with the "Ramanujan-type symmetry = phase closure (unitary slice)" regime of the manuscript.
\par
Below we continue to use the notation from Appendix \ref{subsubsec:app-horizon-weyl-carath-toeplitz}: \(E_{\zeta}(z)=\Xi_{\zeta}(\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}z)\), \(m_{\zeta}(z)=-E_{\zeta}'(z)/E_{\zeta}(z)\), Cayley compactification is \(w=\mathcal{C}(z)\) and \(\mathcal{C}^{-1}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{H}\).
The horizon Carath\'eodory logarithmic derivative function is (see Appendix formula \eqref{eq:app-horizon-carath-def})
\[
  \mathscr{C}_{\zeta}(w):=-\frac{\Xi_{\zeta}'(s(w))}{\Xi_{\zeta}(s(w))},
  \qquad s(w)=\tfrac12+\mathrm{i}\,\mathcal{C}^{-1}(w).
\]

\paragraph{Horizon spectral measure atomization under RH (redshift weights)}
When RH holds, \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) belongs to the Carath\'eodory class and has a pure atomic spectral measure; its closed-form atomic normalization has been given by Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-spectral-measure-atomic}.
For the subsequent tomographic interface, we extract its core formula here in directly citable form:
there exists a pure point positive measure \(\mu_{\zeta}\) (spectral measure) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-mu-atomic-recall}
\boxed{\;
\mu_{\zeta}
:=
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_+}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(\gamma)}{1+\gamma^2}\Bigl(\delta_{\xi_{\gamma}}+\delta_{\xi_{\gamma}^{-1}}\Bigr),
\qquad \xi_{\gamma}:=\mathcal{C}(\gamma)\in\TT,
\;}
\end{equation}
and its Fourier moments (horizon multipole moments) satisfy the absolutely convergent sum representation (Appendix formula \eqref{eq:app-horizon-moment-sum}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-moment-sum-recall}
\boxed{\;
c_n=\int_{\TT}\xi^{-n}\,\dd\mu_{\zeta}(\xi)
:=
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_+}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(\gamma)}{1+\gamma^2}\bigl(\xi_{\gamma}^{-n}+\xi_{\gamma}^{n}\bigr),
\qquad n\ge 0.
\;}
\end{equation}
where \(\Gamma_+\subset(0,\infty)\) is the positive zero multiset (counted with multiplicity) of \(E_{\zeta}\).
\par
The appearance of the weight \((1+\gamma^2)^{-1}\) is not arbitrary: it is structurally forced by the Jacobian of the Cayley coordinate on the boundary (equivalently, by the residue transformation of the logarithmic derivative pole under Cayley pushback); its semantic consequence is to compress high zeros to weakly coupled atoms in the horizon spectrum, and concentrate visibility difficulties in the endpoint neighborhood (see Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-endpoint-window-mass} and Corollary \ref{cor:app-offcritical-horocycle-busemann}).

\paragraph{Comoving horizon tomography: Lorentzian window discrete spectrum of translation family}
To perform position-parametrized scanning of "horizon hair", we introduce the comoving translation family: for any \(x_0\in\RR\) define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-comoving-carath-def}
\boxed{\;
\mathscr{C}_{\zeta,x_0}(w):=-\mathrm{i}\,m_{\zeta}\!\bigl(x_0+\mathcal{C}^{-1}(w)\bigr),
\qquad w\in\mathbb{D}.
\;}
\end{equation}
Under RH, \(m_{\zeta}\) is a Herglotz function (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-weyl-herglotz}), and the translation \(z\mapsto z+x_0\) is an automorphism of \(\mathbb{H}\), so \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta,x_0}\) is still a Carath\'eodory function for each \(x_0\).
Its spectral measure can be taken as the following Lorentzian window weighted pure point measure.

\begin{theorem}[Discrete spectral measure and moment identity of comoving horizon tomography]\label{thm:xi-horizon-comoving-spectral-measure}
Assume RH holds.
Let \(\Gamma\subset\RR\) be the full real zero multiset (counted with multiplicity) of \(E_{\zeta}\), and denote its multiplicity by \(\mathrm{mult}(\gamma)\).
For any \(x_0\in\RR\) define the atomic points
\[
  \xi_{\gamma,x_0}:=\mathcal{C}(\gamma-x_0)\in\TT\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma).
\]
Then the Carath\'eodory representation of \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta,x_0}\) can be taken as the pure atomic measure
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-mu-comoving}
\boxed{\;
\mu_{\zeta,x_0}
:=
\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(\gamma)}{1+(\gamma-x_0)^2}\,\delta_{\xi_{\gamma,x_0}}.
\;}
\end{equation}
Moreover, its moments (horizon hair multipole moments) satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-moment-comoving}
\boxed{\;
c_n(x_0):=\int_{\TT}\xi^{-n}\,\dd\mu_{\zeta,x_0}(\xi)
:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(\gamma)}{1+(\gamma-x_0)^2}\,\xi_{\gamma,x_0}^{-n},
\qquad n\ge 0.
\;}
\end{equation}
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-c0-im-m}
\boxed{\;
c_0(x_0)=\mu_{\zeta,x_0}(\TT)
:=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{mult}(\gamma)}{1+(\gamma-x_0)^2}
:=\Im\,m_{\zeta}(x_0+\mathrm{i}).\;}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\noindent
Its derivation is found in Appendix paragraph \ref{par:app-horizon-comoving-spectral-measure}.

\begin{remark}[Lorentzian window smoothing and tomography semantics]\label{rem:xi-horizon-lorentz-tomography}
Formula \eqref{eq:xi-horizon-c0-im-m} shows that: \(x_0\mapsto c_0(x_0)\) is a convolution-type smoothing of the zero count measure under the kernel \((1+t^2)^{-1}\);
thus \(c_0(x_0)\) exhibits Lorentzian peaks centered at each \(\gamma\) in the \(x_0\)-direction, and can serve as a tomographic scan signal of "total horizon hair mass".
This signal is homologous to the endpoint angular window mass of Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-endpoint-window-mass}: both are induced by the same Cauchy/Poisson weight \((1+t^2)^{-1}\), only the observation switches between the endpoint angular window on \(\TT\) and the translation window on \(\RR\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Contrast with endpoint compression law: comoving localization removes quadratic radial suppression]\label{rem:xi-horizon-comoving-vs-redshift}
If we consider an off-critical-line offset \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) (\(\delta=\tfrac12-\beta>0\)) under disk compactification, its fixed chart depth satisfies the quadratic compression law
\(
h(\rho)=\frac{4\delta}{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}
\)
(Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-quadratic-radial-compression}).
But in the comoving chart with shifted Cayley \(x_0=\gamma\), the depth degenerates to
\(
h_{\gamma}(\rho)=\frac{4\delta}{(1+\delta)^2}\sim 4\delta
\)
(Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-comoving-horizon-localization}), so the offset appears at linear scale without \(\gamma^{-2}\) redshift suppression.
This contrast provides an analytic characterization of the regime meaning of "comoving tomography": the comoving parameter realigns local structure in the endpoint boundary layer to visible scale, but its cost and addressability clauses are in Remark \ref{rem:xi-comoving-horizon-localization-address-null}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Comoving scan profile of off-slice defects: Lorentzian peaks, exponential spectrum, and Hankel rank certificate}
This paragraph supplements a discrete defect scanning interface fully compatible with the above comoving tomography but not requiring prior assumption of RH: under finite point defect (finite Blaschke regime), off-slice zeros induce an auditable Lorentzian peak superposition signal on the comoving parameter \(x_0\); its Fourier transform gives a single-scale exponential spectrum, so that "defect degree = dimension certificate" and "altitude--offset--weight" decomposable reconstruction chain can be given by Hankel rank stability and Prony-type inversion.
\par
Suppose there exists a finite set \(\{\rho_j\}_{j=1}^{\kappa}\) such that \(\rho_j=\beta_j+\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\) are off-slice zeros of \(\Xi_{\zeta}\), with \(\beta_j<\tfrac12\), and denote their multiplicity (or weight) by \(m_j\in\NN\).
Denote the offset \(\delta_j:=\tfrac12-\beta_j>0\).
For any \(x_0\in\RR\), define its disk image in the shifted Cayley chart
\[
w_{j}(x_0):=\mathcal{C}\bigl((\gamma_j-x_0)+\mathrm{i}\delta_j\bigr)\in\mathbb{D}.
\]
By the closed form of the Cayley transform
\(
\mathcal{C}(z)=\frac{1+\mathrm{i}z}{1-\mathrm{i}z}
\)
direct calculation (letting \(z=x+\mathrm{i}y\)) gives
\[
1-\bigl|\mathcal{C}(x+\mathrm{i}y)\bigr|^2
:=\frac{4y}{x^2+(1+y)^2}.
\]
Thus each defect induces a Lorentzian peak on the comoving scan parameter:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-comoving-scan-lorentz-peak}
\boxed{\;
1-|w_{j}(x_0)|^2
:=\frac{4\delta_j}{(\gamma_j-x_0)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}\; }.
\end{equation}
Accordingly define the comoving scan profile (weighted by multiplicity)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-comoving-scan-profile-H}
\boxed{\;
H(x_0):=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{4\delta_j}{(\gamma_j-x_0)^2+(1+\delta_j)^2}\; }.
\end{equation}
\par
Let its Fourier transform (without introducing additional normalization factors)
\(
\widehat H(\xi):=\int_{\RR}H(x_0)\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\xi x_0}\,\dd x_0
\).
By the standard integral formula
\(
\int_{\RR}\frac{e^{-\mathrm{i}\xi t}}{t^2+a^2}\,\dd t=\frac{\pi}{a}e^{-a|\xi|}
\)
(\(a>0\)) and making the translation \(t=x_0-\gamma_j\) for each peak, we obtain the exponential spectrum closed form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-comoving-scan-fourier}
\boxed{\;
\widehat H(\xi)
:=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}\,e^{-(1+\delta_j)|\xi|}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\xi}\; }.
\end{equation}
In particular, stripping off the "redshift factor" gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-comoving-scan-exp-spectrum}
\boxed{\;
\widehat H(\xi)e^{|\xi|}
:=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}\,e^{-\delta_j|\xi|}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\xi}\; }.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Stable readout of scan Hankel rank: dimension certificate of exponential spectrum]\label{thm:xi-comoving-scan-hankel-rank-defect}
Take sampling step \(\Delta>0\), and for \(n\ge 0\) define
\(
u_n:=\widehat H(n\Delta)
\).
For \(M\ge 0\) let the Hankel block
\(
\mathsf H^{(M)}:=(u_{p+q})_{0\le p,q\le M}
\).
If the complex exponentials
\[
z_j:=e^{-(1+\delta_j)\Delta}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j\Delta}\qquad(1\le j\le \kappa)
\]
are pairwise distinct, then for all \(M\ge \kappa-1\) we have
\[
\boxed{\;\mathrm{rank}\bigl(\mathsf H^{(M)}\bigr)=\kappa.\;}
\]
Thus (in the finite defect regime) uniformly bounded Hankel rank is equivalent to finite defect degree:
\[
\kappa<\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \sup_M\mathrm{rank}\bigl(\mathsf H^{(M)}\bigr)<\infty.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:xi-comoving-scan-fourier}, for \(n\ge 0\) we have
\[
u_n
:=4\pi\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}\,e^{-(1+\delta_j)n\Delta}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\gamma_j n\Delta}
:=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}A_j\,z_j^{\,n},
\qquad
A_j:=4\pi m_j\,\frac{\delta_j}{1+\delta_j}>0.
\]
Thus \(\mathsf H^{(M)}\) has the Vandermonde factorization
\(
\mathsf H^{(M)}=V^{(M)}\mathrm{diag}(A_1,\dots,A_\kappa)\bigl(V^{(M)}\bigr)^{\mathsf T}
\),
where \(V^{(M)}_{p,j}:=z_j^{\,p}\).
When \(M\ge\kappa-1\) and \(z_j\) are pairwise distinct, \(V^{(M)}\) has full column rank, thus \(\mathrm{rank}(\mathsf H^{(M)})=\kappa\).
The final equivalence is the standard conclusion "exponential sum \(\Longleftrightarrow\) finite Hankel rank". If \(\sup_M\mathrm{rank}(\mathsf H^{(M)})<\infty\), then the sequence \((u_n)\) satisfies a finite-order linear recurrence, thus can be written as a linear combination of finitely many exponential terms. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exponential spectral separation of single-scale profile: auditable three-decomposition of altitude--offset--weight]\label{thm:xi-comoving-scan-exponential-spectral-separation}
Under the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-scan-hankel-rank-defect}, the exponential spectrum \(\widehat H(\xi)\) is a linear combination of finitely many exponential terms on either side (\(\xi\ge 0\) or \(\xi\le 0\)).
Therefore under no aliasing (e.g., prior altitude window \(|\gamma_j|\le T\) and \(\Delta<\pi/T\), see Proposition \ref{prop:xi-scan-aliasing-threshold}; equivalently can take branch regime \(|\gamma_j|<\pi/\Delta\)), one can uniquely recover \(\{z_j\}\) and \(\{A_j\}\) by Prony-type inversion from \(\{u_n\}_{n\ge 0}\); then by
\[
\boxed{\;
\delta_j= -\frac{1}{\Delta}\log|z_j|-1,\qquad
\gamma_j=-\frac{1}{\Delta}\arg(z_j)\;}
\]
(taking the \(\arg\) branch consistent with the altitude window) uniquely recover \((\delta_j,\gamma_j)\), and by
\[
\boxed{\;
m_j=\frac{A_j}{4\pi}\cdot\frac{1+\delta_j}{\delta_j}\;}
\]
recover the weight/multiplicity \(m_j\).
Thus the single-variable comoving profile gives a strict three-step decomposition chain: decay spectrum \(\Rightarrow\delta\), phase spectrum \(\Rightarrow\gamma\), weight read from residue/coefficient.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:xi-comoving-scan-exp-spectrum}, on \(\xi\ge 0\) the function \(g(\xi):=\widehat H(\xi)e^{\xi}\) has the representation
\(
g(\xi)=\sum_{j=1}^{\kappa}A_j e^{-(\delta_j+\mathrm{i}\gamma_j)\xi}
\),
so for discrete samples \(g(n\Delta)\) we get the exponential sum sequence \(\sum_j A_j z_j^n\).
For this type of sequence, Prony/matrix pencil and other methods can recover \(\{z_j\}\) and \(\{A_j\}\) from finite Hankel data; by \(|z_j|=e^{-(1+\delta_j)\Delta}\) and \(\arg(z_j)=-\gamma_j\Delta\) we obtain the parameter separation formulas. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Unitary spectral reconstruction interface: phase closure and horizon moment sequence}
In the phase closure regime of unitary-slice, the Carath\'eodory representation not only provides positivity certificate language, but also provides a unitary spectral realization interface strictly isomorphic to finite-dimensional CMV/Schur parametrization.

\begin{corollary}[Unitary spectral realization of horizon spectral measure (consistent with Ramanujan phase closure)]\label{cor:xi-horizon-unitary-spectral-realization}
Assume RH holds and let \(\mu_{\zeta}\) be the horizon spectral measure of \eqref{eq:xi-horizon-mu-atomic-recall}.
Then there exists a unitary operator \(U_{\zeta}\) and cyclic vector \(\mathbf{e}_0\) (can be taken as the constant function \(1\) on the spectral model \(L^2(\TT,\dd\mu_{\zeta})\)), such that for all \(n\ge 0\) we have
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-horizon-unitary-moments}
\boxed{\;
c_n=\langle U_{\zeta}^{\,n}\mathbf{e}_0,\mathbf{e}_0\rangle. \;}
\end{equation}
Similarly, for any \(x_0\in\RR\) and \(\mu_{\zeta,x_0}\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-horizon-comoving-spectral-measure}), there also exists a corresponding unitary spectral model realization \(c_n(x_0)=\langle U_{\zeta,x_0}^{\,n}\mathbf{e}_0,\mathbf{e}_0\rangle\).
This spectralization interface is isomorphic to the "unitarity \(\Leftrightarrow\) unit circle roots" CMV realization in the finite-dimensional case within the text (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-cmv}): the difference is only that the limiting object here is the (possibly) infinite-dimensional spectral measure induced by the real \(\Xi_{\zeta}\).
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Splicing with prime-side absolutely convergent patch: weighted explicit formula family}
Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-euler-patch} gives: within \(|w|<1/3\), \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}(w)\) can be represented in uniformly absolutely convergent form by the gamma term and the Dirichlet series of \(\zeta'/\zeta\); Corollary \ref{cor:app-horizon-prime-moment-interface} thus provides an auditable prime weight coefficient interface for \(c_n\).
Juxtaposed with the zero-side discrete spectral formula \eqref{eq:xi-horizon-moment-sum-recall}, we obtain a family of strict identities "prime-side (absolutely convergent) = zero-side (redshift weight phase sum)".

\begin{corollary}[Weighted explicit formula (horizon moment version)]\label{cor:xi-horizon-weighted-explicit-formula}
Assume RH holds.
Then for each \(n\ge 0\), the horizon multipole moment \(c_n\) simultaneously satisfies: \((i)\) the absolutely convergent prime weight expression given by Appendix Corollary \ref{cor:app-horizon-prime-moment-interface}; \((ii)\) the redshift weighted phase sum by zero atomic spectrum \eqref{eq:xi-horizon-moment-sum-recall}.
Moreover, for each \(x_0\in\RR\), the comoving moment \(c_n(x_0)\) also has an isomorphic prime-side absolutely convergent expression; its arithmetic term is equivalent to introducing a phase factor \(n^{\mathrm{i}x_0}\) in the Dirichlet series (i.e., \(n^{-(\sigma-\mathrm{i}x_0)}=n^{\mathrm{i}x_0}n^{-\sigma}\)), and is strictly equal to the zero-side tomography formula \eqref{eq:xi-horizon-moment-comoving}.
\end{corollary}
\noindent
For the absolutely convergent Euler product patch of the comoving version within \(|w|<1/3\), see Appendix Remark \ref{rem:app-horizon-euler-patch-comoving}.
