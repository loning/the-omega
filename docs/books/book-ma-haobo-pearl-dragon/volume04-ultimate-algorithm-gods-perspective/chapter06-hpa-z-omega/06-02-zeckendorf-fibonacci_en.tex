You must have heard of the \textbf{Fibonacci Sequence}: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21\ldots
Each number is the sum of the previous two numbers.

We see it everywhere in nature: the arrangement of sunflower seeds, the shell of the nautilus, the scales of pine cones, and even the spiral arms of galaxies. Usually, popular science books will tell you: "This is because the Golden Ratio ($\varphi$) is the most beautiful proportion."

But this is only an aesthetic explanation. As a programmer, I want to tell you its hardcore truth in \textbf{Data Structure}.

In human computers, we use \textbf{Binary}. The weights are powers of 2: 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32\ldots
Any integer (such as 13) can be written as $8 + 4 + 1$ (binary \texttt{1101}).

But in the computer of the universe, it uses \textbf{"Fibonacci Base"}.
Any integer can be uniquely represented as the sum of several \textbf{non-consecutive} Fibonacci numbers. This is the famous \textbf{Zeckendorf's Theorem}.

For example, the number \textbf{100}.
In Fibonacci base, it is decomposed into: $89 + 8 + 3 = 100$.
The encoding form is: \texttt{1000010100} (marked as 1 at the positions of 89, 8, 3).

Why go through all this trouble? Why use Fibonacci numbers instead of powers of 2?

The answer lies in \textbf{"Fault Tolerance"} and \textbf{"Self-Correction"}. Although binary is compact, it is very fragile. A single bit flip can cause a huge change in value. The Fibonacci sequence has subtle \textbf{Redundancy} (because $F_n = F_{n-1} + F_{n-2}$), and this redundancy constitutes the \textbf{Error Correction Code} of cosmic information.

There is a very strange but crucial constraint in Zeckendorf's Theorem: \textbf{"Non-Consecutive"}.
That is, when you combine an integer with Fibonacci numbers, you \textbf{cannot} select two adjacent Fibonacci numbers at the same time (for example, you cannot select 5 and 8 at the same time, because $5 + 8 = 13$, you should select 13 directly).

What does this mathematical rule correspond to in the physical universe?

It corresponds to the \textbf{"Refractory Period"}.

Think about our neurons. After a neuron fires, it must rest for a while before it can fire again. It cannot scream continuously and uninterruptedly. Without this mechanism, our brain would have a seizure, and consciousness would collapse.

The universe's \textbf{Z Mechanism} is the same. It mandates: \textbf{Between any two "Keyframes" of time, there must be a gap.}

This gap is the space where \textbf{"Lazy Loading"} mentioned in \textbf{Section 1.2} occurs, and also the \textbf{"Interpolation Interval"} mentioned in \textbf{Section 1.3}.

It is precisely because the Zeckendorf ladder prohibits "continuous filling" that the universe has the rhythm of \textbf{Breathing}. It ensures that history does not crush us like an airtight wall, but like a melody with rests. Those gaps are buffer zones left for the observer (Avatar) to perform \textbf{Phase Adjustment} and exert \textbf{Free Will}.
