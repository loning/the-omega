\section{Phase potential and the Newtonian limit}
\label{app:phase_potential}

This appendix records the minimal variational closure used for the phase potential $\Phi$ and its weak-field interpretation.

\subsection{Variational closure and Poisson equation}

Let $\sigma(\mathbf{x})$ denote a coarse-grained mismatch density in three spatial dimensions, and define an effective source density $\rho_\Phi=\kappa_\Phi\sigma$ with coupling constant $\kappa_\Phi$. We define the phase potential $\Phi$ as the stationary point of the quadratic functional
\begin{equation}
S_\Phi[\Phi;\sigma]
:=\int_{\RR^3}\dd^3\mathbf{x}\left(\frac{1}{8\pi}|\nabla\Phi|^2+\kappa_\Phi\,\sigma\,\Phi\right),
\end{equation}
with appropriate boundary conditions (e.g.\ $\Phi\to 0$ at spatial infinity). The Euler--Lagrange equation is the Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
\Delta \Phi(\mathbf{x}) = 4\pi\,\rho_\Phi(\mathbf{x})
=4\pi\kappa_\Phi\,\sigma(\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation}
The associated phase-pressure field is
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{P}_\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = -\nabla \Phi(\mathbf{x}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Point-source solution}

On $\RR^3$ with decay boundary condition at infinity, the Green's function for $\Delta$ gives
\begin{equation}
\Phi(\mathbf{x}) = -\int_{\RR^3} \frac{\rho_\Phi(\mathbf{y})}{|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|}\,\dd^3\mathbf{y}.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\Delta(1/|\mathbf{x}|)=-4\pi\delta(\mathbf{x})$ \cite{ArfkenWeberHarris2013}.

\paragraph{Mismatch charge and exterior monopole.}
Define the total mismatch charge
\begin{equation}
  Q_\sigma := \int_{\RR^3}\sigma(\mathbf{y})\,\dd^3\mathbf{y}.
\end{equation}
For compactly supported $\sigma$, the far-field expansion is
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(r) = -\frac{M_\Phi}{r}+O(r^{-2}),
  \qquad
  M_\Phi := \kappa_\Phi\,Q_\sigma.
\end{equation}
Thus the monopole coefficient is fixed by the total mismatch charge $Q_\sigma$ and the calibration constant $\kappa_\Phi$.
For a localized isolated source $\sigma(\mathbf{x}) = Q\,\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{x})$ one obtains
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(r) = -\kappa_\Phi\,\frac{Q}{r},\qquad r=|\mathbf{x}|.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{P}_\Phi(r) = -\nabla\Phi(r) = -\kappa_\Phi\,\frac{Q}{r^2}\,\hat{\mathbf{r}},
\end{equation}
and $M_\Phi=\kappa_\Phi Q$ as expected.
In the Newtonian limit, $\Phi_N(r)=-G M_g/r$ for gravitational mass $M_g$, so matching the monopole term yields the calibration condition
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_\Phi\,Q_\sigma = G\,M_g.
\label{eq:kappa_phi_calibration}
\end{equation}
Equivalently, one may define an effective mass density $\rho_\sigma := (\kappa_\Phi/G)\,\sigma$ so that $\Delta\Phi=4\pi G\rho_\sigma$ and $M_g=\int\rho_\sigma$.
In this effective template, gravity is interpreted as a deterministic response to readout mismatch density rather than a fundamental interaction.

\subsection{Remarks}

The Poisson closure is a macroscopic effective choice consistent with locality and the existence of a scalar potential sourced by a coarse-grained density. In the full HPA--$\Omega$ program, $\sigma$ is expected to be computable from explicit microscopic readout protocols and local implementation constraints; the present paper uses it as the minimal interface object enabling thermodynamic and phenomenological predictions.

\subsection{Rigidity of the quadratic closure}

The functional $S_\Phi[\Phi;\sigma]$ in Eq.~(A.1) can be viewed as a rigidity statement: under mild symmetry assumptions, the Poisson closure is the unique local quadratic response law for a shift-symmetric scalar potential.

\begin{proposition}[Uniqueness of the local quadratic action]
\label{prop:poisson_rigidity}
Assume an effective macroscopic scalar potential $\Phi$ is defined only up to an additive constant (shift symmetry), and that its stationary response to a source density $\sigma$ is governed by a local action functional that is (i) quadratic in $\Phi$ and its first derivatives, (ii) translation and rotation invariant in $\RR^3$, and (iii) involves no higher than first derivatives of $\Phi$ in the bulk. Then, up to an overall scale and boundary terms, the unique bulk quadratic form is $\int |\nabla\Phi|^2$, and the unique linear coupling to $\sigma$ is $\int \sigma\,\Phi$. Consequently the Euler--Lagrange equation is Poisson:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\Phi = 4\pi\kappa_\Phi\,\sigma,
\end{equation}
with $\kappa_\Phi$ set by calibration.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By shift symmetry, no bulk term proportional to $\Phi^2$ is allowed, and $\Phi$ can enter only through derivatives or linearly through the source coupling. The most general translation- and rotation-invariant quadratic bulk form built from first derivatives is
\[
  \int_{\RR^3} a\,\partial_i\Phi\,\partial_i\Phi\,\dd^3x
\]
for some constant $a>0$ (Einstein summation). Cross-terms differ only by boundary contributions after integration by parts. The only local linear source coupling consistent with symmetries is $\int b\,\sigma\,\Phi\,\dd^3x$. Varying yields $-2a\,\Delta\Phi + b\,\sigma=0$, which is equivalent to Poisson after rescaling and fixing the normalization to the conventional $4\pi$ factor \cite{Jackson1998}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Signed discrepancy vs.\ mismatch charge.}
The signed equidistribution defect $\mu_N-\mu$ has zero total charge and therefore cannot generate a monopole $1/r$ term. The $\sigma$ used in this paper is not a signed measure; it is a nonnegative coarse-grained \emph{cost density} (phase-friction production), which can carry nonzero total charge in isolated defect sectors.

\paragraph{Flux characterization.}
By the divergence theorem and $\Delta\Phi=4\pi\kappa_\Phi\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
\int_{S_R}\mathbf{P}_\Phi\cdot \dd\mathbf{S}
  = -\int_{B_R}\Delta\Phi\,\dd^3\mathbf{x}
  = -4\pi\kappa_\Phi\int_{B_R}\sigma\,\dd^3\mathbf{x},
\end{equation}
so in the limit $R\to\infty$ one has
\begin{equation}
  M_\Phi = \kappa_\Phi Q_\sigma
  = -\frac{1}{4\pi}\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S_R}\mathbf{P}_\Phi\cdot \dd\mathbf{S}.
\end{equation}
