\section{Quantitative closure: standard theorems and bounds}
\label{sec:verification_route}

As a closed constitution, the present framework is only as strong as its \emph{quantitative} consequences. Here we record standard theorems (with explicit constants where possible) and the constant-bearing bounds used throughout the paper.

\subsection{From unitary scan to induced measures: equidistribution}
\label{sec:quant_equidistribution}

Under O5/O6 the scan orbit is an irrational rotation. Weyl's equidistribution theorem implies that the orbit induces the Lebesgue measure on $\RR/\ZZ$ \cite{Weyl1916,KuipersNiederreiter1974}; in particular, for any interval window $W$,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_W(x_t)\to |W|.
\end{equation}
Thus the limiting ``probabilities'' of window readout are not postulated but induced by the scan dynamics and the chosen finite-resolution window.

\paragraph{Digit statistics from the modular-geodesic coding.}
Section~\ref{sec:modular_geodesic_cf} identifies continued-fraction digits as return-symbols of modular geodesics. The Gauss map admits a canonical invariant measure and a closed-form digit law (Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss_digit_law}), so digit frequencies are quantitatively constrained for typical orbits. Beyond almost-everywhere digit frequencies, the Gauss--Kuzmin theorem provides a finite-time relaxation guarantee: for broad absolutely continuous initial laws, the distribution of $G^n$ converges exponentially fast to the Gauss invariant measure (Theorem~\ref{thm:gauss_kuzmin_convergence}). Metrical continued-fraction theory also provides sharp scaling constants (Section~\ref{sec:ostrowski}).

\paragraph{Finite-$N$ error control.}
For any finite set of scan samples $P_N=\{x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\}$ and any interval window $W$, the Koksma inequality gives an explicit finite-$N$ bound
\begin{equation}
\left|\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\mathbf{1}_W(x_t)-|W|\right|\le 2\,D_N^*(P_N),
\end{equation}
see Appendix~\ref{app:koksma_inequality}. In particular, on convergent lengths the closed-form expressions in Proposition~\ref{prop:convergent_star_discrepancy} turn induced probability into a constant-controlled estimate.
More generally, the Ostrowski/Zeckendorf coding of the tick count $N$ controls discrepancy via digit sums (Appendix~\ref{app:ostrowski_discrepancy}), providing a direct quantitative bridge from canonical coding to finite-$N$ readout accuracy.
For the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ at Fibonacci lengths $N=F_n$, Appendix~\ref{app:golden_constant} yields explicit constants: for odd $n$, $D_{F_n}^*=1/F_n$ so the window-frequency error is bounded by $2/F_n$; for even $n$,
\begin{equation}
D_{F_n}^*=\frac{1}{F_n}+\left(1-\frac{1}{F_n}\right)\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}\,F_n},
\end{equation}
so the window-frequency error is bounded by $2\left(1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\right)\frac{1}{F_n}$ up to the vanishing factor $(1-1/F_n)$.

\subsection{Quantitative discrepancy control and a closed form on convergent lengths}
\label{sec:quant_discrepancy}

The same irrational-rotation model admits quantitative, non-asymptotic control. The Denjoy--Koksma inequality yields uniform bounds at convergent lengths $q_n$ \cite{Denjoy1932,Koksma1935,KatokHasselblatt1995}. More sharply, Proposition~\ref{prop:convergent_star_discrepancy} gives a closed form for the one-dimensional star discrepancy at lengths $q$ satisfying $|\alpha-p/q|<1/q^2$.

For the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$, where the convergents are Fibonacci ratios, this implies an explicit constant:
\begin{equation}
F_n\,D_{F_n}^* \to 1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}
\qquad (n\ \text{even}),
\end{equation}
while $F_n\,D_{F_n}^*=1$ for odd $n$.

\subsection{Hecke rigidity in coefficients: recursion, multiplicativity, and Deligne bounds}
\label{sec:quant_hecke_tau}

For a normalized Hecke eigenform, Hecke commutativity and eigen-structure force strong arithmetic constraints on $q$-coefficients (Section~\ref{sec:eigenforms}). For the discriminant cusp form $\Delta$ of weight $12$, the coefficients $\tau(n)$ are Hecke eigenvalues and satisfy multiplicativity on coprime indices and prime-power recursions (standard; see \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}). In addition, Deligne's theorem implies the sharp Ramanujan--Petersson bound
\begin{equation}
|\tau(p)|\le 2p^{11/2}\qquad (p\ \text{prime}),
\end{equation}
see Appendix~\ref{app:deligne_bound}.
For completeness, Appendix~\ref{app:tau_global_bound} derives a standard global growth bound for all integers $n$ from Deligne's prime bound and the prime-power Hecke recursion:
\begin{equation}
|\tau(n)|\le d(n)\,n^{11/2}.
\end{equation}
\par
Finally, the Euler-product identity for $L(\Delta,s)$ and its absolute convergence for $\Re(s)>13/2$ are standard consequences of Hecke theory (see, e.g., \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}). Beyond pointwise bounds, mature distribution results constrain normalized prime coefficients. In the non-CM case, Sato--Tate predicts a semicircle law for the normalized values $a_p/(2p^{(k-1)/2})$; see \cite{Clozel2006SatoTate,Harris2014SatoTate,BarnetLambGeeGeraghty2011} for background and broad theorem statements.

\subsection{Modular invariance of \texorpdfstring{$j$}{j} and truncation error control}
\label{sec:quant_j_invariant}

The $j$-invariant is $\PSL_2(\ZZ)$-invariant by construction; in particular, $j(\tau)=j(-1/\tau)$ \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990}. When $E_4,E_6$ are evaluated via truncated $q$-series (absolutely convergent for $|q|<1$), truncation errors admit explicit tail bounds that decay essentially geometrically in the truncation depth $N$ at fixed $\tau$ (Appendix~\ref{app:eisenstein_tail_bounds}). Moreover, Appendix~\ref{app:j_truncation_bounds} gives a certified truncation-only error propagation bound for the induced $j$-invariant error. Since
\begin{equation}
j(\tau)=1728\,\frac{E_4(\tau)^3}{E_4(\tau)^3-E_6(\tau)^2}=\frac{E_4(\tau)^3}{\Delta(\tau)},
\end{equation}
numerical sensitivity is amplified near the cusp where $|\Delta(\tau)|$ is small, so any finite truncation or finite-precision evaluation should be interpreted through explicit tail bounds and stability estimates.
