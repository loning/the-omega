\subsubsection{Structural Essence of Ramanujan-type Symmetry: From ``Coordinate Symmetry'' to ``Phase-Lifted Symmetry''}\label{subsubsec:ramanujan-phase-lift-symmetry}
This section has compressed the symmetry induced by completion/self-duality into a fixed geometry on the parameter side: the unitary slice \(|u|=1\) and its Dirichlet image \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) (Remark \ref{rem:xi-critical-line-unit-circle}).
It should be emphasized that the so-called ``symmetry'' here is not a coordinate permutation among several readout axes, but an automorphism of the projection system itself (e.g., \((z,u)\mapsto(uz,1/u)\) or \(u\mapsto 1/u\)) and its fixed slice.
Under this calibration, the appearance of \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) does not rely on the ``symmetry axis'' metaphor, but is the unique self-consistent slice of phase-lifted closure in completion coordinates.
\par
Combined with the disk-horizon--multipole interface and the kernel-RH/Ramanujan spectral bound dictionary (see the end of \S\ref{subsubsec:selfdual-blaschke-defect-fixed-slice} and Appendix \ref{subsubsec:app-horizon-weyl-carath-toeplitz}), ``Ramanujan symmetry'' can be structurally reformulated as follows: it does not geometrically exchange coordinate axes, but protocolically enforces \textbf{boundary unitarization/inner-function-ization}, thereby structurally excluding disk interior point spectrum defects (i.e., Blaschke data).
In this sense, the so-called ``symmetry'' is primarily a defect exclusion law: when self-dual (anti-linear) symmetry is compatible with boundary unitarization, index vanishing enforces \(B_F\equiv 1\), and point-like off-slice defects can only be pushed into the boundary support of the fixed slice (Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-defect-zero-fixed-slice-support}).
Meanwhile, disk compactification compresses the geometric contribution of high imaginary parts into the ultra-thin boundary layer at endpoint \(w\approx-1\); if there exists an off-critical-line zero \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) (\(\beta<\tfrac12\), \(\delta=\tfrac12-\beta>0\)), its disk image \(w_\rho=\mathcal{C}(\gamma+\mathrm{i}\delta)\) satisfies the closed-form compression law
\(
1-|w_\rho|^2=\frac{4\delta}{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}
\)
and therefore can only be verified in regimes with sufficient endpoint resolution (Theorem \ref{thm:app-jensen-offcritical-kink-localization}).

\begin{theorem}[Phase Lift: \texorpdfstring{$[-2,2]$}{[-2,2]} Spectral Bound \(\Leftrightarrow\) Pure Phase]\label{thm:phase-lift-spectral-bound}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a complex Hilbert space and \(A\) a bounded self-adjoint operator on \(\mathcal H\).
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)\subseteq[-2,2]\);
  \item There exists a unitary operator \(U\) such that \(A=U+U^{-1}\).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, when (1) holds, one can take
\[
\boxed{\ U:=\exp\!\bigl(\mathrm{i}\,\arccos(A/2)\bigr)\ }
\]
by continuous functional calculus, so that \(A=U+U^{-1}\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
\textbf{(2)\(\Rightarrow\)(1).}
If \(A=U+U^{-1}\) with \(U\) unitary, then \(\mathrm{Spec}(U)\subseteq\TT\).
By the spectral mapping theorem, \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)=f(\mathrm{Spec}(U))\) where \(f(z)=z+z^{-1}\).
For \(z=e^{i\theta}\in\TT\), \(f(z)=2\cos\theta\in[-2,2]\), hence \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)\subseteq[-2,2]\).
\par
\textbf{(1)\(\Rightarrow\)(2).}
On \([-2,2]\), define the continuous function \(g(x):=\exp(\mathrm{i}\arccos(x/2))\), then \(|g(x)|=1\).
Let \(U:=g(A)\) be the operator given by continuous functional calculus, then \(U\) is unitary.
Since \(g(x)+g(x)^{-1}=2\cos(\arccos(x/2))=x\) holds identically on \([-2,2]\),
the same identity under functional calculus gives
\(
U+U^{-1}=A
\).
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Operator-theoretic Version of Joukowski Compression]\label{rem:phase-lift-joukowski-operator}
Theorem \ref{thm:phase-lift-spectral-bound} gives the operator-level correspondence of the \((y,S)\)-compression in Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}: if \(Uv=e^{i\theta}v\), then \((U+U^{-1})v=2\cos\theta\,v\).
Thus \(\mathrm{Spec}(U)\subseteq\TT\) is compressed to \([-2,2]\) via the Joukowski map; conversely, spectrum on \([-2,2]\) is lifted to unit circle phase.
This ``compression \(\Leftrightarrow\) lift'' isomorphism appears synchronously in the completion variable \(S=y+y^{-1}\) of this section and the half-angle completion variable \(s=\mathfrak{r}+\mathfrak{r}^{-1}\) of the appendix (Remark \ref{rem:xi-joukowski-toy-rh-alignment} and Remark \ref{rem:xi-half-angle-endpoint-paradigm}).
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Amplitude Defect: Amplitude Burden in Polar Decomposition]\label{def:amp-defect}
Let \(T\) be an invertible bounded operator on a complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\), with polar decomposition
\[
T=V|T|,\qquad |T|:=(T^\ast T)^{1/2}\succ 0,
\]
where \(V\) is unitary.
Define the \textbf{amplitude defect} of \(T\) as
\[
\boxed{\ \mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{amp}}(T):=\bigl\|\log|T|\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}\ \in[0,\infty)\ },
\]
where \(\log|T|\) is defined by continuous functional calculus.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Pure Phase Evolution \(\Leftrightarrow\) Zero Amplitude Defect]\label{prop:amp-defect-unitary}
Under the notation of Definition \ref{def:amp-defect},
\[
\boxed{\ \mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{amp}}(T)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad |T|=I\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad T\ \text{is unitary}\ }.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{amp}}(T)=0\) is equivalent to \(\log|T|=0\), hence \(|T|=I\).
Then \(T=V|T|=V\) with \(V\) unitary, so \(T\) is unitary.
The converse is obvious: if \(T\) is unitary, then \(|T|=(T^\ast T)^{1/2}=I\), hence \(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{amp}}(T)=0\).
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Alignment with Three-Axis Address Semantics]\label{rem:amp-defect-address-axis}
In this paper's ``address precedes value'' three-axis address packing \(a=(a_{\mathrm{vis}},a_{\mathrm{res}},a_{\mathrm{mode}})\) (Definition \ref{def:multi_axis_address}), the radial/amplitude channel belongs to the mode axis \(a_{\mathrm{mode}}\).
Therefore, if the readout regime fixes the visible domain as phase closure and refuses to externalize this mode axis, then any evolution with \(\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{amp}}(T)>0\) cannot maintain reversible verification in closed loop: amplitude information must be externalized as orthogonal certificate, otherwise it can only degenerate to \(\mathsf{NULL}\) (see the protocol orthogonality entry in Theorem \ref{thm:xi-orthogonal-slice-structure}).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Ramanujan-type Phase-Lift Principle (Regime-Internal Sufficient Condition)]\label{thm:ramanujan-phase-lift-principle}
Let \(\{\widetilde T_\alpha\}\) be a family of bounded self-adjoint operators realized on a complex Hilbert space \(\mathcal H\), satisfying the spectral bound
\[
\mathrm{Spec}(\widetilde T_\alpha)\subseteq[-2,2]\qquad(\forall \alpha).
\]
Then for each \(\alpha\) there exists a unitary operator \(U_\alpha\) such that
\[
\boxed{\ \widetilde T_\alpha=U_\alpha+U_\alpha^{-1}\ }.
\]
Therefore, the above spectral bound compresses the degrees of freedom that might carry amplitude information into pure phase: in a pure-phase verification regime that refuses to introduce continuous amplitude registers, \(\widetilde T_\alpha\) can be viewed as the visible image of the phase operator family \(\{U_\alpha\}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:amp-defect-unitary}).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For each \(\alpha\), directly applying Theorem \ref{thm:phase-lift-spectral-bound} yields the existence and explicit construction of \(U_\alpha\); unitarity of \(U_\alpha\) implies zero amplitude defect (Proposition \ref{prop:amp-defect-unitary}). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Isomorphism with Kernel-Version RH/GRH: Radius Bound \texorpdfstring{$\Leftrightarrow$}{<->} Phase-Lift]\label{rem:ramanujan-phase-lift-kernel}
In finite-dimensional kernel calibration, ``Ramanujan/RH-sharp radius bound'' appears in the form \(|\mu|\le\sqrt{\lambda}\) (e.g., Theorem \ref{thm:pom-rh-sharp}; see also Remark \ref{rem:pom-profinite-ihara-ramanujan} for Ihara--Ramanujan isomorphic interpretation).
Writing it in normalized phase \(y=\mu/\sqrt{\lambda}\), the saturating mode \(|y|=1\) under Joukowski compression \(S=y+y^{-1}\) falls into \([-2,2]\) (Remark \ref{rem:xi-joukowski-toy-rh-alignment}), thereby manifesting as a structural constraint of ``phase-liftability'' in completion coordinates.
Therefore, symmetry in the Ramanujan context does not correspond to ``coordinate axes being interchangeable,'' but to pure-phase verifiability induced by forcing the symmetric operator family into phase-lifted closure.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Zero Localization of Unitary Determinant: \texorpdfstring{$|u|=1$}{|u|=1}]\label{prop:unitary-determinant-zero-unit-circle}
Let \(U\in\CC^{n\times n}\) be a unitary matrix, and let
\[
D(u):=\det(I-uU)\qquad(u\in\CC).
\]
If \(D(u)=0\), then \(|u|=1\).
Furthermore, for any \(L>1\) let \(u_L(s)=L^{2s-1}\), then
\[
D(u_L(s))=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \Re(s)=\tfrac12.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\(D(u)=0\) if and only if \(\det(I-uU)=0\), equivalent to \(1/u\in\mathrm{Spec}(U)\).
All spectral points of a unitary matrix satisfy modulus \(1\), hence \(|1/u|=1\), i.e., \(|u|=1\).
For \(u=u_L(s)=L^{2s-1}\), we have \(|u|=L^{2\Re(s)-1}\), so \(|u|=1\) is equivalent to \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-Slice Property of Phase-Lifted Closure: Regime-Structural Constraint on Verifiable Zeros]\label{thm:phase-lift-fixed-slice}
In the unitary-slice readout regime, if a verification object can be represented in finite-dimensional auditable calibration as
\[
\mathcal{D}(s):=\det\!\bigl(I-u_L(s)U\bigr),
\qquad
u_L(s)=L^{2s-1},\ L>1,
\]
where \(U\) is a unitary matrix, and the readout regime does not externalize radial/amplitude registers on the mode axis,
then all zero events of \(\mathcal{D}\) satisfy \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
Equivalently, \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) is the unique fixed slice of phase-lifted closure in completion coordinates.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition \ref{prop:unitary-determinant-zero-unit-circle}, \(\mathcal{D}(s)=0\Rightarrow |u_L(s)|=1\Rightarrow \Re(s)=\tfrac12\).
The regime assumption ``refuses to externalize radial/amplitude registers'' ensures that zero events can be verified within phase closure without additional mode-axis certificates, so the above localization is the regime-internal zero slice constraint. This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Finite-Type Hilbert--P{\'o}lya: Unitary Spectral Realization of \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_L$}{PhiL} and CMV Canonical Form]\label{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-cmv}
Under the notation of Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal}, let \(d:=\deg P_L\) and denote
\[
\Phi_L(y)=y^dP_L(y+y^{-1})\in\RR[y],
\qquad
D:=\deg \Phi_L=2d.
\]
Let \(\widetilde\Phi_L(y):=\Phi_L(y)/\mathrm{lc}(\Phi_L)\) be the monic version (not changing the zero set).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If all roots of \(\widetilde\Phi_L\) are \(\{e^{i\theta_j}\}_{j=1}^{D}\subset\TT\), then there exists an explicit unitary matrix
  \[
  U_L^{\mathrm{diag}}:=\mathrm{diag}(e^{i\theta_1},\dots,e^{i\theta_D})
  \]
  such that
  \[
  \boxed{\ \widetilde\Phi_L(y)=\det(yI-U_L^{\mathrm{diag}})\ }.
  \]
  Thus \(\Xi\)-RH (equivalent to all roots of \(\Phi_L\) lying on \(|y|=1\)) can be formulated as a \textbf{unitary operator spectral problem}: zero phases are exactly unitary spectrum.
  \item Furthermore, there exists a five-diagonal CMV-like companion matrix \(U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\in\CC^{D\times D}\) such that
  \[
  \boxed{\ \widetilde\Phi_L(y)=\det(yI-U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}})\ }.
  \]
  This companion matrix satisfies the ``unitarity \(\Leftrightarrow\) unit-circle roots'' criterion: \(U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\) is unitary if and only if all roots of \(\widetilde\Phi_L\) satisfy \(|y|=1\).
  In this construction, \(U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\) can be expressed via a sequence of Verblunsky/Schur parameters \(\alpha_0(L),\dots,\alpha_{D-1}(L)\); these parameters can be obtained explicitly from coefficients of \(\widetilde\Phi_L\) via Schur recursion (reflection coefficient algorithm), and satisfy the equivalent constraint for unitarity
  \[
  \boxed{\ |\alpha_k(L)|<1\ (0\le k\le D-2),\qquad |\alpha_{D-1}(L)|=1\ }.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The first statement is direct spectral decomposition: taking roots of \(\widetilde\Phi_L\) as \(e^{i\theta_j}\), we have \(\det(yI-U_L^{\mathrm{diag}})=\prod_{j=1}^{D}(y-e^{i\theta_j})=\widetilde\Phi_L(y)\).
The second statement is the CMV-like companion matrix conclusion. Any monic polynomial can be realized as the characteristic polynomial of some CMV-like Hessenberg/five-diagonal companion matrix, and this companion matrix can be explicitly parameterized using Schur (Verblunsky) parameters; in this parameterization, unitarity is equivalent to parameters lying in the unit disk/unit circle constraint, which is equivalent to characteristic polynomial roots distributed on the unit circle (see \cite{CanteroMoralVelazquez2003CMV,Simon2005OPUC1,BevilacquaDelCorsoGemignani2015CMVCompanion}). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cayley--Hilbert--P{\'o}lya Unification: Unitary Spectrum and Self-Adjoint Spectrum as the Same Object]\label{rem:xi-cayley-hilbert-polya-unify}
Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-cayley-hyperbolicity} completely translates the ``unit-circle root condition'' into the ``real-axis all-real-root condition.''
In conjunction, if \(U\) is a unitary matrix with \(1\notin\mathrm{Spec}(U)\), then the Cayley transform
\[
\boxed{\ H:=\mathrm{i}(I+U)(I-U)^{-1}\ }
\]
gives a self-adjoint matrix (with \(\mathrm{Spec}(H)\subset\RR\)).
More specifically, if \(Uv=yv\) with \(|y|=1\) (\(y\neq 1\)), then the same eigenvector satisfies
\[
Hv=xv,\qquad x=\mathrm{i}\,\frac{1+y}{1-y}=\mathcal{C}^{-1}(y)\in\RR,
\]
consistent with the root coordinate transform \eqref{eq:xi-cayley-y-from-x}.
Thus in Cayley coordinates, the unitary spectral realization of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-cmv} can equivalently be understood as a self-adjoint spectral realization: unitary Hilbert--P{\'o}lya and self-adjoint Hilbert--P{\'o}lya are not parallel interfaces here, but two coordinate readouts of the same object (\(y\in\TT\) vs. \(x\in\overline{\RR}\)).
When \(\widetilde\Phi_L\) has a zero at \(y=1\), this corresponds to a projective root \(x=\infty\), equivalently manifesting as degeneracy of \(I-U\); one can then unambiguously extend the above correspondence using projective compactification language.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Inverse Spectral Interface: From Critical-Line Phase to CMV/Schur Parameters]\label{rem:xi-cmv-inverse-spectral}
The significance of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-cmv} lies not only in interpreting ``unit-circle roots'' as ``unitary spectrum,'' but also in providing a set of computable \textbf{parameterization coordinates}:
the degrees of freedom of the CMV-like companion matrix \(U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\) can be recursively encoded by Verblunsky/Schur parameters \((\alpha_k)\), with unitarity equivalent to \(|\alpha_k|<1\) (last entry on unit circle) as an explicit constraint set.
Therefore, the task of specifying the critical-line zero structure of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) as some target phase set (or phase distribution) can be rewritten as a finite-dimensional \textbf{unitary matrix inverse spectral/parameter fitting problem}:
first construct (or fit) a parameter sequence satisfying unitarity constraints in \((\alpha_k)\) coordinates, then recover \(\widetilde\Phi_L\) and its phase zeros via Schur recursion.
This interface is structurally isomorphic to the finite-window zero design task of the toy-explicit-formula in Appendix \ref{app:real-input-40-finite-rh}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Pointwise Stabilization of Finite CMV Certificates \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} Infinite-Dimensional CMV Spectral Realization]\label{thm:xi-cmv-stabilization-infinite}
Let \(\{U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\}_{L}\) be a family of finite-dimensional CMV-like companion matrix realizations (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-cmv}), with dimension denoted \(D_L\to\infty\).
Denote the corresponding Verblunsky/Schur parameters as
\[
\alpha_0(L),\dots,\alpha_{D_L-1}(L),
\qquad
|\alpha_k(L)|<1\ (0\le k\le D_L-2),\ |\alpha_{D_L-1}(L)|=1.
\]
If for each fixed index \(n\ge 0\), there exists \(L_0(n)\) such that when \(L\ge L_0(n)\), \(\alpha_n(L)\) is independent of \(L\), and denote its stable value as \(\alpha_n\), then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item There exists a unique infinite-dimensional CMV unitary operator \(U_\infty\) acting on \(\ell^2(\ZZ_{\ge 0})\), with Verblunsky coefficient sequence \((\alpha_n)_{n\ge 0}\) (in particular, for each \(n\), \(|\alpha_n|<1\)).
  \item If we view \(U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\) as a unitary operator on \(\ell^2(\ZZ_{\ge 0})\) via
  \[
  \widetilde U_L:=U_L^{\mathrm{CMV}}\oplus I_{\ell^2(\{D_L,D_L+1,\dots\})},
  \]
  then for any finitely supported vector \(\xi\), \(\widetilde U_L\xi\to U_\infty\xi\); in other words, \(\widetilde U_L\to U_\infty\) in strong operator topology (and is eventually identical on finitely supported vectors).
  \item Let \(\mu_L,\mu_\infty\) be the spectral measures of \(\widetilde U_L\) and \(U_\infty\) with respect to basis vector \(e_0\), then \(\mu_L\Rightarrow \mu_\infty\) (weak convergence).
  Equivalently, the Carath\'eodory function
  \[
  F_L(z):=\int_{\TT}\frac{w+z}{w-z}\,d\mu_L(w)\qquad(|z|<1)
  \]
  converges locally uniformly on compact subsets of the unit disk to \(F_\infty\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Regarding (1): for any sequence \((\alpha_n)_{n\ge 0}\) satisfying \(|\alpha_n|<1\), there exists a unique corresponding infinite CMV unitary operator (see \cite{Simon2005OPUC1}).

Regarding (2): the CMV matrix is five-diagonal, and its first few rows/columns depend only on finitely many Verblunsky coefficients \(\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{N}\).
Given a finitely supported vector \(\xi\), take \(N\) such that \(\mathrm{supp}(\xi)\subset\{0,1,\dots,N\}\). By pointwise stabilization, there exists \(L\) large enough so that \(D_L>N+2\) and \(\alpha_k(L)=\alpha_k\) for \(0\le k\le N+1\); then \(\widetilde U_L\) and \(U_\infty\) have identical matrix elements on the subspace spanned by the first \(N+2\) basis vectors, hence \(\widetilde U_L\xi=U_\infty\xi\). This yields strong convergence.

Regarding (3): by (2), for each integer \(m\ge 0\), the moment \(\langle e_0,\widetilde U_L^{\,m}e_0\rangle\to \langle e_0,U_\infty^{\,m}e_0\rangle\).
This is equivalent to all trigonometric polynomial moments of \(\mu_L\) converging to those of \(\mu_\infty\) as \(L\to\infty\); by density of trigonometric polynomials in \(C(\TT)\), we obtain weak convergence of spectral measures.
The Carath\'eodory function has a power series expansion in \(|z|<1\) with coefficients determined by the above moments, hence local uniform convergence of \(F_L\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Certified Zero Verification: From \texorpdfstring{$P_L$}{PL} or \texorpdfstring{$\Phi_L$}{PhiL} to Hardy Cross-Section Zero Intervals]\label{rem:xi-hard-zero-certificate}
In the completion representation,
\[
\Xi_{\Delta,L}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)=P_L\!\bigl(2\cos(t\log L)\bigr),
\]
thus the critical-line zero problem can be completely compressed into a certificate problem for univariate polynomial roots.
For example, one can adopt the following ``machine-verifiable'' finite procedure: first use Sturm sequences (or equivalent rational interval arithmetic) to isolate real roots of \(P_L\) in \([-2,2]\) into mutually disjoint rational intervals; then map each root interval via the monotonic \(\arccos(\cdot/2)\) to a \(t\)-zero interval and replicate periodically with period \(2\pi/\log L\); finally use explicit derivative bounds of trigonometric polynomials to give an ``at-most-one-zero'' uniqueness certificate in each interval.
In the synchronous kernel-weighted example, the script \texttt{scripts/exp\_sync\_kernel\_xi\_cubic\_cos\_polynomial.py} directly outputs roots of \(P_L\) and corresponding off-critical-line parameters, which can serve as a minimally auditable instance of this certificate procedure.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Sturm--Jacobi Normalization: Self-Adjoint Tridiagonal Realization of \texorpdfstring{$P_L$}{PL}]\label{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-jacobi}
Following the notation of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}, let \(P_L(S)\in\RR[S]\) and denote its monic version
\[
\widetilde P_L(S):=\frac{P_L(S)}{\mathrm{lc}(P_L)}\qquad(\text{leading coefficient normalized to }1).
\]
If all roots of \(\widetilde P_L\) are real (in particular, this is necessarily the case if \(\Xi\)-RH holds), then there exists a real symmetric tridiagonal matrix
\[
J_L=\begin{pmatrix}
b_1 & a_1 \\
a_1 & b_2 & a_2\\
& a_2 & \ddots & \ddots\\
& & \ddots & b_{d-1} & a_{d-1}\\
& & & a_{d-1} & b_d
\end{pmatrix},\qquad a_k>0,\ b_k\in\RR,\ d:=\deg P_L,
\]
such that
\[
\boxed{\ \widetilde P_L(S)=\det(SI-J_L)\ }.
\]
Moreover, the ``root interval criterion'' can be rewritten as a self-adjoint spectral condition
\[
\boxed{\ \mathrm{Roots}(P_L)\subseteq[-2,2]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \mathrm{Spec}(J_L)\subseteq[-2,2]\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \|J_L\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le 2\ }.
\]
Furthermore, \(J_L\) can be constructed from the Euclidean--Sturm chain of \(P_L\) (or equivalent Stieltjes continued fraction data): in other words, the Sturm sequence not only gives a decision for ``roots lying in an interval,'' but also simultaneously provides a \textbf{canonical finite-dimensional Hilbert--P{\'o}lya self-adjoint operator candidate}.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Jacobi tridiagonal realization and its construction from Euclidean--Sturm chain/continued fraction data can be found in \cite{Fiedler1990SymmetricCharpoly,Schmeisser1993TridiagonalGivenCharpoly}; the equivalence follows directly from \(\mathrm{Spec}(J_L)=\mathrm{Roots}(\det(SI-J_L))\) and the self-adjoint spectral bound \(\mathrm{Spec}(J_L)\subset[-2,2]\iff \|J_L\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le 2\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Artin Channelization Completion Criterion: Decomposing Kernel-Version (G)RH into Finitely Many Unit-Circle/Interval Root Tests]\label{rem:xi-artin-channel-pipeline}
Section \ref{sec:A-kernel-compare-main}'s Definition \ref{def:kernel-group-twist} introduced channel matrix families \(M_{K,\rho}(u)\) with finite group labels and channel factors
\(
L_{K,\rho}(z,u):=\det(I-zM_{K,\rho}(u))^{-1}
\), and gave Artin factorization of covering determinants in Proposition \ref{prop:kernel-artin-factorization}.
If some kernel's self-dual/completion structure is compatible with group action, so that for each irreducible representation \(\rho\), the channel determinant
\[
\Delta_\rho(z,u):=\det(I-zM_{K,\rho}(u))
\]
still satisfies two-variable self-duality \(\Delta_\rho(z,u)=\Delta_\rho(uz,1/u)\) and can be written in completion form
\[
\Delta_\rho(z,u)=\widehat\Delta_\rho\!\left(z\sqrt{u},\ \sqrt{u}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{u}}\right),
\]
then for any base \(L>1\) one can define channel cross-section
\[
\Xi_{\rho,L}(s):=\Delta_\rho(L^{-s},L^{2s-1})
=\widehat\Delta_\rho\!\bigl(L^{-1/2},S_L(s)\bigr),
\qquad S_L(s)=L^{s-1/2}+L^{1/2-s}.
\]
Thus Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion} and Corollary \ref{cor:xi-rh-unit-circle-reciprocal} give channel-by-channel equivalent criteria:
\[
\Xi_{\rho,L}\ \text{all zeros on}\ \Re(s)=\tfrac12
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
P_{L,\rho}(S):=\widehat\Delta_\rho(L^{-1/2},S)\ \text{all roots in}\ [-2,2].
\]
Therefore, ``completion + Artin factorization'' compresses kernel-version (G)RH checking into finitely many univariate polynomial root certificate problems (Sturm sequences/interval arithmetic can verify independently); this can form an executable verification pipeline with the channel-level spectral radius bounds in Section \ref{sec:A-kernel-compare-main} (Definition \ref{def:kernel-rh}).
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Strip-Version \texorpdfstring{$\Xi$}{Xi}-RH: Constraining Only Off-Line Zeros within the Open Critical Strip}
The semantics of RH is not ``all zeros on the critical line,'' but only constraining non-trivial zeros in the open critical strip \(0<\Re(s)<1\);
zeros outside the strip are regarded as trivial zeros.
For \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) there is also a natural ``strip-version'' kernel criterion: in the variable \(y=L^{s-1/2}\),
the open critical strip corresponds exactly to the annulus \(L^{-1/2}<|y|<L^{1/2}\), so boundaries \(\Re(s)=0,1\) collapse in the trace coordinate \(S=y+y^{-1}\) into an explicit forbidden window.

\begin{proposition}[Forbidden Zone Criterion for Off-Line Zeros within Strip (Real Root Case)]\label{prop:xi-strip-forbidden-real}
Under the completion representation of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}, still let
\[
P_L(S):=\widehat\Delta\!\bigl(L^{-1/2},S\bigr)\in\RR[S],
\]
and additionally assume all roots of \(P_L\) are real.
Then the following two statements are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item There exists a zero \(s\) of \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}\) satisfying \(0<\Re(s)<1\) and \(\Re(s)\neq \tfrac12\);
  \item \(P_L\) has roots in the forbidden zones
  \[
  (2,\sqrt{L}+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{L}})\ \cup\ (-\sqrt{L}-\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{L}},-2).
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let \(y:=L^{s-1/2}\). Then \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=0\) if and only if \(S_0:=y+y^{-1}\) is some root of \(P_L\) (see proof of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}).
When \(S_0\in\RR\setminus[-2,2]\), the two roots of the quadratic equation \(y+y^{-1}=S_0\) are
\[
y_\pm=\frac{S_0\pm\sqrt{S_0^2-4}}{2},\qquad y_+y_-=1,
\]
satisfying \(|y_+|\ge 1\ge |y_-|\).
Also
\[
0<\Re(s)<1\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad L^{-1/2}<|y|<L^{1/2},
\]
so off-critical-line zeros appear within the strip if and only if for some root \(S_0\) we have \(1<|y_+|<\sqrt{L}\).
For real roots, the map \(y\mapsto y+y^{-1}\) is strictly increasing on the interval \((1,\sqrt{L})\), mapping endpoints to \(2\) and \(\sqrt{L}+1/\sqrt{L}\);
similarly it is strictly increasing on \((-\sqrt{L},-1)\), mapping endpoints to \(-\sqrt{L}-1/\sqrt{L}\) and \(-2\).
Thus the equivalence holds.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Complex Root Case: Forbidden Zone is Elliptic Domain, Not Just Two Real Segments]\label{rem:xi-strip-ellipse-forbidden}
If \(P_L\) allows complex roots, then ``off-critical-line zeros within strip'' correspond to roots falling into the ellipse interior mapped out by the Joukowski transform \(S=y+y^{-1}\) from the circle \(|y|=\sqrt{L}\) (avoiding \([-2,2]\)):

+i\Bigl(\sqrt{L}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{L}}\Bigr)\sin\theta,\qquad \theta\in[0,2\pi).
\]
The two real forbidden segments above are just the intersection of this ellipse with the real axis.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Virtual Period Rigidity of Single-Scale Dirichletization: From ``Comb Lattice'' to Zero Density Barrier}
In finite-dimensional/single-scale calibration, compressing the complex variable \(s\) to a single exponential \(z=L^{-s}\) (or finitely many exponential powers of the same base) forces strict imaginary periodicity, thereby restricting zeros/poles to finitely many arithmetic vertical line clusters.

\begin{proposition}[Strict Imaginary Periodicity of Single-Scale Dirichletization]\label{prop:single-scale-dirichlet-imag-period}
Take constant \(L>1\) and let \(g\) be a meromorphic function of one variable. Define
\[
f(s):=g(L^{-s}).
\]
Then \(f\) has strict period in the imaginary direction
\[
T_L:=\frac{2\pi i}{\log L},\qquad f(s+T_L)=f(s).
\]
In particular, for any finite-dimensional matrix \(M\), define
\(
\widehat{\zeta}_{M,L}(s):=\det(I-L^{-s}M)^{-1}
\), then \(\widehat{\zeta}_{M,L}(s+T_L)=\widehat{\zeta}_{M,L}(s)\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By \(L^{-(s+T_L)}=\exp(-(s+T_L)\log L)=\exp(-s\log L)\exp(-2\pi i)=L^{-s}\), this follows directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Comb Lattice of Zeros/Poles and Linear Counting Upper Bound]\label{cor:single-scale-dirichlet-lattice}
Under the notation of Proposition \ref{prop:single-scale-dirichlet-imag-period}, if \(g\) is a rational function, then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item All zeros and poles of \(f\) are periodically replicated in the imaginary direction with step \(2\pi/\log L\); more specifically, they form a union of finitely many arithmetic vertical line clusters.
  \item In any fixed vertical strip \(\sigma_1\le \Re(s)\le \sigma_2\), the total number of zeros and poles satisfying \(|\Im(s)|\le T\) is \(O(T)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since zeros/poles of \(g\) in the \(z\)-plane are a finite set \(\{z_j\}\) (counted with multiplicity), solving \(L^{-s}=z_j\) gives
\[
s=\frac{-\log z_j+2\pi i k}{\log L},\qquad k\in\ZZ,
\]
so each \(z_j\) contributes one arithmetic vertical line; combining finitely many \(j\) gives (1).
The counting bound (2) follows immediately since each vertical line contains at most \(O(T)\) points in the height window \(|\Im(s)|\le T\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why This is a Structural Threshold toward RH]\label{rem:single-scale-dirichlet-obstruction}
The non-trivial zeros of Riemann \(\zeta(s)\) satisfy \(N_\zeta(T)\sim \frac{T}{2\pi}\log\frac{T}{2\pi}\), with zero density growing as \(\log T\) with \(T\).
However, any rational model of single-scale Dirichletization (such as \(\det(I-L^{-s}M)^{-1}\) and its finite products/quotients) has zero/pole count at most \(O(T)\) (Corollary \ref{cor:single-scale-dirichlet-lattice}), thus incompatible with real \(\zeta\) phenomena even at the ``zero density order.''
This explains why introducing multi-scale (e.g., continuous base mixing below) is a necessary upgrade from toy-RH toward RH research morphology.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Scale Mellin Superposition Completion (Continuous Base Mixing)}
The manuscript has proven: under self-dual conditions, strict functional equations (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-delta-L-functional-equation}) and critical-line real-valuedness (Proposition \ref{prop:xi-delta-L-critical-line-real}) hold for any base \(L>1\).
Therefore, \(L\) can be viewed as a continuous scale parameter and subjected to Mellin-type superposition.

\begin{definition}[Scale Superposition Completion]\label{def:xi-scale-mellin-superposition}
Take a real-valued weight function \(w:[0,\infty)\to\RR\) such that the following integral converges for the \(s\) of interest (e.g., \(w\in L^1(0,\infty)\) with sufficient decay).
Define
\[
\boxed{\ \mathcal X_{\Delta,w}(s):=\int_{1}^{\infty} w(\log L)\,\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)\,\frac{dL}{L}\ }.
\]
By variable substitution \(x=\log L\), this gives the equivalent form
\[
\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}(s)=\int_0^\infty w(x)\,\Delta\!\bigl(e^{-sx},e^{(2s-1)x}\bigr)\,dx.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Scale Superposition Preserves Functional Equation and Critical-Line Real-Valuedness]\label{prop:xi-scale-mellin-fe-real}
Under the conditions of Definition \ref{def:xi-scale-mellin-superposition}, the strict identity holds
\[
\boxed{\ \mathcal X_{\Delta,w}(s)=\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}(1-s)\ }.
\]
Moreover, for any \(t\in\RR\), Hardy-type real-valuedness holds
\[
\boxed{\ \mathcal X_{\Delta,w}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\in\RR\ }.
\]
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For each \(L\), \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)=\Xi_{\Delta,L}(1-s)\); substituting pointwise into the integral gives the functional equation.
When \(s=\tfrac12+it\), Proposition \ref{prop:xi-delta-L-critical-line-real} gives \(\Xi_{\Delta,L}(s)\in\RR\) for each \(L\); since \(w\) is real-valued, the integral is real.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Motivation: Continuous Scale Mixing Breaks Finite Vertical Lattice Rigidity]\label{rem:xi-scale-mellin-break-lattice}
For fixed finite-dimensional \(M\), \(\det(I-\lambda^{-s}M)\) (or finite products/quotients of such objects) can have zeros/poles only on finitely many arithmetic vertical line clusters (Corollary \ref{cor:single-scale-dirichlet-lattice}; see also the spectrum--pole dictionary in Appendix \ref{app:real-input-40-finite-rh}), hence counting function at most \(O(T)\).
However, \(\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}\) is a superposition over continuous scale family \(L\in(1,\infty)\): on the critical line \(s=\tfrac12+it\)
\[
\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)=\int_0^\infty w(x)\,\Delta\!\bigl(e^{-x/2}e^{-itx},e^{2itx}\bigr)\,dx,
\]
an oscillatory integral with \(x\) as logarithmic scale and \(t\) as frequency; the adjustable knob is the tail of \(w\) (determining the superposed frequency window).
This provides a structurally natural channel for the transition ``from finite lattice to possibly non-lattice zero sets.''
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Zero Density Upper Bound for Finite Bandwidth/Finite Scale: \texorpdfstring{$N(T)=O(T)$}{N(T)=O(T)}]\label{prop:xi-scale-mellin-zero-density-upper}
Suppose \(\Delta(z,u)\) has finite bandwidth on the unitary slice: there exist integers \(N,K\ge 0\) and coefficients \(a_{j,k}\in\CC\) such that
\[
\Delta(z,u)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{k=-K}^{K} a_{j,k}\,z^j u^k
\qquad(\text{finite sum}).
\]
Further suppose the weight function \(w\) has compact support \(\mathrm{supp}(w)\subseteq [0,X]\) (some \(X<\infty\)).
Let
\[
F(t):=\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\qquad(t\in\RR),
\]
and let \(N_F(T)\) denote the number of real zeros of \(F(t)\) on the interval \([-T,T]\) (counted with multiplicity).
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(F\) as a function of \(t\)-variable extends to an entire function of bounded exponential type (whose type is controlled by \(X\) and bandwidth \(N,K\)).
  \item Thus zero count satisfies linear upper bound
  \[
  \boxed{\ N_F(T)=O(T)\qquad(T\to\infty)\ }.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Under the assumptions, taking \(s=\tfrac12+it\) and writing \(u=e^{2itx}\), \(z=e^{-x/2}e^{-itx}\),
\[
F(t)=\int_0^X w(x)\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sum_{k=-K}^{K} a_{j,k}\,e^{-jx/2}\,e^{i(2k-j)tx}\,dx
=\sum_{j,k}a_{j,k}\,\widehat g_{j}( (2k-j)t),
\]
where \(g_j(x):=w(x)e^{-jx/2}\,\mathbf{1}_{[0,X]}(x)\in L^1(\RR)\), and \(\widehat g_j(\xi):=\int_{\RR} g_j(x)e^{i\xi x}\,dx\) is its Fourier transform.
By \(\mathrm{supp}(g_j)\subseteq[0,X]\), Paley--Wiener type results give that \(\widehat g_j\) is an entire function of exponential type \(\le X\), so \(F\) is also an entire function of finite exponential type (type can be controlled by \(X\max_{j,k}|2k-j|\le X(2K+N)\)).
Zero distribution of entire functions of exponential type satisfies linear density upper bound, hence \(N_F(T)=O(T)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Density Threshold: Approaching \texorpdfstring{$T\log T$}{T log T} Requires Breaking ``Finite Bandwidth'']\label{rem:xi-scale-mellin-density-threshold}
Proposition \ref{prop:xi-scale-mellin-zero-density-upper} shows: if \(\Delta\)'s \(u\)-bandwidth is fixed and \(w\) has effective truncation on logarithmic scale (compact support or hard truncation in the same order sense), then even introducing continuous scale mixing, critical-line zero count is still at most \(O(T)\).
Therefore, if the goal is to approach \(\zeta\)'s zero count growth order (\(N_\zeta(T)\asymp T\log T\)), at least one hard upgrade mechanism must be introduced:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Unbounded scale tail}: so that the ``effective scale upper bound'' \(X(T)\) for frequency \(T\) grows with \(T\) (e.g., scale law \(X(T)\asymp \log T\)); or
  \item \textbf{Complexity growing with scale}: let \(\Delta\)'s bandwidth/state count (e.g., \(N,K\)) grow with scale parameter, rather than being fixed finite constants.
\end{enumerate}
This threshold further refines ``breaking vertical lattice'' into ``breaking finite bandwidth'': relying solely on finite-scale mixing is insufficient to model \(\zeta\)-type zero sets at density order.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[\texorpdfstring{$\log T$}{log T} Budget: Same-Order Threshold of Scale Tail Window Width \texorpdfstring{$X(T)$}{X(T)} and Phase Mode Count \texorpdfstring{$B(T)$}{B(T)}]\label{rem:xi-scale-mellin-logT-budget}
The proof of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-scale-mellin-zero-density-upper} actually gives a more quantitative result: for any \(X<\infty\), if the scale integral is hard-truncated to
\[
\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}^{(\le X)}(s):=\int_{0}^{X} w(x)\,\Delta\!\bigl(e^{-sx},e^{(2s-1)x}\bigr)\,dx,
\]
then \(t\mapsto \mathcal X_{\Delta,w}^{(\le X)}(\tfrac12+it)\) has exponential type at most \(O_\Delta(X)\), so its height-window zero count satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;N^{(\le X)}(T)=O_\Delta(X\,T)\; }.
\]
Therefore, if for given height \(T\), the ``effective scale window width'' of \(\mathcal X_{\Delta,w}(\tfrac12+it)\) must grow to \(X=X(T)\) (e.g., ignoring tail terms \(x>X(T)\) causes unacceptable error in this height window), then wanting to achieve Riemann-type density \(N(T)\asymp T\log T\) forces
\[
\boxed{\;X(T)\asymp \log T\; }.
\]
\par
The same \(\log T\) budget also appears in the minimal infinity mechanism of ``phase/role axis'': in the finite-dimensional critical-line comb framework, if trying to achieve \(N(T)\asymp T\log T\) by introducing critical-circle mode count \(B=B(T)\) growing with scale (e.g., injecting unit-root phases via cyclic lift Artin factorization with \(q=q(T)\to\infty\)), necessarily \(B(T)\asymp (\log T)/(\log\lambda)\) is required (Corollary \ref{cor:critical-circle-mode-growth}).
Therefore, in this paper's framework, any minimal infinity source for ``approaching \(\zeta\)-type zero density'' essentially needs to inject \(\Theta(\log T)\) degrees of freedom in some direction: either releasing \(\log T\)-level window width in the scale tail, or releasing \(\log T\)-level mode count in the phase/role direction; Proposition \ref{prop:xi-scale-mellin-bessel-kernel} gives an explicitly calibratable implementation example via Bessel--cosh kernel.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Bessel--cosh Scale Kernel Gives \texorpdfstring{$T\log T$}{T log T} Critical-Line Zero Density and \texorpdfstring{$2\pi$}{2pi} Constant Anchor]\label{prop:xi-scale-mellin-bessel-kernel}
Take parameter \(x_0>0\) and define even weight kernel
\[
w_{x_0}(t):=\exp\!\bigl(-x_0\cosh t\bigr)\qquad(t\ge 0).
\]
Then Paris's integral representation gives (\(\nu\in\RR\))
\[
\boxed{\;
K_{i\nu}(x_0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x_0\cosh t}\cos(\nu t)\,dt
\;} \qquad\text{\cite{Paris2022KiNuZeros}},
\]
so the ``Bessel completion module'' along the critical line
\[
\Xi_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(s):=K_{\frac{s-\frac12}{2}}(x_0)
\]
satisfies strict symmetry and Hardy real-valuedness
\[
\boxed{\ \Xi_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(s)=\Xi_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(1-s)\ },\qquad
\boxed{\ \Xi_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}\!\left(\tfrac12+it\right)\in\RR\ }.
\]
Moreover, its critical-line zeros are given by \(\nu\)-zeros of \(K_{i\nu}(x_0)\): if \(K_{i\nu}(x_0)=0\) with \(\nu>0\), then
\(\Xi_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(\tfrac12+2i\nu)=0\).
Denote
\[
N_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(T):=\#\Bigl\{\,0<\gamma\le T:\ \Xi_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}\!\left(\tfrac12+i\gamma\right)=0\,\Bigr\},
\]
then Paris's large-order asymptotic formula \cite{Paris2022KiNuZeros} yields
\[
\boxed{\;
N_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(T)
=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\!\Bigl(\frac{T}{e\,x_0}\Bigr)+O(1)\qquad(T\to\infty).\;}
\]
In particular, when taking \(\boxed{x_0=2\pi}\),
\[
\boxed{\;
N_{\mathrm{Bessel},2\pi}(T)
=\frac{T}{2\pi}\log\!\Bigl(\frac{T}{2\pi}\Bigr)-\frac{T}{2\pi}+O(1),\;}
\]
with first two terms' constants completely isomorphic to Riemann--von Mangoldt zero count leading terms.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Integral representation and \(K_\nu(x)=K_{-\nu}(x)\) give symmetry; when \(s=\tfrac12+it\), the order is purely imaginary \(i t/2\), so \(K_{i(t/2)}(x_0)\in\RR\) (\(x_0>0\)), yielding Hardy real-valuedness.
Let positive zeros of \(K_{i\nu}(x_0)\) be ordered as \(\nu_n(x_0)\); Paris's zero asymptotic formula gives count
\(\#\{\nu_n(x_0)\le V\}=\frac{V}{\pi}\log\!\bigl(\frac{2V}{e x_0}\bigr)+O(1)\).
Taking \(V=T/2\) yields the conclusion for \(N_{\mathrm{Bessel},x_0}(T)=\#\{\nu_n(x_0)\le T/2\}\); setting \(x_0=2\pi\) immediately gives constant alignment.
\end{proof}
