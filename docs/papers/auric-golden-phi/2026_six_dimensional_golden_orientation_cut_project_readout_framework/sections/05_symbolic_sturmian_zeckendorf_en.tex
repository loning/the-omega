\section{Symbolic Readout, Sturmian Invariants, and Zeckendorf Stabilization}\label{sec:symbolic}

\subsection{One-Dimensional Toroidal Factor and Binary Symbolization (H5.1, D5.1)}

To project internal scanning onto verifiable discrete output, adopt the replaceable assumption:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(H5.1)} There exists a measurable factor map $\phi:E_{\perp}/\Gamma\to \TT^{1}=\RR/\ZZ$ such that $\theta_t:=\phi(u_t)$ approximately satisfies circle rotation
  \[
  \theta_{t+1}=\theta_t+\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1),
  \]
  where $\alpha\in(0,1)$ is the rotation number. Take a bipartition $\mathcal{P}=\{I_0,I_1\}$ (measurable, disjoint, covering $\TT^1$) and define binary readout
  \[
  \omega_t:=\ind_{I_1}(\theta_t)\in\{0,1\}.
  \]
\end{itemize}
A window word of length $m$ is defined as
\[
\omega^{(m)}_t:=(\omega_t,\omega_{t+1},\dots,\omega_{t+m-1})\in\{0,1\}^{m}.
\]

\subsection{Sturmian Complexity and Fibonacci Branch (T5.2)}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{(T5.2)} Under irrational rotation and the condition that the bipartition boundary trajectory has zero measure, $(\omega_t)$ is a Sturmian sequence with factor complexity satisfying
  \[
  p(n)=\#\{\text{distinct subwords of length }n\}=n+1,\qquad \forall n\ge 1.
  \]
  \item At the golden branch value, $(\omega_t)$ is a Fibonacci-type Sturmian word, and its return structure exhibits Fibonacci-scale stratification in typical statistics (Appendix \ref{app:sturmian-test}).
\end{itemize}
The above invariants provide reference checks: periodic sequences and general random sequences do not satisfy the full-scale linear law $p(n)=n+1$.

\subsection{Zeckendorf Legal Language (D5.3)}

Define the Zeckendorf legal language of length $m$ as
\[
X_m:=\{x\in\{0,1\}^{m}:\text{no substring }11\}.
\]
This language can also be viewed as a finite-window truncation of the golden-mean shift.

\begin{proposition}[Counting]\label{prop:zeckendorf-count}
(T5.3) We have
\[
|X_m|=F_{m+2},
\]
where $F_k$ is the Fibonacci sequence.
\end{proposition}
The proof is provided in Appendix \ref{app:zeckendorf-fold}.

\subsection{Stabilization Kernel \texorpdfstring{$\Fold_m$}{Fold\_m} (D5.4, T5.4)}

To normalize the raw window word $\omega^{(m)}_t$ to $X_m$, define a surjection
\[
\Fold_m:\{0,1\}^{m}\twoheadrightarrow X_m,
\]
satisfying:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Fold_m(x)=x$ for all $x\in X_m$ (fixed-point property);
  \item $\Fold_m$ is implemented by a local rewriting system and terminates in finitely many steps;
  \item The terminal state is unique (canonical form is unique).
\end{enumerate}
In a standard class of constructions, the core rewriting rules can be chosen as local transformations of the ``carry-elimination of adjacency'' type (e.g., with $011\mapsto 100$ as the prototype accompanied by boundary truncation), with termination and uniqueness guaranteed by potential function descent and Zeckendorf unique representation (Appendix \ref{app:zeckendorf-fold}).

Let the stabilized output be
\[
x_t:=\Fold_m(\omega^{(m)}_t)\in X_m.
\]
This paper calls $(x_t)$ the ``stabilized readout sequence.''

\subsection{Time Fibers and Closure Fixed-Point Semantics (D5.5, T5.5)}

For any stable state $x\in X_m$, define the time fiber (preimage)
\[
\mathcal{F}_m(x):=\Fold_m^{-1}(x)\subseteq\{0,1\}^{m}.
\]
Define the closure operator $\Phi$ acting on $\mathcal{P}(\{0,1\}^{m})$ as:
\[
\Phi(S):=\Fold_m^{-1}\big(\Fold_m(S)\big).
\]

\begin{theorem}[Closure and Fixed-Point Characterization]\label{thm:closure-fixed-points}
(T5.5) $\Phi$ is a closure operator (extensive, monotone, idempotent), and
\[
\Phi(S)=S\quad \Longleftrightarrow\quad
S=\bigcup_{x\in A}\mathcal{F}_m(x)\ \text{for some}\ A\subseteq X_m.
\]
\end{theorem}
The proof is provided in Appendix \ref{app:closure-proof}. This conclusion objectifies the relationship between the ``visible stable state set'' and the ``indistinguishable realization families'' as a fixed-point structure.
