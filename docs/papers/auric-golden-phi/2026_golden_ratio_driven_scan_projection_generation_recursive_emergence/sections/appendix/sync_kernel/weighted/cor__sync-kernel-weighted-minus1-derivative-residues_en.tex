\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$p$}{p}-adic derivative residues at \texorpdfstring{$u=-1$}{u=-1}: \texorpdfstring{$\ZZ_p$}{Zp}-valued fingerprint family induced by Dwork congruence]\label{cor:witt-minus1-padic-derivative-residues}
Adopting the notation of Corollary \ref{cor:witt-dwork-congruence}, take an odd prime \(p\) and let \(a_n(u)\in\ZZ[u]\) be the trace ghost sequence.
Denote the Euler derivative operator by \(D:=u\,\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\).
Then for each fixed order \(r\ge 1\), when \(k>r\), we have divisibility
\[
\boxed{\ p^{\,k-r}\ \big|\ (D^r a_{p^k})(-1)\ },
\]
thus we can define the integer sequence
\[
R_{p,r}(k):=p^{-(k-r)}(D^r a_{p^k})(-1)\in\ZZ\qquad(k>r).
\]
Moreover, this sequence is permanently stable modulo \(p\):
\[
\boxed{\ R_{p,r}(k)\equiv R_{p,r}(k-1)\pmod p\qquad(k>r+1)\ }.
\]
Therefore \(\{R_{p,r}(k)\}_{k>r}\) is a Cauchy sequence in \(\ZZ_p\) and converges to the limit
\[
R_{p,r}^{(\infty)}:=\lim_{k\to\infty}R_{p,r}(k)\in\ZZ_p,
\]
thus for each odd prime \(p\), we obtain a family of \(\ZZ_p\)-valued derivative residue invariants \(\{R_{p,r}^{(\infty)}\}_{r\ge 1}\) that can be audited level by level.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
By Corollary \ref{cor:witt-dwork-congruence}, in \(\ZZ[u]\) we have
\(
a_{p^k}(u)\equiv a_{p^{k-1}}(u^p)\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p^k)
\).
Applying \(D^r\) to the congruence (where \(D\) is a \(\ZZ\)-linear differential operator) preserves modulo \(p^k\); and for any \(f\), the identity
\(
D(f(u^p))=p\,(Df)(u^p)
\)
holds, thus \(D^r(f(u^p))=p^r(D^rf)(u^p)\).
Taking \(f=a_{p^{k-1}}\) and specializing at \(u=-1\) (since \(p\) is odd, \((-1)^p=-1\)), we obtain
\[
(D^ra_{p^k})(-1)\equiv p^r(D^ra_{p^{k-1}})(-1)\pmod{p^k}.
\]
Thus there exists \(t\in\ZZ\) such that
\(
(D^ra_{p^k})(-1)=p^r(D^ra_{p^{k-1}})(-1)+p^k t
\).
When \(k=r+1\), the right side contains a factor of \(p\), so \(p\mid (D^ra_{p^{r+1}})(-1)\); induction on \(k\) then yields \(p^{k-r}\mid (D^ra_{p^k})(-1)\).
Dividing the above equation by \(p^{k-r}\) yields
\[
R_{p,r}(k)=R_{p,r}(k-1)+p^r t.
\]
Since \(r\ge 1\), we have \(p^r t\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)\), thus obtaining the stability modulo \(p\). By stability, it is a Cauchy sequence in \(\ZZ_p\) and converges to some \(R_{p,r}^{(\infty)}\in\ZZ_p\). This completes the proof.
\end{proof}
