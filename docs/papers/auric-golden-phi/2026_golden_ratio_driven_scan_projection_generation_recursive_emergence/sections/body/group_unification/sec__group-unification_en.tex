\subsection{Stable States as ``Golden Eigenstates''}

Consider the adjacency matrix corresponding to the golden mean constraint:
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
1 & 1\\
1 & 0
\end{pmatrix},
\]
with spectral radius $\rho(A)=\varphi$. By Perron--Frobenius theory, one obtains a unique positive eigenvector (which after normalization gives the stationary distribution/maximum entropy Markov measure). In the symbolic dynamics context, this stationary measure is the most stable statistical state with respect to the grammatical constraint, serving as the purely mathematical counterpart of a ``stable eigenstate'':
its explicit form (Parry maximum entropy measure) is given in Remark~\ref{prop:parry-measure-golden}.

\begin{remark}[Parry baseline of the golden mean SFT]\label{prop:parry-measure-golden}
The Parry maximum entropy measure of the golden mean shift is a two-state Markov chain with transition probabilities
\[
P_{00}=\varphi^{-1},\quad P_{01}=\varphi^{-2},\quad P_{10}=1.
\]
Its stationary distribution is
\[
\pi(0)=\frac{\varphi^2}{\varphi^2+1},\qquad
\pi(1)=\frac{1}{\varphi^2+1}.
\]
See \cite{LindMarcus1995}.
\end{remark}

From this, the measure on the length-$m$ stable word space $X_m$ can be written explicitly: for $u=u_1\cdots u_m\in X_m$,
\[
\pi_m(u)=\pi(u_1)\prod_{j=1}^{m-1} P_{u_j u_{j+1}}.
\]
Remark~\ref{prop:parry-measure-golden} thus provides a closed baseline for the ``fitting criterion'' of Section~\ref{sec:quant-fit}; its derivation is entirely consistent with the general finite-type subshift case, see \cite{LindMarcus1995}.

\subsection{Protocol Time Scale: Entropy Rate Time and the Auditable Decomposition of the Object-Layer Isometric Factor}\label{subsec:time-protocol-entropy-vs-rotation}
\paragraph{Two-layer decomposition of the problem (object layer \texorpdfstring{$\neq$}{/=} protocol layer)}
Under the scan--projection readout and auditable certificate caliber of this paper, ``whether time is essentially a golden section rotation'' is not a single proposition but can be precisely decomposed into two mutually independent, separately auditable assertions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Object layer (isometric factor):} Given an observable system \((X,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)\), whether its maximal isometric factor (if it exists) can be taken as a Kronecker translation on a compact abelian group; in the one-dimensional circle case, this translation is an irrational rotation on \(S^1\), and one may take the anti-resonance canonical representative \(\alpha=\varphi^{-1}\) fixed in Section~\ref{sec:principles} as one auditable baseline branch (see also Appendix~\ref{app:kronecker-discrepancy}).
  \item \textbf{Protocol layer (endogenous time metrization):} After fixing the readout and stable grammar, the ``time unit'' should be given by the exponential scale of information refinement/uncertainty contraction, whose irreducible scale is locked by the entropy rate (equivalently, the logarithmic scale of the spectral radius); this scale is independent of whether the object layer adopts a circle rotation coordinatization.
\end{enumerate}
The above decomposition rigorously locates ``golden rotation'' as a \emph{representation choice} at the object layer, and rigorously locates \(\log\varphi\) as the \emph{auditable scale invariant} at the protocol layer.
\par

\begin{definition}[Entropy rate time (information-time)]\label{def:entropy-rate-time}
Under the frequency statement setting (Subsection~\ref{subsec:freq-setting}), let \(\mathcal{P}\) be the observable partition induced by the readout \(s_t(x)=\ind_W(T^t x)\in\{0,1\}\).
For any finite observation string \(a_0^{t-1}:=a_0a_1\cdots a_{t-1}\in\{0,1\}^t\), write its cylinder set
\[
C(a_0^{t-1})
:=\bigl\{x\in X:\ s_0(x)=a_0,\ s_1(x)=a_1,\ \dots,\ s_{t-1}(x)=a_{t-1}\bigr\}\in\sigma(\mathcal{P},T^{-1}\mathcal{P},\dots,T^{-(t-1)}\mathcal{P}).
\]
Define the \textbf{entropy rate time} (or information time)
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{info}}(t)
:=-\log \mu\bigl(C(a_0^{t-1})\bigr).
\]
Under the standard assumptions that \(\mathcal{P}\) is generating and \(T\) is ergodic, the Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem (which can also be viewed as the ergodic form of the AEP) gives
\[
\frac{1}{t}\tau_{\mathrm{info}}(t)\ \longrightarrow\ h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})
\qquad\text{almost surely},
\]
where \(h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})\) is the Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy (see \cite{WaltersErgodicTheory,CoverThomas2006}).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$\log\varphi$}{log phi} scale lock: the time unit comes from the entropy rate, not from rotation]\label{prop:time-unit-logphi-protocol}
When the stable grammar is chosen as the golden mean constraint \(X_\infty\) and the protocol layer uses its maximum entropy equilibrium state (Parry measure; Remark~\ref{prop:parry-measure-golden}) as the calibration baseline,
taking the object layer as the left shift \(T=\sigma\) of this SFT and \(\mathcal{P}\) as the single-step partition, the Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy satisfies
\[
h_\mu(\sigma,\mathcal{P})=\log\varphi.
\]
By Definition~\ref{def:entropy-rate-time} and the Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem, for almost every sample string \(a_0^{t-1}\),
\[
\tau_{\mathrm{info}}(t)=t\log\varphi+o(t),
\qquad
\mu\bigl(C(a_0^{t-1})\bigr)=\exp\!\bigl(-(\log\varphi+o(1))t\bigr)=\varphi^{-t+o(t)}.
\]
Therefore, the protocol scale of the ``time unit'' is locked by \(\log\varphi\): the natural unit for normalizing \(\tau_{\mathrm{info}}\) to the step-count scale is \((\log\varphi)^{-1}\), and this scale does not depend on whether the object layer adopts a Kronecker rotation as the coordinatization of the scan phase.\par
As a spectral anchor point independent of the above entropy rate scale, the correlation time of the real-input \(40\)-state kernel at \(t=0\) satisfies the closed form
\(\tau_{\mathrm{corr}}(0)=1/\log\varphi\) (Remark~\ref{cor:chi-slice-t0-golden-tau}), so within the auditable pipeline of this paper, \(\log\varphi\) appears simultaneously in two ways---as an entropy rate scale and as a spectral gap scale---and these can be cross-checked against each other.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Object-layer Kronecker phase and the irreducibility of protocol-layer time]\label{rem:isometric-factor-vs-time-metric}
If the object layer possesses a maximal isometric factor, then the ``scan phase'' can be understood as a Kronecker translation on a compact abelian group; in the one-dimensional circle case, its canonical realization is an irrational rotation (the classical Sturmian/rotation picture; see \cite{MorseHedlund1940}).
In Section~\ref{sec:principles}, this paper selects the golden branch \(\alpha=\varphi^{-1}\) as the anti-resonance baseline via the Hurwitz/Markov worst-case approximation caliber; its role is to provide an auditable minimax representative for the \emph{object-layer phase}.\par
However, the irreducible expression of protocol-layer ``time'' is the logarithmic scale derived from cylinder set masses (Definition~\ref{def:entropy-rate-time}) or spectral gap decay (Remark~\ref{prop:chi-slice-time-correlation}): under the golden grammar choice, this scale is locked at \(\log\varphi\) and can be cross-checked across different audit channels.
Therefore, ``golden section rotation'' should be understood within the caliber of this paper as a replaceable representation of the object layer, not as a necessary prerequisite for protocol-layer time metrization.
\end{remark}
