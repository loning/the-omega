\section{Introduction: the continuous--discrete tension and the role of a ``constitution''}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern foundational physics confronts a structurally persistent mismatch:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Dynamics layer}: quantum theory is organized around unitary evolution---continuous, reversible, and information-preserving.
  \item \textbf{Readout layer}: observation presents discrete outcomes---counts, bits, integer labels, and finite-precision statistics.
\end{itemize}

Standard treatments delegate continuity to an ontic object (Hilbert space, fields) and discreteness to measurement postulates (projection, POVMs) plus external probabilistic interpretation. Here we invert the emphasis: \emph{readout is treated as part of physical structure}, and we demand a closed, minimal dependency chain in which \emph{time/probability/discreteness} are not external semantics but endogenous objects of a scan--projection protocol.

This manuscript is written as a \emph{mathematical constitution}. The goal is not to assert an experimentally complete physical law, but to provide an auditable chain of definitions and standard theorems that connects:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{modular action}
&\Rightarrow \text{cusp $q$-expansion}
\Rightarrow \text{discrete coefficients}
\\
&\Rightarrow \text{Hecke/prime skeleton}
\Rightarrow \text{canonical coding}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Relation to HPA and $\Omega$.
}
We follow the HPA--$\Omega$ framework in which the universe is specified by a static global state together with intrinsic automorphisms and holographic/finite-information constraints \cite{Ma2025FoP}. We also follow the HPA tool-chain in which unitary scanning, window projection readout, and Ostrowski/Zeckendorf coding form the minimal continuous--discrete bridge \cite{Ma2025HPA}. The present paper adds a constitution-level upgrade: it places that scan--projection structure on an explicit arithmetic-geometric mother space (the modular curve) and uses Hecke operators to supply a symmetry-preserving cross-scale skeleton generated by primes.

\paragraph{What is new here.}
Beyond fixing the layering discipline (Section~\ref{sec:layers_axioms}), the main new content is a concrete embedding:
\begin{itemize}
  \item modular geometry provides a canonical ``cusp interface'' where analytic data becomes discrete $q$-series data;
  \item Hecke algebras provide a prime-generated family of commuting symmetry-preserving operators, offering a clean cross-scale structure;
  \item continued fractions/Ostrowski numeration provide a canonical map from integer time to locally checkable digit strings, with the golden branch degenerating to Zeckendorf/Fibonacci coding.
\end{itemize}

Finally, we include lightweight numerical tests (Appendix~\ref{app:repro_code}) designed to be reproducible in plain Python and to verify internal closure of the proposed chain inside standard mathematics.
