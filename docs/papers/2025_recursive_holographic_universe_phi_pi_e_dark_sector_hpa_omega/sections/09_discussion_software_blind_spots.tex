
\section{Discussion: four software-layer blind spots and theory boundaries}
\label{sec:discussion}

The main body focused on hardware-level readout, folding, and overhead.
Any readout cosmology also faces ``software'' questions: choices and constraints that sit above the minimal finite-resolution structure.
We record four boundary issues that delimit what has been established versus what remains open.

\subsection{Scan orientation and handedness}
\label{subsec:handedness}

Hilbert recursion requires selecting an orientation family.
A discrete orientation choice may provide a geometric origin for parity violation or chiral asymmetry, but doing so requires a dynamical mechanism that breaks layout symmetries while preserving the stability channels.
This is currently an open problem.

\subsection{Zero versus null: the semantics of ``empty''}
\label{subsec:zero_null}

In $\Omega_m$, the all-zero word is a written state, not an unscanned void.
If the readout expends resources to maintain the ``zero'' baseline, vacuum energy may be interpreted as an idle power cost.
Formally, this suggests encoding vacuum contributions in the $\e$-channel pressure language (weighted transfer operators) rather than treating $\Lambda$ as a primitive constant.

\subsection{Observer and readout}
\label{subsec:observer_readout}

If readout operators are part of physics, then the semantics of ``who reads'' cannot be ignored.
The present paper remains operational: the system implements its own readout, and the theory is assessed only by falsifiable predictions on readout statistics.
Questions about consciousness and agency are not used as explanatory primitives here.

\subsection{Halting, teleology, and the boundary of falsifiability}
\label{subsec:teleology}

A recursive universe invites teleological interpretations (what is being computed?).
We separate this from the dynamical closure problem: even if computational teleology is considered, it must be constrained by data protocols rather than invoked to replace equations.
The stable-period data protocol provides one candidate route for making teleological claims testable \cite{Ma2025Tele}.
