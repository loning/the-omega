\section{Discussion and Conclusion}

\subsection{Definite Content and Replaceable Assumptions}

The definite conclusions of this paper concentrate on:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Unified definitions of cut--projection model sets and kernelized readout (Sections 2--3);
  \item Algebraic structure of coherent superposition terms (T3.3);
  \item Sturmian complexity invariants and Zeckendorf legal language counting (T5.2, Proposition \ref{prop:zeckendorf-count});
  \item Normalization properties of $\Fold_m$, fibers, and closure fixed-point structure (Theorem \ref{thm:closure-fixed-points});
  \item SMB mechanism providing rigorous template for information-theoretic temporal arrow (T6.3);
  \item Degeneracy spectrum frequency law and error bounds for pushforward distribution (T8.2).
\end{itemize}

Connection to physical semantics depends on assumptions in layer (H): irrational orientation and ergodicity, toroidal factor approximation, and the mapping between readout kernel and experimental apparatus. The above assumptions possess replaceability and verifiability, belonging to modeling tasks rather than theorem conclusions.

\subsection{Interfaces with Existing Theory and Open Questions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item To rigorously align with unitary dynamics of quantum field theory, we must provide evolution rules for $\sigma$ and phase structure for amplitude mapping $a$, and prove that under appropriate limits we recover standard interference statistics and conservation laws.
  \item To directly interface with quasicrystal diffraction theory, we must establish rigorous correspondence between statistics of $I_{\varepsilon,u}$ and pure-point diffraction spectra and their window Fourier weights, and provide controllable corrections to spectral lines from finite kernel width.
  \item To express ``speed-of-light bound'' as readout propagation bounds, we must introduce explicit local interaction structure and propagation cone constraints, and discriminate among group velocity, signal velocity, and causally reachable domains.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Conclusion}

This paper provides a six-dimensional golden-orientation cut--projection readout framework unifying three-dimensional visible structure, coherent intensity reconstruction, symbolic stabilization, and information-theoretic temporal arrow within a single protocol system. The framework's core advantages lie in: all key propositions correspond to definite mathematical objects (projections, windows, kernels, scannings, normalization operators, fibers, entropy rates), and are amenable to falsifiable interfaces through statistical verification templates. Its further development depends on: systematically coupling object-layer configurations with dynamical closure, and with error-budget interfaces to precision experiments.
