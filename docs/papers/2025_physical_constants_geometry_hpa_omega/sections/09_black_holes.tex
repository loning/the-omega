\section{Black holes and boundaries: the area law as an extreme form of readout channel counting}
\label{sec:black_holes}

Black-hole thermodynamics provides an established link between boundary geometry and entropy: the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy is proportional to horizon area \cite{Bekenstein1973,BardeenCarterHawking1973,Hawking1975,Wald1993Noether},
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathrm{BH}}=\frac{k_B c^3}{4G\hbar}A=\frac{k_B A}{4\ell_P^2}.
\end{equation}

In the HPA language, readout is an orthogonal cut: it partitions a conserved whole into distinguishable channels and counts the residual mismatch as impedance. Accordingly, for horizons saturating the covariant entropy bound, the area law is the saturation of boundary readout channel count, and deviations are attributed to
\begin{enumerate}
    \item lattice effects of discrete scan (e.g., non-Euclidean counting induced by canonical coding);
    \item additional channel impedances from internal fiber defects;
    \item finite-resolution coarse-graining in the readout window.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Channel counting and entropy bounds}
We import the covariant entropy bound and the semiclassical area law as external inputs, and we isolate the minimal internal identification needed to interface scan--readout channel counting with gravitational entropy bounds.

\begin{axiom}[Boundary readout entropy as channel count]\label{ax:boundary_channel_entropy}
For a boundary screen of area $A$ at resolution $r$, let $\mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A,r)$ denote the maximal number of mutually distinguishable readout outcomes allowed by the protocol. Define the associated boundary readout entropy by
\begin{equation}
S(A,r):=k_B\log \mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A,r).
\end{equation}
This is the canonical channel-count entropy of a finite outcome set \cite{Shannon1948,CoverThomas2006}.
\end{axiom}

\begin{proposition}[Covariant entropy bound as a channel-capacity bound]\label{prop:bousso_channel_bound}
The covariant entropy bound implies, at leading order,
\begin{equation}
\log \mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A,r)\le \frac{A}{4\ell_P^2},
\end{equation}
up to subleading corrections.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The covariant entropy bound gives $S(A,r)\le k_BA/(4\ell_P^2)$ \cite{Bousso2002}. Substitute Axiom~\ref{ax:boundary_channel_entropy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Area law from boundary channel counting]\label{thm:area_law_channel_count}
If a horizon saturates the covariant entropy bound (as in the Bekenstein--Hawking entropy law), then
\begin{equation}
S(A)=\frac{k_BA}{4\ell_P^2}
\end{equation}
at leading order.
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A)=\exp\!\left(\frac{A}{4\ell_P^2}\right)
\end{equation}
at the level of maximal channel count.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Saturation turns Proposition~\ref{prop:bousso_channel_bound} into
\[
\log \mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A)=\frac{A}{4\ell_P^2}.
\]
Substitute Axiom~\ref{ax:boundary_channel_entropy}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Bekenstein bound route to the coefficient $1/4$]
The Bekenstein bound states $S\le 2\pi k_B ER/(\hbar c)$ for a system of energy $E$ contained in radius $R$ \cite{Bekenstein1981}. For a Schwarzschild black hole, take $E=Mc^2$, $R=R_s=2GM/c^2$, and $A=4\pi R_s^2$ \cite{Wald1984}. Then
\begin{equation}
S\le 2\pi k_B\frac{(Mc^2)(2GM/c^2)}{\hbar c}=\frac{4\pi k_B GM^2}{\hbar c}
=k_B\frac{A}{4\ell_P^2},
\end{equation}
where $\ell_P^2=G\hbar/c^3$. Thus the same coefficient $1/4$ follows from the bound plus Schwarzschild geometry, with equality for black holes.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Subleading corrections as a data-facing target}
Quantum and statistical-mechanical analyses of black-hole entropy generically predict subleading corrections to the area law, often of logarithmic form, with coefficients that depend on the field content and the ensemble \cite{Fursaev1995,KaulMajumdar2000,Sen2013LogBH}.
\begin{equation}
S(A)=k_B\left(\frac{A}{4\ell_P^2}+\beta\,\log\!\frac{A}{\ell_P^2}+O(1)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\beta$ is theory/ensemble dependent \cite{Fursaev1995,KaulMajumdar2000,Sen2013LogBH}.

Within the HPA--$\Omega$ program, these corrections are finite-resolution and discrete-coding effects in the boundary channel count $\mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A,r)$. Equivalently, they are controlled subleading terms in $\log \mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A,r)$ beyond the leading term $A/(4\ell_P^2)$.

Matching the sign and scaling of the leading correction provides a falsifiability interface for any explicit construction of $\mathcal{N}_{\partial}(A,r)$ from scan--readout primitives.
