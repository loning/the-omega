
\section{HPA readout dynamics: from unitary scan to Zeckendorf windows}
\label{sec:hpa_readout}

\subsection{Unitary scan and a Weyl pair: time as iteration count}
\label{subsec:weyl_scan}

In HPA, microscopic dynamics is modeled as a unitary scan with a Weyl-pair structure \cite{Weyl1931}.
Abstractly, one may encode noncommutativity by operators $(U,V)$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl_pair}
UV=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}\,VU,
\end{equation}
with an irrational slope $\alpha$.
At the level of a circle rotation model, let $x_0\in\RR/\ZZ$ and define the orbit
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kronecker_orbit}
x_n:=x_0+n\alpha \pmod{1},\qquad
z_n:=\e^{2\pi\iu x_n}\in\TT.
\end{equation}
Time is not imposed as an external parameter; it is realized as the iteration count $n\in\ZZ$ along the scan.

\begin{remark}[Scan rate, units, and the speed-of-light dictionary]\label{rem:scan_rate_c_dictionary}
The scan iteration count $n$ is dimensionless.
In this paper, we treat any conversion between ``one step per iteration'' and a physical time unit, and therefore any identification of an effective maximal signal speed with the measured constant $c$, as part of a matching-layer and units dictionary rather than as a theorem-level output of the finite folding core.
Once a locality basis is fixed (e.g.\ via Hilbert addressing), a constant step advance induces a protocol-invariant notion of maximal propagation rate on the associated locality graph; the physical value of $c$ enters only after calibration.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Delay observables as an operational time dictionary]\label{rem:delay_time_dictionary}
When the protocol is matched to an experimental platform, ``time per update'' can be accessed through standard delay observables.
In scattering settings where a unitary $S$-matrix $S(\omega)$ is measured as a function of frequency, the Wigner--Smith time delay provides an operational proxy for additional overhead via $\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)=\Tr\!\bigl(-\iu S^\dagger \dd S/\dd\omega\bigr)$ \cite{Wigner1955,Smith1960}.
We record this dictionary (and its relation to relativistic lapse/redshift templates) as an interface section (Section~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Window projection and symbolic words: finite observability}
\label{subsec:window_projection}

Let $W\subset\TT$ be a measurable ``readout window''.
Define the binary readout word by thresholding the scan orbit:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:window_word}
w_n:=\ind\{z_n\in W\}\in\{0,1\}.
\end{equation}
The resulting sequence $w=(w_n)_{n\in\ZZ}$ is a symbolic coding of the orbit (mechanical/Sturmian in classical settings; see, e.g., \cite{Lothaire2002,LindMarcus1995}).
Finite observers access only finite windows, hence finite words $w_1\cdots w_m\in\Omega_m$.
In operational quantum language, such coarse readout corresponds to a POVM-like description, where the ``particle spectrum'' is readout-induced discreteness rather than an \emph{a priori} field continuum; see, e.g., \cite{NielsenChuang2010} for standard measurement and POVM formalism.
In particular, what is experimentally accessible is not the underlying continuous orbit, but the empirical statistics of finite window words.
The finite readout alphabet $\Omega_m$ and its stability-filtered subsets therefore serve as the primary objects for any auditable finite-resolution model.

\begin{remark}[Irrational rotations and Sturmian minimal complexity]\label{rem:sturmian_minimal_complexity}
For an irrational circle rotation and an interval window $W$, the resulting binary coding is Sturmian (a mechanical word): it is aperiodic and has minimal factor complexity among aperiodic binary sequences, namely $p(\ell)=\ell+1$ distinct length-$\ell$ subwords for each $\ell\ge 1$ \cite{Lothaire2002,AlloucheShallit2003,LindMarcus1995}.
This gives a standard mathematical benchmark for ``minimal'' nontrivial symbolic structure at finite resolution.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The golden branch and Zeckendorf/Ostrowski coding}
\label{subsec:zeckendorf_coding}

At the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$, the symbolic language is constrained by Fibonacci/Sturmian structure and connects naturally to Ostrowski and Zeckendorf numeration \cite{Zeckendorf1972,AlloucheShallit2003,Lothaire2002}.
In particular, every $N\in\NN$ admits a unique Zeckendorf expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:zeckendorf}
N=\sum_{k\ge 1} c_k F_{k+1},
\qquad c_k\in\{0,1\},\qquad c_kc_{k+1}=0,
\end{equation}
which is a forbidden-word grammar (no adjacent ones) on the digit string $(c_k)$.
This grammar is the mathematical origin of the $\varphi$-channel in the folding model: it selects a stable type set $X_m\subset\Omega_m$ by excluding the forbidden substring ``11'' at finite window length (Section~\ref{sec:folding_core}).

\paragraph{A coding bridge (symbol $\leftrightarrow$ arithmetic).}
At the golden branch, Fibonacci weights provide a canonical arithmetic bridge between symbolic digits and integers.
Given a binary digit sequence $(c_k)$ satisfying the Zeckendorf grammar, the value map is
\[
V(c):=\sum_{k\ge 1} c_k F_{k+1}.
\]
Conversely, given a binary readout word $(w_k)$ produced by a window protocol, one may form a Fibonacci-weighted observable
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fib_weighted_readout}
Z:=\sum_{k\ge 1} w_k F_k,
\end{equation}
which makes explicit the ``arithmetic--symbol--geometry'' loop emphasized in the HPA program: window projection induces symbolic words; the golden branch constrains them by a forbidden grammar; and Fibonacci weights convert the resulting digits into arithmetic invariants.

\begin{remark}[Why the golden branch is singled out among irrational slopes]\label{rem:golden_branch_principled}
For a general irrational slope $\alpha$, interval codings of the rotation \eqref{eq:kronecker_orbit} are Sturmian and admit an Ostrowski numeration determined by the continued-fraction expansion of $\alpha$ \cite{Lothaire2002,AlloucheShallit2003}.
The choice $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}=[0;1,1,1,\dots]$ is distinguished by its extremal Diophantine property: among irrationals it is ``most poorly approximable'' by rationals (all continued-fraction digits are minimal), yielding a canonical and maximally rigid substitution structure (the Fibonacci word) and therefore a canonical digit grammar that closes to Zeckendorf/Fibonacci weights \cite{HardyWright2008,Lothaire2002}.
This is the mathematical sense in which the golden branch is the minimal nontrivial symbolic/arithmetic bridge: it is the unique slope whose Ostrowski system reduces to the Fibonacci/Zeckendorf system used throughout the folding layer.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Tick-only reading: the base \texorpdfstring{$\varphi$}{phi} as the intrinsic clock-ratio.}
In a tick-first ontology, the scan index $n$ is the only primitive time variable.
Choosing a slope $\alpha$ therefore fixes, at finite depth, how the tick stream distributes symbols under window projection and how mismatch accumulates along time.
Proposition~\ref{prop:golden_least_discrepancy} makes this selection auditable: at every finite depth, the golden branch is the unique minimizer of a natural continued-fraction complexity proxy and therefore the unique choice that closes the symbolic/arithmetic bridge to a canonical digit grammar.
This is why the Fibonacci growth rate $\log\varphi$ becomes the intrinsic normalization constant for scale and time dictionaries downstream: it is the entropy rate of the admissible language induced by the tick stream on the golden branch (cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem:phi_base_entropy}).
Accordingly, the resolution coordinate $r(\mu)=\log(\mu/m_e)/\log\varphi$ used later is simultaneously a log-frequency and (up to sign) a log-time coordinate (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}), providing a unified tick-derived scale language.

\begin{proposition}[Finite-depth least-discrepancy rigidity of the golden branch]\label{prop:golden_least_discrepancy}
Let $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\dots]\in(0,1)\setminus\QQ$ and fix a depth $m\ge 0$.
Define the finite-depth continued-fraction proxy
\[
C_m(\alpha):=\sum_{k=0}^{m} a_{k+1}.
\]
Then $C_m(\alpha)\ge m+1$, with equality if and only if $a_1=\cdots=a_{m+1}=1$.
In particular, $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}=[0;1,1,1,\dots]$ uniquely minimizes $C_m(\alpha)$ at every depth.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Since $a_i\in\NN$ for all $i$, one has $a_i\ge 1$, hence $C_m(\alpha)\ge m+1$.
Equality holds if and only if each term satisfies $a_{k+1}=1$ for $k=0,\dots,m$, i.e.\ $a_1=\cdots=a_{m+1}=1$.
\end{proof}
The CAP reading is that, among irrational scan slopes, the golden branch is rigidly selected as the unique minimizer of a natural finite-depth complexity/discrepancy proxy, and it is the unique choice that closes the Ostrowski numeration to Zeckendorf/Fibonacci ticks used throughout the folding layer.

\subsubsection{Accumulated mismatch and discrepancy certificates (interface)}
\label{subsubsec:discrepancy_certificates}

Beyond finite-depth proxies, mismatch accumulation along scan time admits a standard certificate formulation in terms of discrepancy.
For the orbit $x_n=x_0+n\alpha\pmod 1$ (Section~\ref{subsec:weyl_scan}), define the star discrepancy of the length-$N$ prefix by \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:star_discrepancy_def}
D_N^\ast
:=
\sup_{a\in[0,1]}
\left|
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\ind_{[0,a)}(x_n) - a
\right|,
\end{equation}
and the accumulated mismatch by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:accumulated_mismatch_def}
E_N := N\,D_N^\ast.
\end{equation}
In this language, sustainable readout corresponds to keeping $E_N$ controlled over long horizons, while phase locking corresponds to linear growth in $E_N$ for rational (or effectively rational) slopes.
For bounded-type irrational slopes (notably the golden branch), $E_N$ admits explicit logarithmic upper bounds tied to continued-fraction data \cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}; in the HPA--$\Omega$ program this provides a quantitative mismatch dynamic that complements the symbolic/grammar viewpoint and supports least-discrepancy selection principles \cite{Ma2025HPT,Ma2025CAP}.

\begin{remark}[What changes if $\alpha$ is varied]\label{rem:alpha_variation}
If one replaces $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ by another irrational, the theorem-level Sturmian minimal-complexity property remains (Remark~\ref{rem:sturmian_minimal_complexity}), but the canonical digit system changes: the natural numeration becomes the Ostrowski system associated to the continued fraction of $\alpha$ rather than Zeckendorf/Fibonacci \cite{AlloucheShallit2003,Lothaire2002}.
Accordingly, the specific Fibonacci counts and $\varphi$-based depth coordinates used in later sections are tied to the golden branch choice; a generalized program would replace Fibonacci weights by the corresponding Ostrowski weights and would induce a different admissibility grammar.
In the present paper we therefore fix $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ as the CAP-minimal choice within this audited class (Proposition~\ref{prop:golden_least_discrepancy}), and we audit robustness primarily under window-length uplift $m\mapsto m'$ and balanced refinement $m=2n$ (Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables}), rather than under arbitrary irrational-slope substitution.
\end{remark}
