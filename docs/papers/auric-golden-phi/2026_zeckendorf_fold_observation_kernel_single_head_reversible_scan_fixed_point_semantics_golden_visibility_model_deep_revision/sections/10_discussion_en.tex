\section{Discussion: Boundary Artifacts, Degeneracy Spectrum, and Connection to Interpretive Layer}

\subsection{Combinatorial Origin of Boundary Artifacts}

The splitting $21=18\oplus 3$ is an inevitable consequence of the inconsistency between linear window semantics and periodic boundary semantics. This conclusion depends on no dynamical choice, only on the forbidden-word constraint and the semantic difference of ``head-tail adjacency.''

\subsection{Structural Significance of Degeneracy Spectrum}

Theorem \ref{thm:deg-spectrum-6} shows that in the minimal kernel $m=6$, the fiber degeneracy is determined jointly by tail hidden digit feasibility and bound constraints, not empirical phenomena. This mechanism provides a discrete prototype: visible structure is determined by canonical form, while invisible structure is determined by the number of feasible combinations of tail degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Connection to Interpretive Layer (not entering theorem layer)}

In the interpretive framework of high-dimensional periodic—low-dimensional projection, visible and internal coordinates separate; the fibers $\mathcal{F}_m(x)$ in this paper can serve as a discrete prototype of internal labels, and closure fixed points can be viewed as self-consistency conditions for the reality set under ``observation consistency'' requirements.

\subsection{Resolution-Invariant Correct Expression (interpretive layer terminology discipline)}

In this paper, parameter $m$ is the Zeckendorf window length; changing $m$ alters the scale and fiber degeneracy statistics of the finite output space $X_m$, so cross-scale relationships should not be expressed as ``different $m$ yields the same primitive output.'' The strict calibration of cross-scale ``identity'' is coherence: high-resolution output, via natural restriction projection, recovers low-resolution output (see Lemma \ref{lem:foldm-pointwise-compat}), so low-resolution structure is retained as a quotient of high-resolution structure; larger $m$ merely provides longer prefixes and finer conditional structure.
