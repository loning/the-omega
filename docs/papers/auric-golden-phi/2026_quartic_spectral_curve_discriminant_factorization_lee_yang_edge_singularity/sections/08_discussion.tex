\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

We presented a four-layer, auditable analysis of a quartic spectral family:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Finite-order spectral closure.} A reduced rational generating function yields a minimal order-\(4\) linear recurrence and fixed Hankel rank \(4\) outside a finite degeneracy set.
  \item \textbf{Branch locus and equimodularity.} The complete factorization of \(\Disc_\lambda\Pi(\lambda,y)\) determines the algebraic branch locus \(\Branch\), isolates the unique real branch point \(\yLY<0\), and admits a separate non-bifurcating negative-axis equimodular point \(\yEQ\in(-1,0)\).
  \item \textbf{Regular closure geometry.} The elliptic normalization upgrades to a degree-\(24\) regular \(S_4\)-cover \(X\to\mathbb{P}^1_y\) of genus \(16\), with normal quotients \(X_A\) and \(X_V\), an \'etale cyclic cubic tower \(X_V\to X_A\), an associated Prym surface, and a concrete \(S_4\)-module decomposition of \(H^0(X,\Omega_X^1)\).
  \item \textbf{Dominant-sheet fluctuations.} The dominant spectral branch near \(y=1\) controls the limiting log-moment generating function, and implicit differentiation gives the closed-form constants \(\mu=5/18\) and \(\sigma^2=67/972\) for LLN/CLT via Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff-nonneg}.
\end{enumerate}

The new structural conclusion is that the discriminant geometry is only the visible shadow of a larger regular closure.
Indeed, Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic} shows that the conductor-\(37\) elliptic normalization \(E:\xi^2+\xi=\lambda^3-\lambda\) carries a degree-\(4\) map \(\varphi:E\to\mathbb{P}^1_y\) whose regular closure has full monodromy \(S_4\) and genus \(16\).
Its quadratic quotient is the discriminant ridge curve \(X_A:w^2=-y(y-1)c(y)\), while the Klein-four quotient is the resolvent curve \(X_V\); the map \(X_V\to X_A\) is an \'etale cyclic cubic cover, so the branch cubic is accompanied by a canonical Prym surface and by the decomposition
\[
H^0(X,\Omega_X^1)\cong \mathrm{sgn}^{\oplus 2}\oplus V_2\oplus V_3\oplus (V_3\otimes\mathrm{sgn})^{\oplus 3}.
\]
More sharply, the \(V_3\)-isotypic abelian factor of \(\Jac(X)\) is isogenous to \(E^3\), so the original elliptic curve is not merely the normalization of the quartic sheet but a repeated building block inside the Jacobian of the regular closure.
This gives a geometric explanation for why the same arithmetic data reappear simultaneously in the discriminant, the resolvent cubic, and the quadratic resolvent field.

From a methodology perspective, the present workflow is the one-dimensional, branch-dominance regime: a single dominant root controls \(\log Z_m\) on a punctured neighborhood of a distinguished parameter regime, and all fluctuation statements are obtained by quasi-powers differentiation.
This is complementary to the multivariate ACSV framework for multiple competing critical points and full higher-dimensional saddles (e.g.\ \cite{PemantleWilson2013}); in particular, near branch collisions where several sheets compete at equal modulus, ACSV provides a systematic route to local asymptotics beyond the single-sheet hypothesis used in this note.

Several extensions suggest themselves:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item for broader reduced rational families, one may ask which Hurwitz passports force a similar chain from branch cubic to regular closure, Prym data, and Hodge decomposition;
  \item under fixed Hankel rank constraints, the geometry of non-bifurcating equimodular loci deserves a systematic algebraic treatment;
  \item at the probabilistic level, the local Legendre rate function constructed here should be compared with a genuine large-deviation theory once global control of the dominant sheet is available;
  \item on the arithmetic side, the conjectural ray class description of the splitting field of \(c(y)\) (Remark~\ref{rem:ray-class-field}) and the reduction theory of the discriminant ridge curve \(C_\Delta\) remain natural next problems.
\end{enumerate}
