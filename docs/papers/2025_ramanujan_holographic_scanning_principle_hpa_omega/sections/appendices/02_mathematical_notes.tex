\section{Mathematical notes (proof sketches)}
\label{app:math_notes}

\subsection{B.1 Hecke prime-power recursion and prime generation}
\label{app:hecke_recursion}

Starting from the multiplication relation \eqref{eq:hecke_multiplication}, take $m=p^r$ and $n=p$ for a prime $p$. Using the divisors $d\mid (p^r,p)$, i.e.\ $d\in\{1,p\}$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
T_{p^r}T_p = T_{p^{r+1}} + p^{k-1}T_{p^{r-1}},
\end{equation}
which rearranges to the prime-power recursion \eqref{eq:hecke_prime_power}:
\begin{equation}
T_{p^{r+1}} = T_p T_{p^r} - p^{k-1}T_{p^{r-1}}.
\end{equation}
This shows all $T_{p^r}$ are generated by $T_p$, and together with \eqref{eq:hecke_multiplication} it implies that the full Hecke algebra is generated by $\{T_p\}_{p\ \mathrm{prime}}$.

\subsection{B.2 Weyl pairs and intrinsic incompatibility}
\label{app:weyl_incompatibility}

Suppose $UV=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}VU$ with $\alpha\notin\QQ$. If $\psi$ were a common eigenvector, $U\psi=\lambda\psi$ and $V\psi=\mu\psi$, then
\begin{equation}
UV\psi = \lambda\mu \psi,\qquad VU\psi=\mu\lambda\psi,
\end{equation}
but the Weyl relation forces $\lambda\mu = \e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}\mu\lambda$, hence $(1-\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha})\lambda\mu=0$. Since $\alpha\notin\QQ$, $\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}\ne 1$, so $\lambda\mu=0$, impossible for unitary eigenvalues. Thus no nonzero vector can be a simultaneous eigenvector of $U$ and $V$.

\subsection{B.3 Why local admissibility constraints matter}
\label{app:local_constraints}

Ostrowski/Zeckendorf representations impose local admissibility rules (e.g.\ Zeckendorf's ``no adjacent $1$''). These rules turn a global integer representation into a locally checkable grammar. In finite-resolution and quasi-local settings, this locality is essential: a readout protocol that cannot be implemented by local checks is not physically actionable for a finite observer sector.
