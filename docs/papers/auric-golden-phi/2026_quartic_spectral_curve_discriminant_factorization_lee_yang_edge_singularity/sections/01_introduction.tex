\section{Introduction}

The zero geometry of fixed-order spectral recurrences lies at the intersection of algebraic combinatorics, complex asymptotics, and arithmetic geometry.
In the present quartic model, the entire structure can be organized by a finite-dimensional spectral closure:
\emph{minimal recurrence and Hankel rank} \(\to\) \emph{branch locus and equimodularity} \(\to\) \emph{regular \(S_4\) closure} \(\to\) \emph{limit laws and certified computation}.

\subsection*{Main theorems}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item
  The ordinary generating function \(Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge0}Z_m(y)t^m\) has minimal order-\(4\) spectral data for \(y\notin\{-1,0,1\}\): the denominator degree is \(4\), giving minimal recurrence order and fixed Hankel rank (\(4\)) (Definition~\ref{def:minimal-spectral-closure}, Theorem~\ref{thm:minimal-order}).
  \item
  The quartic spectral polynomial \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) has complete discriminant factorization
  \[
  \Disc_\lambda \Pi(\lambda,y)=-y(y-1)c(y),\qquad c(y)=256y^3+411y^2+165y+32,
  \]
  hence the branch locus is exactly \(\{0,1\}\cup\{c(y)=0\}\); there is a unique nontrivial real branch point \(\yLY\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:unique-real-root}) (Theorem~\ref{thm:disc-factor}).
  \item
  There is a unique non-bifurcating negative-axis equimodular point \(\yEQ\) (Definition~\ref{def:equimodular-negative}, Theorem~\ref{thm:yeq}), and
  \(\yEQ\in(-160092/10^6,\,-160091/10^6)\subset(-1,0)\).
  \item
  The elliptic normalization extends to a regular \(S_4\)-closure \(X\to\mathbb{P}^1_y\) of genus \(16\), whose normal quotients are the discriminant ridge curve \(X_A:w^2=-y(y-1)c(y)\) of genus \(2\) and a genus-\(4\) resolvent curve \(X_V\); the map \(X_V\to X_A\) is an \'etale cyclic cubic cover, giving a Prym surface with \(\QQ(\zeta_3)\)-multiplication, a representation-theoretic decomposition of \(H^0(X,\Omega_X^1)\), and an elliptic identification \(A_{V_3}\sim E^3\) for the three-dimensional standard isotypic factor (Proposition~\ref{prop:resolvent-cubic}, Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-closure}, Proposition~\ref{prop:normal-quotients}, Corollary~\ref{cor:resolvent-prym}, Theorem~\ref{thm:regular-closure-hodge}, Corollary~\ref{cor:v3-elliptic-factor}).
  \item
  The dominant spectral branch \(\lambda_+(y)\) controls the limiting log-mgf, and from it one gets
  \[
  \mu=\frac{5}{18},\qquad \sigma^2=\frac{67}{972},
  \]
  together with the LLN/CLT and the local Legendre rate function determined by the dominant sheet (Theorem~\ref{thm:lambda-plus}, Theorem~\ref{thm:lambda-mgf}, Theorem~\ref{thm:lln-clt}, Proposition~\ref{prop:local-rate-function}); this is underpinned by nonnegativity (Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff-nonneg}).
\end{enumerate}

Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic} is not merely auxiliary: it contains the regular-closure geometry that explains why the branch cubic, the discriminant ridge, and the resolvent tower are forced by the same \(S_4\)-monodromy data.

The paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:model} defines the model and the quartic spectral polynomial.
Sections~\ref{sec:hankel}--\ref{sec:free} develop the minimality, discriminant, and limit-law chain in the order above.
Section~\ref{sec:zeros} states the BKW consequences; Section~\ref{sec:algo} gives a reproducible and fully certifiable numerical workflow.
Appendix~\ref{app:elliptic} develops the elliptic normalization, the regular \(S_4\)-closure, the resolvent quotients, and the Prym/Hodge consequences.

Methodologically, this note stays in the one-dimensional dominant-branch regime:
limit laws are derived from quasi-powers and branch-geometry of \(\Pi\).
For genuinely multivariate critical-point settings, modern ACSV saddle-point machinery
offers a systematic framework for competing dominant points and multivariate large deviations
(e.g.\ \cite{PemantleWilson2013}).
Those methods are complementary to our finite-dimensional spectral-closure template.

We also present this as a precise algebraic template:
the model is introduced from a reduced transfer recurrence, while a concrete statistical-mechanical
interpretation of \(Z_m(y)\) is left open for future work.
