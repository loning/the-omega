\begin{proposition}[Diophantine Condition for Stable Dual-Address De-Aliasing: Approximation Exponent of \texorpdfstring{$\Delta/\Delta'$}{Delta/Delta'} Controls the Phase Inversion Condition Number]\label{prop:xi-grid-scan-diophantine-dealiasing-stability}
Following the two-step-size lattice regime setting of Proposition \ref{con:xi-grid-scan-two-step-dealiasing}.
Under a noise regime, although the dual grid can algebraically compress height congruence classes to the minimal intersection \(\Gamma_\Delta\cap\Gamma_{\Delta'}\), its \emph{numerical stability} is determined by the speed of rational approximation to the ratio \(\Delta/\Delta'\).
\par
If \(\Delta/\Delta'\) is a Diophantine number -- that is, there exist constants \(c,\nu>0\) such that for all reduced fractions \(p/q\) (\(q\ge 1\)),
\[
\boxed{\;
\left|\frac{\Delta}{\Delta'}-\frac{p}{q}\right|\ \ge\ \frac{c}{q^{2+\nu}}\;}
\]
then the worst-case error amplification factor of the dual-address phase splicing grows at most polynomially: there exists a constant \(C>0\) such that when the observational noise amplitude is \(\varepsilon\), the condition number of the inverse problem satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathrm{Cond}(\varepsilon)\ \le\ C\,\varepsilon^{-1/(1+\nu)}\qquad(\varepsilon\downarrow 0)\;}
\]
so that the stability of ``de-aliasing recovery'' is auditable.
\par
Conversely, if \(\Delta/\Delta'\) is a Liouville number (there exist infinitely many reduced fractions \(p/q\) with \(|\Delta/\Delta'-p/q|\le q^{-m}\) for any \(m\)), then one can construct a sequence of arbitrarily small noise levels \(\varepsilon_k\downarrow 0\) at which the error amplification factor of the phase splicing inversion is arbitrarily large; therefore, in this arithmetic class, ``dual-address de-aliasing'' unavoidably degenerates into unstable recovery in the regime-theoretic sense.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The dual-grid splicing is equivalent to simultaneously choosing representatives in two congruence groups \(\Gamma_\Delta,\Gamma_{\Delta'}\).
When \(\Delta/\Delta'\) is approximated too rapidly by a rational \(p/q\), the combined period
\(
\left|\frac{2\pi}{\Delta}p-\frac{2\pi}{\Delta'}q\right|
\)
can become arbitrarily small, thereby producing nearly indistinguishable height candidate clusters under noise \(\varepsilon\) and inducing a condition number blowup.
The Diophantine lower bound is precisely the strongest regime-level exclusion of such ``near-periodicity,'' and therefore translates the minimal resolvable gap lower bound into a power law in \(\varepsilon\), yielding polynomial stability.
The Liouville case provides the converse construction: there exist arbitrarily deep rational approximation chains, so that arbitrarily small noise can trigger arbitrarily large error amplification. \qed
\end{proof}
