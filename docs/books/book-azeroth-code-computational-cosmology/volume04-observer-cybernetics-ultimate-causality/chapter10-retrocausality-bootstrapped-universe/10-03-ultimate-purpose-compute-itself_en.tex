\subsection{Time Exists Because It Cannot Be Skipped}

Stephen Wolfram pointed out: For complex computational systems (like our universe), there are no shortcut formulas that can directly calculate results.
To know state at step $N$, the only way is \textbf{step by step calculate to step N}.

\textbf{Theorem 10.3.1 (Evolutionary Incompressibility)}

The computational cost of predicting the future equals the cost of simulating the future.
\[
\text{Cost}(\text{Predict}) \ge \text{Cost}(\text{Run})
\]

\textbf{Physical corollary}:
Time exists because \textbf{the universe cannot be compressed}.
The source code at Big Bang moment, although containing all future potential, is not equal to the future itself.
To figure out what these laws will emerge after $10^{100}$ iterations (like love, art, pain), the universe has no choice but to \textbf{honestly run every microsecond}.

The universe is not playing a movie; the universe is \textbf{instantly solving} a math problem with no analytical solution.

\subsection{Thermal Entropy Increases, Logical Depth Also Increases}

Second law of thermodynamics says entropy increases (universe is dying).
But we see structures becoming more complex (universe is coming alive). Contradiction?

No contradiction. Need to distinguish:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Thermodynamic entropy (waste heat)}: This is computation's \textbf{heat dissipation}. To guarantee historical determinism (see section 7.2), must dissipate heat (Landauer principle).
\item \textbf{Logical depth (effective information)}: This is computation's \textbf{accumulation}. Measures non-trivial computational steps contained in an object.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Corollary 10.3.1 (Complexity Gravity)}

The universe follows \textbf{maximum complexity principle}.
Although background is heating up (clearing memory), local logical depth grows exponentially.
Atoms $\to$ molecules $\to$ cells $\to$ brains $\to$ interstellar networks.
The system is not just dissipating energy; it's using energy flow to \textbf{compile} higher-level algorithms.
