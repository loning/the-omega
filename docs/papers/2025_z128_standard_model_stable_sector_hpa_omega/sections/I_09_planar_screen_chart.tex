\section*{Planar holographic screen as a chart (stereographic projection; interface)}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Planar holographic screen as a chart (stereographic projection; interface)}
\InterfaceTag When we speak of a ``screen'' in this paper, the concrete object is a finite display graph induced by an addressing basis (Section~\ref{sec:hilbert_addressing}).
The $2$D Hilbert grid is used for fully explicit finite diagnostics at the anchor, and it may also be viewed as a discrete sampling of a planar chart.

\InterfaceTag A standard continuous display chart that connects bearing/boundary language to planar coordinates is the stereographic projection of the unit sphere $S^2\subset\RR^3$ to the complex plane.
For a direction $\omega=(\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3)\in S^2$ (away from the projection pole), define
\[
z(\omega) := \frac{\omega_1+\iu\omega_2}{1-\omega_3}\in\CC.
\]
Conversely, for $z\in\CC$ define the inverse map
\[
\omega(z) := \left(\frac{2\RePart z}{1+|z|^2},\frac{2\ImPart z}{1+|z|^2},\frac{|z|^2-1}{1+|z|^2}\right)\in S^2.
\]
In this chart, planar distance and density are display-dependent quantities; the protocol distance used for operational statements in this paper remains the graph metric on the chosen display graph (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_distance}).

\AuditTag This stereographic chart is a display convention (interface dictionary) and is not used as a premise for theorem-level folding statements or for CAP minimality audits; it provides a compact coordinate language for ``planar screen'' discussions when a continuum chart is convenient.
