\subsection{Dual Boundary Normal Form of Riemann Infinity: Homotopy Compression of \texorpdfstring{$(s\to 1^{+})$}{(s->1+)} and \texorpdfstring{$(t\to\infty)$}{(t->infty)}}\label{subsec:riemann-infinity-dual-boundary}
Appendix~\ref{app:real-input-40-dirichlet} provides the complete equivalent closed-form interface of the Abel--Dirichlet caliber: \(z=\lambda^{-s}\), \(r=\lambda^{1-s}\in(0,1)\) (\(s>1\)), and the Abel-caliber logarithmic singularity \(\mathcal{P}(r)=-\log(1-r)+\log\mathfrak{M}+o(1)\) is strictly rewritten in the Dirichlet caliber as
\[
\mathcal{P}(s)=-\log(s-1)-\log\log\lambda+\log\mathfrak{M}+o(1)\qquad(s\to 1^{+}),
\]
so that the pole information at \(s=1\) is compressed into a finite-part constant at the disk boundary \(r\to 1^{-}\).
On the other hand, the phase compression \(\iota(x)=\frac{1+\mathrm{i}x}{1-\mathrm{i}x}\) compresses \(x\to\pm\infty\) to the unit circle endpoint \(w=-1\), and the Koenigs variable \(\mathfrak{w}(u)=\frac{1+u}{1-u}\) linearizes the endpoint neighborhood to \(\mathfrak{w}\to 0\), with depth \(Y_x:=-\log|\mathfrak{w}|=\log|x|+O(1)\) (equation~\eqref{eq:endpoint-busemann-potential}).
Accordingly, introduce the two-dimensional boundary depth coordinates
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dual-boundary-depth-coords}
\boxed{\ Y_r:=-\log(1-r),\qquad Y_t:=-\log|\mathfrak{w}|\ }.
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Dual boundary normal form and orthogonal invariant]\label{thm:dual-boundary-normal-form}
On the corner domain \((r,u)\in(0,1)\times(\TT\setminus\{-1\})\), any auditable object simultaneously satisfying (i)~the Abel-caliber logarithmic pole normal form (given by the expansion of \(\mathcal{P}(r)\)) and (ii)~the phase compression dilation linearization (given by \(\mathfrak{w}(\psi_m(u))=\mathfrak{w}(u)/m\), equation~\eqref{eq:scale-linearization-identity}) has its ``infinity'' structure decomposed in \((Y_r,Y_t)\) into one depth-growth direction and one orthogonal invariant direction:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Depth growth)} Carried by the additive growth of \(Y_r\) and \(Y_t\), corresponding respectively to the boundary layers \(r\to 1^{-}\) and \(u\to -1\);
  \item \textbf{(Orthogonal invariant)} One may take
  \[
  \mathcal{I}:=Y_r-Y_t,
  \]
  which characterizes the relative phase load between ``pole depth'' and ``height depth''; for any Witt-type dilation that acts only via scale weighting (the manuscript's \(s\mapsto ms\), \(z\mapsto z^{m}\) pipeline) and phase dilation (\(x\mapsto mx\), \(u\mapsto\psi_m(u)\) pipeline), \(\mathcal{I}\) is the natural conserved coordinate.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:dual-boundary-depth-coords} and the respective actions of the Witt dilation chain on \(r\) and \(\mathfrak{w}\) (\(r\mapsto r^{m}\) and \(\mathfrak{w}\mapsto \mathfrak{w}/m\)), \(Y_r\) and \(Y_t\) each evolve with additive time \(\log m\); hence \(\mathcal{I}=Y_r-Y_t\) is invariant under both types of dilation. \qed
\end{proof}
This theorem provides a closed restatement: the two types of infinity in the Riemann system (the pole and the height) become, after compression, a normal crossing of a pair of transverse boundaries, and ``information migrating to precision'' is manifested in this normal form as the ineliminability of \(\mathcal{I}\)---if one refuses to explicitly externalize as orthogonal records the discrete certificate channels (e.g., character channels or integer-layer audit channels) forced by the automorphisms of the projection system, then changes in \(\mathcal{I}\) can only flow back in the form of boundary-layer precision (depth jets). This viewpoint is consistent with the integer-layer audit stratification principle of the manuscript: the divisibility depth of the Frobenius defect \(\Delta_{p,k}(u)\in p^k\ZZ[u]\) (Definition~\ref{def:witt-frobenius-defect}) provides the arithmetic certificate for the above discrete channel; once it fails, the spectral-layer boundary interpretation loses structural significance.
