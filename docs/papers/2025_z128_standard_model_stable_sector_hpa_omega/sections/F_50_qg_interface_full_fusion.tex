\subsection{The asymptotic limit: black holes as high-\texorpdfstring{$m$}{m} particles (interface dictionary)}
\label{subsec:bh_high_m_particles}
The full-fusion suite includes a budget-triggered cloud/horizon rule and reports a horizon-occupancy fraction $f_{\mathrm{hor}}$ (Section~\ref{subsec:qg_interface_suite_maintext} and Section~\ref{subsec:full_fusion_maintext}).
In the protocol language, $m$ is the single-window bit budget (word length), and increasing $m$ increases the information content that can be routed into a localized region on the chosen screen.
At fixed screen size $4^n$, the budget rule depends explicitly on a required site count that scales like $|{\mathcal R}_\star|_{\min}=\lceil cI_{\mathrm{obs}}/m\rceil$ and is capped by the finite screen size (see the saturation discussion in Paragraph~R2 of Section~\ref{subsec:full_fusion_rigidity_takeaways}).
When the required cloud becomes comparable to the full screen, the interface loses geometric discriminability and the dynamics exhibits trapping/leakage behavior that is operationally horizon-like: information becomes difficult to extract and delays grow.
This motivates an interface dictionary: \emph{high-$m$ excitations behave as black-hole-like particles} in the sense that capacity constraints induce effective horizons for readout and transport.

\paragraph{Entropy and capacity: a protocol-auditable bound chain.}
Appendix~\ref{app:holo_capacity_bound} fixes the entropy/capacity conventions used in this manuscript: capacities and entropies are measured in \emph{bits} and the protocol capacity of a screen region $R$ is
$I_{\mathrm{prot}}(m,n;R)=m|R|$ (Definition~\ref{def:regional_protocol_capacity}).
At fixed $m$, a raw readout window has $|\Omega_m|=2^m$ microstates, hence a maximal raw entropy scale of $m$ bits per site.
However, the stable sector is not $\Omega_m$ but the admissible set $X_m$ selected by the Zeckendorf forbidden-word grammar (no adjacent ones) together with the closure predicate (Section~\ref{sec:hpa_readout} and Section~\ref{sec:folding_core}).
In particular, $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:xm_fib}), so the stable-sector per-site entropy scale is
\[
S_{\mathrm{stab,site}}(m):=\log_2|X_m|=\log_2 F_{m+2}\sim m\log_2\varphi
\qquad (m\to\infty),
\]
which is strictly smaller than the raw $m$ bits per site at large $m$ since $\log_2\varphi<1$.
For a region $R$ on the screen, this yields the auditable bound chain
\[
S_{\mathrm{stab}}(m;R):=|R|\log_2|X_m|
\ \le\ |R|\log_2|\Omega_m|
\ =\ m|R|
\ =\ I_{\mathrm{prot}}(m,n;R),
\]
consistent with the general finite-dimensional capacity bound (Corollary~\ref{cor:holo_protocol_capacity_bound}).
Thus the forbidden grammar provides an explicit finite-state mechanism that suppresses entropy growth relative to unconstrained bits, while remaining compatible with the manuscript's protocol capacity bounds.

\paragraph{Relation to the Bekenstein bound and black-hole entropy (matching dictionary).}
In conventional units, the Bekenstein bound constrains the entropy $S$ of a system of energy $E$ contained in radius $R$ by
\[
S\le \frac{2\pi ER}{\hbar c}
\]
\cite{Bekenstein1981}, and black holes saturate an area-law entropy scale $S_{\mathrm{BH}}=A/(4G\hbar/c^3)$ \cite{BardeenCarterHawking1973,Hawking1975,Bousso2002}.
In the present manuscript we do not claim a theorem-level derivation of these relations from the folding core.
Rather, we use them as a matching dictionary: the protocol assigns an information budget to localized readout regions, and the budget-triggered horizon rule supplies an auditable operational surrogate of information trapping.
Appendix~\ref{app:holo_capacity_bound} records the finite-resolution capacity bound used in the manuscript's holographic scope contract, and Appendix~\ref{app:bh_wormholes_pointer} records the corresponding black-hole/wormhole interface notes used by the full-fusion suite.

\subsection{Rigidity takeaways and identifiability boundaries (audit)}
\label{subsec:full_fusion_rigidity_takeaways}

\paragraph{R1: cost--gain rigidity (no free shortcut channels).}
\AuditTag The wormhole-like module is closed as a paid interface: its effect is reported only through an explicit cost ledger $E_{\mathrm{wh}}(t)$ and counterfactual deltas relative to the wormhole-off baseline.
Accordingly, observable gains appear as \emph{trade-offs} rather than free upgrades; the bounded sweep and its Pareto rows (\texttt{full\_fusion\_wormhole\_sweep\_rows.tex} and \texttt{full\_fusion\_wormhole\_pareto\_rows.tex}) are the auditable record of this rigidity.

\paragraph{R2: horizon saturation and loss of geometric identifiability at fixed \texorpdfstring{$(m,n)$}{(m,n)}.}
\AuditTag The budget-triggered cloud rule depends on a required site count $|{\mathcal R}_\star|_{\min}=\lceil cI_{\mathrm{obs}}/m\rceil$ but is capped by the finite screen size $4^n$.
When $|{\mathcal R}_\star|_{\min}>4^n$ (infeasible regime), the selection saturates to the full screen and geometry-facing diagnostics (occupancy fraction, boundary perimeter, curvature peaks restricted to the cloud) lose discriminative power within that fixed $(m,n)$ class.
This saturation boundary is visible directly in the QG interface suite rows and summary (\texttt{qg\_interface\_suite\_rows.tex}, \texttt{qg\_interface\_suite\_summary.tex}).

\paragraph{R3: observation-class rigidity (final-snapshot deltas can miss intermittent channels).}
\AuditTag Several wormhole-facing observables are \emph{gated} by near-endpoint events (Section~\ref{subsec:full_fusion_maintext}): for example, the extra delay component $\tau_{\mathrm{wh}}$ is only active near endpoints, and the pointer-jump exit channel is only eligible on a near-endpoint$\cap$trap subset.
Therefore, a metrics-only contract that reports a single final-snapshot delta (as in \texttt{full\_fusion\_compare\_rows.tex}) is sensitive to robust accumulated quantities (e.g.\ $\Delta E_{\mathrm{emit}}$, $\Delta\tau_{\mathrm{trap}}$) but may systematically miss intermittent channels (e.g.\ endpoint-gated $\tau_{\mathrm{wh}}$).

\paragraph{R4: budget normalization across oracle windows (adaptive search vs full reruns).}
\AuditTag The adaptive wormhole search uses a metrics-only oracle run over a shorter default step budget than the full rerun (see \texttt{scripts/exp\_full\_fusion\_wormhole\_adaptive\_search.py}).
Since $E_{\mathrm{wh}}$ is accumulated as a time-integrated ledger, an absolute budget constraint $E_{\mathrm{wh}}\le B$ is not directly comparable across different run lengths without an explicit normalization convention (e.g.\ $E_{\mathrm{wh}}/T$ or $E_{\mathrm{wh}}/\text{steps}$).
For this reason, recommended configurations should be interpreted within the declared oracle window unless the run length is matched.

\paragraph{R5: structural reason for zero jump-rate rows in bounded sweeps.}
\AuditTag In the present default parameter box and timestep, the pointer-jump probability scales as $p\propto \lambda_{\mathrm{jump}}\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ptr}}^2\,dt$ and is further gated by near-endpoint$\cap$trap exposure.
As a result, bounded sweeps can legitimately report zero jump-rate throughout their grid (a sparsity effect of the declared contract), which should be read as an identifiability boundary of this particular observation class rather than as an assertion that ``wormholes do nothing''.
