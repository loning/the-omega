\subsubsection{Structural Essence of Ramanujan-Type Symmetry: From "Coordinate Symmetry" to "Phase-Lift Symmetry"}\label{subsubsec:ramanujan-phase-lift-symmetry}
This section has compressed the completion/self-dual induced symmetry to fixed geometry on the parameter side: the unitary slice \(|u|=1\) and its Dirichlet image \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) (Remark \ref{rem:xi-critical-line-unit-circle}).
It must be emphasized: the so-called "symmetry" here is not coordinate permutation among several readout axes, but rather automorphisms of the projection system itself (such as \((z,u)\mapsto(uz,1/u)\) or \(u\mapsto 1/u\)) and their fixed slices.
Under this caliber, the appearance of \(\Re(s)=\tfrac12\) does not rely on "axis of symmetry" metaphor, but is the unique self-consistent slice in the phase-lift closure within completion coordinates.
\par
Combining the disk-compactified horizon—multipole moment interface with the kernel-RH/Ramanujan spectral bound dictionary (see end of \S\ref{subsubsec:selfdual-blaschke-defect-fixed-slice} and Appendix \ref{subsubsec:app-horizon-weyl-carath-toeplitz}), "Ramanujan symmetry" can be structurally restated as follows: it does not geometrically exchange coordinate axes, but rather structurally enforces \textbf{boundary unitarization/inner-functionalization} at the protocol level, thereby structurally excluding disk point spectral defects (i.e., Blaschke data).
In this sense, the so-called "symmetry" is primarily a defect exclusion law: when self-dual (antilinear) symmetry is compatible with boundary unitarization, index vanishing enforces \(B_F\equiv 1\), and point-like off-slice defects can only be pushed to boundary support of the fixed slice (Theorem \ref{thm:blaschke-defect-zero-fixed-slice-support}).
Simultaneously, disk compactification compresses the geometric contribution of high imaginary part to an extremely thin boundary layer at endpoint \(w\approx-1\); if there exists an off-critical-line zero \(\rho=\beta+\mathrm{i}\gamma\) (\(\beta<\tfrac12\), \(\delta=\tfrac12-\beta>0\)), its disk image \(w_\rho=\mathcal{C}(\gamma+\mathrm{i}\delta)\) satisfies the closed-form compression law
\(
1-|w_\rho|^2=\frac{4\delta}{\gamma^2+(1+\delta)^2}
\)
and thus can only be verified under regime with sufficient endpoint resolution (Theorem \ref{thm:app-jensen-offcritical-kink-localization}).

\begin{theorem}[Phase Lift: \texorpdfstring{$[-2,2]$}{[-2,2]} Spectral Bound \(\Leftrightarrow\) Pure Phase]\label{thm:phase-lift-spectral-bound}
Let \(\mathcal H\) be a complex Hilbert space and \(A\) a bounded self-adjoint operator on \(\mathcal H\).
Then the following conditions are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)\subseteq[-2,2]\);
  \item There exists a unitary operator \(U\) such that \(A=U+U^{-1}\).
\end{enumerate}
Moreover, when (1) holds, one can take by continuous functional calculus
\[
\boxed{\ U:=\exp\!\bigl(\mathrm{i}\,\arccos(A/2)\bigr)\ }
\]
so that \(A=U+U^{-1}\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{(2)\(\Rightarrow\)(1).}
If \(A=U+U^{-1}\) and \(U\) is unitary, then \(\mathrm{Spec}(U)\subseteq\TT\).
By spectral mapping theorem, \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)=f(\mathrm{Spec}(U))\) where \(f(z)=z+z^{-1}\).
For \(z=e^{i\theta}\in\TT\) we have \(f(z)=2\cos\theta\in[-2,2]\), hence \(\mathrm{Spec}(A)\subseteq[-2,2]\).
\par
\textbf{(1)\(\Rightarrow\)(2).}
On \([-2,2]\), define continuous function \(g(x):=\exp(\mathrm{i}\arccos(x/2))\), then \(|g(x)|=1\).
Let \(U:=g(A)\) be the operator given by continuous functional calculus, then \(U\) is unitary.
Since \(g(x)+g(x)^{-1}=2\cos(\arccos(x/2))=x\) holds identically on \([-2,2]\), the same identity under functional calculus gives \(U+U^{-1}=A\).
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Operator-Theoretic Version of Joukowski Compression]\label{rem:phase-lift-joukowski-operator}
Theorem \ref{thm:phase-lift-spectral-bound} provides the operator-layer counterpart to the \((y,S)\)-compression of Proposition \ref{prop:xi-rh-interval-criterion}: if \(Uv=e^{i\theta}v\), then \((U+U^{-1})v=2\cos\theta\,v\).
Therefore \(\mathrm{Spec}(U)\subseteq\TT\) is compressed via Joukowski map to \([-2,2]\); in the reverse direction, spectrum on \([-2,2]\) is lifted to unit circle phase.
This "compression \(\Leftrightarrow\) lift" isomorphism appears synchronously in the completion variable \(S=y+y^{-1}\) of this section and the half-angle completion variable \(s=\mathfrak{r}+\mathfrak{r}^{-1}\) of the appendix (Remark \ref{rem:xi-joukowski-toy-rh-alignment} and Remark \ref{rem:xi-half-angle-endpoint-paradigm}).
\end{remark}
