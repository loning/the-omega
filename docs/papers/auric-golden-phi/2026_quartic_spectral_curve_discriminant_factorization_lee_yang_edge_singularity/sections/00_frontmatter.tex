\begin{abstract}
Given a quartic spectral family \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\), we study its branch geometry, limit laws, and arithmetic closure in a strictly auditable finite-dimensional setting.
The ordinary generating function \(Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m\) is shown to be reduced and minimal in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:minimal-spectral-closure}, yielding a minimal linear recurrence of order \(4\) and fixed Hankel rank \(4\) outside a finite degeneracy locus.
We then compute the complete discriminant factorization of the quartic spectral polynomial \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\), proving that branching occurs exactly at \(y\in\{0,1\}\) and at the roots of \(c(y)=256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\).
The unique real branch value is \(\yLY\in(-1.13446,-1.13445)\), where one real spectral pair coalesces and leaves the real axis; in addition, we prove the existence of a unique non-bifurcating negative-axis equimodular point
\[
\yEQ\in\left(-\frac{160092}{10^6},-\frac{160091}{10^6}\right),
\]
at which a negative real branch and a complex conjugate pair have exactly equal modulus.

The discriminant factorization is governed by an elliptic normalization: the spectral curve \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) is isomorphic to the elliptic curve \(E:\xi^2+\xi=\lambda^3-\lambda\) of conductor \(37\), and \(y=\lambda^2+\xi\) defines a degree-\(4\) morphism \(\varphi:E\to\mathbb{P}^1_y\).
Passing to the regular closure produces a degree-\(24\) \(S_4\)-cover \(X\to\mathbb{P}^1_y\) of genus \(16\) with passport \(\bigl((4),(2)^5\bigr)\).
Its normal quotients are the genus-\(2\) discriminant ridge curve \(X_A:w^2=-y(y-1)c(y)\) and a genus-\(4\) resolvent curve \(X_V\), and the map \(X_V\to X_A\) is an \'etale cyclic cubic cover.
This yields a Prym surface with \(\QQ(\zeta_3)\)-multiplication and the Hodge decomposition
\[
H^0(X,\Omega_X^1)\cong \mathrm{sgn}^{\oplus 2}\oplus V_2\oplus V_3\oplus (V_3\otimes\mathrm{sgn})^{\oplus 3}.
\]
Moreover, the \(V_3\)-isotypic abelian factor is shown to be isogenous to \(E^3\), so the original elliptic curve reappears canonically inside the genus-\(16\) regular closure.

Finally, after normalizing by \(Z_m(e^s)/Z_m(1)\), we show that the limiting log-moment generating function is governed by the dominant spectral branch \(\lambda_+(y)\).
This gives closed-form constants for a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem (mean \(5/18\), variance \(67/972\)), together with the local Legendre rate function associated with the dominant branch.
\end{abstract}
