\section{Information Budget and Geometric Time}
\label{chap:info_budget}

\subsection{The Information Velocity Circle}
Standard Relativity posits a constant speed of light $c$. In this manuscript we use $c$ as a convenient normalization for an effective per-tick information-processing capacity and introduce a parametrization for how this capacity is allocated among several channels in the regulated/QCA description.
Let $\mathcal{I}_{\text{total}}$ be an effective per-tick computational budget in the regulated description (e.g.\ a bounded local gate-depth or operation count per step on the 1D substrate), interpreted as the rate at which distinguishable updates can be realized at finite resolution. This capacity is partitioned into:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{External Propagation ($v_{\text{ext}}$)}: Moving information through the spatial graph (speed of particles).
    \item \textbf{Internal Processing ($v_{\text{int}}$)}: Processing information locally (mass/inertia).
    \item \textbf{Environment/Geometry ($v_{\text{env}}$)}: (i) holographic entanglement updates on $\partial G$; and (ii) routing overhead required to realize bulk-local PQCA updates on an effectively 1D computational substrate (see Section \ref{sec:routeA_routing}).
\end{enumerate}
In the microscopic picture of Omega Theory, localized ``matter'' degrees of freedom are modeled as topologically protected excitations (wrappings/knots) of the internal fiber (Part~IV). Maintaining a non-trivial topological sector requires a persistent local processing overhead, which we model as a non-zero internal allocation $v_{\text{int}}$ in the budget circle. This viewpoint is consistent with the geometric-time definition in Part~II, where a non-vanishing uncertainty (hence non-trivial projective motion) is required for intrinsic duration to advance.
Here the quantities $v_{\text{ext}},v_{\text{int}},v_{\text{holo}},v_{\text{route}}$ should be read as effective information-processing budget components (defined per computational step on the 1D substrate), not as directly measured physical velocities.
\paragraph{Remark (finite per-tick capacity and quantum speed limits).}
In a regulated finite-depth QCA/PQCA description, one works with a bounded number of local gate layers per step, so it is natural to model a finite per-tick budget of distinguishable updates. Independently, in standard quantum mechanics the speed of distinguishable state change is bounded by energy scales: the Mandelstam--Tamm and Margolus--Levitin quantum speed limits bound orthogonalization rates in terms of $\Delta E$ and mean energy above the ground state, and Lloyd's bound gives an ``ultimate'' estimate of operations per unit time for a system of fixed energy \cite{MandelstamTamm1945,MargolusLevitin1998,lloyd2000ultimate}. In this manuscript we use $c$ only as a convenient normalization constant for the resulting effective allocation parameters.
We introduce the following budget parametrization:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\text{ext}}^2 + v_{\text{int}}^2 + v_{\text{holo}}^2 + v_{\text{route}}^2 = c^2,
\label{eq:info_velocity_circle}
\end{equation}
where we have decomposed $v_{\text{env}}^2 \equiv v_{\text{holo}}^2 + v_{\text{route}}^2$. In empty space ($v_{\text{int}} = v_{\text{holo}} = v_{\text{route}} = 0$), we recover $v_{\text{ext}} = c$. Near a region requiring larger routing overhead, $v_{\text{route}}$ increases and the locally available budget for propagation decreases, providing a convenient parametrization of gravitational time dilation and propagation delay in this model.

\paragraph{Finite budget resolution and Zeckendorf discretization (golden case).}
Eq.~(\ref{eq:info_velocity_circle}) can be viewed as an orthogonal decomposition of a fixed-norm ``budget'' vector in a low-dimensional Euclidean space whose components are the effective allocation fractions of a finite information-processing capacity.
Under the finite-information postulate, this allocation is not infinitely refinable: in any finite causal patch the number of distinguishable micro-configurations is bounded, so any operational readout of the budget components has finite resolution (Planck-limited in the idealized microscopic setting).
When the intrinsic tick index is generated by an irrational rotation (Part~II) and read out by a binary partition, the resulting discrete scheduling/labeling of budget reallocations can be expressed in Ostrowski numeration; for the golden-slope choice it specializes to Zeckendorf digits (no consecutive Fibonacci summands). In that sense, the ``budget circle'' decomposition is compatible with a canonical Fibonacci/Zeckendorf discretization of scale-activation events in the Golden construction, rather than requiring an arbitrary ad hoc clock.

\begin{definition}[Computational lapse from routing cost]
\label{def:computational_lapse}
Let $\kappa(x)$ denote the coarse-grained local routing cost induced by the exact 1D compilation construction (Definition \ref{def:routing_cost_field}). Define the \emph{computational lapse} by
\begin{equation}
    N(x)\equiv \frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(x)},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_0>0$ is a reference minimal cost (e.g., the infimum of $\kappa$ in a weak-field baseline region). We assume $\kappa(x)\ge \kappa_0$, so that $0<N(x)\le 1$. If $t$ denotes the computational time defined by 1D nearest-neighbor gate depth on the tape, we identify the local proper time increment as
\begin{equation}
    d\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=N(x)\,dt.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Remark (consistency with modular intrinsic time).}
In a regulated operator-algebraic description, modular theory provides a canonical intrinsic parameter $\tau$ on an observer algebra (Definition~\ref{def:modular_time}). In the thermal-time reading \cite{ConnesRovelli1994}, $\tau$ plays the role of an internal clock for the restricted state. The lapse identification above should be read as a bridge between two \emph{parametrizations}: $t$ is a bookkeeping parameter tied to nearest-neighbor gate depth on the 1D substrate, while $\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}$ is an intrinsic parameter tied to the local effective state. The routing-cost ratio $N(x)=\kappa_0/\kappa(x)$ expresses that, in regions with larger compilation overhead, fewer intrinsic (local) updates are realized per unit substrate depth, matching the interpretation of gravitational time dilation as computational slowdown in the present model.

\begin{proposition}[Lapse normalization from compilation depth]
\label{prop:lapse_from_depth}
Fix a finite region $R$ and an ordering $f_R$ of its degrees of freedom onto a 1D nearest-neighbor substrate. Let $\kappa_R(x;f_R)$ be the local routing cost of Definition~\ref{def:routing_cost_field} and set $\kappa_R^{\max}(f_R)\equiv \max_{x\in R}\kappa_R(x;f_R)$.
Then the exact compilation in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact_1d_compilation} yields a nearest-neighbor circuit implementing one restricted PQCA step on $R$ with depth bounded by
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{depth}(\mathcal C_R)\ \le\ C\cdot D\cdot \kappa_R^{\max}(f_R),
\end{equation}
where $C$ depends only on the bounded neighborhood size and on-site dimension.
If computational time $t$ is measured in nearest-neighbor depth layers, this bound implies that the number of logical PQCA steps executable per unit $t$ is suppressed by a factor proportional to $1/\kappa_R^{\max}(f_R)$ for that region.
Normalizing to a weak-field reference region with cost $\kappa_0$ motivates the lapse ratio $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$ used above.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
Immediate from Proposition~\ref{prop:exact_1d_compilation} and Definition~\ref{def:routing_cost_field} by interpreting $t$ as nearest-neighbor circuit depth.
\end{proof}

As a computational--geometric dictionary (i.e.\ a choice of time coordinate tied to nearest-neighbor tape depth), we adopt the effective identification
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{-g_{00}(x)}\ \equiv\ N(x)\ \equiv\ \frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(x)}.
\end{equation}
\noindent
\textit{Remark (coordinate choice vs.\ invariant content).} In an ADM decomposition one writes $ds^2=-N^2dt^2+h_{ij}(dx^i+N^i dt)(dx^j+N^j dt)$. The identification above corresponds to working in an effective gauge with negligible shift ($N^i\approx 0$) and interpreting the coordinate parameter $t$ as a bookkeeping variable for circuit depth. Physical observables such as redshift are coordinate-invariant, while the mapping of those observables to the routing-cost field $\kappa$ is part of the modeling dictionary adopted in this manuscript.
\begin{proposition}[Gravitational redshift from routing-cost lapse]
\label{prop:redshift_from_kappa}
In any (approximately) static situation where the effective metric takes the form $ds^2=-N^2(\mathbf{x})\,dt^2+\cdots$ and observers are at rest in the $t$-foliation, the local proper time satisfies $d\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}=N(\mathbf{x})\,dt$. Hence the clock-rate ratio between two locations $\mathbf{x}_1,\mathbf{x}_2$ is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{x}_1)}{d\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{x}_2)}=\frac{N(\mathbf{x}_1)}{N(\mathbf{x}_2)},
\end{equation}
and the corresponding frequency redshift for exchanged signals is $\nu_1/\nu_2=N(\mathbf{x}_2)/N(\mathbf{x}_1)$. Under the computational-lapse identification $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$, this implies
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\nu_1}{\nu_2}=\frac{\kappa(\mathbf{x}_1)}{\kappa(\mathbf{x}_2)},
\end{equation}
so gravitational redshift is mapped to a routing-cost ratio in this model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is the standard gravitational redshift relation in a static metric written with lapse $N(\mathbf{x})$; see, e.g., \cite{Carroll2004}. Substituting $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$ yields the stated routing-cost form.
\end{proof}

For later convenience, one may parametrize the routing component by
\begin{equation}
    \frac{v_{\text{route}}(x)}{c}\equiv \sqrt{1-N(x)^2}
    \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
    N(x)=\sqrt{1-\frac{v_{\text{route}}(x)^2}{c^2}}.
\end{equation}

\section{The Omega Action and Informational Gravity}
\label{chap:omega_action}

Our approach builds upon the foundational insights of \textbf{Jacobson} \cite{Jacobson1995} (thermodynamics of spacetime) and \textbf{Verlinde} \cite{Verlinde2011} (entropic gravity). While these authors derived Einstein's equations from general thermodynamic equations of state ($\delta Q = T dS$), the Omega Action adopts a concrete information-geometric ansatz in which a coarse-grained ``informational sector'' is governed by a Fisher-information functional.
Related information-theoretic variational principles and Fisher-information-based derivations of quantum dynamics appear in, e.g., Frieden's program and Reginatto's formulation \cite{Frieden1998, Reginatto1998}.
\paragraph{Remark (why Fisher information is canonical).}
In classical information geometry, the Fisher information metric is uniquely characterized (up to an overall constant) by monotonicity under coarse-graining/sufficient statistics (Čencov's theorem) \cite{Chentsov1982,AmariNagaoka2000}. In the quantum setting, monotone Riemannian metrics on the space of density matrices are classified by operator-monotone functions (Petz) \cite{Petz1996}. In this manuscript we adopt the Fisher-information functional as the minimal two-derivative information-geometric term compatible with these canonicality principles in the classical/coarse-grained sector.

\subsection{The Fisher-Information Lagrangian}
We postulate the \textbf{Omega Action} as a covariant information-geometric functional. Let $\Psi(x)=\sqrt{\varrho(x)}\,e^{i\theta(x)}$ be a coarse-grained continuum wavefunction, where $\varrho$ is an information/probability density. It is convenient to define both the amplitude field
\begin{equation}
    \chi \equiv \sqrt{\varrho},
\end{equation}
and the logarithmic field
\begin{equation}
    \varphi \equiv \ln \sqrt{\varrho}.
\end{equation}
Throughout this section we assume $\varrho$ is a positive scalar field on the spacetime region of interest, so that $\chi$ and $\varphi$ are well-defined; handling zeros or sharp interfaces requires additional regularization beyond the present effective description, and whenever a regularization-dependent constant term must be extracted we adopt the orbit finite-part convention of Convention~R1 (Section~\ref{conv:orbit_regularization}; Appendix~\ref{app:orbit_calculus}).
The (classical) Fisher information functional of $\varrho$ admits the covariant form
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{I}_F[\varrho]\ \equiv\ \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\, g^{\mu\nu}\,\frac{\nabla_\mu \varrho\,\nabla_\nu \varrho}{\varrho}
\ =\ 4\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\, g^{\mu\nu}\,(\nabla_\mu \chi)(\nabla_\nu \chi)
\ =\ 4\int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\varrho\, g^{\mu\nu}\,(\nabla_\mu \varphi)(\nabla_\nu \varphi).
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Remark (orbit-calculus normalization).}
In the regulated scan/QCA picture, continuum derivatives and integrals are effective limits of discrete orbit functionals tied to the scan (Convention~R1). The Fisher term above should be read as a long-wavelength approximation to an orbit-averaged squared discrete gradient; Appendix~\ref{app:orbit_calculus} records a minimal orbit-calculus template.
Accordingly, we take the minimal Omega Action to be Einstein--Hilbert gravity coupled to the Fisher-amplitude scalar $\chi$:
\begin{equation}
    S_{\Omega} = \int d^4x \sqrt{-g} \left[ \frac{R}{16\pi G} - \lambda_{F} g^{\mu\nu} (\nabla_\mu \chi) (\nabla_\nu \chi) - V(\varrho) \right],
\label{eq:omega_action_chi}
\end{equation}
with $\varrho=\chi^2$. The first term is the Einstein--Hilbert action, the second is proportional to Fisher information, and $V(\varrho)$ is an effective potential encoding coarse-grained constraints (including vacuum-energy-like components in homogeneous regimes). At this two-derivative level the model is a standard scalar field minimally coupled to GR.

\paragraph{Ansatz (Cost--density identification).}
To connect the informational field to compilation overhead, it is convenient to work with the dimensionless overhead ratio
\begin{equation}
    s(x)\ \equiv\ \frac{\kappa(x)}{\kappa_0}\ =\ \frac{1}{N(x)}\ \ge\ 1.
\end{equation}
In general one could posit a local coupling $\varrho=\varrho_0\,F(s)$ with $F(s)>0$ and $F(1)=1$. In the weak-field/slow-variation regime where $s$ stays close to unity, any smooth $F$ reduces at leading order to a power law; we record this as a short proposition and adopt the minimal truncation:
\begin{proposition}[Local power-law form of the cost--density coupling]
\label{prop:cost_density_powerlaw}
Let $s>0$ and suppose $\varrho=\varrho_0\,F(s)$ with $F\in C^2$ in a neighborhood of $s=1$ and $F(1)=1$. Writing $s=e^{u}$, one has for $|u|\ll 1$
\begin{equation}
    \varrho\ =\ \varrho_0\, s^{p}\,\bigl(1+\mathcal{O}(u^2)\bigr),\qquad
    p\ \equiv\ \left.\frac{d}{du}\ln F(e^{u})\right|_{u=0}
    \ =\ \left.s\,\frac{F'(s)}{F(s)}\right|_{s=1}.
\end{equation}
In particular, keeping only the leading log-linear coupling corresponds to the exact power-law choice $F(s)=s^{p}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is a direct Taylor expansion in $u=\ln s$ about $u=0$.
\end{proof}
\noindent
Accordingly, we adopt the leading-order power-law identification
\begin{equation}
    \varrho(x)\equiv \varrho_0\left(\frac{\kappa(x)}{\kappa_0}\right)^p,\qquad p>0,
\end{equation}
so that
\begin{equation}
    \varphi = \ln\sqrt{\varrho} = \ln\sqrt{\varrho_0} + \frac{p}{2}\ln\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_0}\right).
\end{equation}
Consequently the Fisher-amplitude gradient becomes a routing-gradient term:
\begin{equation}
    g^{\mu\nu}(\nabla_\mu\chi)(\nabla_\nu\chi)
    = \frac{p^2}{4}\,\varrho\,g^{\mu\nu}\Bigl(\nabla_\mu\ln\!\bigl(\kappa/\kappa_0\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigl(\nabla_\nu\ln\!\bigl(\kappa/\kappa_0\bigr)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
so that the informational stress tensor contains a positive gradient-energy contribution controlled by spatial variations of the routing-cost field. In solutions with inhomogeneous $\kappa(x)$, this contribution sources curvature; the lapse dictionary $N(x)=\kappa_0/\kappa(x)$ then ties routing overhead to gravitational time dilation in the computational-time parametrization.

\subsection{Derivation of Field Equations}
We perform the variation with respect to the metric $g^{\mu\nu}$.
Using the standard identities $\delta \sqrt{-g} = -\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g} g_{\mu\nu} \delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and $\delta R = R_{\mu\nu} \delta g^{\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu v^\mu$, the variation $\delta S_{\Omega} = 0$ yields:
\begin{equation}
    G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\, T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{info}}.
\end{equation}
The informational stress-energy tensor combines the Fisher-information (gradient) term with the effective potential:
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{info}} =
    2\lambda_F \left( \nabla_\mu \chi \nabla_\nu \chi - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} (\nabla \chi)^2 \right)
    - g_{\mu\nu} V(\varrho).
\end{equation}
In the limit where the information density is uniform ($\nabla \chi \approx 0$), the gradient contribution vanishes and $V(\varrho)$ acts as an effective vacuum energy. In regimes where the gradient term dominates, the effective equation of state approaches $w \approx 1$ (stiff fluid), as for a kinetic-energy-dominated scalar field.

Varying with respect to $\chi$ gives the scalar equation of motion,
\begin{equation}
    2\lambda_F \Box \chi - \frac{dV}{d\chi} = 0,
\end{equation}
with $\frac{dV}{d\chi} = 2\chi\,\frac{dV}{d\varrho}$.

\subsection{Homogeneous limit and effective fluid form}
For a spatially homogeneous configuration $\chi=\chi(t)$ on an FLRW background with lapse $N(t)$ (Section~\ref{chap:cosmology}), the informational sector can be written as an effective perfect fluid. The corresponding energy density and pressure measured in proper time are
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\mathrm{info}} = \lambda_F \left(\frac{d\chi}{d\tau}\right)^{\!2} + V(\varrho)
    = \lambda_F \frac{\dot{\chi}^{\,2}}{N^2(t)} + V(\varrho), \qquad
    p_{\mathrm{info}} = \lambda_F \left(\frac{d\chi}{d\tau}\right)^{\!2} - V(\varrho)
    = \lambda_F \frac{\dot{\chi}^{\,2}}{N^2(t)} - V(\varrho),
\end{equation}
so that the effective equation-of-state parameter is
\begin{equation}
    w_{\mathrm{info}} \equiv \frac{p_{\mathrm{info}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{info}}}
    = \frac{\lambda_F \dot{\chi}^{\,2}/N^2(t) - V(\varrho)}{\lambda_F \dot{\chi}^{\,2}/N^2(t) + V(\varrho)}.
\end{equation}
In the kinetic-dominated regime, $w_{\mathrm{info}}\to 1$ (stiff); in the potential-dominated regime, $w_{\mathrm{info}}\to -1$ (effective vacuum energy). This provides a controlled bridge between the action principle above and the cosmological parameterizations below.

\paragraph{EFT interpretation and constraints (minimal sector).}
At the two-derivative level, Eq.~(\ref{eq:omega_action_chi}) is simply GR coupled to a minimally coupled scalar. In particular, the tensor sector propagates luminally ($c_T=1$) and the pure-gravity part coincides with Einstein--Hilbert; deviations from GR arise only through the scalar stress tensor and any nontrivial $V(\varrho)$. Any additional higher-derivative operators or non-minimal curvature couplings must be parametrically suppressed to remain consistent with precision tests; in this manuscript we restrict to the minimal sector above when deriving field equations.

\subsection{Dark Matter as Geometric Knots and Modified Gravity}
The Omega Action contains terms sensitive to gradients of the information field, which in low-acceleration regimes can contribute comparably to the Newtonian potential and can mimic MOND-like scaling. As a phenomenological benchmark we record the empirically relevant acceleration scale \cite{Milgrom1983MOND}
\begin{equation}
    a_0 \approx \frac{c H_0}{2\pi} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m/s}^2.
\end{equation}
Independently, the microscopic Omega Network allows additional topological sectors. In an effective order-parameter description, a ``geometric knot'' can be modeled as a localized configuration carrying a nontrivial integer winding/topological charge in the internal sector (e.g.\ the $SU(2)$ winding functional $B[U]$ discussed in Part~IV) while remaining neutral under the long-range gauge fluxes that define ordinary charges. If such sectors are stable and dilute, they behave as collisionless dark-matter-like components. The corresponding mass scale is determined by the microscopic energy cost of sustaining the topological sector and is treated here as a model parameter (no unique first-principles estimate is derived in this manuscript).


\section{The Omega-FRW Framework}
\label{chap:cosmology}
In the remainder of this section, $\rho$ and $p$ denote the cosmological energy density and pressure (not the information density $\varrho$ introduced in Section \ref{chap:omega_action}).
To embed cosmological parameterizations covariantly in General Relativity while remaining consistent with the computational-lapse identification of Definition~\ref{def:computational_lapse}, we use an FLRW metric with a (homogeneous) time-dependent lapse $N(t)$. In this interpretation, $t$ is a coarse-grained \emph{computational time} (e.g.\ tape-depth time), while the local proper time is $d\tau = N(t)\,dt$ (and in weakly inhomogeneous regimes $d\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}=N(t)N(\mathbf{x})\,dt$ with $N(\mathbf{x})=\kappa_0/\kappa(\mathbf{x})$).

\subsection{FRW metric with computational lapse}
We postulate a modified FLRW line element with a time-dependent lapse $N(t)$:
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = -N^2(t)\,dt^2 + a^2(t) \left[ \frac{dr^2}{1-kr^2} + r^2 d\Omega^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In a weak-field inhomogeneous extension compatible with the lapse identification above, one may write
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = -N^2(t)\,N^2(\mathbf{x})\,dt^2 + a^2(t)\,\gamma_{ij}\,dx^i dx^j,
    \qquad N(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(\mathbf{x})},
\end{equation}
which reduces to the homogeneous FLRW form when $N(\mathbf{x})\approx 1$. When $N(\mathbf{x})$ varies appreciably, the geometry is not strictly FLRW, and the homogeneous Friedmann equations below are used only in the approximately homogeneous limit.
The Einstein field equations are solved in the standard covariant way with the Levi-Civita connection compatible with the metric.

\subsection{Friedmann equations with lapse}
Define the proper-time Hubble rate $H_{\tau}\equiv \frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{d\tau}=\frac{\dot a}{a\,N}$, where a dot denotes $d/dt$. Substituting the lapse-FRW metric into the Einstein equations yields the standard Friedmann system in proper time:
\begin{align}
    H_{\tau}^2 &= \frac{8\pi G}{3}\rho + \frac{\Lambda(t)}{3} - \frac{k}{a^2}, \\
    \frac{1}{a}\frac{d^2a}{d\tau^2} &= -\frac{4\pi G}{3}\left(\rho+3p\right) + \frac{\Lambda(t)}{3}.
\end{align}
Rewriting the acceleration equation in the computational-time coordinate $t$ gives the equivalent form
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\ddot a}{a}
    =
    -\frac{4\pi G}{3}N^2(t)\left(\rho+3p\right)
    +\frac{\Lambda(t)}{3}N^2(t)
    +\frac{\dot N}{N}\frac{\dot a}{a},
\end{equation}
where the term $\dot N/N$ acts as a friction/anti-friction term in the $t$-coordinate description.

\paragraph{Continuity equation.}
Covariant conservation $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}=0$ yields
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d\rho}{d\tau} + 3H_{\tau}(\rho+p)=0,
\end{equation}
equivalently $\dot{\rho}+3(\dot a/a)(\rho+p)=0$ in the $t$-coordinate (the lapse cancels).

\paragraph{Parametrization of lapse drift.}
In phenomenological applications one may parameterize a slow cosmological drift of the homogeneous lapse by, e.g.,
\begin{equation}
    N(t)=N_0\,(1+z)^{-\delta_N},\qquad \delta_N\ll 1,
\end{equation}
as an effective summary of how average routing/encoding overhead changes with cosmological scale.

\subsection{Holographic Dark Energy Decay}
Standard $\Lambda$CDM assumes constant $\Lambda$. In holographic-energy models, near-saturation of a UV--IR consistency bound motivates an effective vacuum sector whose density is controlled by an infrared length scale $L$ (Axiom~O2; Assumption~\ref{assump:C1}). We record the standard energy bound in a causal patch \cite{CohenKaplanNelson1999}:
\begin{proposition}[Holographic energy bound in a causal patch]
\label{prop:hde_bound}
Let a causal patch be characterized by an infrared length scale $L$ (e.g.\ a horizon scale). Requiring that the total energy $E$ contained in the patch not exceed the mass of a black hole of the same size gives the bound
\begin{equation}
    \rho\ \le\ \frac{3}{8\pi G\,L^2},
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the coarse-grained energy density.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
See, e.g., \cite{CohenKaplanNelson1999} for the standard UV--IR energy bound argument in a causal patch.
\end{proof}
\noindent
Parameterizing near-saturation by a factor $\eta=\mathcal{O}(1)$ (Assumption~\ref{assump:C1}) gives the working relation $\rho_\Lambda=\eta\cdot \frac{3}{8\pi G L^2}$ used below.
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{\Lambda}(t) = \frac{3 \,\eta}{8\pi G\, L^2}, \quad L \sim H_{\tau}^{-1}(t) \implies \Lambda(t) \propto H_{\tau}^2(t),
\end{equation}
where $\eta=\mathcal{O}(1)$ parametrizes the near-saturation level (Assumption~\ref{assump:C1}). Such $L^{-2}$ scaling is the starting point of holographic dark energy models \cite{Li2004}; different choices of the IR cutoff $L$ lead to different phenomenologies. Since $\rho_{\mathrm{crit}}=3H_\tau^2/(8\pi G)$, the choice $\rho_\Lambda=3\eta/(8\pi G L^2)$ with $L\sim H_\tau^{-1}$ implies $\Omega_\Lambda\simeq \eta$ (approximately constant). The dynamics mimics a tracking dark-energy component with $w_{DE}\approx -1+\delta$ in regimes where $\eta$ drifts slowly.

\subsection{Linear Perturbations and Growth Factor}
To confront large-scale structure (LSS) data, one ultimately needs a full perturbation treatment of the coupled matter+informational sectors. As a minimal consistency check, we record here how the standard sub-horizon growth equation transforms under the time reparameterization $d\tau=N(t)\,dt$ used throughout this section.

\begin{proposition}[Growth equation under lapse time-reparametrization]
\label{prop:growth_lapse_reparam}
Assume that in proper time $\tau$ the matter density contrast $\delta_m$ obeys the standard sub-horizon growth equation
\begin{equation}
    \frac{d^2\delta_m}{d\tau^2}+2H_\tau\,\frac{d\delta_m}{d\tau}-4\pi G\,\rho_m\,\delta_m\approx 0,
\end{equation}
with $H_\tau\equiv \frac{1}{a}\frac{da}{d\tau}$. Writing $d\tau=N(t)\,dt$ and denoting $d/dt$ by a dot, the same equation becomes
\begin{equation}
    \ddot{\delta}_m + \left( 2H - \frac{\dot{N}}{N} \right)\dot{\delta}_m - 4\pi G\,N^2(t)\,\rho_m\,\delta_m \approx 0,
\end{equation}
where $H\equiv \dot a/a$ is the $t$-coordinate Hubble rate.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This follows immediately from the time reparameterization $d/d\tau=(1/N)\,d/dt$ and $H_\tau=\dot a/(aN)$.
\end{proof}
\noindent
Thus a slowly varying $N(t)$ modifies the effective friction term in computational time by a relative amount $|\dot N/(N H)|$ and rescales the source term by $N^2$. A dedicated confrontation with LSS/BAO data requires specifying the inhomogeneous dynamics of $N(t)$ and the informational sector and is beyond the scope of this manuscript.\par
\noindent
At high redshift, an integrated lapse drift modifies the inferred sound horizon through
\begin{equation}
    r_s = \int \frac{c_s\,d\tau}{a}=\int \frac{c_s\,N(t)\,dt}{a(t)},
\end{equation}
so that a slowly varying $N(t)$ can shift the mapping between early-universe microphysics and late-time distance ladders in a controlled way.


\subsection{The Initial Singularity and Bouncing Cosmology}
Standard Big Bang cosmology predicts a singularity at $t=0$. In a discrete finite-information substrate, it is natural to seek non-singular effective templates in which coarse-grained densities remain bounded and the evolution undergoes a bounce rather than a divergence. Interpreting such a bounce within Omega Theory requires specifying how the coarse-grained density is induced from microscopic information-capacity data; in this manuscript we therefore use a controlled effective template rather than claiming a derived microscopic theorem.
As a phenomenological template for a non-singular bounce, we adopt the standard LQC-inspired effective modification \cite{AshtekarPawlowskiSingh2006}:
\begin{equation}
    H_\tau^2 = \frac{8\pi G}{3} \rho \left( 1 - \frac{\rho}{\rho_{\text{crit}}} \right),
\end{equation}
where $H_\tau$ is the proper-time Hubble rate and $\rho_{\text{crit}}$ is a model parameter. In the Golden-scaling benchmark we set $\rho_{\text{crit}} \equiv \rho_{Pl} / \phi^3$ as an order-of-magnitude parametrization. When $\rho \to \rho_{\text{crit}}$, one has $H_\tau \to 0$ and the effective evolution undergoes a bounce.

\subsection{Black Hole Information and Islands (interpretation layer)}
The black hole information puzzle arises if one assumes that the semiclassical Hawking calculation yields exactly thermal radiation that remains uncorrelated with the interior, together with a naive subsystem factorization that assigns independent degrees of freedom to the late mode, its interior partner, and the early radiation (Part~VIII, Proposition~\ref{prop:amps_monogamy}). Omega Theory adopts the holographic/error-correction viewpoint: a horizon-sized causal patch carries a finite effective number of degrees of freedom (Axiom~O2) and bulk locality is encoded in a boundary code subspace (Axiom~O4). In this setting, exact thermality is not a microscopic premise; it is a coarse-grained approximation.
\par
\noindent
As a minimal kinematic benchmark, one expects Page-curve behavior when a finite-dimensional black-hole sector evaporates into radiation under a sufficiently scrambling unitary: after an order-one fraction of the entropy has been radiated, additional radiation quanta begin to carry net information about the initial state (Page's theorem \cite{Page1993}; see, e.g., \cite{Penington2020,Almheiri2019} for the modern island/Page-curve context). In semiclassical gravitational calculations, the corresponding entanglement-entropy formula includes an \emph{island} contribution: the dominant generalized-entropy saddle can switch from a no-island to an island configuration after the Page time \cite{Penington2020,Almheiri2019}. In Omega Theory, we model the interior ``deep memory'' buffer as an island-like subsystem in this sense: in tensor-network codes, entanglement entropies are controlled by minimal cuts (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}, Theorem~\ref{thm:mincut_entropy}), and Page-curve/island-like behavior arises from competition between no-island and island cut classes (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:page_curve_mincut}). When the relevant cut switches, bulk operators in the corresponding wedge become reconstructable on $R$ (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}, Theorem~\ref{thm:ewr_perfect} and Corollary~\ref{cor:ewr_eps}), providing a concrete network-level mechanism.
\begin{equation}
    S(R) = \text{min} \left\{ S_{\text{semi-cl}}(R), S_{\text{semi-cl}}(R \cup I) + \frac{\text{Area}(\partial I)}{4G} \right\}.
\end{equation}
\noindent
At the present stage, the firewall/information-paradox ``resolution'' claim is therefore operational: it reduces to verifying that the microscopic encoding map of Axiom~O4 realizes the necessary reconstruction/recovery properties in the relevant regime, rather than asserting a standalone new theorem about black hole evaporation.

\subsection{Boltzmann Brains and Non-thermalization}
In a standard infinite universe with thermal fluctuations, "Boltzmann Brains" (randomly assembled observers) should vastly outnumber evolved biological observers, leading to an epistemic paradox.
Omega Theory avoids the standard premise of thermal equilibrium. In the simplest diagonal golden-spectrum benchmarks, the discrete update $\hat{U}$ (Part~VI) induces a torus-translation dynamics on relative phases: irrational eigenphase differences yield quasiperiodic motion confined to an invariant orbit closure (a subtorus). Such torus-translation dynamics is not mixing (Appendix~\ref{app:torus_rotation}, Proposition~\ref{prop:torus_not_mixing}), so standard equilibrium-typicality arguments do not automatically apply.
\par
\noindent
However, ``not mixing'' does not mean that operational time fractions are undefined. Axiom~O5 defines macrostates by finite-resolution readout: for a resolution $\varepsilon>0$ let $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$ be the induced effects with $\sum_k E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=I$, and for any coarse event $M$ define $E_M^{(\varepsilon)}\equiv \sum_{k\in M}E_k^{(\varepsilon)}$. Along a tick index $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$, the empirical frequency of $M$ is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{Freq}_N(M;\varepsilon)\ \equiv\ \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\rho_n\,E_M^{(\varepsilon)}\right),
\end{equation}
for the effective observer-sector states $\rho_n$ induced by the orbit/readout at tick $n$. In the torus-translation regime, $\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_n E_M^{(\varepsilon)})$ is a bounded function sampled along a translate orbit on the relevant orbit closure $H$. By equidistribution on $H$ (Appendix~\ref{app:torus_rotation}, Theorem~\ref{thm:equidistribution_closure}) and the orbit-trace normalization of Convention~R1, the long-time frequency is fixed (for continuous readout observables, and more generally after the standard regularizations of Appendix~\ref{app:orbit_calculus}) by a Haar/orbit integral on $H$.
\par
\noindent
Accordingly, in this framework the Boltzmann-brain question reduces to a concrete diagnostic: specify (i) the effective orbit closure/code subspace $H$ for the sector under consideration and (ii) the readout coarse-graining $E_M^{(\varepsilon)}$, then compute the induced orbit-trace/Haar weight of ``BB-like'' versus ``ordinary-observer'' macrostates.
