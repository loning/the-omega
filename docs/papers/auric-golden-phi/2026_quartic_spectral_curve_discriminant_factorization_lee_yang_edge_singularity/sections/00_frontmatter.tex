\begin{abstract}
Given a family of partition functions \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) governed by a quartic spectral curve, we characterize the spectral branch locus and its consequences in a strictly auditable, finite-dimensional setting.
We first show that the ordinary generating function \(Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m\) is reduced and minimal in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:minimal-spectral-closure}, which implies a minimal linear recurrence of order \(4\) and fixed Hankel rank \(4\) for all parameters \(y\) outside a finite degeneracy set.
We then compute and completely factor the discriminant of the quartic spectral polynomial \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) (as a polynomial in \(\lambda\)), obtaining that branching occurs only at \(y\in\{0,1\}\) and at the roots of \(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\).
The real branch point is \(\yLY\in(-1.13446,-1.13445)\); at this point two real spectral roots collide and transition to a complex conjugate pair.
Separately, we prove the existence of a non-bifurcating real negative equimodular point \(\yEQ\approx-0.16009\), where a negative real spectral branch and a complex pair have equal modulus.
The discriminant factorization is underpinned by an elliptic normalization: the spectral curve \(\Pi(\lambda,y)=0\) is isomorphic to the elliptic curve \(E:\xi^2+\xi=\lambda^3-\lambda\) of conductor~\(37\) (Cremona \(37\mathrm{a}1\)), the parameter \(y=\lambda^2+\xi\) defines a degree-\(4\) morphism \(\varphi:E\to\mathbb{P}^1\), and the branch cubic is the resultant image of the ramification locus of~\(\varphi\); the Galois group of \(\Pi\) over \(\QQ(y)\) is~\(S_4\), and the discriminant equals the norm of the different of the associated function field extension.
Next, introducing an exponential family via the normalization \(Z_m(e^s)/Z_m(1)\), we show that the limiting log-moment generating function is controlled by the dominant spectral branch \(\lambda_+(y)\), and we obtain closed-form constants for a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem (mean \(5/18\), variance \(67/972\)) using Theorem~\ref{thm:coeff-nonneg}.
Finally, we outline a certificate-oriented computational workflow based on the order-\(4\) recurrence, enabling polynomial-time evaluation of \(Z_m(y)\), localization of zeros, and verification of the branch-locus structure.
\end{abstract}
