\begin{abstract}
We study the finite-window Zeckendorf stabilization map
\[
  \Fold_m(\omega)=V_m^{-1}\bigl(N_m(\omega)\bmod F_{m+2}\bigr),
\]
where \(V_m\) identifies the stable length-\(m\) Fibonacci words with the
residue interval \(\{0,\dots,F_{m+2}-1\}\).  Our main theorem shows that, after
an explicit affine permutation of residues, each fiber multiplicity is exactly
the Fibonacci-lag discrete derivative
\[
  R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n)-R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n-F_{m+1})
\]
of the classical Fibonacci partition function.  Here
\(R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\) denotes the indexed partition function with two
distinguishable unit weights, so that
\(R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n)=R(n)+R(n-1)\).  Consequently every collision moment is a
difference-power sum of the Fibonacci partition function.  In the quadratic
case this yields the exact recurrence
\(T_2(m)=2T_2(m-1)+2T_2(m-2)-2T_2(m-3)\) for \(m\ge 4\), together with a cubic
characteristic polynomial, a closed generating function, and an eventually
alternating second-order term.  This identifies finite-window Zeckendorf fibers
as a classical partition-theoretic object rather than a separate rewriting
construction.  As a further application, the fiber-uniform reflector
\(K_m=Q_mP_m\) satisfies exact entropy and Kullback--Leibler decomposition
formulas, which identify \(K_m\mu\) as the unique information projection of
\(\mu\) onto the visible model.
\end{abstract}
