
\section{A selection principle: stability under finite resources}
\label{sec:selection_principle}

The realization theorem shows that, under equidistribution, the asymptotic value of a scan protocol depends only on the period datum. In particular, for a fixed kernel $f_{\mathcal{R}}$, the integral is independent of the scan slope $\alpha$ as long as \eqref{eq:rational_independence} holds. This shifts the locus of ``selection'' away from the infinite-time limit and toward the finite-resource regime: what differs across protocols is not the limit value but the \emph{stability and cost} with which that value is realized.

\subsection{Value--rate separation}
\label{sec:value_rate_separation}

Let $\mathcal{P}=(\TT^d,\alpha,x_0,f,\mathcal{R})$ and $\mathcal{P}'=(\TT^d,\alpha',x_0',f,\mathcal{R})$ share the same kernel but use different scan slopes. If both slopes satisfy \eqref{eq:rational_independence}, then
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\avgN{\mathcal{P}}
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}\avgN{\mathcal{P}'}
=\int_{[0,1)^d} f_{\mathcal{R}}.
\]
However, the finite-$N$ errors generally differ:
\[
\Big|\avgN{\mathcal{P}}-\int f_{\mathcal{R}}\Big|
\ \text{depends on}\ \alpha
\quad\text{through}\quad
\DNstar(P_N),
\]
where $P_N=\{x_0,\dots,x_{N-1}\}$ denotes the scan-orbit prefix on $[0,1)^d$.
This is the operational content of value--rate separation: selection does not choose the integral, but chooses how efficiently the protocol realizes it.

\subsection{Stability metrics and bounded description budgets}
\label{sec:stability_metrics}

To make the notion of selection falsifiable, we require a cost functional that can be evaluated (or upper-bounded) from auditable protocol data.

\begin{definition}[Finite-horizon stability bound]
\label{def:stability_bound}
For a protocol $\mathcal{P}\in\ScanAlg$ at horizon $N$, define a stability bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:stability_bound}
\mathrm{Stab}_N(\mathcal{P})
:= \HKVar(f_{\mathcal{R}})\,\DNstar(P_N)
\end{equation}
whenever $\HKVar(f_{\mathcal{R}})<\infty$.
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{def:stability_bound} uses Corollary~\ref{cor:sampling_error} as a canonical, auditable choice. Alternative bounds (e.g.\ bounds in terms of continued-fraction digits in $d=1$) may be substituted, provided they remain auditable within the protocol.

Selection also requires a cost for \emph{describing} the protocol. In the present controlled setting, the dominant contributions are: the symbolic complexity of the kernel (degree, coefficient sizes, number of terms), and the regularization parameters (e.g.\ the truncation depth $M$).

\begin{definition}[Protocol description complexity (schematic)]
\label{def:protocol_complexity}
Let $\mathrm{Comp}(\mathcal{P};N)$ denote a computable surrogate for the description-and-implementation cost of running a protocol $\mathcal{P}$ up to horizon $N$. A concrete default choice in $\ScanAlg$ is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:comp_default}
\mathrm{Comp}(\mathcal{P};N)
:=
d
\;+\;
\mathrm{size}(f_{\mathcal{R}})
\;+\;
\mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{R})
\;+\;
\log(1+N),
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{size}(f_{\mathcal{R}})$ is a syntactic size of a rational presentation of $f_{\mathcal{R}}$ (e.g.\ total number of nonzero coefficients plus degree and coefficient height), and $\mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{R})$ accounts for regularization parameters (e.g.\ truncation depth $M$). Different reasonable encodings change \eqref{eq:comp_default} by at most multiplicative/additive constants and are interchangeable at the level of the programmatic conjecture.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Naive height of period data]
\label{def:naive_height}
Let $f_{\mathcal{R}}$ be represented as a reduced rational function $p/q$ with $p,q\in\ZZ[x_1,\dots,x_d]$ having no common factor. Define the naive height
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:naive_height}
H(f_{\mathcal{R}}):=\max\{H(p),H(q)\},
\qquad
H(p):=\max\{|c|:\ c\ \text{is a coefficient of }p\}.
\end{equation}
Any rational presentation in $\QQ(x_1,\dots,x_d)$ can be put into this form by clearing denominators and dividing by the content $\gcd$; the resulting height is well-defined up to the obvious sign normalization.
For a cubical period datum $\HSP(\mathcal{P})=([0,1)^d,f,\mathcal{R})$, define
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:height_default}
\mathrm{Height}(\HSP(\mathcal{P}))
:=\log\big(1+H(f_{\mathcal{R}})\big)+\deg(p)+\deg(q),
\end{equation}
as a computable arithmetic-geometric size surrogate.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Example: truncated geometric kernels]
For $g_M(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{M-1}(\prod_{j=1}^d x_j)^n$, one has $H(g_M)=1$ and $\mathrm{cost}(\mathcal{R})$ is naturally linear in $M$ if $g_M$ is evaluated as a length-$M$ sum, or logarithmic in $M$ if evaluated via the closed form $(1-p^M)/(1-p)$ with fast exponentiation. In either case, the truncation depth is a direct, auditable complexity knob in \eqref{eq:comp_default}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Selection principle (programmatic conjecture)}
\label{sec:selection_conjecture}

We now state a programmatic, falsifiable selection principle. It is not used as an input to any closed theorem.

\begin{conjecture}[Selection by stability--complexity optimization]
\label{conj:selection_principle}
There exists a functional $\mathcal{J}_N$ on a class of admissible protocols $\mathcal{C}\subset\ScanAlg$ of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:selection_functional}
\mathcal{J}_N(\mathcal{P})
=
\mathrm{Stab}_N(\mathcal{P})
\;+\;
\lambda\,\mathrm{Comp}(\mathcal{P};N)
\;+\;
\mu\,\mathrm{Height}(\HSP(\mathcal{P})),
\end{equation}
with $\lambda,\mu\ge 0$, such that the period data empirically observed as ``constants'' correspond to near-minimizers (or extremizers) of $\mathcal{J}_N$ as $N$ ranges over accessible horizons.
\end{conjecture}

The term $\mathrm{Height}(\HSP(\mathcal{P}))$ is optional and programmatic: it is intended to encode arithmetic/geometric ``size'' of the period datum (in the spirit of heights in arithmetic geometry) and to penalize excessively high-complexity realizations. The conjecture is falsifiable because each term in \eqref{eq:selection_functional} can be operationalized: $\mathrm{Stab}_N$ can be upper-bounded by discrepancy bounds, $\mathrm{Comp}$ is a bounded description budget, and candidate height surrogates can be compared by predictive performance.

\subsection{Why the golden branch appears}
\label{sec:golden_branch}

In one dimension, the discrepancy of Kronecker sequences is controlled by Diophantine properties of $\alpha$, typically expressed via continued fractions \cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974,IosifescuKraaikamp2002}. Badly approximable slopes (bounded continued-fraction coefficients) yield improved uniformity bounds at finite horizons. The golden ratio $\varphi$ is extremal in the sense that it is the \emph{most} badly approximable number: it maximizes the constant $c(\alpha)$ in inequalities of the form
\[
\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|\ge \frac{c(\alpha)}{q^2},
\]
over irrational $\alpha$.

More precisely, Hurwitz's theorem implies that for every irrational $\alpha$ there exist infinitely many rationals $p/q$ with
\[
\left|\alpha-\frac{p}{q}\right|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}\,q^2},
\]
and the constant $1/\sqrt{5}$ is best possible; the extremal case is attained on the golden branch (continued fraction $[1;1,1,\dots]$) and its $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ orbit \cite{HardyWright2008,IosifescuKraaikamp2002}.

Within the selection principle framework, the golden branch is therefore not a numerological artifact: it is a concrete scan choice that improves stability bounds for a wide class of kernels under finite resources.

\subsection{Wishes as stable period structures}
\label{sec:wishes_as_stable_periods}

Definition~\ref{def:wish} can now be read as a stability constraint: a wish is a period datum that admits realizations whose $\mathcal{J}_N$ cost remains small under bounded resources, and whose value is invariant under protocol equivalence. Under this perspective, ``motive'' provides a language for organizing relations among such stable period data, while selection is enforced at the protocol layer through finite-horizon stability and complexity.

\subsection{Dimension cost and an effective curse of dimensionality}
\label{sec:dimension_cost}

The selection principle becomes sharper when dimension is made explicit. For explicit low-discrepancy constructions in quasi-Monte Carlo integration, one has upper bounds of the form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:star_discrepancy_upper}
D_N^\ast \le C_d\,\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N},
\end{equation}
for some dimension-dependent constant $C_d$ for explicit low-discrepancy constructions \cite{Niederreiter1992,DickPillichshammer2010}. Combined with Koksma--Hlawka, this yields the generic finite-horizon estimate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:qmc_dimension_penalty}
\Big|\avgN{\mathcal{P}}-\mathrm{per}(\HSP(\mathcal{P}))\Big|
\le
\HKVar(f_{\mathcal{R}})\,C_d\,\frac{(\log N)^{d-1}}{N}.
\end{equation}
Thus, for fixed horizon $N$, the auditable bound deteriorates rapidly with dimension $d$; equivalently, achieving a target accuracy $\varepsilon$ requires a horizon satisfying
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:inverse_discrepancy_requirement}
N \ge \frac{\HKVar(f_{\mathcal{R}})\,C_d}{\varepsilon}\,(\log N)^{d-1}.
\end{equation}

In the present program, this motivates an explicit \emph{dimension penalty} in the selection functional \eqref{eq:selection_functional}: high-dimensional period data are ``expensive'' to stabilize under bounded resources, and low-dimensional periods (1D/2D/3D) are naturally favored as stable observables.

\begin{remark}[Lower bounds: the dimension penalty is not removable]
The deterioration with $d$ is not merely an artifact of a particular construction. For $d\ge 2$, Roth's theorem gives a general lower bound on star discrepancy: for any $N$-point set in $[0,1]^d$, one has
\[
D_N^\ast \ge c_d\,\frac{(\log N)^{(d-1)/2}}{N}
\]
for a positive constant $c_d$ depending only on $d$ \cite{Roth1954,Matousek1999}. In particular, in dimension $d=2$ one can strengthen the logarithmic exponent \cite{Schmidt1972}. These bounds support the interpretation that high-dimensional stable realizations are intrinsically more expensive under finite resources.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Robustness under target perturbations (rigidity via gap)}
\label{sec:robustness_gap}

Many ``selection'' statements are metrological in nature: one compares a finite candidate class against a target value known only up to an error bar. The following lemma isolates a purely quantitative rigidity mechanism: a large best-vs-second-best gap implies robustness under perturbations.

\begin{lemma}[Gap-stability of a unique minimizer]
\label{lem:gap_stability}
Let $V$ be a finite set of real candidates and let $T\in\RR$ be a target. Let $v_\star\in V$ be the unique minimizer of $|v-T|$, and let $v_2\in V$ be a minimizer among $V\setminus\{v_\star\}$. Define the margin
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:margin_def}
m:=|v_2-T|-|v_\star-T|>0.
\end{equation}
Then for every perturbed target $T'\in\RR$ with $|T'-T|<m/2$, the unique minimizer of $|v-T'|$ over $v\in V$ is still $v_\star$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For any $v\in V$, triangle inequality gives
\[
|v-T'|\ge |v-T|-|T'-T|,
\qquad
|v_\star-T'|\le |v_\star-T|+|T'-T|.
\]
Hence for $v\neq v_\star$,
\[
|v-T'|-|v_\star-T'|
\ge
\big(|v-T|-|v_\star-T|\big)-2|T'-T|
\ge m-2|T'-T|>0.
\]
\end{proof}
