\begin{conclusion}[Non-Archimedean conceptual geometry and completion identity induced by budget localization]\label{con:xi-budget-nonarchimedean-geometry-completion}
Let \(\mathbb{B}\) be a totally ordered set with a minimal element, equipped with the idempotent join semilattice operation
\(
B\vee B':=\max\{B,B'\}
\)
representing the composition law of budget thresholds.
Let \(B\mapsto\mathcal{G}_{\le B}\) be a family of localization groupoids that embed monotonically with budget: if \(B\le B'\) then there exists a faithful functor \(\iota_{B}^{B'}:\mathcal{G}_{\le B}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}_{\le B'}\), and the budget cost of any invertible certificate satisfies the maximum law (the composite cost equals the \(\vee\) of individual costs).
For objects \(x,y\) define
\[
d(x,y):=\min\{\,B\in\mathbb{B}:\ x\simeq y\ \text{in}\ \mathcal{G}_{\le B}\,\},
\qquad d(x,x):=\min\mathbb{B}.
\]
By convention, when the set is empty, \(d(x,y)=+\infty\).
Then \(d\) is a non-Archimedean quasi-metric: for all \(x,y,z\),
\[
d(x,z)\le d(x,y)\vee d(y,z).
\]
Moreover, the completion of the topology generated by \(d\)-balls \(B(x;B):=\{y:\ d(x,y)\le B\}\) is homeomorphic to the inverse limit space of isomorphism classes:
\[
\widehat{\mathrm{Ob}}(\mathcal{G})
:=\varprojlim_{B\in\mathbb{B}}\ \pi_0(\mathcal{G}_{\le B})
\ \cong\
\overline{\mathrm{Ob}}(\mathcal{G})\qquad(\text{\(d\)-completion}).
\]
Thus, under this assumption, connectivity growth is equivalent to refining the resolution of a non-Archimedean conceptual space; the limit objects are precisely the "concept family addressable at full budget" (consistent with the inverse limit calibration of Conclusion \ref{con:xi-connectivity-persistent-barcode}).
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Boolean spectrum of audit propositions and spectral representation of addressable concepts]\label{con:xi-audit-boolean-stone-spectrum}

\[
\mathrm{Stone}(\mathfrak{A})\ \cong\ \varprojlim_{B}\ \pi_0(\mathcal{G}_{\le B}).
\]
Moreover, each stable concept corresponds uniquely to a maximally consistent complete set of audit propositions (an ultrafilter).
This homeomorphism identifies "globally publishable assertions" as clopen sets in the Stone spectrum, thereby converting conceptual connectivity into a separable logical structure on a compact zero-dimensional spectral space.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Moduli space of semantics-preserving rewrite implementations characterized by second-order anomaly cohomology]\label{con:xi-implementation-moduli-h2-anom}
Under the condition of fixed generating \(1\)-morphisms and conservative semantic functor \(\mathsf{Sem}\), an \emph{implementation} is defined as: for each pair of composable \(1\)-morphisms \((u,v)\), a choice of auditable \(2\)-isomorphism
\(
\phi_{u,v}:u\circ v\Rightarrow uv
\)
satisfying unit constraints; deviations are recorded by the anomaly group \(\mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}\), so that differences arising from different bracketings/rewrite paths fall within the central coordinates of \(\mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}\).
Two implementations are called \emph{gauge equivalent} if there exists a family of \(2\)-isomorphisms (gauge transformations) that twist one into the other.

If the third-order associativity obstruction vanishes at budget \(B\) (i.e., an implementation exists; the \(\omega_B=0\) case of Conclusion \ref{con:xi-connectivity-third-order-strictification-obstruction}), then all implementation gauge equivalence classes are in natural bijection with the second-order anomaly cohomology group:
\[
{\textup{implementations}}/{\textup{gauge equivalence}}\ \cong\ H^2(\mathcal{G}_{\le B};\mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}).
\]
Thus, when the obstruction vanishes, the degrees of freedom in "increasing connectivity while preserving semantics" are completely controlled by a computable \(H^2\) parameter space; its non-triviality provides a strict criterion for the existence of multiple compatible implementations under the same semantic interface.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Canonical word uniqueness and geodesic cost under strictly convex audit energy]\label{con:xi-strict-convex-energy-canonical-word-geodesic}
Suppose the anomaly group \(\mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}\) contains a real vector space component \(V_B\), equipped with a strictly convex norm \(\|\cdot\|_B\).
For a fixed semantic morphism \(f\) (in \(\mathcal{G}_{\le B}\)), let \(\mathcal{R}_B(f)\subset V_B\) be the reachable residual set of all implementations of \(f\), and assume \(\mathcal{R}_B(f)\) is a non-empty closed affine subspace (guaranteed by residual externalization and concatenative completeness).
Then there exists a unique energy-minimal residual
\[
r^\star(f)=\arg\min_{r\in\mathcal{R}_B(f)}\ \|r\|_B,
\]
and there exists a canonical implementation \(w_f^\star\) (unique up to \(2\)-isomorphism) whose residual is \(r^\star(f)\).
If furthermore the composition satisfies Minkowski additivity
\(
\mathcal{R}_B(g\circ f)=\mathcal{R}_B(g)+\mathcal{R}_B(f)
\),
then canonical residuals satisfy
\[
\|r^\star(g\circ f)\|_B\le \|r^\star(g)\|_B+\|r^\star(f)\|_B,
\]
so that canonical implementations induce an additive geodesic cost, endowing conceptual connectivity paths with a unique canonical representative in the audit sense.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Gluing criterion for addressability presheaf: existence of global concepts decided by first-order cohomology obstruction]\label{con:xi-addressability-presheaf-gluing-h1-obstruction}

Thus, "non-globalizability due to insufficient connectivity" can be strictified as a computable first-order cohomology obstruction.
\end{conclusion}

\begin{conclusion}[Two-pullback decomposition under triple projection joint conservativity: register separability of connectivity and anomaly]\label{con:xi-three-projection-2pullback-register-separability}

\(
P_{\mathrm{prim}},P_{\mathrm{op}},P_{\mathrm{prime}}

  \item Joint conservativity: if all three projections are isomorphisms, then the original object is an isomorphism;

  \item Anomaly splitting: \(\mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}\cong \mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}^{\mathrm{prim}}\oplus \mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}^{\mathrm{op}}\oplus \mathrm{Anom}_{\le B}^{\mathrm{prime}}\).
\end{enumerate}
Then there exists a groupoid equivalence
\[
\mathcal{G}_{\le B}\ \simeq\
\mathcal{G}_{\le B}^{\mathrm{prim}}\times^{(2)}\mathcal{G}_{\le B}^{\mathrm{op}}\times^{(2)}\mathcal{G}_{\le B}^{\mathrm{prime}},
\]
where \(\times^{(2)}\) denotes the weak \(2\)-pullback in the bicategorical sense.

This decomposition provides a register separability principle: genuine new connectivity in the system must produce ineradicable structural changes in at least one register component.
\end{conclusion}
