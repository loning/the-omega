\section{The vacuum sector: ontology of protocol-unstable states}
\label{app:ghost_sector}

In the HPA scan--projection paradigm, a vacuum is not the absence of microstates but the absence of \emph{stable localized outputs} under a fixed finite-resolution protocol.
Two distinct mechanisms contribute: sub-geometric admissible types at $m<6$ that cannot lock a $3$D frame (Section~\ref{subsubsec:geometric_vacuum}), and protocol-unstable microstates that fail the admissibility grammar even at fixed $m$.
This appendix concerns the latter.

At window length $m$, the microstate alphabet is $\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m$ and the $\varphi$-stable sector $X_m\subset\Omega_m$ is defined by golden-mean admissibility (no consecutive ones).
The complement $\Omega_m\setminus X_m$ consists of binary strings that are mathematically valid as microscopic readout patterns yet physically unstable under the Zeckendorf admissibility grammar.
In the physical identification layer, we interpret this protocol-unstable complement (the \emph{ghost sector}) as a concrete model of vacuum microstructure: these microstates can transiently appear in the scan but are rejected by the readout protocol upon local checksum failure, behaving as virtual fluctuations rather than stable particle-like outputs.

At the anchor $m=6$, one has $|\Omega_6|=64$ and $|X_6|=21$, hence $|\Omega_6\setminus X_6|=64-21=43$.
This same hidden count also appears as folding redundancy in the many-to-one map $\mathrm{Fold}_6$ (Section~\ref{subsec:fold6_map}):
each stable type $w\in X_6$ has at least one representative input, and the remaining preimage elements account for $64-21=43$ additional microscopic degrees of freedom suppressed by projection.

\paragraph{A minimal instability witness.}
As a minimal instability witness on the full alphabet, define the adjacent-ones count
\[
N_{11}(w):=\#\{\,i\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}\ :\ w_i=w_{i+1}=1\,\}.
\]
Then $X_m=\{w\in\Omega_m:\ N_{11}(w)=0\}$ and the protocol-unstable complement (ghost sector) is $\Omega_m\setminus X_m$.

\paragraph{Distance to admissibility.}
As an additional diagnostic, we define a minimal repair cost $c(w)$ as the minimum number of bit flips $1\to 0$ required to remove all adjacent-ones violations.
Equivalently, if a word contains maximal runs of consecutive ones of lengths $L$, then $c(w)=\sum \lfloor L/2\rfloor$.
