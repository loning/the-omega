\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{$\Xi_\zeta$}{Xi\_zeta} Single-Point Jet to the Verblunsky--CMV Reflection Spectrum: Scalarization of the Toeplitz Certificate and LMI Lifting}\label{subsubsec:xi-verblunsky-cmv-from-local-jet}
This section further scalarizes the "positivity of the horizon logarithmic derivative \(\Leftrightarrow\) Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy" (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}) into a sequence of reflection coefficients (Verblunsky/Schur parameters), and emphasizes its \textbf{single-point local closure}: at each finite level, the reflection coefficients and the corresponding CMV (pentadiagonal) objects can be explicitly generated from the finite-order logarithmic derivative data \(\{\ell_k\}\) of \(\Xi_\zeta\) at \(s=-\tfrac12\) (Proposition \ref{prop:app-horizon-coeff-local}).
This yields an auditable "inverse spectral interface":
\[
\boxed{\;
(\ell_k)_{k\ge 1}\ \Longrightarrow\ (c_n)_{n\ge 0}\ \Longrightarrow\ (\alpha_n)_{n\ge 0}
\ \Longrightarrow\
\text{CMV (unitary) reflection spectral object}\;}
\]
and reduces the failure localization of the Toeplitz hierarchy from "existence of a negative eigenvalue of a matrix" to the single-scalar event of "the smallest reflection coefficient exceeding its bound."

\paragraph{From Carath\'eodory to Schur: Definition of the Canonical Reflection Spectrum}
Let \(\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}\) be the horizon Carath\'eodory logarithmic derivative function (Appendix equation \eqref{eq:app-horizon-carath-def}), and write its power series
\[
\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}(w)=c_0+2\sum_{n\ge 1}c_n w^n,
\qquad c_{-n}:=\overline{c_n}.
\]
When RH holds, \(\mathscr{C}_\zeta\) belongs to the Carath\'eodory class with \(c_0=\Re\mathscr{C}_\zeta(0)=\mu_\zeta(\TT)>0\) (Corollary \ref{cor:app-horizon-carath-rh}; see also Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-spectral-measure-atomic} for the measure interpretation).
Furthermore, by self-dual symmetry one may adopt the real structure \(c_{-n}=c_n\in\RR\) (Corollary \ref{cor:app-horizon-ramanujan-selfdual-measure}), so that the Toeplitz principal submatrix \(\mathbf{T}_N=(c_{j-k})_{0\le j,k\le N}\) is a real symmetric matrix.
\par
To compress the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy into a scalar recursion, let
\[
F(w):=\frac{\mathscr{C}_{\zeta}(w)}{c_0},
\qquad F(0)=1.
\]
and define the corresponding Schur function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-schur-from-carath}
\boxed{\;
S(w):=\frac{1}{w}\frac{F(w)-1}{F(w)+1}\; }.
\end{equation}
When \(F\) is Carath\'eodory, \(S\) is a Schur class function (\(|S(w)|\le 1\) on \(\mathbb{D}\); see \cite{Simon2005OPUC1}).
Therefore there exists a unique Verblunsky sequence \((\alpha_n)_{n\ge 0}\subset\overline{\mathbb{D}}\) for which the Schur recursion holds:
\[
S_0=S,\qquad \alpha_n:=S_n(0),\qquad
S_{n+1}(w):=\frac{1}{w}\frac{S_n(w)-\alpha_n}{1-\overline{\alpha_n}\,S_n(w)}.
\]
Under RH, from \(\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0\) for all \(N\) (Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-toeplitz-lmi}), it follows that \(|\alpha_n|\le 1\) (see Theorem \ref{thm:xi-toeplitz-det-verblunsky} below), so that \((\alpha_n)\) constitutes the canonical "reflection spectrum" within the framework.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical Reflection Spectrum of \texorpdfstring{$\Xi_\zeta$}{Xi\_zeta}: Finite-Order Closed Forms for \texorpdfstring{$\ell$}{ell}\,--\,\texorpdfstring{$c$}{c}\,--\,\texorpdfstring{$\alpha$}{alpha}]\label{thm:xi-verblunsky-from-local-jet}
Under the above notation, suppose \(c_0>0\) and let \(S\) be defined by \eqref{eq:xi-schur-from-carath}.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \(\alpha_0=S(0)\) and \(\alpha_1=S_1(0)\) are given in strict closed form by \((c_0,c_1,c_2)\):
  \[
  \boxed{\;
  \alpha_0=\frac{c_1}{c_0},\qquad
  \alpha_1=\frac{c_0c_2-c_1^2}{c_0^2-c_1^2}\;}
  \]
  (under the real structure \(c_1\in\RR\); for the general complex structure, the denominator should be written as \(c_0^2-|c_1|^2\)).
  \item If one further uses the single-point jet
  \(
  \ell_k=\left.\frac{\dd^k}{\dd s^k}\log\Xi_\zeta(s)\right|_{s=-1/2}
  \)
  to represent the local data, then by Proposition \ref{prop:app-horizon-coeff-local} with \(c_0=-\ell_1,c_1=-\ell_2,c_2=\ell_2-\ell_3\), one obtains
  \[
  \boxed{\;
  \alpha_0=\frac{c_1}{c_0}=\frac{\ell_2}{\ell_1},\qquad
  \alpha_1=\frac{-\ell_1\ell_2+\ell_1\ell_3-\ell_2^2}{\ell_1^2-\ell_2^2}\;}
  \]
  \item More generally, for each \(n\ge 0\), \(\alpha_n\) is a rational function of \((c_0,\dots,c_{n+1})\), and hence also a rational function of \((\ell_1,\dots,\ell_{n+2})\); therefore the reflection spectrum at any finite level is completely generated by single-point local data.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From \(F(0)=1\), the quotient \(\frac{F(w)-1}{F(w)+1}\) vanishes to first order at \(w=0\), so \eqref{eq:xi-schur-from-carath} defines an analytic \(S\).
Substituting \(F(w)=1+2(c_1/c_0)w+2(c_2/c_0)w^2+O(w^3)\) and performing power series division yields
\[
S(w)=\frac{c_1}{c_0}+\left(\frac{c_0c_2-c_1^2}{c_0^2-c_1^2}\right)w+O(w^2),
\]
hence \(\alpha_0=S(0)=c_1/c_0\).
Applying the Schur recursion
\(
S_1(w)=\frac{1}{w}\frac{S(w)-\alpha_0}{1-\overline{\alpha_0}\,S(w)}
\)
and taking the limit \(w\to 0\) yields the closed form for \(\alpha_1=S_1(0)\); under the real structure \(\overline{\alpha_0}=\alpha_0\), this gives the stated expression.
The second item follows by substitution from Proposition \ref{prop:app-horizon-coeff-local}.
The third item follows from the Schur algorithm: at each step, \(S_{n+1}\) is obtained from \(S_n\) via a M\"obius transformation and division by \(w\), so its value at \(w=0\), namely \(\alpha_{n+1}=S_{n+1}(0)\), depends only on finitely many Taylor coefficients of \(S_n\);
the \(n\)-th Taylor coefficients of \(S\) depend only on the first \(n{+}1\) coefficients of \(F\), and hence only on \((c_0,\dots,c_{n+1})\) (together with the conjugate symmetry under the real structure).
Finally, by Proposition \ref{prop:app-horizon-coeff-local}, each \(c_k\) is rationally generated from finitely many \(\ell\)-jet data, so \(\alpha_n\) is likewise.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Reflection Product Decomposition of the Toeplitz--PSD Hierarchy and Minimal Failure Step Localization}
The reflection coefficients not only provide a scalarized parametrization of "positivity" but also yield a product decomposition of Toeplitz determinants, thereby localizing the finite-dimensional falsifier to the minimal exceeding step.

\begin{theorem}[Reflection Product Decomposition of Toeplitz Determinants and the Minimal Failure Index]\label{thm:xi-toeplitz-det-verblunsky}
Following the above notation, let \(\mathbf{T}_N=(c_{j-k})_{0\le j,k\le N}\).
If \(\mathbf{T}_N\) is positive definite, then there exist unique \(\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{N-1}\in\mathbb{D}\) (the first \(N\) terms of the Verblunsky sequence) such that for all \(1\le m\le N\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-toeplitz-det-product}
\boxed{\;
\det \mathbf{T}_m
=c_0^{m+1}\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}\bigl(1-|\alpha_k|^2\bigr)^{\,m-k}\; }.
\end{equation}
Therefore \(\det \mathbf{T}_m>0\) if and only if \(|\alpha_k|<1\) for all \(0\le k\le m-1\).
Conversely, if there exists a minimal \(m_\star\ge 1\) such that \(\mathbf{T}_{m_\star}\not\succeq 0\), then the Schur recursion is strictly feasible for the first \(m_\star-1\) steps, and at the minimal failure step one must have
\[
|\alpha_{m_\star-1}|\ge 1,
\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad
\det \mathbf{T}_{m_\star}\le 0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Equation \eqref{eq:xi-toeplitz-det-product} is a standard corollary of the OPUC/Schur algorithm and the Levinson--Durbin factorization: the Cholesky decomposition of \(\mathbf{T}_m\) can be constructed recursively via the reflection coefficients, with each step introducing the factor \(1-|\alpha_k|^2\) (see \cite[Ch.~1--2]{Simon2005OPUC1} or the Carath\'eodory--Fej\'er theory of truncated moment problems).
This yields \(\det \mathbf{T}_m>0 \Longleftrightarrow |\alpha_k|<1\) (\(k<m\)).
For the reverse localization: if \(\mathbf{T}_{m_\star}\not\succeq 0\) and \(m_\star\) is minimal, then for all \(m<m_\star\), \(\mathbf{T}_m\succeq 0\) and (under \(c_0>0\)) \(\mathbf{T}_m\succ 0\), so the Schur recursion is feasible for the first \(m_\star-1\) steps, producing \(\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{m_\star-2}\in\mathbb{D}\).
If one still had \(|\alpha_{m_\star-1}|<1\), then by \eqref{eq:xi-toeplitz-det-product}, \(\det\mathbf{T}_{m_\star}>0\), contradicting \(\mathbf{T}_{m_\star}\not\succeq 0\); hence \(|\alpha_{m_\star-1}|\ge 1\), yielding \(\det\mathbf{T}_{m_\star}\le 0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}["Horizon Principal Minor" Consistency: Log-Concavity of the Toeplitz Determinant Sequence and Reflection Parameter Bounds]\label{cor:xi-toeplitz-principal-minor-logconcavity}
Following the notation of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-toeplitz-det-verblunsky}, let \(\Delta_N:=\det\mathbf{T}_N\) (\(N\ge 0\)).
When \(\mathbf{T}_N\succ 0\) for all \(N\), the determinant sequence \((\Delta_N)_{N\ge 0}\) satisfies the explicit ratio identity
\[
\boxed{\;
\frac{\Delta_{N+1}\Delta_{N-1}}{\Delta_N^{2}}=1-|\alpha_N|^2\qquad(N\ge 1),\;}
\]
and hence \((\Delta_N)\) is a log-concave sequence:
\[
\boxed{\;
\Delta_N^2\ \ge\ \Delta_{N-1}\Delta_{N+1}\qquad(N\ge 1).\;}
\]
In particular, \(\Delta_{N+1}\Delta_{N-1}/\Delta_N^2\in(0,1]\) is equivalent to \(|\alpha_N|\le 1\);
therefore in the nondegenerate case (\(\Delta_N>0\)), \(|\alpha_N|\le 1\) (for all \(N\)) can be viewed as a set of purely scalar consistency checks for the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy:
it compresses "all-level positivity" into "log-concavity of principal minors (cross-level consistency) + determinant positivity (intra-level feasibility)" as two types of monotone constraints.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
From \eqref{eq:xi-toeplitz-det-product}, direct computation gives
\[
\frac{\Delta_{N+1}}{\Delta_N}
=c_0\prod_{k=0}^{N}\bigl(1-|\alpha_k|^2\bigr),
\qquad
\frac{\Delta_{N}}{\Delta_{N-1}}
=c_0\prod_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigl(1-|\alpha_k|^2\bigr),
\]
and dividing yields \(\Delta_{N+1}\Delta_{N-1}/\Delta_N^2=1-|\alpha_N|^2\).
Log-concavity follows immediately from \(1-|\alpha_N|^2\le 1\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Compatibility with the \texorpdfstring{$\ZZ/2$}{Z/2} Sector Failure Localization]\label{rem:xi-verblunsky-z2-compatible}
Appendix Corollary \ref{cor:app-horizon-toeplitz-z2-cert-split} provides the even/odd (\(\ZZ/2\)) certificate splitting of the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy under the real structure, and can localize the minimal failure pair \((N_\star,\epsilon)\).
The reflection coefficient localization of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-toeplitz-det-verblunsky} provides a finer recursive coordinate: it compresses the same failure witness to the minimal index \(m_\star\) and the single-scalar event \(|\alpha_{m_\star-1}|\ge 1\).
The two are essentially of the same origin: both \((N_\star,\epsilon)\) and the \(\alpha\)-prefix are auditably generated from the same truncated moment data \((c_0,\dots,c_{N_\star})\) (and hence from finite-order \(\ell\)-jet data), but the latter provides higher-resolution failure step localization.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Schur--Cohn LMI Lifting of the Toeplitz Certificate}
In the main text, the Schur--Cohn Hermitian form matrix \(H(p)\) has been introduced for finite-dimensional channel polynomials, with positive semidefiniteness serving as an auditable LMI certificate for root distribution within the unit disk (Remark \ref{rem:xi-schur-cohn-sdp-interface}).
The following result lifts the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy itself into the same \(H(p)\) syntax, thereby unifying the two certificate interfaces.

\begin{theorem}[Schur--Cohn Lifting of the Carath\'eodory--Toeplitz Certificate]\label{thm:xi-toeplitz-schur-cohn-lift}
For each \(N\ge 0\), the truncated moment data \((c_0,\dots,c_N)\) (equivalently, the single-point jet \((\ell_1,\dots,\ell_{N+1})\), Proposition \ref{prop:app-horizon-coeff-local}) uniquely determines a segment of reflection coefficients
\(
\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{N-1}
\)
(which lie in \(\mathbb{D}\) when \(\mathbf{T}_N\succ 0\), Theorem \ref{thm:xi-toeplitz-det-verblunsky}).
Let \(p_N\) be the monic Szeg\H{o} polynomial generated by this segment of reflection coefficients (i.e., the \(N\)-th level prediction polynomial of the Schur recursion; see \cite{Simon2005OPUC1}), of degree \(N{+}1\).
Then there exists an invertible matrix \(R_N\) such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-toeplitz-schur-cohn-congruence}
\boxed{\;
R_N^\ast\,\mathbf{T}_N\,R_N
\;=\;
H(p_N)\;}
\end{equation}
where \(H(\cdot)\) is the Schur--Cohn Hermitian form matrix defined in Remark \ref{rem:xi-schur-cohn-sdp-interface}.
Therefore
\[
\mathbf{T}_N\succeq 0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
H(p_N)\succeq 0,
\]
and when \(\mathbf{T}_N\) fails at the minimal level \(N_\star\), the negative inertia index of \(H(p_{N_\star})\) provides a same-order algebraic failure witness.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
This result is a standard equivalence between the Carath\'eodory--Fej\'er truncated moment problem and the Schur algorithm: feasibility of the truncated moments \((c_0,\dots,c_N)\) is equivalent to the existence of Schur parameters \(\alpha_0,\dots,\alpha_{N-1}\) satisfying \(|\alpha_k|\le 1\), from which one can construct the \(N\)-th level prediction polynomial \(p_N\).
Equation \eqref{eq:xi-toeplitz-schur-cohn-congruence} can be viewed as the representation of the same quadratic form between the Schur--Cohn form of this prediction polynomial and the Toeplitz matrix form under two natural bases; the transformation matrix \(R_N\) can be taken as the triangular factor produced by the Levinson--Durbin recursion (see \cite{DymYoung1990SchurCohnMatrixPolynomials,Simon2005OPUC1} for a survey of the relationship between Schur--Cohn and Schur parameters/prediction polynomials).
The inertia index is invariant under congruence transformation, so failure witnesses transfer losslessly between \(\mathbf{T}_N\) and \(H(p_N)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Alignment with the Finite-Type CMV/Schur Interface]\label{rem:xi-verblunsky-cmv-align-finite}
Proposition \ref{prop:xi-hilbert-polya-cmv} in the finite-window/finite-dimensional framework translates the "unit circle root condition" into a CMV-like unitary spectral realization, with \(|\alpha_k|<1\) as the auditable interface.
The construction of this section provides the infinite-dimensional/limiting version: when RH holds, the reflection spectrum \((\alpha_n)\subset\mathbb{D}\) of \(\mathscr{C}_\zeta\) generates a canonical CMV operator whose spectral measure is precisely the horizon positive measure \(\mu_\zeta\) (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-horizon-spectral-measure-atomic}).
Therefore, "finite-level certificates of the Toeplitz--PSD hierarchy" can be equivalently understood as "prefix constraints on the CMV reflection spectrum," and their failure can be precisely localized by the minimal exceeding reflection coefficient \(|\alpha_{m_\star-1}|\ge 1\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-toeplitz-det-verblunsky}).
\end{remark}
