\subsubsection{Two-dimensional slow-mode fingerprint of \texorpdfstring{$\rho_{m,2}$}{rho(m,2)} and the second scale for the order-projection gate}\label{subsec:rho-m2-2d-fingerprint}
This subsection extends the slowest convergence ratio
\(\rho_{m,2}/\lambda\) of the \(((m,2))\) series from Appendix \ref{app:real-input-40-arity-2d} beyond a single numerical characterization to a mechanistically meaningful metric: it structurally encodes the competition information between two orthogonal slowing channels,
thereby serving as the second scale of the order-projection gate (congruence stratification) at the spectral gap level (the first scale being the leading scale \(\lambda=\rho(M)\)).

\paragraph{Two slowing channels: input skeleton vs.\ sign/boundary residual}
On the real input \(40\)-state kernel, the \(\zeta\)-determinant admits a structural factorization
\[
\det(I-zM)=(1-z)^2(1+z)\cdot \det\!\bigl(I-z(A\otimes A)\bigr)\cdot \det(I+zA)
\]
(Proposition \ref{prop:pom-zeta-factorization-40}), thereby splitting the nonzero spectrum into three blocks:
\[
\sigma(M)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(A\otimes A)\ \cup\ \sigma(-A)\ \cup\ \{1,1,-1\}.
\]
Here \(A\otimes A\) corresponds to the input skeleton (the tensor skeleton of the two golden-mean inputs), while \(-A\) corresponds to the sign-flip/boundary residual channel (Remark \ref{rem:pom-minusA-factor-critical-line}).

\paragraph{Definition: two-dimensional slow-mode fingerprint}
Fix \(m\ge 2\) and consider the two-dimensional Dirichlet twist family \(M(q,r)\) (\(q^\chi r^\nu\) weighted) from Appendix \ref{app:real-input-40-arity-2d}, and write
\[
\rho_{m,2}:=\max_{(j_1,j_2)\neq(0,0)}\rho\!\left(M(\omega_m^{j_1},(-1)^{j_2})\right),
\qquad \omega_m=e^{2\pi i/m}.
\]
To decompose the ``slowest convergence'' into two auditable channels, we introduce the following two-dimensional fingerprint:
\begin{definition}[Two-dimensional slow-mode fingerprint]\label{def:rho-m2-fingerprint}
Define the \textbf{input channel slow mode} and the \textbf{residual channel slow mode} of \(((m,2))\) as
\[
\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}:=\max_{1\le j\le m-1}\rho\!\left(M(\omega_m^{j},1)\right),
\qquad
\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}:=\rho\!\left(M(1,-1)\right),
\]
and define the two-dimensional slow-mode fingerprint as
\[
\boxed{\ \mathbf{R}_{m,2}:=\left(\frac{\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}}{\lambda},\ \frac{\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}}{\lambda}\right)\in(0,1)^2\ }.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Slow-mode anisotropy index (log-ratio of channel competition)]\label{def:rho-m2-anisotropy-index}
Define the \textbf{slow-mode anisotropy index} of \(((m,2))\) as
\[
\boxed{\;
A_m
:=\log\frac{\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}}{\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}}
=\log\frac{\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}/\lambda}{\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}/\lambda}\; }.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Prime-like vs.\ trace channel attribution (measurable classifier)]\label{rem:rho-m2-anisotropy-classifier}
The index \(A_m\) compresses ``which side the worst slowing comes from'' into a measurable scalar:
\begin{itemize}
  \item If \(A_m\gg 0\), then \(\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}\) is significantly larger than \(\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}\), and the worst slowing is dominated by the \textbf{input skeleton} (corresponding to prime-like/input-side bottleneck);
  \item If \(A_m\ll 0\), then the worst slowing is dominated by the \textbf{boundary residual} (corresponding to trace/boundary anomaly and projection residual bottleneck);
  \item If \(A_m\) exhibits systematic drift with \(m\) or undergoes a sign flip along the scale flow, then the two orthogonal channels alternate in dominance at different scales: the so-called dominance switching between ``prime-like side vs.\ trace side'' can be precisely defined within the framework of this paper as the scale running of \(A_m\).
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Relation to \texorpdfstring{$\rho_{m,2}/\lambda$}{rho/lambda}: two-channel competition law]\label{rem:rho-m2-competition-law}
\(\rho_{m,2}\) is the maximum spectral radius over all nontrivial twist channels, while \(\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2},\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}\) are the axial maxima from ``twisting only the input side/twisting only the sign side'' respectively.
Hence \(\mathbf{R}_{m,2}\) compresses the source of slowest convergence into a two-dimensional fingerprint: when
\[
\max_{1\le j\le m-1}\rho\!\left(M(\omega_m^{j},-1)\right)\ \le\ \max\{\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2},\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}\},
\]
it necessarily follows that
\[
\boxed{\ \frac{\rho_{m,2}}{\lambda}=\max\left\{\frac{\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}}{\lambda},\ \frac{\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}}{\lambda}\right\}\ }.
\]
This allows the ``slowest convergence ratio'' to be interpreted as a selection law taking the maximum between two orthogonal channels; moreover, this selection law is structurally compatible with the spectral factorization of Proposition \ref{prop:pom-zeta-factorization-40}: the input skeleton and the sign-flip channel each contribute a class of slow-mode candidates.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{As the second scale of the order-projection gate}
In the Chebotarev-type estimates for congruence stratification, the error exponent is controlled by \(\rho/\lambda\) (the two-dimensional error bound from Appendix \ref{app:real-input-40-arity-2d}, together with the spectral gap--mixing time scale framework earlier in this section).
Definition \ref{def:rho-m2-fingerprint} upgrades this to a two-dimensional scale:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Input-side slowing (channel A)}: \(\rho^{\mathrm{in}}_{m,2}/\lambda\) measures the worst spectral gap brought by ``character-twisting the input skeleton'';
  \item \textbf{Boundary residual slowing (channel B)}: \(\rho^{\mathrm{bd}}_{m,2}/\lambda\) measures the slowing intensity of the ``sign-flip/boundary residual'' channel;
  \item \textbf{Competition law}: \(\rho_{m,2}/\lambda\) is determined by the competition between the two (Remark \ref{rem:rho-m2-competition-law}).
\end{itemize}
Hence \(\mathbf{R}_{m,2}\) serves as the second scale of the order-projection gate: it does not alter the leading scale \(\lambda\) (exponential growth rate) but directly determines the mixing scale and worst-channel source of the error decay constant for ``congruence classes tending toward equidistribution''.
