\section{Six-Dimensional Superspace Periodic Ontology}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Superspace and $(3+3)$ Decomposition}{Superspace and (3+3) Decomposition}}

Let $E^{6}$ be six-dimensional Euclidean space with decomposition
\[
E^{6}=E_{\parallel}^{3}\oplus E_{\perp}^{3},
\]
where $E_{\parallel}^{3}$ is the physical subspace and $E_{\perp}^{3}$ is the internal subspace. Let $\ZZ^{6}\subset E^{6}$ be the hypercubic lattice, and denote the projections by $P_{\parallel},P_{\perp}$.

Let $\rho_s:E^{6}\to\RR_{\ge 0}$ be a $\ZZ^{6}$-periodic density. The superspace entry provides the embedding form of quasiperiodic functions and the restriction relation $\rho(r)=\rho_s(r,0)$, and indicates that $\rho_s$ is a lattice-periodic function whose symmetry group is an $n$-dimensional space group \cite{IUCRSuperspace}.

\subsection{Acceptance Domain and Cut-and-Project}

Given an acceptance domain (window) $W\subset E_{\perp}^{3}$ and internal phase $u\in E_{\perp}^{3}$, define the point set
\[
\Lambda(W,u)=\{P_{\parallel}n:\ n\in\ZZ^{6},\ P_{\perp}n\in W+u\}\subset E_{\parallel}^{3}.
\]
The acceptance domain entry provides the equivalence of the intersection method and cut-and-project method, and explains via the ``pipe'' formalism that only lattice points falling within the pipe are projected to physical subspace \cite{IUCRAcceptanceDomain}.

To accommodate multi-path/distinguishability interfaces, we allow the window to have a finite branch decomposition:
\[
W=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{J} W_j,\qquad W_i\cap W_j=\varnothing\ (i\neq j),
\]
where each $W_j\subset E_{\perp}^{3}$ is a measurable sub-window.

The atomic surface entry further provides: for point atoms or tiling vertices, the high-dimensional structure consists of periodically arranged objects of dimension $(n-m)$, and these objects transversely intersect physical space to generate physical points; the dimension of the atomic surface equals the dimension of the internal space \cite{IUCRAtomicSurface}.
Within the model class of rank $n=6$ and physical dimension $m=3$, the dimension of internal space is $3$.

\subsection{Typical Examples in Six-Dimensional Description}

The icosahedral quasicrystal structure model explicitly states: the structure can be described both as a three-dimensional quasiperiodic density function and as a six-dimensional periodic density function; the actual structure is obtained from a specific three-dimensional cross-section of the six-dimensional density function \cite{PRB43_929}.

Another six-dimensional hypercubic model related to the golden parameter gives: the atom distribution within the six-dimensional unit cell lies along $15$ face diagonals and exhibits a partition ratio of $\tau=(1+\sqrt5)/2$; rational $(1/1)$ strip projection yields the periodic phase, and irrational $(\tau/1)$ strip projection yields the icosahedral quasicrystal phase \cite{PRB46_6091}.
At the realization level, this work uses this comparison to connect the geometric structure of ``rational approximation-to-irrational limit''.

\subsection{Fiberized Extension of Internal Degrees of Freedom (No Dimension Increase)}
\begin{definition}[Fiberized Extension of State Space]\label{def:sigma-geo-times-V}
Let the geometric state space be $\Sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}$ (for example, the realization space parametrized by cut-and-project and internal phase). Introduce the internal fiber
$$
\mathcal{V}\cong \CC^{3},
$$
and consider a subset of the extended state space
$$
\Sigma\subseteq \Sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}\times \mathcal{V}.
$$
\end{definition}\begin{definition}[Gauge Equivalence Classes and Readout Factorization]\label{def:gauge-quotient-readout}
Let $G=\mathrm{SU}(3)$ act on $\mathcal{V}$ (fundamental representation), thereby inducing an action on $\Sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}\times\mathcal{V}$. Stipulate that the readout protocol depends only on gauge equivalence classes: there exists a factorized readout
$$
(\Sigma_{\mathrm{geo}}\times\mathcal{V})/G \ \longrightarrow\ X_6^{\mathrm Z},
$$
i.e., any observable output is a function of $G$-invariant information.
\end{definition}\begin{remark}
The above formulation does not introduce an observability assumption of ``color labels'': readout only retains gauge-invariant information; remaining differences reside in the fiber direction or their invariants. The geometric foundation is still borne by rank-$6$ superspace closed objects, and gauge degrees of freedom are not resolved by increasing the superspace rank.
\end{remark}
