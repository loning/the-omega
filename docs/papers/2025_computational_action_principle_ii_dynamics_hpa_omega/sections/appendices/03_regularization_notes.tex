\section{Regularization notes: finite parts and counterterms}
\label{app:regularization_notes}

When orbit sums or traces diverge in regulated-to-continuum passages, CAP adopts a canonical finite-part prescription.
The present manuscript uses this only at the level of model specification: the choice of finite part is part of the effective action via renormalization conditions.

\subsection{Abel regularization and finite part}
\label{app:abel_finite_part}

Given a divergent series $\sum_{n\ge 0} a_n$, define its Abel generating function
\begin{equation}
  A(r) := \sum_{n\ge 0} a_n r^n,
  \qquad 0<r<1.
\end{equation}
If $A(r)$ admits an asymptotic expansion as $r\uparrow 1$ of the form
\begin{equation}
  A(r) = \frac{c_{-m}}{(1-r)^m} + \cdots + \frac{c_{-1}}{1-r} + c_0 + o(1),
\end{equation}
we define the Abel finite part by $\FP\,\sum_{n\ge 0} a_n := c_0$.

\subsection{Scheme dependence}
\label{app:scheme_dependence}

Different prescriptions for extracting $c_0$ correspond, at the level of an effective action, to different local counterterm choices.
Therefore CAP-II treats the regularization scheme as part of the model specification (Assumption~\ref{ass:canonical_regularization}) and isolates it from the operational definition of $\kappa$.

\subsection{A concrete counterterm correspondence (one worked interface)}
\label{app:finite_part_counterterm_example}

This subsection records a minimal calculation showing how a finite-part choice translates into a local counterterm in the CAP-II effective action.

\paragraph{Constant finite parts shift the cosmological term.}
Suppose a regulated protocol-derived scalar contribution to the effective Lagrangian density takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(r)
  = A(r),
  \qquad 0<r<1,
\end{equation}
with an Abel expansion as $r\uparrow 1$:
\begin{equation}
  A(r)=\frac{c_{-1}}{1-r}+c_0+o(1).
\end{equation}
Under the Abel finite-part rule, the renormalized contribution is $\Delta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ren}}:=c_0$.
If instead one chooses a different finite part $c_0\mapsto c_0+\delta c_0$, then the action shifts by
\begin{equation}
  \delta S = \int \dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\delta c_0,
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to a shift of the cosmological term in \eqref{eq:omega_action_cap2}:
\begin{equation}
  \Lambda \ \mapsto\ \Lambda - 8\pi G\,\delta c_0.
\end{equation}
Thus, at the level of local covariant dynamics, scheme dependence of constant finite parts is exactly the usual cosmological-constant counterterm freedom.

\paragraph{Density-proportional finite parts shift the potential.}
Similarly, if a regulated contribution takes the form
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{reg}}(r)
  = B(r)\,\chi^2,
  \qquad
  B(r)=\frac{b_{-1}}{1-r}+b_0+o(1),
\end{equation}
then a finite-part change $b_0\mapsto b_0+\delta b_0$ shifts the action by
\begin{equation}
  \delta S=\int \dd^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,(\delta b_0)\,\chi^2,
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to adding a local counterterm to the scalar potential:
\begin{equation}
  V(\chi^2)\ \mapsto\ V(\chi^2)-(\delta b_0)\,\chi^2.
\end{equation}
More generally, finite-part choices for protocol-level traces can renormalize the coefficients of any local invariant allowed in the chosen effective-theory class; CAP-II fixes a canonical prescription (Assumption~\ref{ass:canonical_regularization}) and interprets any residual finite ambiguity as an explicit renormalization condition on $(\Lambda,V)$.
