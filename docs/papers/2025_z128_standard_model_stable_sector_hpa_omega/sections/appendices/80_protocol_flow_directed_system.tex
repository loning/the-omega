\section{Protocol flow as a directed system (resolution uplift and coarse graining)}
\label{app:protocol_flow_directed_system}

This appendix upgrades the narrative notion of ``dynamic resolution'' into a compact mathematical object.
The goal is to make the composability of resolution updates explicit, without importing any new physical inputs.
We work in the tick\,+\,CAP discipline: the finite objects are primary, and any continuum/QFT language is used only as an interface dictionary.

\subsection{Resolution parameters and refinement maps}

\paragraph{Resolution index set.}
Let $\mathcal{I}\subseteq \NN\times \NN$ be the set of admissible protocol resolutions $(m,n)$ used by the manuscript:
$m$ is the window length (finite readout resolution) and $n$ is the Hilbert addressing order (spatial readout scale).
In many audited constructions the paper restricts to a chosen subfamily (e.g.\ a balanced chain), but this appendix keeps the index set abstract.

\paragraph{Window refinement by prefix projection.}
For integers $m'\ge m$, the paper already fixes a deterministic truncation map on stable words (prefix projection)
\[
\pi_{m'\to m}:X_{m'}\to X_m,
\qquad
\pi_{m'\to m}(w_1\cdots w_{m'}) := w_1\cdots w_m,
\]
specialized to $m=6$ in Appendix~\ref{app:functorial_refinement}.
The key structural point is that these maps form a directed inverse system.

\begin{lemma}[Functoriality of prefix projections]\label{lem:prefix_projection_functoriality}
\MathTag For all integers $m''\ge m'\ge m\ge 1$ one has
\[
\pi_{m''\to m}=\pi_{m'\to m}\circ \pi_{m''\to m'}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Both sides drop the last $m''-m$ bits of a length-$m''$ word, hence agree.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Resolution preorder.}
Define a preorder on $\mathcal{I}$ by declaring
$(m,n)\preceq (m',n')$ if $m\le m'$ and $n\le n'$.
This preorder captures the intended refinement direction: larger parameters correspond to finer readout resolution and/or finer spatial addressing.

\subsection{Observable families and refinement transport}

The paper uses multiple finite observable families at each resolution: stable-type counts, degeneracy histograms, holonomy distributions, coarse-grained scalar summaries, and derived effective parameters (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_flow_step}).
To avoid over-specifying, we record a minimal abstract package that covers all such examples.

\begin{definition}[Finite observable space at resolution]\label{def:protocol_observable_space}
\MathTag Fix $(m,n)\in\mathcal{I}$.
Let $\mathcal{O}_{m,n}$ denote a chosen real vector space of protocol observables at resolution $(m,n)$.
Concretely, elements of $\mathcal{O}_{m,n}$ are functions of the finite readout data at $(m,n)$ (for example, functions on $X_m$, or functions of spatially indexed $X_m$-labels on a Hilbert-addressed grid at order $n$).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Refinement pullback along window uplift]\label{def:window_uplift_pullback}
\MathTag For $m'\ge m$ and fixed $n$, define the pullback operator
\[
\pi_{m'\to m}^\ast:\ \mathcal{O}_{m,n}\to \mathcal{O}_{m',n}
\]
whenever the observable family is compatible with prefix projection, by the rule
\[
(\pi_{m'\to m}^\ast q)(\text{data at }(m',n)) := q(\text{data at }(m,n)\ \text{obtained by applying }\pi_{m'\to m}\text{ pointwise}).
\]
In words: an observable at window length $m$ is evaluated on an $m'$-resolution configuration by first truncating each local stable label to its length-$m$ prefix.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Pullback functoriality]\label{lem:window_pullback_functoriality}
\MathTag If $(\pi_{m'\to m}^\ast)$ is defined on a compatible observable family, then for all $m''\ge m'\ge m$ one has
\[
\pi_{m''\to m}^\ast=\pi_{m''\to m'}^\ast\circ \pi_{m'\to m}^\ast.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is immediate from Lemma~\ref{lem:prefix_projection_functoriality} and the definition of pullback.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Coarse graining as a morphism family}

Spatial coarse graining is treated in the manuscript as a declared deterministic map $\mathsf{C}$ (block averaging, kernel readout, etc.; Definition~\ref{def:protocol_flow_step} and Section~\ref{sec:kernel_view}).
For theorem-level bookkeeping, it is useful to record the minimal algebraic structure needed for composable multi-step flows.

\begin{definition}[Coarse-graining operators]\label{def:coarse_graining_operators}
\MathTag For each comparable pair $(m,n)\preceq(m',n')$ in $\mathcal{I}$, let
\[
\mathsf{C}_{(m',n')\to(m,n)}:\ \mathcal{O}_{m',n'}\to \mathcal{O}_{m,n}
\]
be a deterministic coarse-graining operator (for example: block averaging from a Hilbert grid of order $n'$ to order $n$, optionally together with histogramming or other fixed summarization rules).
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Composable coarse graining]\label{ass:coarse_graining_composable}
\AuditTag For all $(m,n)\preceq(m',n')\preceq(m'',n'')$ in $\mathcal{I}$, assume
\[
\mathsf{C}_{(m'',n'')\to(m,n)}=
\mathsf{C}_{(m',n')\to(m,n)}\circ \mathsf{C}_{(m'',n'')\to(m',n')}.
\]
This is a consistency requirement on the declared coarse-graining rules.
When a bounded counterfactual family of coarse-graining choices is audited, this assumption is checked within the declared family rather than imposed globally.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{The directed-system view of protocol flow}

\paragraph{Two complementary viewpoints.}
There are two equivalent ways to package the protocol flow algebraically:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(V1) Refinement as an inverse system.}
The prefix maps $\pi_{m'\to m}$ define an inverse system of finite objects in $m$, and observables transport contravariantly by pullback $\pi^\ast$.
\item \textbf{(V2) Flow step as an endomorphism generator.}
Given a chosen coarse-graining rule $\mathsf{C}$, a single protocol step can be written as
\[
\mathcal{R}_{(m,n)\to(m',n')}:=
\mathsf{C}_{(m',n')\to(m',n')}\circ \pi_{m'\to m}^\ast,
\]
matching the kernel-view notation in Section~\ref{sec:kernel_view} (where $\mathcal{R}$ is introduced as a bookkeeping symbol for an iterable step operator).
\end{itemize}

\begin{proposition}[Protocol flow as a directed system of observable spaces]\label{prop:protocol_flow_directed_system}
\MathTag Fix an admissible index set $\mathcal{I}$ and a compatible observable family $\{\mathcal{O}_{m,n}\}_{(m,n)\in\mathcal{I}}$.
Assume window-uplift pullbacks $\pi_{m'\to m}^\ast$ are defined and functorial (Lemma~\ref{lem:window_pullback_functoriality}), and assume coarse-graining operators $\mathsf{C}$ are composable (Assumption~\ref{ass:coarse_graining_composable}).
Then the collection $\{\mathcal{O}_{m,n}\}$ together with the morphisms generated by $\pi^\ast$ and $\mathsf{C}$ forms a directed system in the sense that multi-step protocol flows can be composed unambiguously and reduced to a single morphism between endpoints.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Functoriality of $\pi^\ast$ gives unambiguous composition along window-uplift chains.
Composable coarse graining gives unambiguous composition along addressing/coarse-graining chains.
Combining the two yields an unambiguous composite for any finite zig-zag that refines by pullback and then summarizes by coarse graining, provided the intermediate objects lie in $\mathcal{I}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Audit-facing failure points.}
\AuditTag The theorem-level content above is deliberately minimal: it only records that, once one declares (i) a refinement transport rule and (ii) a composable coarse-graining rule, then multi-step resolution updates are mathematically well-posed.
Failure modes are therefore explicit and checkable:
(a) an observable family may be incompatible with pointwise prefix truncation (so $\pi^\ast$ is undefined),
or (b) a coarse-graining family may fail the composability check, in which case the flow depends on the chosen intermediate staging.
In both cases the remedy is to (re)declare the observable family and coarse-graining operators as part of the audited protocol pack, and to record bounded sensitivity within that declared family.
