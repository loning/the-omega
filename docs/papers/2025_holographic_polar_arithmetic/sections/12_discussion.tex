\section{Discussion: meaning, boundaries, and testable intermediate claims}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Two notions of minimality}
The classical picture realizes a Cartesian minimality in which addition and multiplication are primitive and orthogonal. HPA aims at a polar minimality:

\begin{itemize}
    \item radial/phase separation $(\rho,\theta_\times)$ linearizes multiplicative structure;
    \item scan time is supplied by a genuine unitary $\Theta$ and is decoupled from multiplicative phase;
    \item binary window readout together with canonical numeration (Zeckendorf in the golden branch) yields a computable, locally constrained coordinate system.
\end{itemize}

In this sense, ``minimal complexity'' refers to the shortest generative description: one unitary generator, one binary window, one canonical encoding.

\subsection{Conceptual framework: rotation, factorization, projection}
\label{subsec:conceptual_framework}
The technical construction in the preceding sections can be read through a compact conceptual triad: rotation supplies the conservative ontology, factorization supplies discrete generators, and projection supplies observable bookkeeping.

\paragraph{Rotation (the smooth unitary circle).}
At the most primitive level, HPA treats norm-preserving rotation as fundamental and translation as derived.
In the minimal scan model, a single unitary $\Theta$ iterates an irrational rotation on $S^1$ (Section~\ref{sec:theta}), so the scan spectrum lies on $U(1)$ and time is the iteration count.
In the internal octonionic extension, states are coordinatized on a unit sphere $S^7$ via a norm-preserving lift (Definition~\ref{def:octonions} and Proposition~\ref{prop:norm_consistency}), preserving the same guiding principle: dynamics live on a compact ``great-circle'' geometry, and linear time is a low-dimensional readout of a rotation-driven process.
In this sense, the Hilbert-space formalism is not an additional layer of mystery but the natural container for conservative rotations and their projections.

\paragraph{Factorization (rigid discrete generation).}
Prime factorization is the intrinsic discrete decomposition of the skeleton $M$ (Section~\ref{sec:skeleton}).
Lifting primes into an internal algebra via $\mathcal{L}$ (Definition~\ref{def:lift_map}) turns this decomposition into internal composition data; non-associativity then makes the \emph{history} of composition (bracketing) a genuine degree of freedom measured by the associator $\mathcal{A}$ (Definition~\ref{def:associator}).
This provides a concrete sense in which ``dimension'' and ``structure'' are generated by decomposition and composition rules rather than being postulated as independent coordinates.

\paragraph{Projection (observation as a rigid cut).}
Observable arithmetic arises only after a projection/readout interface: continuous synthesis in $\CC$ is coerced back to a discrete lattice image $\mathcal{Z}(M)$ (Section~\ref{sec:interference}).
Theorem~\ref{thm:no_go_additive} makes this cut unavoidable: a nontrivial multiplicative embedding cannot simultaneously preserve linear addition, so any additive bookkeeping must incur a residual gap.
Within this viewpoint, $\delta$ is not merely a numerical error but a geometric necessity of forcing a smooth rotational ontology into discrete coordinates; probabilistic uncertainty arises as the statistics of this cut (Definition~\ref{def:projection_kernel}).
Geometrically, the difference between arc-based transport and chord-based shortcuts (Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_transport_duality}) is the same distinction: boundary readout follows the surface, while bulk access exploits the interior geometry.

\paragraph{Phase as an oriented area (bivector viewpoint).}
In the complex embedding, multiplication by $\e^{\iu\theta}$ is literally a rotation in a plane.
In geometric algebra, plane rotations are generated by unit bivectors, so ``phase'' can be viewed as an oriented-area generator of a rotor rather than as a purely scalar label \cite{DoranLasenby2003}.
We do not develop this formalism here, but the rotor viewpoint aligns naturally with HPA's emphasis on rotation as ontology and with the appearance of spin-like internal degrees of freedom in noncommutative scan algebras.

\subsection{Orthogonality as the price of readout}
\label{subsec:orthogonality_price_of_readout}
The monolith contains all information, but a readout must produce \emph{distinctions}; orthogonality is the geometric extremum of distinguishability.

\paragraph{Distinguishability and orthogonal channels.}
In a Hilbert-space description, inner products quantify overlap: states that are orthogonal have zero overlap and can be perfectly distinguished by a projective measurement in an appropriate orthonormal basis.
Non-orthogonal axes, by contrast, inevitably mix information: attempting to read out one coordinate necessarily carries components of the other, producing cross-talk rather than independent distinctions.
In this sense, an orthogonal decomposition is not merely convenient; it realizes the extremal limit of perfect distinction (zero overlap) and thus provides the natural grammar for maximally separated readout channels.

\paragraph{The cost: residual structure and incompatible cuts.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:no_go_additive} shows that in HPA one cannot choose a single embedding that is simultaneously multiplicative and exactly additive, so any additive bookkeeping requires a projection/cut (Section~\ref{sec:interference}).
Geometrically, forcing a smooth rotational object (a unitary circle/sphere) into orthogonal discrete coordinates creates an unavoidable residual: the quantum gap $\delta$ measures the mismatch between the continuous projection and the discrete lattice representative (Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_holographic_slice} and Remark~\ref{rem:observation_cut}).
Moreover, orthogonality comes with a second rigidity: different desirable readouts correspond to different slices, and incompatible slices cannot be realized simultaneously. This is the structural content of Weyl-pair uncertainty in the scan algebra (Theorem~\ref{thm:weyl_uncertainty}).
Thus, within the rotation--projection viewpoint, uncertainty is the price of insisting on maximal distinction, and the gap is the geometric remainder left behind by that insistence.

\subsection{Core prediction: emergence of addition}
HPA predicts that addition is not a fundamental primitive of the object domain. At the $\mathbb{F}_1$ ontological layer only multiplicative structure is intrinsic, while additive bookkeeping arises only after imposing a readout: synthesized vectors in $\CC$ must be projected back to a discrete coordinate lattice, and that projection is necessarily lossy (Section~\ref{sec:interference}). In this view, the linear conservation laws and quantum probabilistic uncertainties of low-energy physics are effective statements about projected statistics rather than exact microscopic identities. Within this projection viewpoint, probability need not be taken as fundamental indeterminism; it can be understood as the inevitable shadow of higher-dimensional geometric structure (beyond the octonionic division-algebraic boundary) when encoded on a one-dimensional linear time/readout axis. A concrete consequence is that at sufficiently high resolution one should expect additive bookkeeping to be replaced by a more refined holographic conservation principle in which conservation is expressed through the geometry of $\mathcal{Z}$, the scan dynamics of $\Theta$, and the statistics of projection gaps.

\subsection{Octonionic internal phase: a non-abelian extension module}
The basic embedding $\mathcal{Z}:M\to\CC^*$ supplies an intrinsically abelian phase space: $S^1$ supports only a $U(1)$ degree of freedom.
If one aims to couple HPA to a richer internal sector with non-abelian symmetry, a minimal arithmetic extension is to lift the prime data into a normed division algebra with a large automorphism group.
The octonions $\OO$ are the terminal Cayley--Dickson division algebra and satisfy $\mathrm{Aut}(\OO)=\Gtwo$.
Section~\ref{sec:embedding} introduced an octonionic lift $\mathcal{L}$ and the induced embedding $\mathcal{Z}_{\OO}$ (Definition~\ref{def:Z_O_embedding}); the failure of associativity is measured by the associator $\mathcal{A}$ (Definition~\ref{def:associator}).

\paragraph{The limit of division-algebraic coordinatization.}
We use $\OO$ not merely for exceptional symmetry, but because the octonions are terminal among finite-dimensional normed division algebras: beyond dimension $8$, one cannot retain a division-algebraic structure with a multiplicative norm \cite{Baez2002}. In this sense, the octonionic sector marks a natural boundary for a finite-dimensional ``computable law'' written purely in algebraic coordinates. The Hilbert-space layer then remains as an ambient container in which higher structure can be represented and in which observables can appear through statistical projections.

\begin{remark}[The causal boundary of algebra]
The restriction to the octonions ($\OO$) is not arbitrary but structural.
In the Cayley--Dickson construction, the next doubling step (dimension $16$, the sedenions $\mathbb{S}$) loses the division property and admits \emph{zero divisors}: nonzero elements $x,y$ such that $xy=0$ \cite{Baez2002,ConwaySmith2003}.
From the viewpoint of HPA as a scan-and-compose ontology, zero divisors obstruct cancellation and reversibility of composition: distinct nontrivial factors can collapse to a null product, so the resulting relation cannot be inverted within a finite-dimensional algebraic coordinatization.
Thus higher Cayley--Dickson extensions may increase ambient geometric capacity, but they sacrifice the algebraic connectivity required to interpret interactions as robust causal relations rather than irreversible sinks of distinguishability.
In this sense, the octonionic sector provides a natural ``causal boundary'' for algebraic description: it is terminal among normed division algebras while still supporting non-associative protocol dependence.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Mass--associator equivalence (ansatz)]
\label{assump:mass_associator}
There exists an interpretation layer which maps local three-factor internal compositions to effective mass/coupling scales such that, for $x,y,z\in\OO$ representing internal factors at an interaction vertex,
$$
m(x,y,z)\ \propto\ \|\mathcal{A}(x,y,z)\|.
$$
In particular, if $x,y,z$ lie in a common associative subalgebra of $\OO$, then $\mathcal{A}(x,y,z)=0$ and this contribution vanishes at the level of the ansatz.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Ansatz: Generations via Fano Associativity Classes]

Fix a Fano-plane convention for the imaginary units $(e_1,\dots,e_7)$.
Given three internal factors (e.g.\ normalized prime lifts) one may classify the triple by its incidence type in the Fano plane: collinear triples lie in a quaternionic subalgebra (hence have vanishing associator), while non-collinear triples can yield nonzero associator.
We propose that a small number of robust incidence classes provides a combinatorial organizing label for stable interaction/vertex types in an octonionic internal sector.
\end{assumption}

\subsection{Why ``three'': the exceptional Jordan algebra \texorpdfstring{$J_3(\OO)$}{J3(O)} and triality}
\label{subsec:j3o_generations}
The preceding octonionic extension treats internal degrees of freedom as non-associative phases or factors in $\OO$.
There is, however, a stronger rigidity statement available at the level of quantum kinematics: if one insists that \emph{observables} form a formally real Jordan algebra and that octonions are admitted as coordinates, then the exceptional algebra $J_3(\OO)$ is singled out and the number $3$ becomes structural rather than a choice.

\subsubsection{Jordan kinematics and the Jordan--von Neumann--Wigner constraint}
\begin{definition}[Jordan algebra and formal reality]
\label{def:jordan_formally_real}
A (real) Jordan algebra is a real vector space $J$ equipped with a bilinear product $A\circ B$ which is commutative and satisfies the Jordan identity
$$
A^2\circ(A\circ B)=A\circ(A^2\circ B),\qquad A^2:=A\circ A.
$$
It is \emph{formally real} if
$
\sum_{i=1}^k A_i^2=0
$
implies $A_i=0$ for all $i$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Jordan--von Neumann--Wigner classification (finite-dimensional, formally real)]
\label{thm:jnww}
Every finite-dimensional formally real Jordan algebra is a direct sum of simple ideals, each isomorphic to one of the following \cite{JordanVonNeumannWigner1934}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item Hermitian matrices $H_n(\RR)$, $H_n(\CC)$, or $H_n(\mathbb{H})$ with product $A\circ B=\tfrac12(AB+BA)$;
    \item a spin factor;
    \item the exceptional Albert algebra $J_3(\OO)=H_3(\OO)$.
\end{itemize}
Moreover, the octonionic Hermitian construction yields a Jordan algebra only in the $3\times 3$ case \cite{Albert1934}.
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{The Albert algebra and the Cayley projective plane}
\begin{definition}[The Albert algebra \texorpdfstring{$J_3(\OO)$}{J3(O)}]
\label{def:albert}
Let $J_3(\OO)$ denote the space of $3\times 3$ Hermitian matrices over $\OO$,
equipped with the Jordan product $A\circ B:=\tfrac12(AB+BA)$, where $AB$ denotes matrix multiplication using octonion multiplication in entries.
An element can be written as
$$
J=\begin{pmatrix}
c_1 & x_3 & \bar{x}_2 \\
\bar{x}_3 & c_2 & x_1 \\
x_2 & \bar{x}_1 & c_3
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad c_i\in\RR,\ x_i\in\OO.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[States as a projective plane]
In Jordan-algebraic quantum mechanics, observables are elements of a formally real Jordan algebra and states are positive normalized linear functionals.
In the exceptional case, rank-one idempotents of $J_3(\OO)$ can be identified with points of the Cayley (octonionic) projective plane $\OO\mathrm{P}^2$; see, e.g., \cite{Baez2002,ConwaySmith2003}.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Three octonionic slots and triality}
The matrix form in Definition~\ref{def:albert} exhibits three distinguished octonionic slots. Denote them by $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$.
This ``threefold'' structure is not an arbitrary duplication: it is forced by Theorem~\ref{thm:jnww} and is stabilized by exceptional symmetry.
The automorphism group of $J_3(\OO)$ is the exceptional Lie group $F_4$; after fixing a diagonal Jordan frame (a choice of three orthogonal primitive idempotents), one obtains a $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ action on the off-diagonal slots and an $S_3$ permutation symmetry.
This $S_3$ outer symmetry is unique to dimension $8$: the Dynkin diagram of $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ is of type $D_4$ and admits an $S_3$ outer automorphism.
The resulting $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$ representation-theoretic content is governed by triality: the three $8$-dimensional irreducible representations $(8_v,8_s,8_c)$ are permuted by an outer automorphism, and the three octonionic slots transform accordingly \cite{Baez2002,GunaydinGursey1973}.

\begin{assumption}[Ansatz: three fermion families as the \texorpdfstring{$J_3(\OO)$}{J3(O)} off-diagonal sectors]
\label{assump:generations_j3o}
Assume the internal observable algebra at a site admits a Jordan description with an exceptional sector modeled on $J_3(\OO)$.
Then the three octonionic slots $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ provide three a priori equivalent internal sectors.
We propose to identify these sectors with the three fermion families, with approximate family replication arising from triality symmetry and family splitting arising from a triality-breaking selection of additional structure (lift map, readout, or vacuum data).
\end{assumption}

\subsubsection{Cubic norm and a symmetric three-sector coupling}
The exceptional algebra $J_3(\OO)$ carries a canonical cubic form (often denoted $\det$) whose invariance under the structure group is one of its defining features.
For the parametrization in Definition~\ref{def:albert} one may write \cite{Albert1934,ConwaySmith2003}
$$
\det(J)=c_1c_2c_3-\big(c_1 N(x_1)+c_2 N(x_2)+c_3 N(x_3)\big)+2\,\mathrm{Re}\big((x_1x_2)x_3\big),
$$
where $N(x)=x\bar{x}$ and $\mathrm{Re}(\cdot)$ denotes the real part in $\OO$.
This cubic term couples all three octonionic slots in a triality-symmetric way.
In an HPA setting, one can view the ``interpretation layer'' (choice of lift and projection/readout conventions) as the mechanism by which triality symmetry is reduced to an effective low-energy pattern, potentially correlating family splitting with associator-based tension as in Assumption~\ref{assump:mass_associator}.

\subsection{The geometric decomposition of the fine structure constant \texorpdfstring{$\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$}{alpha}}
\label{subsec:alpha_geo_decomposition}
This paper uses $\alpha$ to denote the scan rotation slope in Section~\ref{sec:theta}.
In this subsection, $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ denotes the electromagnetic fine structure constant.
The goal is to formulate a minimal geometric ansatz in which a dimensionless coupling is controlled by internal octonionic geometry.

\subsubsection{Coupling as geometric impedance}
In standard field theory the dimensionless coupling $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}$ controls interaction strengths via gauge-boson exchange.
In the HPA viewpoint one may invert the perspective and treat the inverse coupling $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}$ as an effective geometric ``impedance'': a measure of how much internal phase-space a $U(1)$ flux must coherently explore to produce a stable interaction channel.

\begin{assumption}[Series impedance postulate (geometric impedance)]
\label{assump:alpha_geo_impedance}
We postulate that the readout/projection interface decomposes the relevant geometric constraints into a small number of topologically distinct sectors (bulk, boundary, line) which behave as independent readout channels. Because a gauge flux must satisfy these constraints as a sequential hierarchy of cuts rather than by selecting among parallel alternatives, the resulting phase delays accumulate additively, effectively acting as a series impedance circuit. Concretely, assume that in an octonionic internal sector the effective inverse coupling admits an additive decomposition into a small number of geometric channels,
$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1} = V_{\mathrm{bulk}} + V_{\mathrm{boundary}} + V_{\mathrm{line}},
$$
where the terms represent, respectively, a bulk non-associative phase volume, a holographic boundary/projection impedance, and a minimal worldline/scan contribution.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Orthogonal cut and an \texorpdfstring{$L^1$}{L1} cost (interpretive viewpoint)]
In the orthogonality-as-readout viewpoint (Section~\ref{sec:interference}, Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_holographic_slice}, and Subsection~\ref{subsec:orthogonality_price_of_readout}), distinguishable channels are obtained by cutting a conservative monolith into topologically distinct sectors.
In such a sequential readout picture, the quantity $\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}$ is not a metric length and need not combine by a Pythagorean ($L^2$) rule. It is an accumulated process cost (phase delay/impedance) across independent cuts.
Accordingly, one may interpret the additive decomposition in Assumption~\ref{assump:alpha_geo_impedance} as an $L^1$-type accumulation:
$$
\vec{V}:=(V_{\mathrm{bulk}},V_{\mathrm{boundary}},V_{\mathrm{line}})=(4\pi^3,\pi^2,\pi),
\qquad
\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=\|\vec{V}\|_1=V_{\mathrm{bulk}}+V_{\mathrm{boundary}}+V_{\mathrm{line}}.
$$
The role of orthogonality here is conceptual rather than metrical: it is the requirement that the three channels be topologically distinguishable in readout, so their phase delays can be booked independently and summed.
Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_impedance_decomposition} provides a schematic visualization of this sequential (stepwise) accumulation contrasted with a metric-style diagonal.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{A minimal three-term model}
Fix a projective phase-length unit $L:=\pi$, motivated by the projective identification of quantum states and the use of unitary phases modulo $2\pi$.
Within the ansatz, choose:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Bulk term.} Model non-associative phase leakage by a three-cycle reservoir, represented by a $3$-torus $T^3$ of scale $L$. Include a minimal discrete multiplicity factor $4$ (e.g.\ a quaternionic symmetry normalization), giving
    $$
    V_{\mathrm{bulk}} := 4\,L^3 = 4\pi^3.
    $$
    \item \textbf{Boundary term.} Model holographic/projection encoding by a disk channel of effective area scale $\pi^2$,
    $$
    V_{\mathrm{boundary}} := \pi^2.
    $$
    \item \textbf{Line term.} Model the irreducible $U(1)$ scan/worldline contribution by the projective semi-period,
    $$
    V_{\mathrm{line}} := \pi.
    $$
\end{itemize}
This yields the geometric prediction
$$
\alpha_{\mathrm{geo}}^{-1}=4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi.
$$

\subsubsection{Numerical value and comparison}
Numerically,
$$
4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi \approx 137.0363.
$$
For comparison, the experimentally inferred inverse fine structure constant at low energy is
$
\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}\approx 137.035999
$
(CODATA 2018; see \cite{CODATA2018}).
The relative discrepancy is at the level of a few parts in $10^6$, suggesting that radiative running and/or finite-lattice effects could plausibly account for the difference within an interpretation-layer completion.

\begin{remark}[Critical analysis of the ansatz]
The agreement above should be read as a numerical signal, not a theorem.
Two modeling choices are doing the work:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Additivity.} The linear sum treats bulk/boundary/line as sequential readout costs (an $L^1$-type accumulation). Alternative coupling rules (e.g.\ parallel/product combinations) would produce different values.
    \item \textbf{Scale selection.} The choice $L=\pi$ is a projective normalization; other normalizations would rescale all terms.
\end{itemize}
Nevertheless, the fact that a single geometric scale simultaneously fixes the bulk, boundary, and line contributions to the observed $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}$ to several digits is a strong internal-consistency constraint on any model that adopts this geometric-impedance interpretation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Physical Interpretation: Gravitational Delay and Kinematic Advance}

\paragraph{The Microscopic Origin of Mass-Induced Delay.}
Assumption~\ref{assump:mass_associator} introduces an interpretation layer in which mass/coupling scales arise from ``algebraic tension'' induced by non-associativity in an octonionic internal sector. In this picture, non-associative defects can be modeled as \emph{phase impedance} centers for the unitary scan: resolving a local algebraic obstruction requires additional scan cycles, increasing the local scan density. Macroscopically, such accumulation is naturally read as a dilation of the effective metric---what General Relativity describes as gravitational ``stretching'' (Shapiro delay).

\paragraph{Dimensional locking and the ``vertical leg'' viewpoint (interpretive).}
The ansatz $m\propto\|\mathcal{A}\|$ suggests a concrete geometric reading: associator tension can be viewed as internal ``winding'' that stores energy in degrees of freedom which do not contribute to boundary transport.
In relativistic kinematics, the decomposition $E^2=P^2+m^2$ can be read as a Pythagorean split between the spatial momentum component and an orthogonal component that remains as rest mass.
Within this interpretation layer, mass is \emph{curled dimensional momentum}: an orthogonal budget of scan action trapped in internal winding rather than expressed as boundary transport (cf.\ Assumption~\ref{assump:mass_associator}).
HPA does not derive this identity from first principles, but the analogy is structurally aligned with the scan picture: non-associativity induces an additional internal cycle budget, effectively diverting part of the scan action into an internal rotational/spiral mode and reducing the effective propagation speed along the boundary.
In this sense, mass is the physical manifestation of a logical (algebraic) obstruction: where associativity holds, internal winding vanishes and transport is ``straight''; where it fails, the scan coils and the boundary readout experiences delay.

\paragraph{Geometric Duality of Mass-Energy.}
Motivated by mass--energy equivalence ($E=mc^2$), we propose that spacetime geometry is governed by a competition between two opposing tendencies:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Gravitational Stretching (Mass Regime):} Algebraic defects (which, in the boundary picture of Remark~\ref{rem:prime_traps_inward_dive}, can be modeled as ``prime traps'') increase the effective path length (processing impedance), yielding a Shapiro-type \textbf{gravitational delay}.
    \item \textbf{Kinematic Compression (High-Energy Regime):} High kinematic momentum drives the system into the chord-based transport regime. In the holographic geometry, this acts as a topological ``shortcut'' through the bulk, producing a \textbf{kinematic advance}. Concretely, the discrete-scan dispersion relation (Proposition~\ref{prop:holographic_dispersion}) yields an energy-dependent group-velocity signature via differentiation (Subsubsection~\ref{subsubsec:dispersion_group_velocity}).

\paragraph{The ``Inward'' Shortcut.}
Crucially, this superluminal dispersion does not imply an escape \emph{from} the geometry, but an escape \emph{into} it. While low-energy states are confined to the linear arc (the ``surface''), high-energy states probe the non-commutative bulk via the chord. The standard speed of light $c$ thus emerges as the equilibrium baseline where gravitational stretching and kinematic compression are negligible.
Throughout, the ``advance'' refers to the energy-dependent group velocity inferred from the effective dispersion law and should not be conflated with signal/front-velocity constraints in a local microscopic completion; see Remark~\ref{rem:causality_defense}.

\subsection{Ontological determinism and algebraic agency}
\label{subsec:algebraic_agency}
This subsection is an interpretive outlook motivated by the separation between (i) a static ontological layer (multiplicative skeleton and internal embeddings) and (ii) a dynamical readout layer (unitary scan and projection).
It offers a compatibilist reading in which ``determinism'' concerns the content of the state, while ``agency'' is realized as a procedural degree of freedom in how non-associative composition histories are accessed.

\paragraph{Static structure (determinism of content).}
At the ontological level, HPA fixes rigid algebraic data: the multiplicative skeleton $M$, the embedding geometry $\mathcal{Z}$, and (in an octonionic extension) the lifted internal factors together with their norms.
Given a choice of lift map $\mathcal{L}$ and a specification of the readout/projection conventions, the resulting constraints (e.g.\ multiplicativity and the lattice mismatch) are structural and do not depend on observation.

\paragraph{Dynamic scan (time as access).}
What evolves is not the ontological content itself, but the access pattern supplied by the unitary scan $\Theta$ and the projection/readout interface.
In this sense, ``time'' is tied to a controlled sampling procedure (iteration count and readout), and different operational protocols correspond to different observed histories even when the underlying algebraic data are fixed.

\paragraph{Algorithmic laziness and projective instantiation.}
The separation between unitary scan and discrete readout admits an interpretation in which the scan supplies a conservative ``evaluation'' order, while the readout supplies instantiation.
In particular, the projection step does not merely reveal a pre-existing additive history; it selects a discrete representative of a continuous synthesis (Remark~\ref{rem:observation_cut}).
One may therefore read delayed-choice phenomena as reflecting \emph{algorithmic laziness}: $\Theta$ provides an access order (Remark~\ref{rem:scan_access_order}), while before a readout convention is applied the model retains unitary coherence among alternative coarse-grainings, and the ``history'' exists only as an uninstantiated computation on the higher-dimensional rotational state space \cite{WheelerZurek1983}.

\paragraph{Entanglement as rigidity of global constraints.}
In standard quantum theory, entanglement is a constraint on a joint state in a tensor-product Hilbert space rather than a signal exchanged between subsystems.
In the present rotation--projection viewpoint, this can be read geometrically: a single unit-norm state encodes correlations, and a local readout is a cut of one object rather than a message sent across space.
Developing this rigorously within HPA requires a concrete multi-system extension (tensorized scans, coupled readouts, and a specification of admissible interaction complexes), but the guiding intuition is that ``nonlocal'' correlations arise from global normalization and shared algebraic constraints, while locality bounds constrain signal/front propagation (Remark~\ref{rem:causality_defense}).

\paragraph{Non-associativity as protocol dependence.}
In an associative setting, the grouping of interaction/composition events is immaterial. In an octonionic sector, however, bracketing becomes a genuine degree of freedom: for internal factors $x,y,z\in\OO$ one can have
$$
(xy)z\ne x(yz),
$$
and the discrepancy is measured by the associator $\mathcal{A}(x,y,z)$ (Definition~\ref{def:associator}).
The paper fixes a canonical left-normed convention for prime products (Definition~\ref{def:Pi_L}), but alternative bracketings represent distinct composition histories which are invisible in associative reductions.
When combined with an interpretation layer that ties physical scales to associator tension (Assumption~\ref{assump:mass_associator}), this protocol dependence provides a concrete algebraic locus for ``agency'': the inputs may be fixed, while the operational bracketing (i.e.\ the synthesis path through the non-associative sector) can lead to inequivalent effective outcomes in the readout layer.

The present HPA framework thus provides the \textbf{microscopic ontology} for the geometric time delays and scattering thermodynamics observed in the phenomenological limit.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Readout--encoding consistency.} Quantify the stability of Sturmian factors and Zeckendorf/Ostrowski encodings under window perturbations.
    \item \textbf{Error bounds for finite parts.} For function classes specified by regularity or Fourier decay, derive explicit bounds on the error terms in $\FP_\Theta$.
    \item \textbf{Error bounds for orbit Newton remainders.} Identify function classes and coarse-graining regimes in which the orbit-Newton remainder $R_m(n;f)$ (Definition~\ref{def:orbit_newton_remainder}) decays rapidly and yields controlled ``continuum'' expansions.
    \item \textbf{Statistics of projection gaps.} For specified phase weights $\beta(p)$ and sampling models on $(a,b)$, study the distribution of the quantum gap $\delta$ and its dependence on the projection scale $\sigma$.
    \item \textbf{Cohomology of gap fields.} For a chosen interaction complex $\mathcal{K}$ (Definition~\ref{def:hpa_interaction_complex}), study the decomposition of a gap field into exact and non-exact components and characterize $H^1(\mathcal{K};A)$ as the obstruction to global cancellation (Definition~\ref{def:h1_gap}).
    \item \textbf{Spectral meaning of multiplicative phase choices.} Study how different prime weights $\beta(p)$ shape the distribution of $\mathcal{Z}(M)$ in $\CC^*$ and how this interfaces with spectral statistics.
    \item \textbf{Statistics of associator tension.} For a chosen lift $\mathcal{L}$ and prime sampling model, study the distribution of
    $
    \|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{L}(p),\mathcal{L}(q),\mathcal{L}(r))\|
    $
    and identify $\Gtwo$-invariant summaries.
    \item \textbf{Correlation with readout/projection error.} Probe whether associator-based tension statistics correlate with the quantum gap $\delta$ under a fixed projection kernel and scale.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
    \item The projection step is intentionally left as an application-layer choice: different physical or statistical models correspond to different kernels, scales, and tie-breaking conventions (Definitions~\ref{def:nearest_point}--\ref{def:projection_kernel}).
    \item Octonionic lifts and bracketing conventions introduce additional structure: the lift map $\mathcal{L}$ (Definition~\ref{def:lift_map}) and product convention (Definition~\ref{def:Pi_L}) are not unique, and physically meaningful observables should therefore be formulated in $\Gtwo$-invariant terms or in statistics which are stable under admissible choices.
    \item Many ``unification'' statements should be understood as programmatic. In particular, multi-scale refinements of the readout (refining partitions/windows and enlarging the admissible interaction complexes) naturally generate a hierarchy of effective descriptions; whether different force sectors can be organized as curvature/projection phenomena at different layers of such a hierarchy (a ``fractal onion'' viewpoint) remains an open problem.
    \item Orbit trace/finite-part mechanisms provide a uniform template, but application to concrete models requires specifying weights and actions compatible with the chosen readout and projection scheme.
    \item The present construction keeps $\mathcal{Z}$ and $\Theta$ minimally separated; stronger couplings may exist but may sacrifice minimality or require additional axioms.
\end{itemize}
