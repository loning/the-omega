\begin{conclusion}[Closed form for the zero-weight fiber: \texorpdfstring{$d_m(0^m)=\lfloor (m+2)/2\rfloor$}{d_m(0^m)=floor((m+2)/2)}]\label{con:xi-terminal-zero-weight-fiber-closed}
Let \(0^m\in X_m\) denote the all-zero stable type.
Then its fiber size satisfies the exact formula for all \(m\ge 1\)
\[
d_m(0^m)=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{m+2}{2}\Bigr\rfloor.
\]
Hence for any integer \(q\ge 1\)
\[
Z_m^{(q)}(0)=d_m(0^m)^q=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{m+2}{2}\Bigr\rfloor^q,
\]
yielding a completely solvable subsector of the weight stratification at the \(k=0\) layer; the growth of this layer is polynomial rather than exponential, corresponding to a strict "boundary zero-entropy" extreme phase.
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of Fold (see the Zeckendorf normal form calibration in script \texttt{scripts/common\_phi\_fold.py}),
\(\Fold_m(a)=0^m\) if and only if the Zeckendorf digits of the microstate value \(N(a)\) are all \(0\) at positions \(1,\dots,m\);
equivalently, \(N(a)\in\{0,F_{m+2}\}\).
Here \(N(a)=0\) corresponds only to the all-zero microstate, contributing \(1\).
On the other hand, let \(r_m\) be the number of \(0/1\) subset representations of \(F_{m+2}\) using the weight set \(\{F_2,\dots,F_{m+1}\}\).
Since \(\sum_{i=2}^{m}F_i=F_{m+2}-1\), any representation must include \(F_{m+1}\), hence \(r_m\) equals the representation count of \(F_m\) using \(\{F_2,\dots,F_m\}\).
For the latter, apply the "whether to use the largest term" decomposition: using \(F_m\) yields one representation; otherwise, since \(\sum_{i=2}^{m-2}F_i=F_m-1\), the representation must include \(F_{m-1}\), leaving a remainder of \(F_{m-2}\), hence the representation count satisfies the recurrence \(r_m=1+r_{m-2}\) (\(m\ge 3\)), with \(r_1=0,r_2=1\).
Solving gives \(r_m=\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), therefore
\(
d_m(0^m)=1+r_m=\lfloor (m+2)/2\rfloor
\).
Substituting into \(Z_m^{(q)}(0)\) yields the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Classification of stable words in the maximum-weight layer and closed fiber spectrum: \texorpdfstring{$|x|_1=\lceil m/2\rceil$}{|x|=ceil(m/2)}]\label{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-layer-fiber-spectrum-closed}
Define the maximum-weight layer
\[
X_m^{\max}:=\Bigl\{x\in X_m:\ |x|_1=\Bigl\lceil\frac{m}{2}\Bigr\rceil\Bigr\}.
\]
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(m=2k+1\) is odd, then \(X_{2k+1}^{\max}=\{(10)^k1\}\), and
  \[
  d_{2k+1}\bigl((10)^k1\bigr)=1.
  \]
  \item If \(m=2k\) is even, then \(|X_{2k}^{\max}|=k+1\), and there exists an explicit parametrization
  \[
  x^{(r)}:=(10)^r(01)^{k-r}\qquad(0\le r\le k),
  \]
  where \((10)^r\) denotes the alternating prefix \(10\cdots10\) of length \(2r\).
  The fiber multiplicities on this layer satisfy
  \[
  d_{2k}\bigl(x^{(0)}\bigr)=1,\qquad
  d_{2k}\bigl(x^{(r)}\bigr)=k-r+1\ \ (1\le r\le k),
  \]
  equivalently,
  \[
  \{d_{2k}(x):x\in X_{2k}^{\max}\}=\{1,1,2,3,\dots,k\}.
  \]
\end{enumerate}
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
The classification of \(X_m^{\max}\) relies only on the admissibility constraint \(x_ix_{i+1}=0\): to achieve the maximum \(1\)-count of \(\lceil m/2\rceil\), apart from boundaries each \(1\) must be separated by a \(0\), hence the word must have an alternating structure; when \(m\) is odd the alternating word is unique, when even exactly one \(00\) defect is allowed, yielding the above \(x^{(r)}\) family.
\par
The closed fiber multiplicities use the Zeckendorf value function \(V_m(x)=\sum_{i=1}^m x_iF_{i+1}\) and microstate value \(N(\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^m\omega_iF_{i+1}\) (see calibration in script \texttt{scripts/common\_phi\_fold.py}). When \(x\in X_m\),
\(\Fold_m(\omega)=x\) if and only if the Zeckendorf digits of \(N(\omega)\) at length \(m\) match \(x\); hence on the maximum-weight words stated in this conclusion (all satisfying \(V_m(x)<F_{m+2}\)), the fiber size equals
\[
d_m(x)=\#\Bigl\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^m:\ N(\omega)=V_m(x)\Bigr\}.
\]
Below we prove the closed form for the even case; the odd case follows from the same "representation uniqueness" argument to yield \(d_{2k+1}((10)^k1)=1\).
\par
Let \(m=2k\) and \(0\le r\le k\). By the alternating structure of \(x^{(r)}\) and the two-class summation identities
\[
\sum_{j=1}^{r}F_{2j}=F_{2r+1}-1,\qquad
\sum_{j=r+1}^{k}F_{2j+1}=F_{2k+2}-F_{2r+2},
\]
we obtain its Zeckendorf value in closed form
\[
V_{2k}\bigl(x^{(r)}\bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}F_{2j}+\sum_{j=r+1}^{k}F_{2j+1}
=F_{2k+2}-F_{2r}-1.
\]
Therefore, if we set
\[
\mathcal{R}_{k,r}:=\Bigl\{S\subseteq\{2,3,\dots,2k+1\}:\ \sum_{n\in S}F_n=F_{2k+2}-F_{2r}-1\Bigr\},
\]
then \(d_{2k}(x^{(r)})=\card{\mathcal{R}_{k,r}}\).
\par
\noindent\textbf{(1) Uniqueness when \(r=0\).}
In this case the target sum is \(F_{2k+2}-1\). If the largest term \(F_{2k+1}\) is not included, the maximum achievable sum is
\(\sum_{n=2}^{2k}F_n=F_{2k+2}-2\), insufficient to reach the target; hence every representation must use \(F_{2k+1}\).
Thus the representation set is in bijection with \(\mathcal{R}_{k-1,0}\), and with initial value \(k=1\) we know \(\card{\mathcal{R}_{k,0}}=1\).
\par
\noindent\textbf{(2) Recurrence and closed form when \(1\le r\le k\).}
Decompose \(\mathcal{R}_{k,r}\) by whether the largest term \(F_{2k+1}\) is included. If \(F_{2k+1}\in S\), then the remainder must represent
\[
F_{2k+2}-F_{2r}-1-F_{2k+1}=F_{2k}-F_{2r}-1,
\]
and since \(F_{2k}-F_{2r}-1<F_{2k}\), this representation cannot use \(F_{2k}\), hence is in strict bijection with \(\mathcal{R}_{k-1,r}\) (on weight set \(\{2,\dots,2k-1\}\)).
\par
If \(F_{2k+1}\notin S\), then \(S\subseteq\{2,\dots,2k\}\), and in this case the gap between the target sum and the maximum sum from taking all is
\[
\Bigl(\sum_{n=2}^{2k}F_n\Bigr)-\bigl(F_{2k+2}-F_{2r}-1\bigr)=F_{2r}-1.
\]
Hence representations not using \(F_{2k+1}\) are in bijection with "removal subsets" for the gap \(F_{2r}-1\). By the greedy forcing rule:
if \(T\subseteq\{2,\dots,2r-1\}\) satisfies \(\sum_{n\in T}F_n=F_{2r}-1\), then we must have \(F_{2r-1}\in T\), and recursively deduce
\(
T=\{3,5,\dots,2r-1\}
\) uniquely.
Thus in \(\mathcal{R}_{k,r}\) there exists exactly one representation not containing \(F_{2k+1}\).
\par
In summary, for \(1\le r\le k\) we have the recurrence
\(
\card{\mathcal{R}_{k,r}}=\card{\mathcal{R}_{k-1,r}}+1
\)
with boundary initial value \(\card{\mathcal{R}_{r,r}}=1\) (corresponding to the unique representation of \(F_{2r+1}-1\)), solving to
\(
\card{\mathcal{R}_{k,r}}=k-r+1
\).
Combined with \(d_{2k}(x^{(r)})=\card{\mathcal{R}_{k,r}}\) yields the conclusion.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Top-level coefficient of the weight--collision partition function: maximum-weight layer enforces Faulhaber power sums]\label{con:xi-terminal-zq-top-coefficient-faulhaber}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item If \(m=2k+1\), then \([y^{k+1}]Z_{2k+1}^{(q)}(y)=1\).
  \item If \(m=2k\), then

\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
The top-level coefficient equals the power sum \(\sum_{x\in X_m^{\max}}d_m(x)^q\) on the maximum-weight layer. By the spectrum \(\{1\}\) (odd) and \(\{1,1,2,\dots,k\}\) (even) from Conclusion \ref{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-layer-fiber-spectrum-closed}, the two formulas follow directly.

\begin{conclusion}[Generating function of the top-level sector: denominator order \texorpdfstring{$(1-t)^{q+2}$}{(1-t)^{q+2}}]\label{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-top-sector-ogf}
Let
\(
b_k^{(q)}:=[y^{k}]Z_{2k}^{(q)}(y)=1+\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{q}
\)
(\(q\ge 1\) fixed).
Then its ordinary generating function satisfies the strict identity
\[
\sum_{k\ge 0} b_k^{(q)}t^{k}
=\frac{1}{1-t}+\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{j\ge 1}j^{q}t^{j}.
\]
Hence \(\sum_{k\ge 0} b_k^{(q)}t^{k}\) is a rational function, and has a pole at \(t=1\) of order exactly \(q+2\) (hence the denominator can be taken as \((1-t)^{q+2}\)).
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
By \(b_k^{(q)}=1+\sum_{j=1}^k j^q\), exchange summations:
\[
\sum_{k\ge 0}b_k^{(q)}t^k=\sum_{k\ge 0}t^k+\sum_{k\ge 0}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^q t^k
=\frac{1}{1-t}+\sum_{j\ge 1}j^q\sum_{k\ge j}t^k,
\]
and \(\sum_{k\ge j}t^k=t^j/(1-t)\) yields the stated identity.
Also \(\sum_{j\ge 1}j^q t^j\) is a negative-integer-order polylogarithm, equivalent to a rational function with denominator \((1-t)^{-(q+1)}\);
multiplying by \((1-t)^{-1}\) gives a denominator order at most \(q+2\).
On the other hand, \(b_k^{(q)}\sim k^{q+1}/(q+1)\), so the generating function has pole order at \(t=1\) not less than \(q+2\), hence exactly \(q+2\).
\end{proof}

\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}

For \(\ell=\lceil m/2\rceil\), this coefficient equals the top-level coefficient at \(q=1\) from Conclusion \ref{con:xi-terminal-zq-top-coefficient-faulhaber}; substituting yields the two closed forms; the asymptotic follows from \(\log(1+k(k+1)/2)=2\log k+O(1)\).
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[\texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-tilted limit on the maximum-weight layer: \texorpdfstring{$J_k/k\Rightarrow \mathrm{Beta}(q+1,1)$}{Jk/k -> Beta(q+1,1)}]\label{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-escort-beta-limit}
For even length \(m=2k\), consider the \(q\)-tilted measure on the maximum-weight layer
\[
\mathbb{P}_{k,q}(x\in\cdot):=\frac{\sum_{x\in X_{2k}^{\max}} d_{2k}(x)^{q}\,\mathbf 1_{\{x\in\cdot\}}}{\sum_{x\in X_{2k}^{\max}} d_{2k}(x)^{q}}
\qquad(q\ge 1).
\]
Let \(X\sim\mathbb{P}_{k,q}\), and set \(J_k:=d_{2k}(X)\).
Then by the spectrum \(\{1,1,2,\dots,k\}\) from Conclusion \ref{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-layer-fiber-spectrum-closed}
\[
\mathbb{P}(J_k=j)=\frac{j^{q}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k} i^{q}}\ (2\le j\le k),\qquad
\mathbb{P}(J_k=1)=\frac{2}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k} i^{q}}.
\]
And in the scaling limit there is weak convergence
\[
\frac{J_k}{k}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Beta}(q+1,1),
\qquad \text{with density }(q+1)x^{q}\mathbf 1_{[0,1]}(x).
\]
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
The probability mass function follows from the spectrum's counting (\(j=1\) appears twice, others \(j=2,\dots,k\) appear once each) directly.
For weak convergence, let \(f\) be a continuous function on \([0,1]\), then
\[
\mathbb{E}\Bigl[f\Bigl(\frac{J_k}{k}\Bigr)\Bigr]
=\frac{2\cdot f(1/k)\cdot 1^{q}+\sum_{j=2}^{k} f(j/k)\,j^{q}}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k} i^{q}}
=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k} f(j/k)\,j^{q}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} i^{q}}+o(1),
\]
where \(o(1)\) comes from the double counting of \(j=1\) being negligible under normalization.
Dividing numerator and denominator by \(k^{q+1}\), and treating the sums as Riemann sums, yields the limit
\(
\int_{0}^{1}f(x)x^{q}\,\dd x\,/\,\int_0^1 x^{q}\dd x
\)
corresponding to the \(\mathrm{Beta}(q+1,1)\) distribution.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Scaling limit of the defect location: \texorpdfstring{$R_k/k\Rightarrow \mathrm{Beta}(1,q+1)$}{Rk/k -> Beta(1,q+1)} and centroid ratio]\label{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-defect-localization}
Along the parametrization \(x^{(r)}=(10)^r(01)^{k-r}\) from Conclusion \ref{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-layer-fiber-spectrum-closed}, let \(R_k\) be the random defect parameter (i.e., the index such that \(X=x^{(R_k)}\)).
Then under \(\mathbb{P}_{k,q}\) from Conclusion \ref{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-escort-beta-limit},
\[
\frac{R_k}{k}\ \Rightarrow\ \mathrm{Beta}(1,q+1),
\qquad \text{with density }(q+1)(1-x)^q\mathbf 1_{[0,1]}(x).
\]
In particular, the centroid ratios satisfy
\[
\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{E}[R_k]\to \frac{1}{q+2},
\qquad
\frac{1}{k}\mathbb{E}[d_{2k}(X)]\to \frac{q+1}{q+2}.
\]
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}
By Conclusion \ref{con:xi-terminal-max-weight-layer-fiber-spectrum-closed}, for \(r\ge 1\) there is the deterministic relation
\(
J_k=d_{2k}(x^{(R_k)})=k-R_k+1
\).
And
\(
\mathbb{P}_{k,q}(R_k=0)=\frac{1}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}i^q}\to 0
\).
Therefore for any continuous function \(f:[0,1]\to\RR\), the change of variables \(j=k-r+1\) gives
\[
\mathbb{E}_{k,q}\Bigl[f\Bigl(\frac{R_k}{k}\Bigr)\Bigr]
=\frac{f(0)+\sum_{r=1}^{k} f(r/k)\,(k-r+1)^q}{1+\sum_{i=1}^{k}i^q}
=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k} f\!\bigl(1-j/k+1/k\bigr)\,j^q}{\sum_{i=1}^{k}i^q}+o(1).
\]
Dividing numerator and denominator by \(k^{q+1}\) and taking Riemann sum limits yields
\[
\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{k,q}\Bigl[f\Bigl(\frac{R_k}{k}\Bigr)\Bigr]
=\frac{\int_{0}^{1} f(1-x)\,x^q\,\dd x}{\int_{0}^{1}x^q\,\dd x}
=\int_{0}^{1} f(u)\,(q+1)(1-u)^q\,\dd u,
\]
i.e., \(R_k/k\Rightarrow \mathrm{Beta}(1,q+1)\).
Since \(R_k/k\) and \(J_k/k\) are both uniformly bounded in \([0,1]\), the centroid ratios follow directly from weak convergence and the Beta distribution mean formulas.
\end{proof}

\begin{conclusion}[Zero locus on the \texorpdfstring{$t=\pm1$}{t=±1} lines: algebraic-curve quantization of the weight parameter]\label{con:xi-terminal-zq-line-tpm1-zeros}
Let \(D_q(t,y)\) be the denominator polynomial of \(F_q(t,y)=N_q(t,y)/D_q(t,y)\).
Then for \(q=2,3,4\) there is the strict equality
\[
D_q(1,y)=D_q(-1,y),
\]
and its zero set in the \(y\)-plane quantizes to a finite algebraic set:
\[
D_2(\pm1,y)=-y(y-1)(y-2),
\]
\[
D_3(\pm1,y)=y(y-1)(y^2-4y+6),
\]
\[
D_4(\pm1,y)=-y(y-1)(y^3-5y^2+12y-24).
\]
Therefore, "the appearance of characteristic modes \(\lambda=\pm1\)" occurs only when \(y\) falls into the above algebraic sets.
Moreover, in these three formulas, after removing the common factor \(y(y-1)\), the additional factors satisfy at the constant term
\[
(y-2)\big|_{y=0}=-2,\qquad
(y^2-4y+6)\big|_{y=0}=6,\qquad
(y^3-5y^2+12y-24)\big|_{y=0}=-24,
\]
i.e., exhibiting the universal factorial scaling law \(({-}1)^{q+1}q!\) (for \(q=2,3,4\)).
\end{conclusion}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Substitute \(t=\pm1\) into each \(D_q(t,y)\) and factorize in \(\ZZ[y]\);
the equality \(D_q(1,y)=D_q(-1,y)\) reflects the stability of the even structure under \(t\mapsto -t\) in the denominator across this third-order family.
\end{proof}
