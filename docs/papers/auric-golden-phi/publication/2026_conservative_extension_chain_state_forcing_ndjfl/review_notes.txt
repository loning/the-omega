Referee simulation for NDJFL

Provisional recommendation after revision:
Accept, subject to routine copyediting.

Principal weaknesses identified in the initial draft:
1. The manuscript was too diffuse for NDJFL. It accumulated many semantic layers but did not isolate a small number of theorems with clear technical payoffs.
2. The comparison with Kripke semantics was suggestive but not exact. The draft needed a precise recovery theorem, not just motivational remarks.
3. The sheaf-theoretic content of the local-object layer was implicit. In particular, the draft did not explain what gluing failure measures in standard categorical terms.
4. The null-trichotomy had no explicit finite witness model, which left the reader without a concrete sanity check.
5. The multi-axis section introduced support and indispensability but did not locate the induced decision problems in the usual complexity hierarchy.
6. The exposition was not yet in NDJFL style: the introduction was too long, some sections were overly programmatic, and the realization layer needed compression.

Fixes applied in the revision:
1. Rewrote the paper around five central results: composition of conservative extensions, canonical Kripke reduction, sheafification characterization of compatible local sectionability, refinement-stable null classification with a finite witness model, and complexity upper bounds for axis support.
2. Replaced the broad introductory narrative with a concise theorem-driven introduction.
3. Added Theorem 4.5 and Corollary 4.7 identifying gluing failure with the gap between a presheaf and its sheafification.
4. Added Proposition 4.12 exhibiting all three null modes in one finite model.
5. Added Theorem 5.7 locating support, indispensability, and minimal support in coNP, NP, and D^P under a natural finite encoding.
6. Compressed the observer-spacetime and realization sections to the minimal formal content needed for conservativity and fidelity.

Residual limitations:
1. The paper gives upper bounds for the support problems but not matching lower bounds. This is now stated as an open problem rather than left implicit.
2. The realization layer remains abstract. That is acceptable for this paper because the finite null-mode model already supplies the concrete witness the original draft lacked, and the realization theorem is explicitly framed as a transfer result.
