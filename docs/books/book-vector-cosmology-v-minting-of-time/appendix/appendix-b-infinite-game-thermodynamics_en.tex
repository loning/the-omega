\section{Entropy Flow Equation for Open Systems}

First, we must distinguish between \textbf{isolated systems} and \textbf{open systems}.

For isolated systems, entropy change $dS \ge 0$, and heat death is inevitable.

But life and civilization are typical \textbf{Dissipative Structures}, whose entropy change follows the \textbf{Prigogine Equation}:

\[dS = d_i S + d_e S\]

Where:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{$d_i S$}: Entropy produced internally due to irreversible processes (such as metabolism, computation, friction). According to the second law, \textbf{$d_i S \ge 0$}. This is the thrust of death.

\item \textbf{$d_e S$}: Entropy flow exchanged with the external environment.
\end{itemize}

To maintain the system's high order (i.e., keep $S_{sys}$ below the maximum, or even decrease it), the \textbf{negentropy criterion} must be satisfied:

\[d_e S < 0 \quad \text{and} \quad |d_e S| \ge d_i S\]

This means: \textbf{The system must expel chaos to the environment faster than it creates chaos.}

The question is: Can the environment infinitely receive this chaos? In the classical model, the environment is also finite, and will eventually saturate, causing backflow, and the system will still die.
