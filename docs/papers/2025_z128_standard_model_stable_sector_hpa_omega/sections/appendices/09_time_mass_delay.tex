\section{Time and mass as delay: scattering and relativistic lapse dictionaries (interface)}
\label{app:time_mass_delay}
\label{app:time_mass_delay_sr_gr}
\label{app:time_mass_delay_ws}

This appendix records the matching-layer dictionaries underlying the mass-as-delay discussion in Section~\ref{subsubsec:mass_as_delay_interface}.
It connects protocol-level cost/overhead coordinates to standard measurable proxies (scattering delay, redshift/lapse) and to standard kinematic relations (special and general relativity), while keeping these statements at the interface layer rather than as theorem-level consequences of the folding core.

\subsection{Scattering delay as an operational proxy: Wigner--Smith}
\label{app:wigner_smith_delay_z128}

In platforms where a scattering description is available, delays are directly measurable from complex $S$-parameters as functions of frequency.
Let $S(\omega)$ be a unitary scattering matrix (lossless elastic scattering) at angular frequency $\omega$.
The Wigner--Smith time-delay matrix is defined by \cite{Wigner1955,Smith1960}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wigner_smith_omega}
Q(\omega):=-\iu\,S(\omega)^\dagger\frac{\dd S}{\dd \omega},
\end{equation}
and a common scalar summary is the total delay
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tau_ws_trace_omega}
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega):=\Tr Q(\omega).
\end{equation}
In a one-channel setting $S(\omega)=\e^{\iu\delta(\omega)}$, \eqref{eq:wigner_smith_omega} reduces to the group/phase derivative
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wigner_smith_one_channel}
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)=\frac{\dd\delta}{\dd\omega}.
\end{equation}

\begin{remark}[Calibration and losses]
\label{rem:wigner_smith_calibration_losses}
The definition \eqref{eq:wigner_smith_omega} is standard for unitary $S$.
In realistic settings, absorption and imperfect calibration can make $S$ non-unitary; one should then either restrict to frequency bands where unitarity holds to a stated tolerance or adopt an explicit loss model and treat $\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}$ as an effective delay proxy \cite{Wigner1955,Smith1960}.
An auditable measurement pipeline can be implemented by phase unwrapping, finite differencing, and explicit stability checks under smoothing and step-size variation.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{One-channel resonance check (Breit--Wigner).}
As a minimal analytic benchmark, consider a one-channel exactly unitary resonance model
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breit_wigner_S_omega}
S(\omega)=\frac{\omega-\omega_0-\iu\Gamma/2}{\omega-\omega_0+\iu\Gamma/2},
\end{equation}
whose phase rises by $\pi$ across the resonance.
Writing $S(\omega)=\e^{\iu\delta(\omega)}$, one obtains the Lorentzian delay profile
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:breit_wigner_tau_omega}
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)=\frac{\dd\delta}{\dd\omega}
=\frac{\Gamma}{(\omega-\omega_0)^2+(\Gamma/2)^2},
\end{equation}
and, at resonance,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tau_ws_peak}
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega_0)=\frac{4}{\Gamma}.
\end{equation}
Thus the dimensionless overhead proxy \eqref{eq:kappa_ws_def} is proportional to the inverse linewidth, $\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega_0)=4/(\Gamma\,\tau_0)$.
This is the simplest concrete instance of the interface slogan ``stable obstruction $\Rightarrow$ additional delay'' and provides an experimentally extractable handle (linewidth from phase-jump width) \cite{Wigner1955,Smith1960}.

\subsection{From delay to overhead and lapse (clock-rate dictionary)}
\label{app:delay_to_lapse_dictionary}

Fix a reference tick duration $\tau_0>0$ that defines one unit of baseline protocol time in physical units (seconds).
We compress delay into a dimensionless overhead proxy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa_ws_def}
\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega):=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)}{\tau_0},
\end{equation}
and define an associated lapse proxy by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lapse_ws_def}
N_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega):=\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_0$ is a chosen reference overhead (a normalization convention).
Operationally, larger delay corresponds to larger overhead and hence to a smaller lapse proxy.

This matches the general ``computational lapse'' dictionary used elsewhere in the HPA--$\Omega$ series: a local overhead field $\kappa$ induces a clock-rate factor $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$ so that local proper time satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dtau_lapse_dictionary}
\dd\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=N(x)\,\dd t.
\end{equation}
In this paper, we use the dictionary only as a matching layer: it is not used as a premise for any folding or labeling theorem.

\subsection{Mass as a time scale: Compton clocks and the depth coordinate}
\label{app:compton_clock_dictionary}

Independently of any specific dynamics, a mass scale defines a frequency scale by combining the standard relations $E=mc^2$ and $E=\hbar\omega$ \cite{Einstein1905,NielsenChuang2010}.
Define the Compton angular frequency and time scale
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compton_clock}
\omega_C(\mu):=\frac{\mu c^2}{\hbar},
\qquad
\tau_C(\mu):=\frac{1}{\omega_C(\mu)}=\frac{\hbar}{\mu c^2}.
\end{equation}
Because the present paper works primarily with ratios relative to $m_e$, the constants $c$ and $\hbar$ cancel:
\[
\frac{\omega_C(\mu)}{\omega_C(m_e)}=\frac{\mu}{m_e},
\qquad
\frac{\tau_C(\mu)}{\tau_C(m_e)}=\frac{m_e}{\mu}.
\]
Consequently the resolution coordinate used in the mass-spectrum closure (Section~\ref{sec:mass_spectrum_closure}) can be read equally as a log-frequency or log-time coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r_as_log_compton}
r(\mu)=\log_\varphi\!\left(\frac{\mu}{m_e}\right)
=\log_\varphi\!\left(\frac{\omega_C(\mu)}{\omega_C(m_e)}\right)
=-\log_\varphi\!\left(\frac{\tau_C(\mu)}{\tau_C(m_e)}\right).
\end{equation}
This gives a strong interface meaning to ``mass as depth'': it is also ``mass as a clock-rate coordinate'' in the standard Compton sense.

\subsection{General relativity reference: lapse and Shapiro delay (external target)}
\label{app:gr_lapse_shapiro_target}

In a $3{+}1$ decomposition, the relativistic lapse $N(x)$ relates coordinate time to proper time of static observers by $\dd\tau=N\,\dd t$ \cite{Wald1984}.
Thus, identifying $N(x)=\kappa_0/\kappa(x)$ is a direct interface map between overhead and GR clock rates.

\begin{proposition}[Static redshift law in lapse form (standard)]\label{prop:static_redshift_lapse}
In a static spacetime with lapse $N(x)$, the frequency ratio of the same signal measured by two static observers at $x_{\mathrm{emit}}$ and $x_{\mathrm{obs}}$ satisfies
\[
\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{obs}}}{\omega_{\mathrm{emit}}}=\frac{N(x_{\mathrm{emit}})}{N(x_{\mathrm{obs}})}.
\]
Equivalently, under the interface identification $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$, one has $\omega_{\mathrm{obs}}/\omega_{\mathrm{emit}}=\kappa(x_{\mathrm{obs}})/\kappa(x_{\mathrm{emit}})$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is the standard gravitational redshift law for static observers in a static metric with $g_{tt}=-N^2c^2$; see, e.g., \cite{Wald1984,Will1993}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Schwarzschild lapse.}
In Schwarzschild coordinates, a static clock at radius $r$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:schwarzschild_lapse}
\dd\tau=\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}}\,\dd t,
\end{equation}
so $N(r)=\sqrt{1-2GM/(rc^2)}$ \cite{Wald1984}.
Under the interface identification $\kappa(r)/\kappa_0=1/N(r)$, one obtains
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa_schwarzschild_series}
\frac{\kappa(r)}{\kappa_0}=\left(1-\frac{2GM}{rc^2}\right)^{-1/2}
=1+\frac{GM}{rc^2}+O\!\left(\frac{G^2M^2}{r^2c^4}\right).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Shapiro delay.}
For light propagation past a gravitating body, the Shapiro time delay for a radar signal is, at leading post-Newtonian order,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:shapiro_delay}
\Delta t_{\mathrm{Shapiro}}\;\approx\;\frac{2GM}{c^3}\log\!\left(\frac{4r_1 r_2}{b^2}\right),
\end{equation}
for endpoints at radii $r_1,r_2$ and impact parameter $b$ \cite{Shapiro1964,Will1993}.
These standard formulas provide external operational targets for any scan-based identification of additional protocol overhead with effective delay/clock slowing.

\subsection{Special relativity reference: kinematic time dilation and dispersion}
\label{app:sr_time_dilation_dispersion}

In special relativity, proper time and coordinate time relate by the Lorentz factor
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sr_time_dilation}
\dd\tau=\frac{\dd t}{\gamma},\qquad
\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^2/c^2}},
\end{equation}
and a relativistic particle satisfies the dispersion relation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sr_dispersion}
E^2=p^2c^2+m^2c^4,
\end{equation}
with group velocity $v=\dd E/\dd p=pc^2/E$ \cite{Einstein1905,Rindler2006}.
In a scan/protocol interpretation, \eqref{eq:sr_time_dilation} is treated as a matching dictionary:
once a locality basis and a baseline signal speed $c$ are fixed (Remark~\ref{rem:scan_rate_c_dictionary}), any additional protocol overhead that reduces the available local update budget can be encoded as an effective $\gamma$-factor or lapse factor.
This provides a clean place to connect the discrete cost coordinates used in the present paper (depth, fiber multiplicity, connection/holonomy overhead) to standard relativistic observables \emph{without} importing relativistic axioms into the folding layer.
