
\begin{abstract}
Linear presentations of arithmetic take additive translation as the primitive operation. While locally economical, this choice systematically obscures multiplicative scaling, phase and periodicity, and spectral data, and it pushes ``infinity'' outside the object domain, where endpoint pathologies (singularity and divergence) are then handled by external prescriptions. This paper proposes a self-contained coordinate upgrade---\emph{Holographic Polar Arithmetic} (HPA)---in which multiplication is ontological and addition becomes a derived, lossy readout.

Starting from the $\mathbb{F}_1$-inspired multiplicative monoid skeleton $M=(\NN_{>0},\cdot)$, we define a radial character $\rho$ and a multiplicative phase $\theta_\times$ which is additive under multiplication, yielding a polar embedding
$$
\mathcal{Z}(n)=\rho(n)\e^{\iu\theta_\times(n)}\in\CC^*.
$$
We show that any nontrivial such embedding is multiplicative but cannot be additive in the classical linear sense: no map $\Phi:\NN_{>0}\to\CC^*$ can simultaneously preserve multiplication and linear addition except the identity. Consequently, vector synthesis $\mathcal{Z}(a)+\mathcal{Z}(b)$ is not constrained to land on the discrete image $\mathcal{Z}(M)$. This mismatch between continuous vector composition and discrete integer coordinates defines a \emph{quantum gap}. Macroscopic ``addition'' is therefore modeled as a probabilistic projection of synthesized vectors onto a discrete coordinate lattice, canonically realized by Zeckendorf/Ostrowski coding.

To decouple intrinsic multiplicative phase from time/readout, we introduce a genuine unitary scan operator $\Theta$ (a Koopman operator for an irrational rotation on $L^2(S^1)$), with iteration count as time. The scan model naturally yields a Weyl pair: together with the basic phase multiplication operator $V$, the shift $U=\Theta$ satisfies $UV=\e^{2\pi\iu\alpha}VU$, i.e. the irrational rotation algebra. A binary readout is obtained by a window projection $\Pi_W$, producing a two-letter mechanical word. The golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ is singled out by a binary-minimality requirement: its continued fraction coefficients are all $1$, so the characteristic Sturmian word is the Fibonacci word and Ostrowski numeration degenerates to the binary Zeckendorf representation. On this basis we construct an orbit-wise ``cyclic calculus'' (difference, ergodic trace, and Abel-type finite part) and package three reusable modules---topologizing singularities, extracting finite parts from divergences, and replacing ill-posed continuum path integrals by controlled sums over Zeckendorf-coded lattices---closing a consistent loop from multiplicative skeleton to discrete readout. Crucially, the geometric rigidity of the octonionic scan implies a vacuum impedance $\alpha_{\mathrm{em}}^{-1}\approx 4\pi^3+\pi^2+\pi\approx 137.036$ derived from topological measures (bulk $T^3$, boundary $D^2$, path $S^1$), and yields an exact lattice dispersion $E=\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\arcsin(\varepsilon P/2)$ with low-energy expansion $E^2=P^2+\frac{1}{12}\varepsilon^2 P^4+O(\varepsilon^4 P^6)$, providing a falsifiable supersonic group-velocity signature.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Keywords}: $\mathbb{F}_1$, polar arithmetic, multiplicative phase, unitary scan, irrational rotation, Sturmian mechanical word, cut-and-project, Ostrowski numeration, Zeckendorf decomposition, orbit trace, finite part.
