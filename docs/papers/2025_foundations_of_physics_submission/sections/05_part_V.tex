\subsection{The Information Velocity Circle}
Standard Relativity posits a constant speed of light $c$. In Omega Theory, this is a special case of a more general "Conservation of Information Processing Rate."
Let $\mathcal{I}_{\text{total}}$ be the total rate at which the quantum state $|\Omega\rangle$ processes information (updates bits). This capacity is partitioned into:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{External Propagation ($v_{\text{ext}}$)}: Moving information through the spatial graph (speed of particles).
    \item \textbf{Internal Processing ($v_{\text{int}}$)}: Processing information locally (mass/inertia).
    \item \textbf{Holographic Entanglement ($v_{\text{env}}$)}: Updating the boundary connection (gravity).
\end{enumerate}
The conservation law is:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\text{ext}}^2 + v_{\text{int}}^2 + v_{\text{env}}^2 = c^2.
\end{equation}
In empty space ($v_{\text{int}} = v_{\text{env}} = 0$), we recover $v_{\text{ext}} = c$. Near a massive body, $v_{\text{env}}$ increases (gravitational time dilation), forcing $v_{\text{ext}}$ to decrease (Shapiro delay).

The first term is the Einstein-Hilbert action (geometry). The second is the relativistic Fisher Information (intrinsic statistical cost). The term $\lambda_F$ is a coupling constant of order $\hbar^2$.

\subsection{Derivation of Field Equations}
We perform the variation with respect to the metric $g^{\mu\nu}$.
Using the standard identities $\delta \sqrt{-g} = -\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g} g_{\mu\nu} \delta g^{\mu\nu}$ and $\delta R = R_{\mu\nu} \delta g^{\mu\nu} + \nabla_\mu v^\mu$, the variation $\delta S_{\Omega} = 0$ yields:
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
    T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{info}} \equiv -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}} \frac{\delta S_{\text{fisher}}}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} = \frac{\lambda_F}{4} \left[ \nabla_\mu \ln \rho \nabla_\nu \ln \rho - \frac{1}{2} g_{\mu\nu} (\nabla \ln \rho)^2 \right].
\end{equation}
In the limit where the information density is uniform ($\nabla \rho \approx 0$), $T_{\mu\nu}^{\text{info}}$ vanishes, and we recover the vacuum Einstein equations. If $\rho$ varies slowly, it acts as a stiff fluid with equation of state $w=1$, mimicking a scalar field source. Thus, gravity (curvature) is the response of potential geometry to the gradient of information.
