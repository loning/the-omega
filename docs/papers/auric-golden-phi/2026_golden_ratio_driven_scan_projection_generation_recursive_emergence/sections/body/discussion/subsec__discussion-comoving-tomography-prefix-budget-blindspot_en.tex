\subsection{Comoving Tomography and Complexity Barriers: Invertible Profiles, Prefix Gains, and Finite Blind Spots}\label{subsec:discussion-comoving-tomography-prefix-budget-blindspot}
This subsection elevates the contrast between ``endpoint quadratic compression (fixed chart)'' and ``comoving localization (externalized prefix)'' into three propositions suitable for direct submission and consolidation.
The key point is: off-slice information is not invisible, but is regime-wise \emph{compressed into the boundary layer}; to lift the blind spot, one must either externalize an addressable prefix (comoving chart) or enter an invertible tomography channel (encoding two-dimensional parameters as a one-dimensional spectrum).

\paragraph{Invertible tomography (finite defect)}
Under the finite offline zero-set caliber, the comoving scan depth
\(
h_{x_0}(\rho)=1-|w_{\rho,x_0}|^2
\)
is a Lorentzian window with \((\gamma,\delta)\) as center/width parameters (Appendix Theorem \ref{thm:app-comoving-horizon-localization}), and the aggregated profile
\(
H(x_0)=\sum_j m_j h_{x_0}(\rho_j)
\)
possesses a fully invertible exponential spectral decomposition: its Fourier transform is a finite exponential sum, so that \(\{(\gamma_j,\delta_j,m_j)\}\) can be uniquely recovered from the single-variable function \(H\) (Theorem \ref{thm:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-fourier-inversion}; see also the conservation trace Corollary \ref{cor:xi-comoving-horizon-scan-mass}).
\par
\textbf{Supplement (Poisson semigroup perspective):}
If the offline zero set is organized as a defect measure and a scale scan \(t\mapsto G_t\) is introduced, then the scale evolution is strictly equivalent to a Poisson semigroup orbit \(G_{t+s}=P_s*G_t\), with conserved and dissipated quantities strictly separated; moreover, under the finite defect caliber, taking any single scale \(t>0\) suffices to recover all offline parameters via rational Herglotz pole inversion; the accompanying uniform detection covering complexity lower bound can also be precisely quantified by the semigroup scale \(t+\delta\) (see Theorems \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-poisson-semigroup-homomorphism}--\ref{thm:xi-offcritical-poisson-matched-filter-covering} and consolidation Corollary \ref{cor:xi-offcritical-poisson-field-classification}).

\paragraph{Prefix irreplaceability (bit budget deficit)}
Under the regime of no externalized comoving prefix and insistence on a fixed Cayley chart, the offline offset is structurally compressed into the endpoint boundary layer within the height window \(|\gamma|\le T\), forcing an irreducible leading bit budget term of \(2\log_2T\):
any certificate mechanism using the endpoint margin minimum resolvable scale \(2^{-p}\) as an exclusion strategy must satisfy
\(
p\ge 2\log_2T+\log_2(1/\delta_0)+O(1)
\)
(Theorem \ref{thm:xi-offcritical-endpoint-resolution-lower-bound}).
\par
If one allows externalizing an addressable prefix and takes comoving localization \(x_0=\gamma\), the quadratic redshift is lifted to a first-order manifestation \(h_{\gamma}(\rho)=\frac{4\delta}{(1+\delta)^2}\), so that the bit budget is reduced to a constant threshold independent of \(T\):
\(
p\ge \log_2\!\bigl((1+\delta_0)^2/(4\delta_0)\bigr)
\)
(Corollary \ref{cor:xi-comoving-prefix-bit-budget-gain}).
Therefore, the net bit budget deficit ``recovered'' by the comoving prefix is \(2\log_2T+O(1)\), and this deficit cannot be compensated by merely increasing the resolution of the fixed chart (Remark \ref{rem:xi-comoving-horizon-localization-address-null}).

\paragraph{Finite certificate blind spot (boundary layer indiscernibility)}
The Jensen defect \(D_\zeta(\varrho)\) as a function of \(\log\varrho\) exhibits a kink atom at \(\varrho=|w_\rho|\), whose slope jump equals the multiplicity of the corresponding zero (Corollary \ref{cor:app-jensen-defect-kink-structure}).
Therefore, any verification strategy using only finitely many sampled radii \(\{\varrho_j\}\) has a structural blind spot: if \(\varrho_{\max}\le |w_\rho|\), then the kink threshold has not been crossed, the zero is strictly invisible on the sample, and cannot be excluded (Corollary \ref{cor:app-jensen-finite-sampling-blindspot}).
In the high-height interval, the scale of this blind spot is of the same order as the endpoint quadratic compression law: the invisibility threshold satisfies \(1-\varrho_{\max}\lesssim \delta/\gamma^2\).

\paragraph{Closed loop (compression \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} blind spot \texorpdfstring{$\Rightarrow$}{=>} externalized prefix or invertible tomography)}
The above three propositions form an implementation-independent closed loop:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Endpoint compression of the fixed chart pushes off-slice information into the boundary layer, thereby producing the indiscernibility blind spot of finite certificates;
  \item The two auditable paths to lift the blind spot are respectively: externalizing an addressable prefix for comoving localization (eliminating the \(T^{-2}\) redshift), or entering an invertible tomography channel (encoding two-dimensional parameters as a one-dimensional exponential spectrum that can be inverted);
  \item Consequently, the regime consequence of ``address precedes value'' can be quantified as: whether a prefix is externalized determines whether the leading bit budget term includes \(2\log_2T\), and determines whether the boundary layer blind spot can be penetrated.
\end{itemize}
