\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$E_8$}{E8} square spectrum collapse: fourth-degree certificate, discriminant, and trace invariants]\label{prop:pom-e8-square-spectrum-collapse-trace7}
Let
\[
\mathcal R_{E_8}:=\left\{4\cos^2\!\left(\frac{\pi k}{30}\right):\ k\in\{1,7,11,13\}\right\}.
\]
Then the four elements of \(\mathcal R_{E_8}\) are precisely all the real roots of the same integer-coefficient fourth-degree polynomial; the closed-form certificate, discriminant, and elementary symmetric sums derived from Vieta's formulas are given in equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_E8_square_spectrum_collapse}.
In particular,
\[
\sum_{r\in\mathcal R_{E_8}}r=7,
\]
thus the integer \(7\) appears as a trace invariant of \(\mathcal R_{E_8}\) in integer-coefficient form.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By the definition of \(\mathcal R_{E_8}\), we can view \(4\cos^2(\pi/30)=2+\zeta_{30}+\zeta_{30}^{-1}\) as an algebraic integer in \(\QQ(\zeta_{30})^+\).
Computing its minimal polynomial and discriminant yields the fourth-degree certificate and discriminant factorization in equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_E8_square_spectrum_collapse}; moreover, this fourth-degree certificate indicates that the generated number field has degree \(4\), consistent with the degree of \(\QQ(\zeta_{30})^+\).
The symmetric sums are read directly from the coefficients of this fourth-degree polynomial via Vieta's formulas. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$E_8$}{E8} intersection field discriminant minimality and index factorization of \texorpdfstring{$t_{E_8}$}{tE8}]\label{prop:pom-e8-intersection-field-discriminant-index}
Let \(r_{E_8}:=4\cos^2(\pi/30)\), and set \(K_{E_8}:=\QQ(r_{E_8})\).
Then \(K_{E_8}\) is a totally real quartic field, and its field discriminant satisfies
\[
\mathrm{Disc}(K_{E_8})=1125=3^2\cdot 5^3
\]
(see the cyclotomic certificate in equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_E8_square_spectrum_collapse}), and
\[
\mathcal O_{K_{E_8}}=\ZZ[r_{E_8}].
\]
On the other hand, let \(t_{E_8}=t(I_{E_8})\) be the \(E_8\) intersection parameter of the fourth-order kernel family \(A_4(t)\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-a4t-jones-ade-intersections}), then \(t_{E_8}\in K_{E_8}\) and \(\QQ(t_{E_8})=K_{E_8}\), but its power-integral subring is generally not the maximal order;
moreover, the index satisfies the closed form
\[
[\mathcal O_{K_{E_8}}:\ZZ[t_{E_8}]]=5^2\cdot 29\cdot 59\cdot 1229.
\]
Therefore
\[
\mathrm{Disc}(\ZZ[t_{E_8}])=\mathrm{Disc}(K_{E_8})\cdot[\mathcal O_{K_{E_8}}:\ZZ[t_{E_8}]]^{2},
\]
thus the "huge discriminant" associated with \(t_{E_8}\) has prime factors other than \(3,5\) that are not ramified but arise from the power-integral generator's index artifact.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
From \(r_{E_8}=4\cos^2(\pi/30)=2+\zeta_{30}+\zeta_{30}^{-1}\) we know \(K_{E_8}\subseteq \QQ(\zeta_{30})^+\);
and by the fourth-degree certificate of equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_E8_square_spectrum_collapse} we know \([K_{E_8}:\QQ]=4\), while \([\QQ(\zeta_{30})^+:\QQ]=\tfrac12[\QQ(\zeta_{30}):\QQ]=4\), thus \(K_{E_8}=\QQ(\zeta_{30})^+\).
Equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_E8_square_spectrum_collapse} simultaneously gives \(\mathrm{Disc}(\QQ(\zeta_{30}))\) and its square root \(\mathrm{Disc}(\QQ(\zeta_{30})^+)\), consistent with the fourth-degree certificate discriminant of \(r_{E_8}\), thus the index of \(\ZZ[r_{E_8}]\) is \(1\), i.e., it is the maximal order.
\par
On the other hand, \(t_{E_8}=t(r_{E_8})\) is a rational function of \(r_{E_8}\), so \(t_{E_8}\in K_{E_8}\); and equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_E8_square_spectrum_collapse} gives the integer-coefficient minimal polynomial \(P_{E_8}(t)\) of \(t_{E_8}\), thus \(\ZZ[t_{E_8}]\) is a power-integral subring of \(K_{E_8}\), and
\(\mathrm{Disc}(\ZZ[t_{E_8}])=\mathrm{Disc}_t(P_{E_8})\).
By the discriminant-index identity
\(
\mathrm{Disc}(\ZZ[\alpha])=\mathrm{Disc}(K)\,[\mathcal O_K:\ZZ[\alpha]]^2
\)
taking \(\alpha=t_{E_8}\) yields the closed index formula and the factorization of the "huge discriminant." \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$q=4$}{q=4} cyclotomic--ADE layer and Newman critical layer: real/imaginary separation at quadratic resolution]\label{cor:pom-q4-arithmetic-two-layer-real-imag}
At the \(E_8\) intersection of the fourth-order kernel family \(A_4(t)\), the cyclotomic--ADE layer obtained from Proposition \ref{prop:pom-e8-intersection-field-discriminant-index} is totally real;
while the discriminant quadratic subfield of the Newman critical layer is the imaginary quadratic field \(\QQ(\sqrt{-1151})\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-a4t-newman-k2-class-number}).
Therefore the two layers necessarily have trivial intersection at quadratic resolution: their quadratic subfield intersection is only \(\QQ\).
Thus in mod \(p\) local audits, one can use two mutually orthogonal probes to separate the two types of mechanisms: one primarily based on the small modular residue class structure of the cyclotomic--ADE layer, the other primarily based on the quadratic character (Kronecker symbol) of the critical discriminant.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Totally real fields contain no nontrivial imaginary quadratic subfields.
Since \(\QQ(\sqrt{-1151})\) is an imaginary quadratic field, it cannot embed into the cyclotomic--ADE layer;
thus their intersection at the quadratic resolution level is only \(\QQ\).
The last statement is a direct translation of this separation fact into finite field fingerprint design: real layer probes are controlled by congruence classes/extension degrees, imaginary layer probes are controlled by quadratic characters such as \(\left(\frac{\mathrm{Disc}}{p}\right)\). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$E_8$}{E8} two-layer cyclotomic resolution and non-embeddability of the Newman critical field]\label{cor:pom-a4t-e8-two-resolution-layer-nonembedding}
Let \(c_{30}:=2\cos(\pi/30)\), and let \(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}}\) be the cyclotomic injection parameter of Proposition \ref{prop:pom-a4t-cyclotomic-adjacency-injection} (equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_A4_cyclotomic_adjacency_injection}).
Then \(\QQ(c_{30})\) is a degree \(8\) totally real cyclotomic real subfield, and the minimal polynomial of \(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}}\) has degree \(8\), thus
\[
\QQ(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}})=\QQ(c_{30}).
\]
On the other hand, the \(E_8\) index intersection parameter \(t_{E_8}\) belongs to the totally real quartic field \(K_{E_8}=\QQ(4\cos^2(\pi/30))=\QQ(c_{30}^2)\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-e8-intersection-field-discriminant-index}), and \([\QQ(c_{30}):\QQ(c_{30}^2)]=2\);
therefore
\[
t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}}\notin \QQ(t_{E_8}),
\qquad
[\QQ(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}}):\QQ(t_{E_8})]=2.
\]
Moreover, the minimal polynomial discriminant of the Newman critical parameter \(t_\star\) is negative (its square-free part is \(-1151\), Proposition \ref{prop:pom-a4t-newman-k2-class-number}), thus \(\QQ(t_\star)\) has a complex embedding; therefore \(\QQ(t_\star)\) cannot embed into any totally real cyclotomic real subfield, in particular it cannot embed into \(\QQ(c_{30})\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Since \(c_{30}=\zeta_{60}+\zeta_{60}^{-1}\), we have \(\QQ(c_{30})=\QQ(\zeta_{60})^+\) is totally real, and its degree is \(\varphi(60)/2=8\).
From equation \eqref{eq:collision_kernel_A4_cyclotomic_adjacency_injection}, the integer-coefficient minimal polynomial of \(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}}\) has degree \(8\); and since \(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}}\in\QQ(c_{30})\), we must have \(\QQ(t_{30}^{\mathrm{adj}})=\QQ(c_{30})\).
\par
Proposition \ref{prop:pom-e8-intersection-field-discriminant-index} gives \(\QQ(t_{E_8})=K_{E_8}=\QQ(c_{30}^2)\), which has degree \(4\); and \(\QQ(c_{30}^2)\subsetneq\QQ(c_{30})\) with degree ratio \(2\), thus we obtain the indicated strict inclusion and extension index.
\par
Finally, by Proposition \ref{prop:pom-a4t-newman-k2-class-number} we know \(\mathrm{Disc}_t(P_4)\) is negative, so \(P_4\) must contain non-real conjugate pairs, thus \(\QQ(t_\star)\) is not totally real.
Any subfield of a totally real field is still totally real; therefore \(\QQ(t_\star)\) cannot embed into a totally real cyclotomic real subfield (including \(\QQ(c_{30})\)). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral self-duality freezing of the fourth-order kernel family and four \texorpdfstring{$t$}{t}-invariants]\label{prop:pom-a4t-spectral-selfduality-invariants}
Let the characteristic polynomial of the fourth-order five-state kernel family be written as
\[
P_t(\lambda)=\lambda^5-2\lambda^4-t\lambda^3-2\lambda+2.
\]
Then there exists a strict identity independent of \(t\)
\[
\boxed{\;P_t(\lambda)+P_t(-\lambda)=4(1-\lambda^4)\;}
\]
Equivalently, the even part of \(P_t\) is completely frozen, while \(t\) only changes the single coefficient of its odd part.
Thus if \(\{\lambda_i(t)\}_{i=1}^5\) are all the eigenvalues, there are always four \(t\)-invariants:
\[
\sum_{i=1}^5 \lambda_i(t)=2,\qquad
\prod_{i=1}^5 \lambda_i(t)=-2,\qquad
\sum_{i=1}^5 \frac{1}{\lambda_i(t)}=1,\qquad
\sum_{1\le i<j\le 5}\frac{1}{\lambda_i(t)\lambda_j(t)}=0.
\]
Therefore the fourth-order Newman phase transition is not triggered by high-dimensional parameter tuning, but occurs on a spectral section locked by self-duality constraints, completing the crossing with only a single degree of freedom \(t\).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Expanding \(P_t(-\lambda)\) and adding it to \(P_t(\lambda)\), the \(\lambda^5\) and \(t\lambda^3\) terms cancel, giving
\(
P_t(\lambda)+P_t(-\lambda)=4-4\lambda^4=4(1-\lambda^4)
\).
The remaining conclusions follow from Vieta's formulas: since \(P_t(\lambda)=\prod_i(\lambda-\lambda_i(t))\),
\(\sum_i\lambda_i(t)=2\) and \(\prod_i\lambda_i(t)=-2\) are read from the \(\lambda^4\) and constant terms;
meanwhile
\(
\sum_i \lambda_i(t)^{-1}=\frac{\sum_{i<j<k<\ell}\lambda_i\lambda_j\lambda_k\lambda_\ell}{\prod_i\lambda_i}
=\frac{-2}{-2}=1
\),
and
\(
\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_i\lambda_j)^{-1}
=\frac{\sum_{i<j<k}\lambda_i\lambda_j\lambda_k}{\prod_i\lambda_i}
=0
\),
read from the \(\lambda\) and \(\lambda^2\) coefficients. \qed
\end{proof}

On the other hand, the fourth-order Newman critical \(t_\star\) is characterized by the elimination certificate of the boundary condition \(\Lambda(t)=\sqrt{r(t)}\) (Remark \ref{rem:pom-collision-rh-break-a4-threshold}), while the \(E_8\) index intersection is characterized by the level curve \(r_4(t)=r_{E_8}\) (Proposition \ref{prop:pom-a4t-jones-ade-intersections}).

\end{remark}
