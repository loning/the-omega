\section{Hecke dynamics and the prime skeleton: symmetry-preserving cross-scale scanning}
\label{sec:hecke_dynamics}

The Hecke algebra provides a canonical arithmetic mechanism for ``cross-scale'' structure: it is a commuting family of symmetry-preserving operators acting on modular forms, generated by primes and governed by rigid multiplicative relations. This section records the standard facts we need and clarifies the precise role of primes.

\subsection{Hecke operators and symmetry preservation}
\label{sec:hecke_definition}

Let $M_k$ be a space of modular forms of weight $k$ (for $\PSL_2(\ZZ)$ or a congruence subgroup). For each integer $n\ge 1$, the Hecke operator $T_n$ is a linear endomorphism of $M_k$ preserving modular symmetry. In terms of $q$-expansions, if
\begin{equation}
f(\tau)=\sum_{m\ge 0} a_m q^m,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
(T_n f)(\tau)
=\sum_{m\ge 0}\left(\sum_{d\mid (m,n)} d^{k-1}\, a_{mn/d^2}\right) q^m.
\end{equation}
Hecke operators commute on appropriate subspaces and can be simultaneously diagonalized \cite{Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}.

\subsection{Why primes: generators rather than exclusive definitions}
\label{sec:why_primes}

It is important to state the ``prime skeleton'' correctly:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $T_n$ is defined for all positive integers $n$.
  \item The Hecke algebra is generated by the operators $T_p$ at primes $p$.
\end{itemize}
The key relations are
\begin{equation}
T_m T_n = \sum_{d\mid (m,n)} d^{k-1}\, T_{mn/d^2},
\label{eq:hecke_multiplication}
\end{equation}
and the prime-power recursion
\begin{equation}
T_{p^{r+1}} = T_p T_{p^r} - p^{k-1} T_{p^{r-1}}.
\label{eq:hecke_prime_power}
\end{equation}
Thus the full family $\{T_n\}$ is built from prime data.

\subsection{Eigenforms and stable discrete spectra}
\label{sec:eigenforms}

If $f$ is a (normalized) Hecke eigenform, then for all $n$,
\begin{equation}
T_n f = \lambda_n f,
\end{equation}
and typically $\lambda_n=a_n$ in the $q$-expansion normalization. Consequently the coefficients $\{a_n\}$ satisfy rigid arithmetic constraints (multiplicativity on coprime indices and prime-power recursions). In protocol terms: if ``repeatable discrete readout'' corresponds to stable eigen-structures under symmetry-preserving cross-scale operations, then eigenforms supply a mathematically controlled model of such stability, with primes functioning as the generating skeleton.

\paragraph{Euler product: the prime skeleton in closed form.}
For a normalized eigenform $f(\tau)=\sum_{n\ge 1} a_n q^n$ (with $a_1=1$), the associated Dirichlet series
\begin{equation}
L(f,s)=\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{a_n}{n^s}
\end{equation}
admits an Euler product whose local factors are determined by primes:
\begin{equation}
L(f,s)=\prod_{p}\left(1-a_p p^{-s}+p^{k-1-2s}\right)^{-1}
\qquad (\text{level }1),
\end{equation}
see, e.g., \cite{Serre1973,Apostol1990,DiamondShurman2005}. For the discriminant form $\Delta$ of weight $12$, this reads
\begin{equation}
\sum_{n\ge 1}\frac{\tau(n)}{n^s}
\;=\;
\prod_{p}\left(1-\tau(p) p^{-s}+p^{11-2s}\right)^{-1},
\end{equation}
which makes the ``prime-generated skeleton'' literal: primes control the local factors and the Hecke relations propagate them to all $n$.
A compact numerical closure check of this Dirichlet-series / Euler-product identity in the absolutely convergent regime is given in Appendix~\ref{app:quant_fits_euler_product} (Experiment~15).

\begin{remark}[interpretation-layer metaphor: a prime spectrum]
In a prism vocabulary, the Euler product expresses the idea that ``colors'' are prime-indexed: primes determine local spectral factors, and the commuting Hecke algebra acts as a symmetry-preserving family of ``spectral filters'' whose eigenforms are the stable directions (Section~\ref{sec:eigenforms}). This language is interpretive (Layer~2) and is not used as a premise.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Diagonalization is canonical on cusp forms.}
On spaces of cusp forms equipped with the Petersson inner product, Hecke operators are normal and, in standard settings, self-adjoint. Combined with commutativity, this yields an orthogonal basis of simultaneous Hecke eigenforms (a genuine diagonalization frame). See, e.g., \cite{Serre1973,DiamondShurman2005} and Appendix~\ref{app:petersson_selfadjoint}.

\begin{remark}[interpretation-layer metaphor: holographic resonance]
In an interpretation-layer reading, ``eigenform stability'' may be viewed as a resonance condition: when scan dynamics is aligned with a Hecke-eigen structure, readout becomes exceptionally stable under coarse-graining and finite-resolution noise. Any identification of such resonance with cognitive notions (e.g.\ ``intuition'') is semantic and is not used as a premise.
\end{remark}
