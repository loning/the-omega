\section{A minimal worked example (6D): from superspace geometry to symbolic time and fibers}\label{sec:worked-example}

This section provides a compact, fully reproducible case study that instantiates the full chain
\[
\text{CPS geometry}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{finite-resolution readout}\ \Rightarrow\ \text{symbolic process}\ \Rightarrow\ \tau(t)\ \Rightarrow\ \text{stabilized fibers},
\]
including a diffraction-based spectral check. All figures and artifacts are generated by:
\[
\texttt{python3 scripts/run\_all.py}.
\]
The implementation is \texttt{scripts/demo\_6d\_icosa\_fingerprints.py}, and full audit details are recorded in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:6d}.

\subsection{Object layer: a 6D lattice with an icosahedral (3+3) splitting}
We take $G=\RR^6$ with lattice $\Gamma=\ZZ^6$ and choose a decomposition $G=E\oplus H$ with $E\cong\RR^3$ (physical) and $H\cong\RR^3$ (internal). Concretely, the demo uses two explicit projection matrices
\[
P_E:\ZZ^6\to E,\qquad P_H:\ZZ^6\to H,
\]
constructed from the golden ratio $\varphi$ and its Galois conjugate $\varphi^\star=1-\varphi=-1/\varphi$ in a standard icosahedral embedding. For a finite enumeration $\Gamma_L=\ZZ^6\cap[-L,L]^6$ one forms
\[
x=\gamma P_E\in E,\qquad u=\gamma P_H\in H,\qquad \gamma\in\Gamma_L.
\]

\subsection{Protocol layer: a polytope window with finite-resolution fuzzification}
To keep both auditability and a closed-form Fourier envelope, we use an axis-aligned \emph{box window} (a simple polytope) in $H=\RR^3$,
\[
W_{\mathrm{box}}=\prod_{j=1}^3\left[-\frac{L_j}{2},\frac{L_j}{2}\right],
\]
and replace the sharp indicator by a finite-resolution weight $w_\epsilon:H\to[0,1]$ (a boundary-layer fuzzification) with sharp limit $w_\epsilon\to\mathds{1}_{W_{\mathrm{box}}}$ as $\epsilon\to 0$. In the demo, $w_\epsilon$ is implemented as a product of logistic boundary layers along each axis (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:6d}).

For comparison, the same pipeline can also be run with a spherical window $W=\{u:\|u\|\le R\}$; we use the box window in this section because it yields a closed-form $\widehat w$ and therefore a direct diffraction check.

\subsection{Spectral check: module-supported peaks and a closed-form envelope}
For regular (and weighted) model sets, Bragg intensities on the Fourier module satisfy
\[
I(k)\ \propto\ \bigl|\widehat w(k^\star)\bigr|^2
\]
for module wave vectors $k$ with internal counterpart $k^\star$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:diffraction-intensity}). Under the Fourier convention of Remark~\ref{rem:fourier-convention}, the box-window transform is explicit:
\[
\widehat w(\xi)=\prod_{j=1}^3 \left(L_j\,\mathrm{sinc}(L_j\xi_j)\right),
\qquad
\mathrm{sinc}(z):=\frac{\sin(\pi z)}{\pi z}.
\]

In the demo, a finite accepted point cloud $\{x_i\}_{i=1}^N\subset E$ yields an empirical structure factor
\[
\widehat\rho_N(k)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N e^{-2\pi i\langle k,x_i\rangle},
\qquad
S_N(k)=|\widehat\rho_N(k)|^2,
\]
which is compared (up to normalization) against the predicted envelope $|\widehat w(k^\star)|^2$ across a finite set of dual-lattice candidates (saved as \texttt{sections/generated/demo\_6d\_box\_window\_envelope\_scatter.png} and \texttt{artifacts/demo\_6d\_box\_window\_fourier.json}).

\subsection{Induced symbolic process: a torus factor and a probabilistic readout}
To exhibit an $\epsilon$-dependent information acquisition speed, the demo uses an internal scan on a torus factor,
\[
h_t=h_0+t\beta\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\in\TT^3,
\]
represented numerically in $[-1/2,1/2)^3$. A finite-resolution kernel $\kappa_\epsilon$ defines a time-dependent Bernoulli parameter $p_t=\kappa_\epsilon(h_t)\in(0,1)$, and the recorded symbol is
\[
A_t\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_t),
\]
independently across $t$ conditioned on the scan. This is a skew-product construction: the base rotation supplies stationarity/ergodicity, while the conditional Bernoulli layer injects protocol-controlled randomness. Under the ergodicity hypotheses stated in Section~\ref{sec:info-time}, SMB applies and the refinement functional
\[
\tau(t)=-\log\mu\bigl(C(a_{0:t-1})\bigr)
\]
exhibits asymptotically linear growth with slope equal to the protocol entropy rate (Definition~\ref{def:entropy-time}).

\subsection{Estimating $\epsilon\mapsto h(\epsilon)$ from a single long record}
For each $\epsilon$ in a fixed grid, the demo computes a long record of length $T$ and reports two finite-data proxies:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item a block-entropy proxy $H(A_0^{n-1})/n$ at a fixed block length $n$ (stored under \texttt{block\_entropy\_rate\_proxy} in \texttt{artifacts/demo\_6d\_fingerprints.json});
  \item a sequential Markov log-loss statistic $\widehat{\tau}(t)=-\log \widehat{\mathbb{P}}(A_0^{t-1})$ computed by online conditional-probability estimation (stored as \texttt{tau\_sequential\_markov}); this provides an auditable proxy for the refinement functional $\tau(t)$ and yields an entropy-rate proxy as the per-symbol average log-loss.
\end{itemize}
The resulting $\epsilon\mapsto \widehat h(\epsilon)$ curve is saved as \texttt{sections/generated/demo\_6d\_entropy\_rate\_proxy.png} (Figure~\ref{fig:demo6d-entropy-rate}).

\subsection{Fold stabilization and fiber statistics}
At a fixed stabilization depth $m$, the demo applies a Zeckendorf-based stabilization map $\Fold_m:\{0,1\}^m\twoheadrightarrow X_m$ (Section~\ref{sec:stabilization:zeckendorf}) as a sliding-window factor (Definition~\ref{def:Pi_m}), producing a stabilized type sequence. The following reproducible invariants are reported:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item the exact degeneracy histogram $\{|\mathcal{F}_m(x)|:x\in X_m\}$ computed combinatorially;
  \item empirical stabilized type frequencies and $H(\text{type})$;
  \item empirical transition edge counts for successive stabilized types (a finite approximation to the fold transition graph).
\end{itemize}
These statistics address the ``stable fibers'' part of the geometry-to-data pipeline and are archived (together with parameters and seeds) in \texttt{artifacts/demo\_6d\_fingerprints.json}.
