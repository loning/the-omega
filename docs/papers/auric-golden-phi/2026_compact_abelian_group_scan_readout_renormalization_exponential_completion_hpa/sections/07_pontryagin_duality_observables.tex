\section{Harmonic-Analysis Representation of Concept Observables and Clarification of Orthogonality}

\subsection{State-Circle Factors versus Representation-Circle Factors}

When $G=(S^1)^d$, the state layer contains $d$ circle factors. On the other hand, for any character $\chi\in\widehat G$, values lie in $S^1$. Hence there are two distinct notions of ``circle'':
\begin{itemize}
  \item geometric circles in state space (where dynamics lives);
  \item codomain circles of dual-group characters (where representation/readout lives).
\end{itemize}
Conflating these two leads to incorrect inferences about ``the dimensionality of concept generation.''

\subsection{Concepts as Observable Families in \texorpdfstring{$L^2(G)$}{L2(G)}}

\begin{definition}[Concept observable]
A concept $c$ is represented by $f_c\in L^2(G)$.
\end{definition}
Its Fourier expansion is
\[
f_c(x)=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}\widehat f_c(\chi)\chi(x),
\]
and conceptual differences are determined by spectral coefficients. Define the spectral metric
\[
d_{\mathcal{F}}(c,c')=\left(\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G} w(\chi)\,|\widehat f_c(\chi)-\widehat f_{c'}(\chi)|^2\right)^{1/2},
\]
where $w(\chi)$ is a weight (e.g., bandwidth or regularization choice). This places ``conceptual difference'' on computable Fourier data and avoids treating ``circle factors'' as the difference carrier itself.

\subsection{Formalization of Dimension Gating}

Let $\mathcal{H}^{(d)}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^d \mathcal{K}_k$ with $\mathcal{K}_i\perp\mathcal{K}_j$ for $i\neq j$.

\begin{definition}[Adding a dimension]
Adding a dimension means adding a new orthogonal subspace $\mathcal{K}_{d+1}$.
\end{definition}
The readout mechanism can be viewed as selective visibility (gating) of certain $\mathcal{K}_k$, while coordinate renaming does not add dimensions. This distinction is necessary to avoid spurious ``infinite meta-layer ascent caused by coordinate recursion.''
