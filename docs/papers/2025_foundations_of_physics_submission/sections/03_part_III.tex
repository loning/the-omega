\subsection{Decomposition of the Global State}
Can the infinite-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ be decomposed? In standard QFT, spacetime is a continuum, and the Hilbert space is a Fock space over continuous fields. This leads to the ultraviolet divergence problem. Omega Theory takes the opposite view: the continuum is an approximation.
We motivate a regulated localization structure in which the operational state space of a causal region admits an effective finite-dimensional description consistent with holographic entropy bounds (see Theorem \ref{thm:factorization} in Appendix \ref{app:formal_proofs}). In the microscopic/QCA description this is realized as a local tensor-factor model together with a holographically constrained code subspace:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H} \cong \bigotimes_{x \in \Lambda} \mathcal{H}_x, \quad \dim \mathcal{H}_x = d < \infty.
\end{equation}
Physically, $x$ represents a "cell" or "atom of space," and $\mathcal{H}_x$ represents its quantum state capacity. The simplest non-trivial case is $d=2$ (qubits), but we will see that the Standard Model structure requires higher dimensions (likely corresponding to the octonions).

\subsection{The Problem of Isotropy on Lattices}
A regular lattice (e.g., hypercubic $\mathbb{Z}^3$) breaks rotational symmetry. At the Planck scale, this would manifest as anisotropic dispersion relations for high-energy particles. Since we observe Lorentz invariance to very high precision, the underlying discrete structure must be statistically isotropic.
This motivates the use of \textbf{Quasicrystals}. The Penrose tiling and its higher-dimensional analogs (e.g., the projection of the $E_8$ lattice) provide discrete point sets that possess long-range order and crystallographically forbidden rotational symmetries (e.g., 5-fold or icosahedral).
We define the \textbf{Omega Network} $\Lambda$ as a subset of points in $\mathbb{R}^3$ generated by the cut-and-project method from a higher-dimensional integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}^N$ (where $N=6$ or $12$ for icosahedral symmetry). The projection is defined by a window $W$ in the orthogonal space $E_\perp$:
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda = \{ P_{||} \vec{n} \mid \vec{n} \in \mathbb{Z}^N, P_{\perp} \vec{n} \in W \}.
\end{equation}
This ensures that while individual links are discrete, the averaged geometry recovers continuous rotational symmetry (isotropy) in the limit of large point density.

\section{Quantum Cellular Automata (QCA) as Fundamental Dynamics}
\label{chap:qca}

\subsection{Definition of QCA on a Quasicrystal}
A Quantum Cellular Automaton is a unitary evolution $\hat{U}$ on the composite Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = \bigotimes_{x \in \Lambda} \mathcal{H}_x$. Following the operational framework of D'Ariano, Perinotti, and Bisio \cite{Dariano2014}, we require:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unitarity}: $\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{U} = \hat{I}$ (Conservation of probability).
    \item \textbf{Causal Locality}: There exists a neighborhood scheme $\mathcal{N}$ such that the state of cell $x$ at step $t+1$ depends only on cells $y \in \mathcal{N}(x)$ at step $t$. Formally, if $\hat{A}_x$ is an observable at $x$, then $\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{A}_x \hat{U}$ is supported on $\mathcal{N}(x)$.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Isotropy}: While the lattice $\Lambda$ is discrete, the distribution of neighbors $\mathcal{N}(x)$ in the quasicrystal approximation recovers $SO(3)$ invariance in the continuum limit of the propagator.
\end{enumerate}

The global evolution is built from local "coin" and "shift" operators (or partitioned unitaries). On our $E_8$-projected lattice $\Lambda$, we define the explicit update rule as a \textbf{Partitioned Quantum Cellular Automaton} (PQCA). The vertex set $V$ is partitioned into disjoint neighborhoods $V = \bigsqcup_k \mathcal{N}_k$. The update operator is a product of non-commuting local unitaries acting on these neighborhoods:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{U} \equiv \prod_{k} \hat{U}_{\mathcal{N}_k} (\theta, \phi),
\end{equation}
where each $\hat{U}_{\mathcal{N}_k}$ acts on the Hilbert space $\bigotimes_{x \in \mathcal{N}_k} \mathcal{H}_x$.

\subsection{Exact 1D Tape Realization and Routing Depth}
\label{sec:routeA_routing}
This construction provides an exact realization of a bulk-local PQCA step on an effectively one-dimensional nearest-neighbor computational substrate. This makes it possible to define a local \emph{routing overhead} as an intrinsic compilation cost.

Let $R \subset \Lambda$ be a finite patch with $|R|=N$ that is a union of PQCA neighborhoods, so that the restricted one-step update on $R$ is
\begin{equation}
    \hat{U}_R \equiv \prod_{k:\,\mathcal{N}_k \subset R} \hat{U}_{\mathcal{N}_k}.
\end{equation}

\begin{definition}[Tape ordering]
\label{def:tape_ordering}
For any finite patch $R \subset \Lambda$ with $|R|=N$, choose an ordering (a bijection)
\begin{equation}
    f_R: R \to \{1,\dots,N\}.
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Tape encoding isometry]
\label{def:tape_encoding_isometry}
Define an isometric relabeling map
\begin{equation}
    E_R:\bigotimes_{x\in R}\mathcal H_x\to\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal H_i,
\end{equation}
by its action on computational basis product states:
\begin{equation}
    E_R\!\Big(\bigotimes_{x\in R}|s_x\rangle\Big)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\big|s_{f_R^{-1}(i)}\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

For each neighborhood block $\mathcal{N}_k \subset R$, define its tape span
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_k \equiv \max_{x\in \mathcal{N}_k} f_R(x)-\min_{x\in \mathcal{N}_k} f_R(x).
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact 1D compilation]
\label{prop:exact_1d_compilation}
There exists a 1D nearest-neighbor circuit $\mathcal C_R$ such that
\begin{equation}
    \hat{U}_R = E_R^\dagger \, \mathcal C_R \, E_R,
\end{equation}
with compilation depth bounded by
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{depth}(\mathcal C_R)\le C\cdot \max_k(\Delta_k+1),
\end{equation}
where the constant $C$ depends only on an upper bound on the neighborhood size $\max_k |\mathcal{N}_k|$.
\end{proposition}
\textit{Proof sketch.}
Fix an ordering $f_R$ and let $m\equiv \max_k|\mathcal{N}_k|<\infty$. For each neighborhood block $\mathcal{N}_k$, let $I_k\equiv[\min_{x\in\mathcal N_k}f_R(x),\,\max_{x\in\mathcal N_k}f_R(x)]$ be its tape interval, of length $\Delta_k+1$. Since $m$ is bounded, the local unitary $\hat{U}_{\mathcal{N}_k}$ admits a fixed-depth nearest-neighbor implementation once its $m$ registers occupy a contiguous segment of the 1D tape (the required depth depends only on $m$ and the on-site dimension).\par
We implement $\hat{U}_R=\prod_k\hat{U}_{\mathcal{N}_k}$ by a \emph{gather--execute--scatter} construction. (i) \textbf{Gather}: using a nearest-neighbor SWAP network on the tape, route the registers belonging to $\mathcal{N}_k$ so that they become contiguous within $I_k$; this requires $\mathcal{O}(\Delta_k+1)$ SWAP layers because no register needs to move more than $\Delta_k$ sites. (ii) \textbf{Execute}: apply the local circuits for all $k$ in parallel. (iii) \textbf{Scatter}: undo the SWAP network. The resulting overall depth is bounded by a constant times $\max_k(\Delta_k+1)$, yielding the stated bound.

\begin{definition}[Local routing cost field]
\label{def:routing_cost_field}
For a fixed tape ordering $f_R$, define the ordering-dependent local routing cost
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_R(x;f_R)\equiv \max_{k:\,x\in \mathcal{N}_k}\big(\Delta_k+1\big).
\end{equation}
Since $f_R$ is not a physical datum but a relabeling choice for the 1D substrate, we define the physical routing cost by minimizing over orderings (equivalently, over exact 1D nearest-neighbor compilation choices compatible with the relabeling):
\begin{equation}
    \kappa_R(x)\equiv \inf_{f_R}\,\kappa_R(x;f_R),
\end{equation}
with $\inf$ replaced by $\min$ when the minimum is attained.
In the coarse-grained limit we write $\kappa(x)$ for the corresponding routing-cost field.
\end{definition}
We will later denote by $\kappa_0>0$ a reference minimal routing cost (e.g., the weak-field baseline), so that $\kappa(x)\ge \kappa_0$ in the regimes where the lapse identification is used.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Coin--shift form and the ``Golden'' texture.}
For the explicit models analyzed in this manuscript we use coined QCA/quantum-walk steps of the form
\begin{equation}
    U = S\,C,
\end{equation}
where $C=\bigoplus_x C_x$ is a strictly on-site unitary acting on a local spinor $\psi_x\in\mathbb{C}^2$ and $S$ is a conditional nearest-neighbor shift (a basis permutation) determined by the adjacency structure of the underlying graph.
A convenient local coin family is the spin rotation
\begin{equation}
    C_x(\theta(x),\hat{n})\equiv e^{-i\theta(x)\,\hat{n}\cdot\vec{\sigma}},
\end{equation}
with $\vec{\sigma}$ the Pauli matrices. The defining ``golden'' ingredient of our quasicrystal models is that the coin texture $\theta(x)$ is determined by the cut-and-project internal coordinate $x_\perp$ (or, in 1D reductions, by the Sturmian/Fibonacci coding below). As an analytic toy model one may take a smooth modulation
\begin{equation}
    \theta(x)=\theta_0\left[1+\epsilon\cos\!\left(2\pi\,\vec{k}_\perp\cdot \vec{x}_\perp\right)\right],
\end{equation}
with $0<\epsilon\ll 1$, while the discrete (fully specified) Fibonacci-textured model is given in Section~\ref{sec:fib_dqca_spec}.

\subsubsection{Fibonacci/Sturmian coin textures}
The smooth phason modulation above is a convenient analytic toy model. A canonical discrete quasiperiodic texture is obtained by Sturmian (rotation) coding, which in the golden case yields the Fibonacci word \cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}. In a 1D reduction (or along a chosen lattice geodesic), fix an irrational slope $\alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ (golden case $\alpha=\phi^{-2}$) and an intercept $\rho\in[0,1)$, and define
\begin{equation}
    I_L=[0,1-\alpha),\qquad I_S=[1-\alpha,1),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \sigma(x)=
    \begin{cases}
    L,& \{x\alpha+\rho\}\in I_L,\\
    S,& \{x\alpha+\rho\}\in I_S,
    \end{cases}
\label{eq:sturmian_sigma}
\end{equation}
where $\{\cdot\}$ denotes the fractional part. We then define a piecewise-constant coin (mass/mixing) field by
\begin{equation}
    \theta(x)=
    \begin{cases}
    \theta_L,& \sigma(x)=L,\\
    \theta_S,& \sigma(x)=S.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
This produces a Fibonacci-textured (quasiperiodic) Dirac walk that is naturally compatible with the cut-and-project viewpoint: the Fibonacci chain is the standard 1D cut-and-project set with a compact window and hence bounded phason fluctuations.

\subsubsection{A fully specified 1D Fibonacci DQCA (golden texture)}
\label{sec:fib_dqca_spec}
To make the quasicrystal QCA proposal checkable, we now give an explicit 1D model on a Fibonacci (cut-and-project) substrate, including its local gates and the quantitative diagnostics used later for numerical verification.\par
\noindent\textbf{Fibonacci substrate (cut-and-project / Sturmian coding).}
Fix the golden slope $\alpha=\phi^{-2}$ and intercept $\rho\in[0,1)$. Define the tile-type word $\sigma(j)\in\{L,S\}$ by Eq.~(\ref{eq:sturmian_sigma}).
Let the physical coordinate of vertex $j\in\mathbb{Z}$ be
\begin{equation}
    r_{0}=0,\qquad r_{j+1}=r_{j}+\ell_{\sigma(j)},
\end{equation}
with $\ell_L\equiv \phi\,\varepsilon$ and $\ell_S\equiv \varepsilon$ for a microscopic length scale $\varepsilon>0$.
The resulting embedded vertex set $\{r_j\}\subset\mathbb{R}$ is the standard Fibonacci quasicrystal (a 1D cut-and-project set). The intercept $\rho$ implements a global \emph{phason shift}; allowing $\rho$ to vary slowly with $j$ defines a \emph{phason strain field} (Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca}).
\par
\noindent\textbf{Hilbert space and single-particle sector.}
For continuum-limit diagnostics we restrict to the single-excitation (single-particle) sector,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{1p} \equiv \ell^2(\mathbb{Z})\otimes \mathbb{C}^2,
\end{equation}
with basis $\{|j\rangle\otimes|R\rangle,\ |j\rangle\otimes|L\rangle\}$ interpreted as a 1D Dirac spinor at vertex $j$.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Local gates and one-step unitary.}
Define two on-site coin rotations (Long/Short) by
\begin{equation}
    C_L \equiv e^{-i\theta_L\sigma_y},\qquad C_S \equiv e^{-i\theta_S\sigma_y},
\end{equation}
and the position-dependent coin
\begin{equation}
    C \equiv \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}} |j\rangle\langle j|\otimes C_{\sigma(j)}.
\end{equation}
Define the conditional shift on the 1D adjacency graph by
\begin{equation}
    S \equiv \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big(|j+1\rangle\langle j|\otimes|R\rangle\langle R| + |j-1\rangle\langle j|\otimes|L\rangle\langle L|\Big).
\end{equation}
The one-step evolution is the coined quantum walk
\begin{equation}
    U \equiv S\,C,
\end{equation}
which is manifestly unitary and strictly local (range-1 on the chain). This model is fully specified by the parameters $(\varepsilon,\theta_L,\theta_S,\rho)$.

\noindent\textbf{Why 1D is sufficient for verification.}
Although the Omega Network is higher-dimensional, the exact 1D tape compilation in Proposition~\ref{prop:exact_1d_compilation} shows that any finite patch update generated by bounded-range local gates admits an equivalent nearest-neighbor 1D realization (at the cost of routing depth). Therefore, a 1D Fibonacci-textured DQCA provides a computationally tractable testbed for the continuum-limit diagnostics (dispersion, group velocity, and norm-error scaling) while remaining faithful to the locality structure of the underlying PQCA.

\noindent\textbf{Diagnostics for the Dirac limit.}
To extract dispersion on the aperiodic substrate we use periodic Fibonacci approximants: replace $\alpha$ by the rational approximant $\alpha_n\equiv F_{n-1}/F_n$ and impose period $N\equiv F_n$ on the coin texture. The resulting $N$-cell Floquet operator admits Bloch decomposition $U^{(n)}(k)$ with quasi-momentum $k\in[-\pi,\pi)$, yielding quasienergies $E^{(n)}_\pm(k)$ via eigenphases of $U^{(n)}(k)$.
We compare these to the target Dirac dispersion $E_D(k)=\sqrt{(c_{\mathrm{eff}}k)^2+m_{\mathrm{eff}}^2}$ in the long-wavelength regime, and we quantify errors by, e.g.,
\begin{equation}
    \delta_E^{(n)}(k_{\max})\equiv \max_{|k|\le k_{\max}} \big|E^{(n)}_+(k)-E_D(k)\big|,
\end{equation}
and wavepacket-propagation errors
\begin{equation}
    \epsilon_{\mathrm{prop}}(T)\equiv \left\|U^T|\psi_0\rangle-e^{-iH_D\,t}|\psi_0\rangle\right\|,\qquad t\equiv T\varepsilon,
\end{equation}
for band-limited initial states $|\psi_0\rangle$ (Appendix~\ref{app:fib_dqca} specifies the numerical protocol). These diagnostics allow a direct test of convergence and of dependence on phason strain.

\subsection{Emergence of the Dirac Equation}
\noindent\textbf{Standard 1D Dirac QCA.}
To make contact with the canonical 1D Dirac quantum cellular automaton, consider a translation-invariant 1D walk in momentum space with
\begin{equation}
    U(k)=
    \begin{pmatrix}
    n\,e^{ik} & -i m\\
    i m & n\,e^{-ik}
    \end{pmatrix},
    \qquad
    n^2+m^2=1,
\end{equation}
which yields a Dirac dispersion in the long-wavelength limit \cite{Dariano2014,Bisio2015}. In our parametrization one may identify $n=\cos\theta$ and $m=\sin\theta$ (up to conventions), so that the coin angle plays the role of an effective mass/mixing parameter.

It is a profound result, rigorously established by \textbf{Arrighi, Facchini, and Forets} \cite{Arrighi2014} and \textbf{D'Ariano and Perinotti} \cite{Dariano2014}, that the Dirac equation emerges naturally as the long-wavelength continuum limit of discrete quantum walks.
Consider a simplified 1D chain. The single-particle dispersion relation for the quantum walk is derived by diagonalizing the evolution operator in momentum space. For the Golden Gate, we obtain:
\begin{equation}
    E(k) = \arccos(\cos(k) \cos(\theta) - \sin(k) \sin(\theta) \vec{n} \cdot \vec{\sigma}).
\end{equation}
Expanding near $k \approx 0$ with $\theta = \epsilon m$:
\begin{equation}
    E(k)^2 \approx k^2 + m^2 + \mathcal{O}(k^4) + \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2 k^2).
\end{equation}
The deviation from the linear Dirac cone $\delta E = |E_{QCA} - E_{Dirac}|$ is bounded by lattice artifacts scaling as $\alpha k^2$ (discreteness) and $\beta ||\phi||$ (phason strain). Numerically, the group velocity $v_g = \partial E / \partial k$ converges to $c=1$ with corrections suppressed by the inverse lattice scale $1/a$.
By scaling the "mixing angle" $\theta$ with the mass $m$ as $\theta = m \varepsilon$, and expanding to first order in the lattice constant $\varepsilon$:
\begin{equation}
    \psi_{R}(x, t) + \varepsilon \partial_t \psi_{R} \approx (1 - \frac{m^2 \varepsilon^2}{2}) \psi_R(x-\varepsilon) - i m \varepsilon \psi_L(x+\varepsilon).
\end{equation}
This system rearranges into the standard 1+1 dimensional Dirac equation in the limit $\varepsilon \to 0$:
\begin{equation}
    i \gamma^\mu \partial_\mu \Psi - m \Psi = 0.
\end{equation}
Recent work on \textbf{quantum simulation of QED} \cite{Arrighi2019} further demonstrates that U(1) gauge invariance can be preserved exactly in such QCA models by introducing link variables that transform under local unitaries, providing a rigorous basis for our gauge field emergence arguments.

\subsubsection{Discrete vs. Emergent Lorentz Covariance}
How is Lorentz symmetry treated on the lattice? Two distinct frameworks exist in the QCA literature:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exact Discrete Lorentz Symmetry}: As pioneered by \textbf{Bisio, D'Ariano, and Perinotti} \cite{Bisio2015}, one can define a "discrete Lorentz transform" on regular Cayley graphs. This transformation maps the cellular automaton to an equivalent one with modified local rules, preserving causal cones exactly. In this framework, Lorentz covariance is an exact symmetry of the discrete structure ($SO(1,3)_{discrete}$).
    \item \textbf{Emergent Isotropic Symmetry}: The Omega Network approach differs. Since a quasicrystal lacks a global subgroup structure to support exact boosts, we rely on \textbf{emergent symmetry}. The lightcone speed $c=1$ is strict (causal locality), but the continuous Lorentz group $SO(1,3)$ is recovered only via the statistical isotropy of the Golden MERA.
\end{enumerate}
\textbf{Precision of the Approximation}: As proven in Theorem \ref{prop:dirac_limit}, the deviation from exact covariance in the massive propagator behaves as:
\begin{equation}
    \delta \mathcal{L} \sim \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2) + \mathcal{O}(||\phi(x)||),
\end{equation}
where $\phi(x)$ is the phason strain. Thus, while we utilize the local unitary principles of the discrete framework, our global symmetry is approximate, becoming exact in the thermodynamic limit of infinite information density.

\subsection{Theorem: The Quasicrystal Continuum Limit}
While convergence is proven for regular grids, the extension to quasicrystals requires controlling the "phason" fluctuations. We now establish the rigorous limit:
\begin{theorem}[Emergent Isotropic Dirac Dynamics]
\label{prop:dirac_limit}
Let $\Lambda \subset \mathbb{R}^3$ be a cut-and-project quasicrystal. The phason strain is naturally bounded by the finite acceptance window $W$. Let $\hat{U}_\varepsilon$ be a unitary QCA with lattice spacing $\varepsilon \sim \rho^{-1/3}$, respecting the local $G_2$ frame symmetries.
If the initial state $|\psi(0)\rangle$ is smooth (band-limited with $k_{max} \ll \varepsilon^{-1}$) and the phason strain field is bounded, then for any fixed time $t$, the expectation value converges in the norm to the continuous Dirac propagator:
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{\varepsilon \to 0} || \hat{U}_\varepsilon^{\lfloor t/\varepsilon \rfloor} |\psi(0)\rangle - e^{-i\hat{H}_{\text{Dirac}}t} |\psi(0)\rangle || < \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{corollary}[Fibonacci-chain / Zeckendorf-textured DQCA]
The 1D Fibonacci quasicrystal is a cut-and-project set with a compact window and hence bounded phason strain. Therefore the homogenization framework of Appendix \ref{app:homogenization} (in particular Lemma \ref{lemma:ergodic}) applies to Fibonacci/Sturmian-textured walks such as $\hat{U}_\varepsilon=S\cdot C_{\theta(x)}$ with $\theta(x)\in\{\theta_L,\theta_S\}$ defined by a Fibonacci coding. In the long-wavelength regime, the resulting dynamics converges to a Dirac equation with an effective (quasiperiodic) mass/mixing term set by the local coin parameter, up to an error controlled by the homogenization remainder.
\end{corollary}
\textit{Proof Sketch.} The proof relies on the \textbf{Homogenization Lemma} (Lemma \ref{lemma:ergodic} in Appendix \ref{app:homogenization}). The irrational winding of the quasicrystal ensures that anisotropic lattice artifacts in the propagator sum $\sum e^{i \vec{k} \cdot \vec{x}}$ destructively interfere. The surviving term is the isotropic spherical average, effectively integrating over the $SO(3)$ measure. The error is bounded by the phason strain field $||\phi||$, as detailed in Appendix \ref{app:homogenization}.

\subsubsection{Fermion doubling and a cut-and-project projection filter (with bounds)}
\label{sec:doubling_filter}
The \textbf{Nielsen--Ninomiya no-go theorem} \cite{Nielsen1981} shows that under locality, hermiticity, and exact translation invariance, lattice fermions generically exhibit species doubling. In the Omega construction translation invariance is broken at the microscopic level by the cut-and-project internal coordinate (or, in 1D reductions, by a Sturmian/Fibonacci texture), so the hypotheses of the theorem do not apply verbatim.
Rather than claiming an evasion, we formulate a concrete \emph{filtering mechanism} with explicit assumptions and an explicit mass-gap bound.\par

\noindent\textbf{Acceptance window and its Fourier decay.}
Let $W\subset E_\perp$ be the compact acceptance window defining the cut-and-project set, and let $\mathds{1}_W$ be its indicator function. Define the normalized window Fourier transform
\begin{equation}
    \widehat{W}(q)\ \equiv\ \frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(W)}\int_W e^{-i q\cdot y}\,dy,\qquad q\in E_\perp^*,
\end{equation}
so that $\widehat{W}(0)=1$ and $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le 1$.
For windows with piecewise $C^1$ boundary one has a quantitative decay bound $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le C_W/|q|$ for large $|q|$ (Riemann--Lebesgue with one integration by parts), and for the 1D Fibonacci window (an interval) the transform is explicitly a sinc function (Appendix~\ref{app:doubling_filter}).\par

\noindent\textbf{Projection-filter mass term.}
In the embedding picture, modes carry momenta $(k_{\parallel},k_\perp)$ and we introduce a momentum-dependent mass term controlled by the internal momentum $q\equiv k_\perp$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{M}(q)\ \equiv\ \mu\,\bigl(1-\widehat{W}(q)\bigr).
\label{eq:proj_filter_mass}
\end{equation}
This has the desired behavior: for physical long-wavelength modes one expects $q\approx 0$ and hence $\mathcal{M}(q)\approx 0$, while for modes pushed into the internal direction one expects $|q|$ of order the UV scale, for which $\widehat{W}(q)$ is small and $\mathcal{M}(q)\approx \mu$.\par

\noindent\textbf{Gap bound (statement).}
Assume that the cut slope is Diophantine (noble/golden case) so that the internal component of a would-be ``mirror'' momentum shift by $\pi/\varepsilon$ is bounded below by
\begin{equation}
    |q_{\mathrm{doubler}}|\ \ge\ \frac{c_0}{\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
for some $c_0>0$ (Appendix~\ref{app:doubling_filter} gives the 1D Fibonacci case explicitly).
Assume further that the window satisfies a decay bound $|\widehat{W}(q)|\le \delta_W(\varepsilon)$ for $|q|\ge c_0/\varepsilon$. Then
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{M}(q_{\mathrm{doubler}})| \ \ge\ \mu\bigl(1-\delta_W(\varepsilon)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Choosing the UV normalization $\mu=\mu_0/\varepsilon$ yields a physical doubler gap of order $1/\varepsilon$:
\begin{equation}
    |\mathcal{M}(q_{\mathrm{doubler}})| \ \ge\ \frac{\mu_0}{\varepsilon}\bigl(1-\delta_W(\varepsilon)\bigr).
\end{equation}
In particular, in the 1D Fibonacci interval-window case one has $\delta_W(\varepsilon)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ from the explicit sinc bound, so the prefactor approaches $\mu_0$ as $\varepsilon\to 0$.\par

\noindent\textbf{Finite-volume check on periodic approximants.}
On the periodic Fibonacci approximants used for dispersion extraction, a doubler would manifest as an additional near-zero quasienergy branch near the Brillouin-edge momenta. Numerically (with fixed, reproducible parameters), the smallest quasienergy magnitude at $k=\pi$ remains bounded away from $0$ across increasing approximant size, consistent with a lattice-scale (hence physical $\sim 1/\varepsilon$) gap for the would-be doubler sector (Appendix~\ref{app:doubling_filter}).
