\section{Introduction: from unitarity to irreversibility}
\label{sec:introduction}

Modern physics accommodates two time concepts that appear mutually incompatible:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Unitary time:} microscopic evolution is implemented by unitary operators and is, in principle, reversible.
  \item \textbf{Thermodynamic time:} macroscopic readout loses accessible information and exhibits irreversibility and entropy increase.
\end{itemize}

\noindent In the minimal axiomatization of the HPA--$\Omega$ framework \cite{Ma2025FoP,Ma2025HPOT}, this tension is not resolved by abandoning unitarity. Instead it is \emph{layered}: the ontic layer is modeled by a single global state and intrinsic unitary scan; the operational layer is defined by finite-resolution scan--projection readout protocols constrained by holographic capacity \cite{tHooft1993,Susskind1995,Bousso2002}. ``Probability'' and ``time'' become properties of the readout interface, not primitive ingredients.

\paragraph{Thesis.}
This paper gives a micro-definitional, computable answer to ``where does entropy come from?'' in that setting:
\begin{center}
\emph{Thermodynamic entropy is phase friction: arithmetic mismatch accumulated when a continuous unitary phase orbit is projected onto a discrete readout lattice.}
\end{center}
In the golden branch, the readout lattice is canonically organized by Ostrowski/Zeckendorf coding; the mismatch admits sharp computable bounds, and periodic locking for rational slopes becomes a concrete model of ``heat death by phase crystallization.'' These statements require no ad hoc stochastic noise assumption: the randomness is not ontic but readout-induced.

\paragraph{From thermodynamics to intelligence.}
Once entropy is understood as an interface cost, it becomes natural to interpret stable low-entropy structures as \emph{error-correcting subsystems}. We propose that life and intelligence are not violations of the second law but its operational complement: an \emph{active error-correction phase} that uses predictive feedback to locally suppress phase friction while exporting dissipation to an environment, paying a geometric Landauer cost.

\subsection{Contributions and structure}
\label{subsec:contributions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Arithmetic Statistical Mechanics (ASM).} We define microstates as scan phase orbits and macrostates as finite-depth readout statistics and codes (Section~\ref{sec:asm}).
  \item \textbf{Phase friction and entropy.} We define phase friction via star discrepancy and accumulated mismatch, derive computable upper bounds for badly approximable slopes, and interpret entropy increase as an operational coarse-graining monotonicity (Section~\ref{sec:asm}).
  \item \textbf{First-law dictionary.} We propose a generalized first law in which energy is computational flux, temperature is scan tick rate, and work includes geometric routing overhead (Section~\ref{sec:first_law}).
  \item \textbf{Computational lapse and cooling.} We relate gravitational redshift to a computational lapse $\lapse=\kappa_0/\kappa$ and derive a rescaling law for externally observed entropy production (Section~\ref{sec:lapse_thermo}).
  \item \textbf{Gravity as phase pressure.} We interpret gravity as an entropic/phase-pressure response sourced by mismatch density, distinguishing it from purely statistical entropic gravity proposals \cite{Verlinde2011} (Section~\ref{sec:entropy_force_gravity}).
  \item \textbf{Intelligence as active error correction.} We define agents operationally and propose a geometric Landauer principle with an impedance term (Section~\ref{sec:intelligence}).
  \item \textbf{Reproducible toy experiments.} We provide minimal Python experiments validating mismatch growth bounds, rational lock-in, and lapse rescaling (Section~\ref{sec:predictions} and Appendix~\ref{app:repro_code}).
\end{itemize}
