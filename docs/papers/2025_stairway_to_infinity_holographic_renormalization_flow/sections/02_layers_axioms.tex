
\section{Layering and axioms: the audit rule}
\label{sec:layers_axioms}

To prevent interpretive narratives from being silently used as derivations, we keep the logic auditable by explicitly separating three layers.

\paragraph{Layer 0 (ontological layer).}
Only the language of \emph{states and algebras} is allowed. No external time parameter and no external probability postulate is introduced.

\paragraph{Layer 1 (protocol layer).}
One may choose a scan and readout protocol under finite resolution. Operational time and statistics are defined \emph{within} the protocol.

\paragraph{Layer 2 (interpretation layer).}
One maps Layer~1 structures to semantic narratives (spacetime, particles, gravity, entanglement). This layer is marked interpretive and may not be used as a premise in Layer~0/1 arguments.

This stratification is consistent with the broader HPA--$\Omega$ program \cite{Ma2025HPA,Ma2025FoP} and is adopted here as a strict audit rule.

\subsection{Basic axioms (Omega O1--O4)}
\label{sec:axioms_O1_O4}

\begin{axiom}[O1 (Omega: static global state)]
The universe is specified by a quasi-local operator algebra $\Acal$ together with a unique normalized global state $\omega_\Omega$ on $\Acal$. There is no externally given time-indexed family of states.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O2 (finite information)]
For any causally closed region, the effective number of degrees of freedom is bounded by a holographic constraint, typically of the form
\begin{equation}
\dim \hilbert_{\mathrm{region}} \le \exp\!\left(\frac{A}{4\ell_P^2}\right),
\end{equation}
where $A$ is an appropriate boundary area and $\ell_P$ is the Planck length.
\end{axiom}
\begin{remark}
For standard reviews of holographic bounds and related formulations of the holographic principle, see, e.g., \cite{Bousso2002}.
\end{remark}

\begin{axiom}[O3 (causally local discrete update)]
There exists a discrete-step automorphism $\mathcal{U}:\Acal\to\Acal$ which, in a controlled representation, is implemented by a unitary $U$ and has finite propagation range (a causal locality condition).
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O4 (holographic map)]
There exists a bulk-to-boundary encoding map $\Phi$ which is approximately isometric on an appropriate code subspace and supports reconstruction in the sense of algebraic quantum error correction / entanglement wedge reconstruction.
\end{axiom}
\begin{remark}
For standard constructions connecting holography, bulk reconstruction, and quantum error correction, see, e.g., \cite{AlmheiriDongHarlow2015,Pastawski2015,Harlow2017}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Upgraded axioms: scan--projection readout and Weyl pairs (O5--O6)}
\label{sec:axioms_O5_O6}

\begin{axiom}[O5 (scan--projection readout and induced measures)]
Finite observers obtain ``time'' and ``probability'' from a finite-resolution scan and projection readout of intrinsic phase data. There exists a pointer phase $x\in\RR/\ZZ$ whose scan orbit is sampled at integer ticks $t\in\ZZ_{\ge 0}$ by
\begin{equation}
x_t = x_0 + t\alpha \pmod 1,\qquad \alpha\notin\QQ.
\end{equation}
Finite-resolution readout is described by a family of effects $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$ (resolution parameter $\varepsilon$) inducing probabilities
\begin{equation}
P_k^{(\varepsilon)} = \omega_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\left(E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\right),\qquad \sum_k E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\ind,
\end{equation}
where $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}$ denotes the effective state in the observer sector.
\end{axiom}
\begin{remark}[measurement-theory interface]
The family $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$ is a POVM in the standard sense, and $E\mapsto \omega_{\mathrm{eff}}(E)$ is a state on the corresponding effect algebra; see \cite{Kraus1983,NielsenChuang2000} for standard foundations. In algebraic formulations, one may regard $\omega_{\mathrm{eff}}$ as a restriction of $\omega_\Omega$ to an observer-accessible subalgebra (or via conditional expectations), recovering reduced-state pictures when tensor factorizations are available; see \cite{BratteliRobinson1997,Haag1996}.
\end{remark}

\begin{axiom}[O6 (unitary scan algebra: a Weyl pair)]
On an effective observer Hilbert space $\hilbert_{\mathrm{eff}}$, there exist a scan unitary $U_{\mathrm{scan}}$ and a conjugate phase unitary $V$ satisfying the Weyl relation
\begin{equation}
U_{\mathrm{scan}} V = \e^{2\pi \iu \alpha} V U_{\mathrm{scan}}.
\end{equation}
A canonical covariant model is given on $L^2(\RR/\ZZ)$ by
\begin{equation}
(U_{\mathrm{scan}}\psi)(x)=\psi(x+\alpha),\qquad (V\psi)(x)=\e^{2\pi \iu x}\psi(x).
\end{equation}
\end{axiom}

\begin{assumption}[R1 (orbit regularization / finite part)]
Regulated-to-continuum passages along scan orbits are fixed by a canonical regularization convention: ``Abel first, then limit'', which selects a unique finite part for orbit traces and related divergent sums when it exists.
\end{assumption}
\begin{remark}
Abel summability and ``Abel first, then limit'' regularization are classical summability prescriptions; see \cite{Hardy1949}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From constitution to climb: fixing a renormalization-flow object}
\label{sec:renormalization_flow_object}

To turn a static interface into a \emph{cross-scale climb}, we must choose a geometric mother space together with an intrinsic flow that produces a computable notion of ``scale time''. We make the following canonical choice.

\paragraph{Mother space.}
We work with the modular curve
\begin{equation}
X(1)=\PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\big(\HH\cup\{\text{cusps}\}\big)
\end{equation}
and the modular surface
\begin{equation}
M=\PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\HH.
\end{equation}
The cusp structure of $X(1)$ has a single cusp class; in particular, the modular inversion $S:\tau\mapsto -1/\tau$ exchanges $0$ and $\infty$ and identifies them in the quotient.

\paragraph{Flow.}
The geodesic flow on $T^1M$ admits a canonical symbolic coding by continued fractions, via a Poincar\'e section whose first-return map is measurably conjugate to the Gauss map on $(0,1)$ \cite{Series1985}. The roof function of the corresponding suspension provides a computable additive ``scale time'' (recorded in Section~\ref{sec:modular_flow_renormalization}).

This choice pins down the climb in Layer~1 terms: the same slope parameter $\alpha$ that controls scan readout also determines a continued-fraction digit sequence under Gauss iteration, and these digits become the local syntax of cross-scale coding.
