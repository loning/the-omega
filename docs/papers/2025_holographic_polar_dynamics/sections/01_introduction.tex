\section{Introduction: The Collapse and Reconstruction of Geometry}

\subsection{The Singularity Crisis}
The Schwarzschild metric, the first exact solution to Einstein's field equations, predicts its own partial failure. At $r=2M$, the coordinate singularity suggests a breakdown of the coordinate system, which is resolved by passing to coordinates regular on the horizon (e.g.\ Kruskal--Szekeres) \cite{Kruskal1960, Wald1984}. However, the essential singularity at $r=0$ remains a fundamental pathology of classical General Relativity: curvature invariants diverge there (e.g.\ the Kretschmann scalar), signaling a genuine geometric singularity \cite{Penrose1965, MisnerThorneWheeler1973}.

\subsection{The Polar Paradigm}
In \textbf{Paper I (Holographic Polar Arithmetic)} \cite{Ma2025HPA}, we established that linear arithmetic fails at the quantum scale ($\rho \to 0$) and must be replaced by polar arithmetic. In this paper, we extend this paradigm to the fabric of spacetime itself. We propose, echoing 't Hooft's Cellular Automaton interpretation \cite{tHooft2016}, that time is not continuous but a discrete updating process (scanning).
Omega Dynamics postulates that the would-be endpoint of radial evolution is replaced by an inversion channel (Axiom~\ref{ax:omega_inversion}). We validate the \emph{exterior} sector by showing that Phase Pressure reproduces the Newtonian limit and, under standard covariant closure assumptions, closes to the Schwarzschild exterior (Section~\ref{sec:covariant_completion}); we then identify the horizon as the phase-locking/redshift surface (Section~\ref{sec:omega_metric}) and use the classical isotropic Einstein--Rosen throat template \cite{EinsteinRosen1935, Kruskal1960, Wald1984} as the geometric input for the Omega continuation rule.

\subsection{Conventions and Minimal Axioms Used in This Paper}
\textbf{Units.} We use geometric units $c=G=\hbar=k_B=1$ throughout, so the Schwarzschild parameter $M$ has dimensions of length. The areal radius is denoted by $r$ (so spheres have area $4\pi r^2$). We reserve $\rho$ for the \emph{isotropic} (conformally flat) radius used to make the inversion symmetry manifest (Section~\ref{sec:omega_metric}).

\textbf{Scan time.} The scan operator $\Theta$ is a unitary on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$, and discrete time is the iteration index $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$:
\begin{equation}
  |\Psi_n\rangle := \Theta^n |\Psi_0\rangle .
\end{equation}
In a macroscopic continuum limit one may introduce a coarse-grained time parameter $t:=n\delta$ for some fixed scan step $\delta>0$ (cf.\ Assumption~\ref{assump:modular_scan}).
A concrete minimal model (Koopman unitary for an irrational rotation) and its basic consequences are given in Paper~I; see also standard references on Koopman operators and ergodic rotations \cite{Koopman1931, KuipersNiederreiter1974, Ma2025HPA}.

\textbf{Holographic readout and discrepancy.} Classical coordinates and phases are obtained by a coarse-graining/projection of the scan orbit. The resulting mismatch is quantified by a discrepancy functional on the observed phase sequence (Section~\ref{sec:phase_pressure}).

\paragraph{What is classical vs.\ what is Omega.}
The isotropic Schwarzschild form and its inversion identities (Section~\ref{sec:omega_metric}) are standard results in GR. Omega Dynamics enters through (i) the interpretation of discrepancy-induced mismatch as an effective force (Phase Pressure, Section~\ref{sec:phase_pressure}), and (ii) the inversion continuation rule that treats the throat as a holographic readout channel (Axiom~\ref{ax:omega_inversion}).
