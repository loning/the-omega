\section{Upgraded axioms}
We keep the baseline axioms O1--O4 and record the upgrade axioms O5--O8.

\begin{axiom}[O1: Omega axiom]
The physical universe is specified by a unique normalized global state $\omega_{\Omega}$ on a quasi-local operator algebra $\mathfrak{A}$ associated with a countable graph of finite-dimensional degrees of freedom. There is no external time parameter; the fundamental description is a single state rather than a family $\{\omega(t)\}$.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O2: Finite information]
For any causally closed region with boundary area $A$, the effective dimension of the regulated state space obeys a holographic bound of the form $\dim \hilbert_{\mathrm{region}}\le \exp(A/4\ell_P^2)$.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O3: Causal locality]
There exists a discrete-time unitary update with finite-range causal propagation: local observables evolve to observables supported only on bounded neighborhoods.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O4: Holographic mapping]
There exists a holographic encoding map $\Phi$ from bulk to boundary that is (approximately) isometric on the relevant code subspace and supports approximate operator-algebra quantum error correction / entanglement-wedge reconstruction.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O5: Scan--projection readout and canonical coding]
Any operational notion of time available to a finite observer is obtained from a finite-resolution scan and projection of intrinsic phase data. In particular, there exists a circle-valued phase readout $x(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ of an intrinsic parameter $\tau$ and a stroboscopic sampling at ticks $t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ such that the sampled readout is modeled (in the generic aperiodic regime) by an irrational circle rotation with slope $\alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}$. A fixed binary partition of the circle induces a Sturmian symbol stream, and the tick index admits a canonical Ostrowski numeration associated with $\alpha$ (specializing to Zeckendorf coding in the golden case).
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O6: Unitary scan algebra (Weyl pair)]
The scan underlying tick-labeled readouts is implemented by a nontrivial unitary $U_{\mathrm{scan}}$ on an effective observer sector $\hilbert_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Moreover, there exists a conjugate unitary $V$ (a phase/pointer mode) such that
\begin{equation}
    U_{\mathrm{scan}}V = \e^{2\pi \iu \alpha} VU_{\mathrm{scan}},
\end{equation}
with the same irrational slope $\alpha$ as in O5. Consequently there are no nonzero states that are simultaneous eigenvectors of $U_{\mathrm{scan}}$ and $V$, and quantitative uncertainty tradeoffs follow from the Weyl relation.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O7: Projection-induced measure]
Finite-resolution readout induces probabilities as a projection measure rather than as an external sampling postulate. Concretely, for a state $\rho$ and a resolution parameter $\varepsilon>0$, outcome statistics are determined by a family of effects $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$ with $\sum_k E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=I$ via
\begin{equation}
    P_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho\,E_k^{(\varepsilon)}).
\end{equation}
In the sharp limit the effects reduce to projectors and conditioning reduces to the standard L\"uders/Kraus instrument.
\end{axiom}

\begin{axiom}[O8: Orbit regularization and finite parts]
Effective continuum observables are defined as limits of regulated scan-orbit functionals. In particular, orbit averages are defined by orbit traces (ergodic/Haar averages on orbit closures), while divergent orbit sums are assigned a canonical finite part via Abel regularization and pole subtraction.
\end{axiom}
