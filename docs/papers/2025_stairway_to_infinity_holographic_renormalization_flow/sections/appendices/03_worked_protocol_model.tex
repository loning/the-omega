\section{Sharpened protocol statements: why \texorpdfstring{$X(1)$}{X(1)}, worked recovery, and falsifiable closure}
\label{app:protocol_sharpening}

\subsection{Why the modular curve \texorpdfstring{$X(1)$}{X(1)} as mother space? Minimality under protocol requirements}
\label{app:why_X1}

Section~\ref{sec:renormalization_flow_object} fixes a mother space and a canonical climb (geodesic/Gauss suspension) to make the ``scale axis'' computable and audit-friendly.
The choice $X(1)=\PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash(\HH\cup\{\text{cusps}\})$ is not meant as a claim that other arithmetic surfaces are impossible; rather, it is the minimal choice once one imposes a small set of protocol-driven requirements.
\par
\paragraph{Protocol requirements.}
The closed chain in this paper relies on three structural inputs:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A cusp with a canonical continuous--discrete coordinate.}
To extract an integer spectrum by Fourier projection with an explicit damping modulus, we require a noncompact end admitting a canonical local parameter $q=\e^{2\pi\iu\tau}$ (Section~\ref{sec:cusp_qexp_interface}).
Compact arithmetic quotients (e.g.\ Shimura curves) may carry Hecke correspondences but have no cusp, hence no $q$-expansion interface.
\item \textbf{A canonical cross-scale flow with digit syntax.}
We require an intrinsic flow whose Poincar\'e return coding produces continued-fraction digits and an additive ``scale time'' (Theorem~\ref{thm:series_suspension} and Section~\ref{sec:geodesic_flow_gauss}), so that the same parameter $\alpha$ controlling scan readout also controls a computable digit law under the climb.
\item \textbf{A prime-indexed commuting correspondence algebra.}
To close the discrete layer by an auditable prime skeleton, we require a large commensurator giving rise to Hecke operators/correspondences and their prime-generated algebraic closure (Section~\ref{sec:hecke_dynamics_prime}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{A minimality statement within congruence covers.}
Among modular curves arising from congruence subgroups of $\PSL_2(\ZZ)$, the above requirements single out level~$1$ as the unique one-cusp option.
\begin{proposition}[one-cusp congruence minimality (standard)]
\label{prop:one_cusp_congruence_minimality}
Let $\Gamma\le \PSL_2(\ZZ)$ be a congruence subgroup of finite index. If the modular curve $X(\Gamma)$ has a single cusp class, then $\Gamma=\PSL_2(\ZZ)$ and hence $X(\Gamma)=X(1)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{remark}
For standard cusp-count formulas for congruence subgroups (e.g.\ $\Gamma_0(N)$, $\Gamma_1(N)$, $\Gamma(N)$) and the fact that proper congruence subgroups have at least two cusps, see \cite{DiamondShurman2005,Apostol1990}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{A no-go for non-arithmetic quotients (conceptual).}
Non-arithmetic finite-area hyperbolic quotients do not carry a nontrivial commuting Hecke algebra: the commensurator is discrete and does not generate prime-indexed correspondences.
In the present framework, this blocks the ``prime skeleton'' closure step and forces the discrete spectrum to be controlled by different (non-Hecke) structures.
This is consistent with the commensurator-based arithmeticity dichotomy in the theory of lattices \cite{Margulis1991}.
\par
In this sense, $X(1)$ is singled out as the simplest hyperbolic orbifold simultaneously exhibiting a cusp $q$-interface, a canonical digit-coded climb, and a prime-generated correspondence algebra.
Congruence covers and higher-level modular curves remain compatible variants, but they introduce additional discrete data (more cusps/levels) beyond the minimal protocol footprint.

\paragraph{A minimal covolume uniqueness theorem (one cusp).}
If one further asks for the simplest possible hyperbolic mother space under the \emph{single-cusp} constraint, then $X(1)$ is also uniquely minimal in hyperbolic area.
\begin{proposition}[minimal area among one-cusp hyperbolic orbifolds (standard)]
\label{prop:min_area_one_cusp_orbifold}
Let $Y$ be an orientable finite-area hyperbolic $2$-orbifold with exactly one cusp.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:min_area_one_cusp}
\mathrm{Area}(Y)\ge \frac{\pi}{3}.
\end{equation}
Moreover, equality holds if and only if $Y$ has orbifold signature $(g; m_1,m_2; s)=(0;2,3;1)$, in which case $Y$ is (isometric to) the modular orbifold $X(1)=\PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\HH$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $Y$ have signature $(g; m_1,\ldots,m_r; s)$ with genus $g\ge 0$, elliptic orders $m_j\ge 2$, and $s=1$ cusp.
Orbifold Gauss--Bonnet gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:orbifold_area_formula}
\mathrm{Area}(Y)=2\pi\!\left(2g-2+s+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{m_j}\right)\right)
=2\pi\!\left(2g-1+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{m_j}\right)\right),
\end{equation}
see, e.g., \cite{Ratcliffe1994}.
\par
If $g\ge 1$, then $2g-1\ge 1$ and the sum is nonnegative, so $\mathrm{Area}(Y)\ge 2\pi>\pi/3$.
Thus the minimum occurs at $g=0$, where positivity of area forces
\[
-1+\sum_{j=1}^{r}\left(1-\frac{1}{m_j}\right)>0
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{m_j}<r-1.
\]
If $r\ge 3$, then $\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{m_j}\le r/2$, hence
\[
\mathrm{Area}(Y)=2\pi\!\left(r-1-\sum_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{m_j}\right)\ge 2\pi\!\left(r-1-\frac{r}{2}\right)=\pi(r-2)\ge \pi>\pi/3.
\]
So the minimum occurs at $r=2$, in which case
\[
\mathrm{Area}(Y)=2\pi\!\left(1-\frac{1}{m_1}-\frac{1}{m_2}\right),
\qquad \frac{1}{m_1}+\frac{1}{m_2}<1.
\]
To minimize area, we maximize $\frac{1}{m_1}+\frac{1}{m_2}$ subject to $m_1,m_2\ge 2$ and the strict inequality $<1$.
The maximal such sum is $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{5}{6}$, achieved by $(m_1,m_2)=(2,3)$, yielding $\mathrm{Area}(Y)=2\pi(1-5/6)=\pi/3$.
This is exactly the signature of the modular orbifold $X(1)$, and the equality characterization follows.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A fully worked model at finite resolution (explicit error budgets)}
\label{app:worked_model_finite_resolution}

We now give a concrete model realizing the scan algebra and a finite-alphabet instrument, together with a fully explicit coefficient-recovery guarantee for a standard modular observable.

\subsubsection{Effective Hilbert space, scan/readout unitaries, and a finite-alphabet instrument}
\label{app:worked_model_scan_instrument}

Let $\hilbert_{\mathrm{eff}}=L^2(\RR/\ZZ)$ and define the Weyl pair as in Axiom~O6:
\[
(U_{\mathrm{scan}}\psi)(x)=\psi(x+\alpha),\qquad (V\psi)(x)=\e^{2\pi\iu x}\psi(x),
\qquad \alpha\in(0,1)\setminus\QQ.
\]
Fix a resolution parameter $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ and let $K=\lceil 1/\varepsilon\rceil$.
Define a partition of unity by interval windows $w_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\ind_{I_k}$ with
\[
I_k=[k/K,(k+1)/K),\qquad k=0,1,\ldots,K-1,
\]
and define effects by spectral calculus of $V$:
\[
E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\int_{\RR/\ZZ} w_k^{(\varepsilon)}(x)\,\dd\Pi_V(x),
\qquad \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\ind.
\]
At tick $t$, the scan orbit induces a phase point $x_t=x_0+t\alpha\pmod 1$ (Section~\ref{sec:covariant_model}); the finite-alphabet readout returns the bin index $k_t$ such that $x_t\in I_{k_t}$.
Let $\widetilde x_t$ denote the bin midpoint:
\[
\widetilde x_t=\frac{k_t+1/2}{K}.
\]
Then $|x_t-\widetilde x_t|\le \varepsilon/2$.

\subsubsection{A certified coefficient estimator with quantization and sampling terms}
\label{app:worked_model_estimator}

Fix $y>0$ and a modular-form observable $f$ with a convergent $q$-expansion at height $y$ (Section~\ref{sec:cusp_qexp_interface}).
Define the slice $F_y(x)=f(x+\iu y)$ and the integrand
\[
g_{n,y}(x)=F_y(x)\,\e^{-2\pi\iu n x}.
\]
The ideal (continuum) coefficient identity is
\[
a_n=\e^{2\pi n y}\int_0^1 g_{n,y}(x)\,\dd x
\]
(Theorem~\ref{thm:slice_coefficient_reconstruction}).
At finite resolution and finite $N$, define the quantized scan-sampled estimator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:quantized_scan_estimator}
\widehat a_{n,N}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)
:=\e^{2\pi n y}\cdot\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1} g_{n,y}(\widetilde x_t).
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[finite-alphabet recovery bound (sampling + quantization)]
\label{thm:finite_alphabet_recovery_bound}
Assume $g_{n,y}$ is absolutely continuous on $[0,1]$. Let $P_N=\{x_0,\ldots,x_{N-1}\}$ be the underlying (unquantized) orbit point set. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:finite_alphabet_recovery_bound}
\big|\widehat a_{n,N}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)-a_n\big|
\le \e^{2\pi n y}\left(\Var(g_{n,y})\,D_N^\ast(P_N)\;+\;\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,\|g_{n,y}'\|_\infty\right).
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write
\[
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1} g_{n,y}(\widetilde x_t)-\int_0^1 g_{n,y}
=\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1} g_{n,y}(x_t)-\int_0^1 g_{n,y}\right)}_{\text{sampling error}}
\;+\;
\underbrace{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\big(g_{n,y}(\widetilde x_t)-g_{n,y}(x_t)\big)}_{\text{quantization error}}.
\]
The sampling term is bounded by $\Var(g_{n,y})D_N^\ast(P_N)$ via Koksma (Theorem~\ref{thm:finiteN_coeff_bound}).
For the quantization term, absolute continuity gives a uniform Lipschitz bound
$|g_{n,y}(\widetilde x)-g_{n,y}(x)|\le \|g_{n,y}'\|_\infty|\widetilde x-x|$,
and $|\widetilde x_t-x_t|\le \varepsilon/2$. Multiplying by $\e^{2\pi n y}$ yields~\eqref{eq:finite_alphabet_recovery_bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[adding certified evaluation/truncation error]
\label{rem:finite_alphabet_plus_eval}
If $F_y(\widetilde x_t)$ is computed by truncating a $q$-series (rather than accessed as an exact observable), then an additional explicit evaluation error term can be added to~\eqref{eq:finite_alphabet_recovery_bound}. For Eisenstein series, Appendix~\ref{app:eisenstein_tail_bounds} provides closed-form tail bounds at fixed $|q|=\e^{-2\pi y}$.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{A worked modular observable: \texorpdfstring{$E_4$}{E4} with explicit constants and exact-integer recovery}
\label{app:worked_model_E4}

Take $f(\tau)=E_4(\tau)=1+240\sum_{m\ge 1}\sigma_3(m)q^m$ (Appendix~\ref{app:variation_bound_e4_explicit}).
Then $a_0=1$ and $a_n=240\,\sigma_3(n)\in\ZZ$ for $n\ge 1$.
Appendix~\ref{app:variation_bound_e4_explicit} gives an explicit coefficient-sum bound
\[
\|g_{n,y}'\|_\infty
\le 2\pi\left(
n + 240\,\zeta(3)\big(S_4(r)+nS_3(r)\big)
\right),
\qquad r=\e^{-2\pi y},
\]
and the same expression upper-bounds $\Var(g_{n,y})$.
Combining with Theorem~\ref{thm:finite_alphabet_recovery_bound} yields a completely explicit bound in terms of $(n,y,N,\varepsilon)$ and $D_N^\ast(P_N)$ (or its Ostrowski digit bounds, Appendix~\ref{app:dk_koksma_ostrowski}).

\begin{corollary}[exact integer recovery by rounding]
\label{cor:exact_integer_recovery_rounding}
Let $f$ have integer coefficients $a_n\in\ZZ$. If the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:finite_alphabet_recovery_bound} is $<\tfrac12$, then rounding $\widehat a_{n,N}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)$ to the nearest integer recovers $a_n$ exactly.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
If $|\widehat a-a_n|<1/2$ and $a_n\in\ZZ$, the nearest-integer map is unique and equals $a_n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[parameter selection for $E_4$: explicit sufficient inequalities]
\label{cor:E4_parameter_selection}
Let $f=E_4$ and fix $(n,y,N,\varepsilon)$ with $r=\e^{-2\pi y}$.
Define the explicit constant
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:B_ny_E4}
B_{n,y}^{(4)}:=2\pi\left(n+240\,\zeta(3)\big(S_4(r)+nS_3(r)\big)\right),
\end{equation}
so that $\Var(g_{n,y})\le B_{n,y}^{(4)}$ and $\|g_{n,y}'\|_\infty\le B_{n,y}^{(4)}$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:E4_total_error_simple}
\big|\widehat a_{n,N}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)-a_n\big|
\le \e^{2\pi n y}\,B_{n,y}^{(4)}\left(D_N^\ast(P_N)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).
\end{equation}
In particular:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Convergent-length choice.}
If $p/q$ is a reduced rational with $0<|\alpha-p/q|<1/q^2$ and $N=q$, then Proposition~\ref{prop:convergent_star_discrepancy} implies $D_N^\ast(P_N)\le 2/N$, hence
\[
\big|\widehat a_{n,N}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)-a_n\big|
\le \e^{2\pi n y}\,B_{n,y}^{(4)}\left(\frac{2}{N}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).
\]
\item \textbf{Constant-type choice.}
If $\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\ldots]$ has bounded partial quotients $a_j\le A$, then Appendix~\ref{app:dk_koksma_ostrowski} gives
$D_N^\ast(P_N)\le \frac{2A(2+\log_\varphi N)}{N}$, hence
\[
\big|\widehat a_{n,N}^{(\varepsilon)}(y)-a_n\big|
\le \e^{2\pi n y}\,B_{n,y}^{(4)}\left(\frac{2A(2+\log_\varphi N)}{N}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right).
\]
\end{itemize}
In either case, the rounding criterion in Corollary~\ref{cor:exact_integer_recovery_rounding} applies whenever the corresponding right-hand side is $<\tfrac12$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Hecke operators at the protocol level: structural closure versus implementable channels}
\label{app:hecke_protocol_level}

Section~\ref{sec:hecke_geometric_scaling} already records the standard analytic action of $T_p$ on modular forms as a finite average over explicit maps (double-coset correspondences) and its induced action on $q$-coefficients (Proposition~\ref{prop:hecke_on_qexp}).
Two distinct protocol-level roles should be distinguished.
\par
\paragraph{(i) Structural/audit role (used in this paper).}
In the closed Layer~0/1 chain, Hecke operators enter as \emph{constraints on the recovered coefficient spectrum}.
Once a candidate coefficient sequence $(a_n)$ is obtained from the cusp slice by the estimator, the Hecke relations provide locally checkable closure constraints:
coprime multiplicativity and the prime-power recursion (Section~\ref{sec:eigenforms_coeff_rigidity}).
These constraints are naturally interpreted as a prime-indexed ``consistency check'' on the discrete layer, and are directly falsifiable.
\par
\paragraph{(ii) Implementable channel role (additional structure).}
If one insists on realizing $T_p$ as a transformation on \emph{observables} or \emph{instruments} in $\hilbert_{\mathrm{eff}}$, additional interface structure is required.
At the level of modular observables, $T_p$ mixes values at different heights (Equation~\eqref{eq:hecke_prime_formula}), so it is intrinsically cross-scale rather than a symmetry acting within a fixed slice.
Operationally, implementing $O_f\mapsto O_{T_p f}$ would require an instrument family capable of accessing (or emulating) the branches $\tau\mapsto p\tau$ and $\tau\mapsto(\tau+b)/p$ across the relevant scale window, together with certified error control for the induced mixing.
This is a natural extension problem but is not needed for the prime-skeleton closure used here.

\subsection{Falsifiable predictions: prime-indexed regularities and distribution laws}
\label{app:falsifiability_prime_regularities}

When the recovered spectrum is hypothesized to arise from a normalized Hecke eigenform, the following prime-indexed statements become quantitative, falsifiable predictions about the discrete readout layer:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Exact algebraic closure.}
For $(m,n)=1$, one must have $a_{mn}=a_ma_n$, and for prime powers $a_{p^{r+1}}=a_pa_{p^r}-p^{k-1}a_{p^{r-1}}$ (Section~\ref{sec:eigenforms_coeff_rigidity}).
\item \textbf{Ramanujan--Petersson bounds.}
For holomorphic cuspidal Hecke eigenforms, Deligne's theorem gives $|a_p|\le 2p^{(k-1)/2}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:tau_bounds} for $\Delta$ as a canonical example).
\item \textbf{Sato--Tate statistics (when applicable).}
For non-CM eigenforms, the normalized prime eigenvalues $a_p/(2p^{(k-1)/2})$ are expected to equidistribute with the Sato--Tate measure; see, e.g., \cite{Clozel2006SatoTate,Harris2014SatoTate,BarnetLambGeeGeraghty2011}.
\end{itemize}
In a protocol interpretation, failure of these prime-indexed regularities falsifies the claim that the observed discrete spectrum is governed by a Hecke eigenstructure.

\subsection{QUE-type inputs and fluctuation bounds (optional strengthening)}
\label{app:que_connection}

The induced-measure picture in Section~\ref{sec:finite_resolution_readout} is based on equidistribution of rotation orbits and discrepancy control.
Independent from this, arithmetic quantum unique ergodicity (QUE) results provide equidistribution statements for Hecke eigenstates/eigenforms on arithmetic surfaces.
For Maass Hecke eigenfunctions on $\PSL_2(\ZZ)\backslash\HH$, Lindenstrauss proved QUE for a full-density subsequence, with refinements by subsequent work \cite{Lindenstrauss2006QUE}.
For holomorphic Hecke eigenforms of large weight, mass equidistribution (a holomorphic QUE analogue) was established by Holowinsky and Soundararajan \cite{HolowinskySoundararajan2010}.
\par
These results suggest an additional route to bounding readout fluctuations when the underlying observable sector is Hecke invariant: beyond generic discrepancy bounds, one can in principle use QUE-type estimates to control the variance of suitable test observables along arithmetic eigenbases.
We do not use QUE as an input in the present paper; we record it as an optional strengthening direction aligned with the prime-skeleton semantics.

\subsection{Vector-valued readouts and vector-valued Hecke operators (extension)}
\label{app:vector_valued_readouts}

The scan--projection formalism extends to multiplets by replacing a scalar pointer with a finite family of commuting pointer phases (or, equivalently, a vector-valued instrument) and by allowing vector-valued modular/automorphic objects at the cusp interface.
On the arithmetic side, this naturally leads to vector-valued modular forms and Hecke operators acting by matrices on the coefficient vectors.
We record this as a straightforward extension path rather than a new premise.

\subsection{QCA micro-models realizing \texorpdfstring{O3/O6}{O3/O6} (sketch)}
\label{app:qca_micro_model_sketch}

At the constitution level, Axiom~O3 posits a causally local discrete-time automorphism and Axiom~O6 posits an effective Weyl pair.
Locality-preserving unitary dynamics (quantum cellular automata / quantum walks) provide standard concrete realizations of O3 \cite{Arrighi2019QCAOverview,GrossNesmeVogtsWerner2012}.
In simple 1D models, shift/clock constructions generate Weyl-type relations on an effective boundary or observer sector, yielding a natural route to O6.
Deriving the modular cusp interface and the appearance of a $q$-expansion-dominated readout from a specific QCA update rule remains an explicit model-building task; it is compatible with the present layer discipline but not required for the closed Layer~0/1 derivations in this paper.
