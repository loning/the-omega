\section{Closure: field-level labeling of the \texorpdfstring{$21$}{21} stable types}
\label{sec:sm_labeling_closure}

This section closes the Standard Model labeling interface by giving an explicit, computable assignment of field-level labels to the finite stable type set $X_6$.
We work at the chosen anchor scale $(m,n)=(6,3)$, where $|X_6|=21$ and $X_6=X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}}\sqcup X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}$ with $|X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}}|=18$ and $|X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}|=3$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_6}).

\subsection{Field-level targets and audit discipline}
\label{subsec:sm_labeling_targets}

We use ``field-level'' in the Standard Model sense of chiral multiplets per generation (rather than individual Weyl components), with the unique minimal extension forced by the closure of the $18$ cyclic types under anomaly-neutrality (Proposition~\ref{prop:nur_forced_by_minimal_anomaly}).
Concretely, for each generation $g\in\{1,2,3\}$, we consider the six chiral multiplets
\[
Q_L^{(g)},\ u_R^{(g)},\ d_R^{(g)},\ L_L^{(g)},\ e_R^{(g)},\ \nu_R^{(g)},
\]
with the usual $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ quantum numbers under the PDG convention $Q=T_3+Y$ \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
Here $\nu_R^{(g)}$ denotes a neutral singlet (hypercharge $Y=0$) that is absent in the minimal SM, but standard in minimal sterile extensions used to parametrize neutrino mass interfaces \cite{PDGRPP2024}.
Within the explicit minimal candidate family ``one additional multiplet per generation'', anomaly neutrality and the global $SU(2)$ consistency condition force the choice of a sterile singlet with $Y=0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:nur_forced_by_minimal_anomaly}); in particular, adding a gauge singlet with $Y=0$ does not change the SM anomaly-cancellation identities (Proposition~\ref{prop:anomaly_nur}).
This yields $18$ fermion multiplets.
The remaining three stable types in the $18\oplus 3$ split are reserved for the three gauge-factor connection classes, aligned with the three-channel template (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}).
\begin{remark}[Higgs sector and what is (not) covered by the $21$ stable types]\label{rem:higgs_not_in_21}
The $21$ stable types at $(m,n)=(6,3)$ are used here to close a minimal \emph{chiral labeling} interface: $18$ cyclic labels for fermion multiplets and $3$ boundary labels for gauge-factor classes.
This does not, by itself, provide a stable-type label for the Higgs doublet.
Accordingly, when we discuss a field-theoretic embedding (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_eft_embedding}), the Higgs is treated as an additional field required for a renormalizable SM EFT, not as a claim that it must appear as a distinct stable type at the minimal window length.
At the protocol-geometry layer, Hilbert addressing enters through an oriented scan order on the grid, and parity-odd sign data such as the discrete chirality index $\chi$ in \eqref{eq:hilbert_chi_def} are built from signed turns along this order (Section~\ref{sec:hilbert_addressing}).
Accordingly, the minimal labeling interface closed at $(m,n)=(6,3)$ emphasizes chiral/gauge degrees of freedom tied to the orientation-class choice (Section~\ref{sec:chirality_antimatter}).
By contrast, Lorentz-scalar sectors carry no intrinsic handedness and are treated, in the present interface language, as composite or protocol-emergent modes whose effective appearance depends on resolution uplift and on the choice of coarse graining, rather than on the minimal $m=6$ stable alphabet.
The corresponding scalar-sector closure in protocol language is recorded in Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar_sector_closed}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Non-negotiable interface constraints (audit contract).}
From an audit viewpoint, the labeling stage is closed under an explicit constraint set:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Split compatibility.} Cyclic vs.\ boundary types are fixed by the $\pi$-predicate $D_\pi$, so the $18\oplus 3$ split is not negotiable (Section~\ref{sec:folding_core}); we therefore require $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}(X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}})\subset\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}(X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}})\subset\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{SM}}$.
\item \textbf{Standard consistency constraints.} The field-level target set obeys the standard anomaly-cancellation identities and hypercharge quantization under $Q=T_3+Y$ \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}; adding a sterile $\nu_R$ with $Y=0$ is the minimal anomaly-neutral closure of the cyclic count (Proposition~\ref{prop:anomaly_nur}).
\item \textbf{No numerical SM targets in the labeling stage.} Masses, couplings, and fit-derived continuous parameters are not used to construct $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}$; only discrete quantum-number invariants enter (representation dimensions and the integer invariant $(6Y)^2$).
\item \textbf{Deterministic closure.} Any remaining ties are resolved by explicit deterministic rules (Definitions~\ref{def:order_x6} and \ref{def:order_fsm}), and the sensitivity to the SM-side ordering-key choice is audited (Table~\ref{tab:labeling_order_sensitivity}).
\end{itemize}
Within this contract, the labeling is not a free-form assignment: once the protocol-side order and SM-side order are fixed, the cyclic-sector bijection is forced as the unique order isomorphism (Theorem~\ref{thm:labeling_unique}).
To address the concern that the result is a vacuous relabeling, we additionally report inverse diagnostics that test recoverability of several SM quantum-number patterns from intrinsic stable-type invariants by bounded-complexity rules (Table~\ref{tab:inverse_diag_summary} and Appendix~\ref{app:inverse_quantum_numbers}).

\begin{definition}[Gauge-sector complexity order]\label{def:gauge_sector_complexity}
For a gauge factor $G\in\{U(1),SU(2),SU(3)\}$, define its protocol-level complexity by the Lie-algebra dimension
\[
\dim(\mathfrak{g}),
\]
equivalently the number of gauge bosons in the corresponding factor.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Dimension order of the SM gauge factors]\label{lem:gauge_dim_order}
For the Standard Model gauge factors one has
\[
\dim(\mathfrak{u}(1))=1,\qquad \dim(\mathfrak{su}(2))=3,\qquad \dim(\mathfrak{su}(3))=8,
\]
hence $U(1)\prec SU(2)\prec SU(3)$ in the complexity order of Definition~\ref{def:gauge_sector_complexity}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is standard: $\dim(\mathfrak{u}(1))=1$ and $\dim(\mathfrak{su}(N))=N^2-1$; see, e.g., \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Uniqueness of compact simple factors at dimensions $3$ and $8$ (standard)]\label{lem:su2_su3_unique_by_dim}
Among compact simple real Lie algebras, the only isomorphism class of dimension $3$ is $\mathfrak{su}(2)\cong\mathfrak{so}(3)$, and the only isomorphism class of dimension $8$ is $\mathfrak{su}(3)$.
Consequently, if the gauge-sector complexity in Definition~\ref{def:gauge_sector_complexity} is identified with $\dim(\mathfrak{g})$ and one assumes compact (semi)simple gauge factors, then the dimension triple $(1,3,8)$ pins the factor Lie algebras uniquely to
\[
\mathfrak{u}(1)\oplus \mathfrak{su}(2)\oplus \mathfrak{su}(3),
\]
up to finite quotients at the group level.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This follows from the Cartan--Killing classification of compact simple Lie algebras and their dimension formulas; see, e.g., \cite{HallLieGroups2015,FultonHarris1991RepresentationTheory}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[A standard $\ZZ_6$ quotient in the global gauge group]\label{lem:sm_global_gauge_group_z6}
Let $G:=SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ and normalize hypercharge by $Q=T_3+Y$ so that $6Y\in\ZZ$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:hypercharge_six_integer}).
Let the $U(1)$ factor act on a field of hypercharge $Y$ by the integer charge $Q_Y:=6Y$, i.e.\ $u\in U(1)$ acts as $u^{Q_Y}$.
Define the central element
\[
z:=\left(\e^{2\pi\iu/3}\,\ind_3,\ -\ind_2,\ \e^{\iu\pi/3}\right)\in SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1).
\]
Then $z$ acts trivially on all Standard Model fermion multiplets used in this paper (including a sterile $\nu_R$), and the resulting faithful gauge group may be taken as $G/\langle z\rangle\cong (SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1))/\ZZ_6$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For each multiplet $(SU(3),SU(2))_Y$, the $SU(3)$ center contributes $\e^{2\pi\iu/3}$ on triplets and $1$ on singlets, the $SU(2)$ center contributes $-1$ on doublets and $1$ on singlets, and the $U(1)$ factor contributes $\e^{\iu\pi Q_Y/3}=\e^{\iu\pi(6Y)/3}$.
Using the standard hypercharges under $Q=T_3+Y$ for $Q_L,u_R,d_R,L_L,e_R,\nu_R$ \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}, one checks that the product phase equals $1$ in each case.
\end{proof}

We note two standard consistency facts for this fermion content (per generation), recorded here as audit-level requirements and referenced to the standard literature:
(i) the hypercharge-squared sum (with multiplicities) equals $\sum Y^2=10/3$ under $Q=T_3+Y$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:hypercharge_sum_10}), and
(ii) the gauge and mixed gravitational anomalies cancel (a neutral singlet does not affect these cancellations) \cite{BouchiatIliopoulosMeyer1972,PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.

\begin{proposition}[Anomaly cancellation is unchanged by adding $\nu_R$]\label{prop:anomaly_nur}
Under the PDG convention $Q=T_3+Y$, the Standard Model chiral fermion content (per generation, without $\nu_R$) has vanishing gauge and mixed gravitational anomalies.
Adding a sterile singlet $\nu_R$ with $Y=0$ preserves these cancellations.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The anomaly-cancellation identities for the Standard Model hypercharge assignments are standard \cite{BouchiatIliopoulosMeyer1972,PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
A sterile singlet $\nu_R$ contributes $Y=0$ to all anomaly sums and therefore does not change them.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Explicit anomaly sums in a left-handed basis (one generation)]\label{lem:sm_anomaly_sums}
Under the PDG convention $Q=T_3+Y$, write one generation in a left-handed Weyl basis
\[
Q_L,\quad u^c,\quad d^c,\quad L_L,\quad e^c
\qquad (\text{and optionally }\nu^c\text{ with }Y=0),
\]
with hypercharges $Y(Q_L)=1/6$, $Y(u^c)=-2/3$, $Y(d^c)=1/3$, $Y(L_L)=-1/2$, and $Y(e^c)=1$.
Then the mixed gravitational--hypercharge anomaly and the cubic hypercharge anomaly vanish:
\[
\sum Y=0,\qquad \sum Y^3=0,
\]
where the sums include multiplicities from color and weak isospin components.
Moreover, the mixed non-abelian anomalies vanish:
\[
SU(3)^2U(1):\quad 2\cdot \frac{1}{6}-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3}=0,
\qquad
SU(2)^2U(1):\quad 3\cdot \frac{1}{6}-\frac{1}{2}=0,
\]
equivalently $\sum Y\,T(R)=0$ for each non-abelian factor (with $T$ the Dynkin index of the fundamental representation).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For the gravitational sum, include multiplicities of left-handed Weyl components:
\[
6\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)+3\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)+3\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)+2\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)+1\cdot (1)=0.
\]
For the cubic $U(1)_Y^3$ sum:
\[
6\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)^3+3\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right)^3+3\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)^3+2\left(-\frac{1}{2}\right)^3+1\cdot (1)^3=0.
\]
For $SU(3)^2U(1)$, only color triplets contribute, and all fundamental/antifundamental contributions share the same Dynkin index; factoring it out yields $2Y(Q_L)+Y(u^c)+Y(d^c)=0$.
For $SU(2)^2U(1)$, only weak doublets contribute; factoring out the $SU(2)$ fundamental Dynkin index yields $3Y(Q_L)+Y(L_L)=0$.
These are the standard anomaly-cancellation identities in a chiral left-handed basis \cite{BouchiatIliopoulosMeyer1972,PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the minimal extension is chosen as a neutral singlet]\label{rem:nur_minimal_choice}
The role of $\nu_R$ in this paper is purely interface-level and audit-driven: it closes the cyclic-cardinality count to $18$ using the smallest additional bookkeeping burden.
Adding new chiral multiplets charged under $U(1)$ and/or $SU(3)$ would introduce nontrivial local anomaly contributions unless accompanied by additional compensating matter \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
Moreover, adding $SU(2)$ doublets is constrained by the global $SU(2)$ anomaly (Witten anomaly), which depends on the parity of half-integer isospin representations \cite{Witten1982SU2Anomaly}.
Choosing $\nu_R$ as a sterile singlet with $Y=0$ preserves both the standard local anomaly cancellations and the $SU(2)$ global consistency condition while matching a minimal sterile extension widely used in neutrino-mass parametrizations \cite{PDGRPP2024}.
\end{remark}

The construction is auditable in the finite protocol language:
we build the labeling map from intrinsic invariants of stable types (Definition~\ref{def:x6_invariants}), with the Hilbert order entering through the depth assignment in Definition~\ref{def:r_star}.

\subsection{A canonical labeling map}
\label{subsec:sm_labeling_map}

We first define an intrinsic protocol depth from stable-type invariants.
\begin{definition}[Effective protocol depth at $(m,n)=(6,3)$]\label{def:r_star}
For $w\in X_6$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:r_star_def}
r_\ast(w):=V(w)+n\,(g(w)-2),
\end{equation}
where $V(w)\in\{0,\dots,20\}$ is the Zeckendorf value, $g(w)\in\{2,3,4\}$ is the folding degeneracy, and $n=3$ is the Hilbert order at the chosen anchor $(m,n)=(6,3)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Discrete range of $r_\ast$ at $(m,n)=(6,3)$]\label{lem:rstar_range}
At the minimal anchor $(m,n)=(6,3)$ one has $r_\ast(w)\in\{0,1,\dots,26\}$ for every $w\in X_6$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:V_bijection}, $V(w)\in\{0,\dots,20\}$.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:fold6_stats}, $g(w)\in\{2,3,4\}$, hence $n(g(w)-2)\in\{0,3,6\}$ for $n=3$.
Therefore $r_\ast(w)=V(w)+n(g(w)-2)\in[0,20]+[0,6]=\{0,\dots,26\}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why $r_\ast$ is used in the labeling order.}
The quantity $r_\ast(w)$ is an intrinsic protocol-level scalar built only from finite-resolution invariants at $(m,n)=(6,3)$.
It is reused consistently across the paper: it is the same unnormalized depth that enters the closed mass template (Definition~\ref{def:mass_template}) and therefore ties the labeling interface to the resolution-depth spectrum closure by a shared protocol cost coordinate.
Using $r_\ast$ in the cyclic ordering makes the labeling stage deterministic without importing any Standard-Model numerical targets as premises.

We now turn the labeling into a closed optimization statement.

\begin{definition}[Ordering of cyclic stable types]\label{def:order_x6}
On the cyclic sector $X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}}$, define the total order $\prec_X$ by
\[
w_1\prec_X w_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl(r_\ast(w_1),V(w_1),w_1\bigr)\ \text{is lexicographically smaller than}\ \bigl(r_\ast(w_2),V(w_2),w_2\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Ordering of SM fermion multiplets]\label{def:order_fsm}
Let $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ be the set of $18$ chiral fermion multiplets used in Section~\ref{subsec:sm_labeling_targets}.
Define the total order $\prec_F$ on $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ by lexicographic comparison of the tuple
\[
\Bigl(g,\ \dim(SU(3)\text{-rep}),\ (6Y)^2,\ \dim(SU(2)\text{-rep}),\ \text{name}\Bigr),
\]
where $g\in\{1,2,3\}$ is the generation index and $Y$ is the hypercharge under the PDG convention $Q=T_3+Y$ \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Hypercharge quantization and the integer invariant $(6Y)^2$]\label{lem:hypercharge_six_integer}
Under the PDG convention $Q=T_3+Y$, every Standard Model chiral multiplet (including a sterile singlet $\nu_R$ with $Y=0$) has hypercharge $Y\in \frac{1}{6}\ZZ$.
Consequently $6Y\in\ZZ$ and $(6Y)^2\in\NN$ is an intrinsic integer invariant of the field-level label.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is the standard hypercharge assignment for one generation: $Y(Q_L)=1/6$, $Y(u_R)=2/3$, $Y(d_R)=-1/3$, $Y(L_L)=-1/2$, $Y(e_R)=-1$, and $Y(\nu_R)=0$ under $Q=T_3+Y$ \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
Each value is an integer multiple of $1/6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why $(6Y)^2$ is used in the ordering]\label{rem:why_6Y_squared}
Using $(6Y)^2$ replaces the rational invariant $Y^2$ by the minimal integer normalization compatible with Lemma~\ref{lem:hypercharge_six_integer}.
This keeps the ordering rule fully discrete and bounded-complexity.
For the minimal fermion multiplet set used here, the discrete gauge-quantum-number tuple already distinguishes the six multiplet types within each generation, so the final deterministic tie-break can be taken either as the SM name or as a name-free integer code (Table~\ref{tab:labeling_order_sensitivity}).
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Representation dimensions in the SM multiplet set]\label{lem:sm_rep_dims}
Within the Standard Model fermion multiplets used in this paper, the $SU(3)$ representation is either the singlet ($\dim=1$) or the fundamental triplet ($\dim=3$), and the $SU(2)$ representation is either the singlet ($\dim=1$) or the fundamental doublet ($\dim=2$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
This is standard for the chiral matter content of the Standard Model (per generation): $Q_L$ and $(u_R,d_R)$ are color triplets, while leptons are color singlets; $Q_L$ and $L_L$ are weak doublets, while the right-handed singlets are weak singlets \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995,PDGRPP2024}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Generation labels and deterministic tie-breaks]\label{rem:order_fsm_generation}
The generation index $g$ in Definition~\ref{def:order_fsm} is a bookkeeping label for the three copies of the same gauge-quantum-number pattern; any permutation of $\{1,2,3\}$ corresponds to a relabeling at the level of gauge interactions.
We fix the conventional naming order and take $g=1$ to be the charged-lepton reference generation used later in the mass template (Definition~\ref{def:mass_template}).
The final ``name'' entry is used only as a deterministic tie-break when the gauge-quantum-number tuple is otherwise identical.
Within the multiplet set $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ used in this paper, that name tie-break is redundant: the tuple $(\dim(SU(3)),(6Y)^2,\dim(SU(2)))$ already separates the six multiplet types per generation.
Table~\ref{tab:labeling_order_sensitivity} includes a name-free variant (replacing the final name tie-break by $Y_{\mathrm{num}}$) and confirms that the induced cyclic labeling is unchanged.
\end{remark}

We define a labeling map
\[
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}:X_6\to \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}\sqcup \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{SM}},
\]
where $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{SM}}:=\{SU(3),SU(2),U(1)\}$ denotes the three gauge-factor connection classes.
The map is chosen to satisfy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{(split compatibility)} $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}(X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}})\subset \mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}(X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}})\subset \mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{SM}}$;
\item \textbf{(protocol covariance)} rotations preserve labels while reflections/traversal reversal act by the protocol-level $P_{\mathrm{prot}}/T_{\mathrm{prot}}$ rules (Section~\ref{sec:chirality_antimatter});
\item \textbf{(minimality)} among all assignments satisfying the above, we select the unique minimal-complexity solution under a fixed lexical tie-break rule.
\end{itemize}

\begin{theorem}[Closed labeling as the unique order-preserving assignment]\label{thm:labeling_unique}
There exists a unique labeling map $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ such that:
\begin{itemize}
\item it is split-compatible;
\item on the cyclic sector it is order-preserving:
\[
w_1\prec_X w_2\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}(w_1)\prec_F \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}(w_2);
\]
\item on the boundary sector, it assigns the three boundary types in increasing $V(w)$ order to $\{U(1),SU(2),SU(3)\}$ in increasing gauge-sector complexity order (Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge_dim_order}).
\end{itemize}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Both $(X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}},\prec_X)$ and $(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}},\prec_F)$ are finite totally ordered sets with the same cardinality $18$, hence admit a unique order isomorphism given by matching ranks.
The boundary words in $X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}$ have distinct values $V(w)$, hence a unique increasing order.
Likewise, the three gauge factors have distinct complexity values $\dim(\mathfrak{g})$, hence a unique increasing order by Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge_dim_order}.
Matching ranks gives the unique order-preserving boundary assignment.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[CAP minimality of the monotone boundary assignment]\label{rem:boundary_monotone_cap}
Among the $3!=6$ boundary permutations, the monotone assignment is the unique one compatible with the complexity order of Definition~\ref{def:gauge_sector_complexity} and therefore the unique CAP-minimal choice under any monotone mismatch cost between intrinsic boundary value and gauge-sector complexity.
Any non-monotone assignment would map a larger intrinsic boundary value to a lower-complexity gauge factor, creating an avoidable ordering mismatch that must be compensated elsewhere in the interface.
\end{remark}

Operationally, the assignment is therefore constructed as follows (equivalently: among the $3!=6$ boundary permutations, it is the unique one that is monotone in the intrinsic value $V(w)$ and the gauge-sector complexity).
\begin{itemize}
\item The three boundary types are sorted by $V(w)$ and assigned to $\{U(1),SU(2),SU(3)\}$ in increasing gauge-sector complexity order (Definition~\ref{def:gauge_sector_complexity}).
\item The $18$ cyclic types are sorted by $\prec_X$ and assigned to the $18$ fermion multiplets sorted by $\prec_F$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{remark}[Explicit boundary-value order at $m=6$]\label{rem:boundary_value_order}
At $m=6$, the boundary words are $100001$, $101001$, and $100101$ (Corollary~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_6}).
Their Zeckendorf values are $V(100001)=14$, $V(101001)=17$, and $V(100101)=19$ (Definition~\ref{def:zeckendorf_value}).
Thus the intrinsic increasing boundary order is
\[
100001\ \prec\ 101001\ \prec\ 100101,
\]
which is the order used for the unique monotone assignment to $U(1)\prec SU(2)\prec SU(3)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gauge_dim_order}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Determinism vs.\ reproducibility]
\label{rem:labeling_script_status}
Theorem~\ref{thm:labeling_unique} is the logical closure: it pins a unique map
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ once the two total orders $\prec_X$ and $\prec_F$
and the boundary monotonicity convention are fixed.
The script \texttt{scripts/exp\_sm\_labeling\_solver.py} is only a deterministic
implementation of this rank-matching construction used to \emph{reproduce} the
table rows and to avoid manual transcription errors; it is not an additional
premise and it does not select among multiple solutions.
\end{remark}
