
\section{Falsifiability: observational tests and executable protocols}
\label{sec:falsifiability}

The framework is only meaningful insofar as it yields quantitative, defeasible predictions.
This section records falsifiable signatures and directs the reader to an executable reconstruction protocol (Appendix~\ref{app:data_protocol}).

\subsection{Dark-matter clustering from Hilbert addresses and folding statistics}
\label{subsec:test_dm_clustering}

\paragraph{Address-binned statistics.}
Fix an address order $n$ and a Hilbert map $H_n$.
Let $\rho_s$ denote an observed (or simulated) mass density assigned to the cell with Hilbert index $s\in\{0,\dots,2^{2n}-1\}$, and define a density contrast $\delta_s=(\rho_s-\bar\rho)/\bar\rho$.
Two minimal address-aware correlators are:
\[
C_1:=\mathbb{E}(\delta_s\delta_{s+1}),
\qquad
C_L:=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\bar{\delta}_{s,L}\,\bar{\delta}_{s+L,L}\right),
\quad
\bar{\delta}_{s,L}:=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}\delta_{s+j},
\]
with expectations taken over $s$ (and over an ensemble if available).
These statistics quantify clustering along the scan order and across block scales $L$.
Hilbert indexing is known to have strong locality-preserving clustering properties relevant for such diagnostics \cite{MoonJagadishFaloutsosSaltz2001}.

\begin{lemma}[Layout invariance of adjacency-based Hilbert statistics]
\label{lem:layout_invariance}
Let $R$ be a dihedral symmetry of the square grid and let $H_n'$ be the Hilbert map obtained by composing $H_n$ with $R$ (a global layout change).
Then any statistic that depends only on index adjacency and block aggregation along $s$ (such as $C_1$ and $C_L$) is invariant under this layout change.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A global layout change is a permutation of grid cells induced by an isometry.
It relabels the index-to-cell correspondence without changing the adjacency structure of the scan order (up to a global reindexing of $s$ and, at most, reversal).
Averages over $s$ of functions of the form $F(\delta_s,\delta_{s+1},\dots,\delta_{s+L})$ are therefore unchanged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Prediction P1 (address-aware clustering invariants).}
If the dark sector is an effective source induced by Hilbert-addressed readout and folding overhead, then dark-matter clustering statistics should exhibit invariances tied to the address family and its layout symmetries.
In particular, adjacency-based statistics computed after Hilbert binning should be rigid under global layout changes of the address family (dihedral symmetries), while still differing from generic isotropic fluid predictions.

\paragraph{How to test.}
Given an $N$-body simulation or galaxy survey, construct a Hilbert index at fixed order and compute:
\begin{itemize}
\item standard two-point functions in Euclidean coordinates;
\item address-neighbor and block-aggregation correlators (e.g.\ $C_1$ and $C_L$) in Hilbert coordinates;
\item the reconstructed overhead proxy $\chi(x)$ from folding statistics (Appendix~\ref{app:data_protocol}).
\end{itemize}
The prediction is that a family of address-aware statistics remains stable under layout changes and correlates with lensing-inferred mass maps more strongly than visible-matter proxies alone \cite{BartelmannSchneider2001}.
In particular, a minimal quantitative diagnostic is the Hilbert-index cross-correlation between $\chi$ and density contrast at block scale $L$,
\[
R_L:=\mathbb{E}\!\left(\chi_s\,\bar{\delta}_{s,L}\right),
\]
which should be positive and scale-stable if overhead is a genuine gravitational source (Section~\ref{subsec:newton_limit}).

\subsection{Geometric drift of effective constants in high-overhead regions}
\label{subsec:test_constants_drift}

\paragraph{Prediction P2 (environment-dependent drift).}
If dimensionless ``constants'' are derived from geometry and readout structure, then regions with anomalously high overhead (large $\chi$) should exhibit a small but systematic drift in effective couplings.
The drift should scale with $\chi$ (or with the corresponding gravitational potential) and be most visible near strong lenses or compact objects \cite{Ma2025PCG}.
For observational reviews and constraint baselines on varying couplings, see \cite{Uzan2011}.

\paragraph{How to test.}
Cross-correlate high-precision spectroscopic measurements with reconstructed gravitational potentials and (where available) overhead proxies inferred from lensing/time-delay data.
The key criterion is not the existence of drift per se, but the specific scaling relation predicted by the model (set by the chosen $\chi$-coupling in the action).

\subsection{Early-universe scanline artifacts and spectral-template biases}
\label{subsec:test_scanline}

\paragraph{Prediction P3 (scanline imprints).}
If the early universe is a finite-resolution readout of a Hilbert-addressed scan, then residual ``scanline'' artifacts should exist at some level in CMB or large-scale-structure statistics.
The artifacts should be template-compatible with Hilbert recursion scales and should not be reducible to generic Gaussian initial conditions.

\paragraph{How to test.}
Construct anisotropy templates based on Hilbert recursion motifs at candidate orders and test against CMB residual maps and higher-order statistics \cite{Planck2018Isotropy}.

\subsection{Dispersion constraints and symmetry selection}
\label{subsec:test_dispersion}

\paragraph{Prediction P4 (leading-order dispersion constraints).}
In an overhead-coupled effective field theory, dispersion corrections may be constrained by symmetry.
If a robust linear-in-energy quantum-gravity dispersion term is observed, it can directly falsify symmetry-protected readout assumptions.
Conversely, the persistent absence of linear terms with higher-order corrections would be compatible with a constrained readout symmetry class.

\subsection{Model closure: parameters, calibration, and what is (not) fitted}
\label{subsec:model_closure}
The framework is intended to be closed in the sense of having a minimal parameter count and a data-executable reconstruction of the key field $\chi$.
At the level used in this paper, the specification consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{discrete} effective window length $m$ (Section~\ref{subsec:energy_budget_fit}), fixed by the background energy budget once the visible-sector proxy is chosen;
\item analysis choices for extracting binary words from data (Hilbert order $n$, window placement, and a threshold/quantile rule), whose role is to define the measurable map from surveys/simulations into $\Omega_m$ (Appendix~\ref{app:data_protocol}); robustness is assessed by varying these choices;
\item a single \emph{dimensionless} coupling $\gamma$ converting overhead ratios into lapse strength, fitted by one-parameter regression from rotation curves or lensing/time-delay data (Section~\ref{subsec:rotation_fit});
\item a background dark-energy normalization (equivalently $\Lambda$ or $V_0$), fixed directly by $\Omega_{\Lambda,0}$ once $G_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is specified (Section~\ref{subsec:de_grid_tension}).
\end{itemize}
All spatial structure is carried by $\chi(x)$ reconstructed from folding statistics rather than introduced as a new matter field; the falsifiability tests evaluate whether the reconstructed $\chi$ obeys the predicted invariances and correlates with gravitational observables.
