\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\varph$}{phi}: Self-similar substitution and Perron--Frobenius eigenvalue}

The appearance of $\varph$ has two equivalent characterizations:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The exponential growth rate of stable type counts: $h_{\mathrm{top}}=\log\varph$;
  \item The largest eigenvalue of the substitution matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ of the Fibonacci substitution (e.g., $0\mapsto 01,1\mapsto 0$).
\end{enumerate}
Thus $\varph$ characterizes ``syntactically stable self-similar growth'' and determines the effective dimension of the golden existence state.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$\pi$}{pi}: Closed phase geometry and periodic orbit invariants}

$\pi$ appears in this paper as the metric normalization constant for closed phase geometry: when the readout object has periodic identification (e.g., phase on $U(1)$) or requires counting and regularization of periodic orbits, $\pi$ appears as the measure unit for circles, group volumes, or orbit integrals.
In the three-channel language of this paper, the $\pi$ channel corresponds to ``endpoint closure'' and ``periodic orbit admissibility'', with its discrete manifestation being the Lucas trace structure of $\card{X_m^{\mathrm{cyc}}}$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$e$}{e}: The unique continuous readout base from multiplicative weight to additive cost}

When interpreting multiplicative composition weights as additive ``cost'' or ``impedance'', the logarithmic readout is uniquely determined by structural necessity, and $e$ thereby becomes the normalization constant for this readout choice:
\[
C=-\log w,\qquad w=e^{-C}.
\]
When analytic stability is characterized through $\zeta$ functions, generating functions, or transfer operator spectral radius, exponential decay and pole barriers further establish $e$ as the natural base for ``analytic stability domains''.

\subsection{Primes: Multiplicative generators and spectral organization skeleton}

The role of primes in this framework derives from the generative property of the multiplicative skeleton: the decomposition of any positive integer $n$ as
\[
n=\prod_{p} p^{v_p(n)}
\]
resolves multiplicative structure into independent counting along prime directions. If the phase data or weight data in the ontological layer is defined as multiplicative homomorphisms (or their generalizations), then all its information is determined by its values at primes.
At higher-order spectral organization, primes can also serve as indices for a family of commuting operators (e.g., labels for Hecke actions), thereby organizing the ``observed spectrum'' as a collection of locally-parametrized data indexed by primes. Here the role of primes can be summarized as: minimal generating labels for internal degrees of freedom.
