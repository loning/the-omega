\[\mathcal{C}_\Omega = \left\{ x^\mu = \sum_{i=1}^4 \lambda_i k_i^\mu \ \bigg| \ 0 \le \lambda_i \le 1 \right\}\]

Unlike standard lattices, the edge lengths of Omega cells are not arbitrary but strictly quantized. According to the holographic principle, each cell encodes finite bits of information.

\subsection{Projection Method Construction and the Golden Ratio}

How do Omega cells arrange in vacuum? They follow the rules of \textbf{Penrose Tiling}. This tiling can be generated from high-dimensional hypercubic lattices through the \textbf{``Cut-and-Project Method''}.

Consider an $N$-dimensional Euclidean space $\mathbb{R}^N$ (for 3D quasicrystals, typically $N=6$), containing a hypercubic lattice $\mathbb{Z}^N$. We select a low-dimensional subspace $E$ (physical space) passing through the origin, such that the direction of $E$ relative to the lattice axes is determined by the \textbf{Golden Ratio $\phi$}.

\textbf{Construction Process}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Slice}: Define a ``strip'' $S = E + K$, where $K$ is the projection of the $N$-dimensional unit hypercube onto the orthogonal complement space $E^\perp$.
\item \textbf{Select}: Choose all lattice points $x \in \mathbb{Z}^N \cap S$ that fall within the strip $S$.
\item \textbf{Project}: Orthogonally project these points onto the physical subspace $E$.
\end{enumerate}

The resulting point set constitutes vertices $V$ in physical space. The edges connecting these vertices form the skeleton of Omega cells. Since the projection slope involves the irrational number $\phi$, the resulting structure has \textbf{Self-similarity} and \textbf{aperiodicity}.

More importantly, Penrose tiling supports \textbf{``Inflation/Deflation''} operations. Through specific rules, each rhombus in the tiling can be decomposed into smaller self-similar rhombi, and vice versa.

In Omega Theory, this mathematical operation corresponds to physical \textbf{cosmic expansion}.

\[a(\tau_{n+1}) = \phi^{-1} a(\tau_n)\]
