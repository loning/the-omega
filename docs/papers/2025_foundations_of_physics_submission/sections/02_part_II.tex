\subsection{Axiom O1: The Static Universe Vector}
We begin with the most radical yet simplest postulate of the theory.

\begin{axiom}[The Omega Axiom]
There exists a countable graph $G=(V,E)$ and a family of finite-dimensional local Hilbert spaces $\{\mathcal{H}_v\}_{v\in V}$ with $\dim \mathcal{H}_v=d<\infty$. Let $\mathfrak{A}$ denote the associated quasi-local operator algebra,
\begin{equation}
    \mathfrak{A}\ \equiv\ \overline{\bigcup_{K\subset V\ \mathrm{finite}}\ \mathcal{B}\!\left(\bigotimes_{v\in K}\mathcal{H}_v\right)}.
\end{equation}
Here the closure is taken in the operator norm (the standard quasi-local $C^\ast$-algebra completion; see, e.g., \cite{BratteliRobinson1987OAQSM1}).
The physical universe is specified by a unique normalized global state $\omega_{\Omega}$ on $\mathfrak{A}$. There is no external time parameter $t_{\text{ext}}$: the fundamental description is a single state rather than a family $\{\omega(t_{\text{ext}})\}$. Equivalently, if one formally introduces an external time label, the state is stationary,
\begin{equation}
    \omega_{\Omega}(t_{\text{ext}})=\omega_{\Omega}.
\end{equation}
The "state of the universe" does not change; it simply \textit{is}.
\end{axiom}

\noindent\textit{Remark (vector notation).} Throughout this manuscript we use Dirac notation and treat $|\Omega\rangle$ as a convenient representative of $\omega_{\Omega}$ in a fixed representation (e.g.\ a GNS representation) of the quasi-local algebra, yielding a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and a cyclic vector $|\Omega\rangle$. In such a representation, the corresponding von Neumann algebra is obtained as the weak closure $\pi_\Omega(\mathfrak{A})''$ \cite{BratteliRobinson1987OAQSM1,Takesaki2002}.

\subsection{Intrinsic Time from Spectral Decomposition}
If the universe is static, whence comes the appearance of change? Time is emergent. It is defined internally by comparing the static vector $|\Omega\rangle$ against a reference basis.
Let $\hat{H}$ be a self-adjoint generator defining an orthogonal basis $\{|E_n\rangle\}$ (e.g.\ an effective Hamiltonian or a modular Hamiltonian in a regulated setting). The vector $|\Omega\rangle$ can be decomposed as:
\begin{equation}
    |\Omega\rangle = \sum_n c_n |E_n\rangle, \quad \sum_n |c_n|^2 = 1.
\end{equation}
We define a 1-parameter family of states $|\Omega(\tau)\rangle$ by injecting phase factors relative to the energy eigenbasis:
\begin{equation}
    |\Omega(\tau)\rangle \equiv \sum_n c_n e^{-\iu E_n \tau/\hbar} |E_n\rangle.
\end{equation}
\begin{definition}[Modular intrinsic time (thermal-time parameter)]
\label{def:modular_time}
Let $\mathfrak{M}$ be an observer algebra (for instance, a type-I factor associated with a regulated region as in the split-property discussion) and let $\omega$ be the restriction of the Omega state to $\mathfrak{M}$. If $\omega$ is faithful, Tomita--Takesaki theory provides a canonical modular automorphism group $\{\sigma_\tau^\omega\}_{\tau\in\mathbb{R}}$ on $\mathfrak{M}$ \cite{Takesaki2002}; the interpretation of the modular parameter as an intrinsic ``thermal time'' is discussed in \cite{ConnesRovelli1994}. In a finite-dimensional regulated description where $\mathfrak{M}\cong\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}})$ and $\omega(A)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho A)$ with full-rank $\rho$, one has explicitly
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_\tau^\omega(A)=\rho^{i\tau}A\rho^{-i\tau}=e^{-i\tau K}A\,e^{i\tau K},\qquad K\equiv -\ln\rho,
\end{equation}
and we call $\tau$ the \emph{modular intrinsic time}. In this manuscript, the phase-twisted family $|\Omega(\tau)\rangle$ should be read as a choice of representative compatible with such an intrinsic flow; concretely one may take $\hat H$ proportional to a modular Hamiltonian $K$ on the relevant effective sector (Appendix~\ref{app:modular_time}).
\end{definition}

\noindent\textit{Remark (picture).} In operator-algebraic language, modular flow is the automorphism $\sigma_\tau^\omega$ acting on observables $A\in\mathfrak{M}$, while the state is invariant in the sense $\omega\circ\sigma_\tau^\omega=\omega$ \cite{Takesaki2002}. When we write $|\Omega(\tau)\rangle$, we are choosing (in a regulated type-I sector) a convenient representative whose adjoint action matches the modular flow on that sector; the physical content is in the induced expectations and readouts, not in the arbitrary phase convention for the representative vector.
\par
\noindent\textit{Remark (Schr\"odinger vs.\ Heisenberg representatives).} Whenever a one-parameter unitary family $U_\tau$ implements an automorphism $\alpha_\tau(A)=U_\tau^\dagger A U_\tau$ on the relevant effective algebra, one may equivalently represent the same expectation data either by evolving observables with a fixed state or by evolving a representative vector $|\Omega(\tau)\rangle=U_\tau|\Omega\rangle$ with fixed observables; see Appendix~\ref{app:formal_proofs}, Proposition~\ref{prop:pictures_automorphisms}.

\subsection{Axiom O5: Scan--Projection Readout (and induced measure)}
\begin{axiom}[Scan--Projection Readout Axiom]
Any operational notion of time available to a finite observer is obtained from a finite-resolution scan and projection of intrinsic phase data. In particular, there exists a circle-valued phase readout $x(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ of an intrinsic parameter $\tau$ and a stroboscopic sampling at discrete ticks $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ such that the sampled readout is modeled (in the generic aperiodic regime) by an irrational circle rotation with slope $\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}$.
\par
Moreover, finite-resolution readout induces probabilities as a projection measure rather than as an external sampling postulate: for an effective observer-sector state $\rho$ and resolution parameter $\varepsilon>0$, outcome statistics are determined by a family of effects $\{E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\}$ with $\sum_k E_k^{(\varepsilon)}=I$ via
\begin{equation}
    P_k^{(\varepsilon)}=\mathrm{Tr}\!\left(\rho\,E_k^{(\varepsilon)}\right).
\end{equation}
In the sharp limit (ideal readout) the effects reduce to projectors and conditioning reduces to the standard L{\"u}ders/Kraus instrument \cite{Kraus1983,NielsenChuang2000}.
\end{axiom}
\noindent\textit{Remark (mathematical realization).} A self-contained scan--projection model (unitary scan, window projection, and canonical Ostrowski/Zeckendorf coordinates) is developed in the companion mathematical manuscript \cite{Ma2025HPA}. Standard consequences of the rotation-and-window template (Sturmian coding and canonical Ostrowski/Zeckendorf coordinates) are recorded below as definitions and propositions, rather than as additional axioms.

\subsection{Axiom O6: Unitary scanning and intrinsic noncommutativity}
\begin{axiom}[Unitary Scan Algebra Axiom]
The scan underlying tick-labeled readouts is implemented by a nontrivial unitary $U_{\mathrm{scan}}$ on an effective observer sector $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}$. Moreover, there exists a conjugate unitary $V$ (a phase/pointer mode) such that
\begin{equation}
    U_{\mathrm{scan}}V \;=\; e^{2\pi i \alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}}\,VU_{\mathrm{scan}},
\end{equation}
with the same irrational slope $\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}$ that controls the scan orbit in Axiom~O5. Consequently, scan-time and phase/pointer localization are incompatible: there are no nonzero states that are simultaneous eigenvectors of $U_{\mathrm{scan}}$ and $V$, and quantitative uncertainty tradeoffs follow from the Weyl relation.
\end{axiom}
\noindent\textit{Remark (minimal model).} A concrete realization is given by $\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{eff}}=L^2(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z})$: define the Koopman (shift) unitary
\begin{equation}
    (U_{\mathrm{scan}}f)(x)\ \equiv\ f(x+\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}),
\end{equation}
and the multiplication (phase) unitary
\begin{equation}
    (Vf)(x)\ \equiv\ e^{2\pi i x}\,f(x).
\end{equation}
Then $U_{\mathrm{scan}}$ and $V$ satisfy $U_{\mathrm{scan}}V=e^{2\pi i \alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}}VU_{\mathrm{scan}}$ by direct computation. The companion manuscript \cite{Ma2025HPA} develops this scan model (including window projections and induced coding) and records quantitative uncertainty inequalities for such Weyl pairs.

\subsection{Convention R1: Orbit regularization and finite parts}
\label{conv:orbit_regularization}
Whenever a regularization-dependent constant term must be extracted in regulated-to-continuum passages, we adopt a canonical orbit-based prescription: orbit averages are taken as orbit traces (ergodic/Haar averages on orbit closures), and divergent orbit sums (when they appear) are assigned a finite part via Abel regularization and pole subtraction. A self-contained orbit-calculus template is developed in \cite{Ma2025HPA} and recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:orbit_calculus}.
The intrinsic parameter $\tau$ serves as a coordinate parametrizing a curve $\mathcal{C}$ in the unit sphere of $\mathcal{H}$ (and hence a trajectory in the projective space $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$). To connect this continuous intrinsic evolution to a discrete computational clock, we introduce a canonical one-dimensional phase readout of the intrinsic phase orbit.

\begin{definition}[Intrinsic phase-orbit readout (projective)]
Fix two reference vectors $|r_0\rangle,|r_1\rangle\in\mathcal{H}$ such that $\langle r_a|\Omega(\tau)\rangle\neq 0$ for $a=0,1$ on the times of interest (e.g., $|r_0\rangle=|E_0\rangle$ with $c_0\neq 0$). Define the phase readout
\begin{equation}
    x(\tau)\ \equiv\ \frac{1}{2\pi}\arg\!\left(\frac{\langle r_1|\Omega(\tau)\rangle}{\langle r_0|\Omega(\tau)\rangle}\right)\ \ \bmod 1,
\end{equation}
which maps the intrinsic phase orbit to a circle variable $x(\tau)\in\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$.
\end{definition}
\noindent\textit{Remark (gauge invariance).} The ratio in the definition is invariant under the $\tau$-dependent global phase choice $|\Omega(\tau)\rangle\mapsto e^{i\chi(\tau)}|\Omega(\tau)\rangle$ and changes only by an additive constant under phase redefinitions of the fixed reference vectors. Thus $x(\tau)$ is a well-defined circle coordinate up to an arbitrary choice of origin (clock offset).
\par
\noindent\textit{Remark (one-dimensional readout vs.\ high-dimensional state).} Although $x(\tau)$ is a circle variable, it is only a chosen \emph{readout} of a curve in projective Hilbert space. The underlying state $|\Omega(\tau)\rangle$ (or its regulated restriction) lives in a typically high-dimensional effective sector and can carry rich multipartite correlations. Thus, representing an intrinsic clock by a circle rotation does not mean the microscopic theory is effectively one-dimensional; likewise, holographic/boundary encoding constrains the scaling of independent degrees of freedom without trivializing the structural complexity of states and decoding maps \cite{NielsenChuang2000}.

\begin{definition}[Circle-rotation sampling and Sturmian coding]
Let $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ index discrete ticks (e.g., QCA steps) and sample $x(\tau)$ stroboscopically as $x_n\equiv x(\tau_0+n\Delta\tau)$. In the irrational-rotation regime this is modeled as
\begin{equation}
    x_{n+1}=x_n+\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}} \pmod 1,\qquad \alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}\in(0,1)\setminus\mathbb{Q}.
\end{equation}
Partition the circle into two intervals
\begin{equation}
    I_S=[0,1-\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}),\qquad I_L=[1-\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}},1),
\end{equation}
and define the symbolic coding $\sigma_n\in\{L,S\}$ by
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_n=
    \begin{cases}
    L,& x_n\in I_L,\\
    S,& x_n\in I_S.
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Such codings of irrational rotations are Sturmian sequences; for the golden-slope choice $\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}=\phi^{-1}$ (with $\phi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt5}{2}$), $\{\sigma_n\}$ is (up to a shift and a symbol exchange) the Fibonacci word \cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Ostrowski numeration and the Zeckendorf specialization (golden case)]
\label{prop:ostrowski_zeckendorf}
For an irrational slope $\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}$, the associated Ostrowski numeration gives a unique digit expansion of each integer tick index $n$. In the golden case $\alpha_{\mathrm{rot}}=\phi^{-1}$, the Ostrowski digit constraints degenerate to the Zeckendorf admissibility rule (no consecutive Fibonacci summands): writing the Fibonacci numbers as $F_0=0$, $F_1=1$, $F_{m+2}=F_{m+1}+F_m$, every $n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ admits a unique expansion
\begin{equation}
    n=\sum_{k\ge 0} b_k F_{k+2},\qquad b_k\in\{0,1\},\qquad b_k b_{k+1}=0,
\end{equation}
and conversely every such digit string represents a unique integer \cite{Zeckendorf1972,AlloucheShallit2003}.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\textit{Proof.} A self-contained proof of the Zeckendorf specialization and a summary of the Ostrowski framework are given in Appendix~\ref{app:ostrowski}.

\noindent\textit{Interpretation.} The intrinsic phase orbit can be read out as a canonical Fibonacci (Long/Short) symbol stream and, equivalently, a Zeckendorf-addressable digit structure for discrete ticks. This provides a natural intrinsic clock and a sparse hierarchical schedule for activating scale-dependent updates.

\subsection{The Fubini-Study Metric as Physical Duration}
To give $\tau$ a geometric meaning independent of the specific parametrization, we employ the natural metric of the projective Hilbert space $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$: the Fubini-Study metric.
This geometric viewpoint (statistical distinguishability as projective distance) has a long history; see, e.g., \cite{ProvostVallee1980,Wootters1981}.
For two infinitesimally close states $|\psi\rangle$ and $|\psi + d\psi\rangle$, the distance $ds$ is:
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = 4\Big(\langle d\psi | d\psi \rangle - |\langle \psi | d\psi \rangle|^2\Big)
    = \frac{4}{\hbar^2}\left( \langle \hat{H}^2 \rangle - \langle \hat{H} \rangle^2 \right) d\tau^2
    = \left(\frac{2}{\hbar}\Delta H\right)^2 d\tau^2.
\end{equation}
This yields the fundamental relation between geometry and dynamics.
\begin{theorem}[Anandan-Aharonov Relation]
The speed of evolution of a quantum state in the projective Hilbert space $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$ is strictly determined by the energy uncertainty (standard deviation of the Hamiltonian) \cite{AharonovAnandan1987,Anandan1990}:
\begin{equation}
    v_{\tau} = \frac{ds}{d\tau} = \frac{2}{\hbar} \Delta H = \frac{2}{\hbar} \sqrt{\langle \hat{H}^2 \rangle - \langle \hat{H} \rangle^2}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
This interprets "Time" not as a coordinate but as a statistical distance. Time flows only because the universe is \textit{not} an eigenstate of its own Hamiltonian ($\Delta H > 0$). If $\Delta H = 0$ (Heat Death), time stops ($v_{\tau} = 0$).

\subsubsection{Spectral fractality as an energy--roughness index}
The Anandan--Aharonov relation links intrinsic time to a geometric speed in ray space. To connect this
geometric picture to the intuition that ``more energetic'' sectors are structurally more complex, we introduce
a dimensionless spectral roughness index associated with a state and a Hamiltonian.\par

\noindent\textbf{State-induced spectral measure.}
Let $\hat{H}$ be a self-adjoint Hamiltonian with spectral projector-valued measure $\Pi_{\hat{H}}(\cdot)$.
For a normalized state $\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}$ define the induced spectral measure
\begin{equation}
    \mu_{\psi}(B)\ \equiv\ \bra{\psi}\,\Pi_{\hat{H}}(B)\,\ket{\psi},
\end{equation}
for Borel sets $B\subset\mathbb{R}$. In canonical quasiperiodic models, spectra can be Cantor-like and the
associated spectral measures can be singular continuous \cite{Hofstadter1976,KohmotoKadanoffTang1983,Suto1987}.
(For general background on quasicrystals as ordered aperiodic structures, see \cite{LevineSteinhardt1984}.)\par

\noindent\textbf{Fractal-energy index.}
When $\mu_{\psi}$ admits generalized fractal dimensions, we define a \emph{spectral-fractality index} by the
information dimension
\begin{equation}
    D_{\mathrm{spec}}(\psi)\ \equiv\ D_1(\mu_{\psi}),
\end{equation}
which we use as a quantitative proxy for spectral ``roughness'' / complexity. Importantly, $D_{\mathrm{spec}}$
is dimensionless: it does not replace the physical energy scale, which is still carried by quantities such as
$\langle\hat{H}\rangle$ or $\Delta H$. Rather, $D_{\mathrm{spec}}$ characterizes how the state distributes weight
across the spectral resolution of $\hat{H}$.\par

\noindent\textbf{Compatibility with geometric time.}
Within our framework, intrinsic time is tied to geometric length through $v_{\tau}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\Delta H$,
so any operational notion of ``rate of change'' remains governed by $\Delta H$.
The role of $D_{\mathrm{spec}}$ is instead to provide a state-dependent complexity scale that can distinguish
spectrally smooth sectors from spectrally rough (quasiperiodic / singular-continuous) ones without invoking
dimensionally inconsistent identifications.

\subsubsection{Geometric phase as holonomy (``clockwork'' mechanism)}
The Fubini--Study geometry comes with a natural $U(1)$ gauge structure on the bundle of normalized state vectors over the ray space $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$. For a smooth family of normalized states $\ket{\psi(\lambda)}$ representing rays $[\psi(\lambda)]\in\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$, define the Berry connection one-form \cite{Berry1984}
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{A} \equiv i\langle\psi|d\psi\rangle,
    \qquad
    \mathcal{F}\equiv d\mathcal{A},
\end{equation}
where $d$ denotes the exterior derivative on the parameter manifold. Under a local phase choice $\ket{\psi}\mapsto e^{i\chi}\ket{\psi}$ one has $\mathcal{A}\mapsto \mathcal{A}+d\chi$, while the curvature $\mathcal{F}$ is gauge-invariant.

For a cyclic path $C$ in projective space, the geometric phase (holonomy) is
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_{\mathrm{geom}}[C]=\oint_C \mathcal{A}=\int_{S:\,\partial S=C}\mathcal{F}.
\end{equation}
In this sense, the physically meaningful phase accumulation is a parallel-transport statement on $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$, independent of parametrization and of local gauge choices.

For general (not necessarily adiabatic) cyclic evolutions in ray space, the geometric phase admits the
Aharonov--Anandan extension \cite{AharonovAnandan1987}. In particular, the total phase accumulated along an
intrinsic-time history can be decomposed into a dynamical part and a geometric part:
\begin{equation}
    \arg \langle \psi(0)|\psi(T)\rangle
    =
    -\frac{1}{\hbar}\int_0^T \langle \hat H\rangle\, d\tau
    \;+\;
    \gamma_{\mathrm{geom}},
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\mathrm{geom}}$ depends only on the corresponding path in $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$.
This suggests that $\tau$ may be interpreted as the bookkeeping parameter that consistently integrates
holonomy along physically allowed projective orbits: once $\Delta H>0$ the state traverses nonzero projective
length per unit $\tau$, and for nontrivial closed loops the geometric holonomy is typically nonzero \cite{Berry1984,AharonovAnandan1987}, while for $\Delta H=0$ the
projective speed vanishes and no intrinsic traversal occurs.

In Omega Theory, localized matter degrees of freedom are modeled as topologically non-trivial sectors (wrappings/knots) of the internal fiber. Such sectors motivate a distinguished class of admissible intrinsic histories $\ket{\psi(\tau)}=\ket{\Omega(\tau)}$ whose associated loops in $\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H})$ can carry non-trivial holonomy. The accumulation of geometric phase along the intrinsic trajectory then provides a gauge-invariant measure of the persistence (``tension'') of a topological excitation; later parts interpret this as a possible organizing ingredient for mass-hierarchy heuristics and for the speculative ``Great Unbinding'' discussion (Part~VII).

\subsection{Comparison with Page-Wootters Formalism}
Our approach resonates with the Page--Wootters mechanism \cite{PageWootters1983}, where time appears as a correlation between a clock readout and the rest of the universe within an overall stationary description. In the present framework, a ``clock'' is not postulated as a fundamental tensor factor; operationally it corresponds to a chosen readout of intrinsic data (e.g.\ the phase-orbit coding above) or, more generally, to a chosen pointer/record subalgebra. Nevertheless, the standard Page--Wootters conditional-evolution mechanism can be stated as a purely kinematic template, which we record here for clarity.

\begin{proposition}[Stationary global state induces conditional evolution (Page--Wootters template)]
\label{prop:page_wootters_conditional}
Let $\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{C}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{S}$ and let $\{|n\rangle\}_{n=0}^{T-1}$ be an orthonormal clock basis in $\mathcal{H}_C$ for some $T\ge 2$. Suppose a global state admits a decomposition
\begin{equation}
    |\Omega\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{T-1} |n\rangle\otimes|\psi_n\rangle
\end{equation}
with (not necessarily normalized) $|\psi_n\rangle\in\mathcal{H}_S$ and satisfies a discrete stationarity constraint
\begin{equation}
    (U_C\otimes U_S)\,|\Omega\rangle=|\Omega\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $U_C|n\rangle=|n{+}1\ (\mathrm{mod}\,T)\rangle$. Then $|\psi_{n+1}\rangle=U_S|\psi_n\rangle$ for all $n$ (with indices understood modulo $T$). In particular, the normalized conditional system states $|\tilde\psi_n\rangle\equiv |\psi_n\rangle/\||\psi_n\rangle\|$ evolve unitarily under $U_S$ whenever $\||\psi_n\rangle\|\neq 0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is the standard Page--Wootters conditioning argument; see, e.g., \cite{PageWootters1983}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Axiom O2: Finite Information}
Formal quantum mechanics allows for idealizations with infinite information density (e.g., distinguishing $|x\rangle$ from $|x+\epsilon\rangle$ for arbitrarily small $\epsilon$). However, holographic/entropy bounds motivate treating such idealizations as unphysical at finite resolution: any operational description of a bounded causal region should admit only a finite number of distinguishable states.

\begin{axiom}[Finite Information Axiom]
For any causally closed region with boundary area $A$, the dimension of its effective Hilbert space is bounded by the holographic area law:
\begin{equation}
    \dim \mathcal{H}_{\text{region}} \le \exp\left(\frac{A}{4 l_P^2}\right).
\end{equation}
Saturation may hold only for special high-entropy states (e.g.\ horizon-like situations). Consequently, we take the microscopic description to be fundamentally regulated and to admit a decomposition into finite-dimensional local factors; continuous variables (fields, coordinates) are emergent approximations.
\end{axiom}

\subsection{Remark: Local operator algebras and approximate factorization}
In continuum relativistic QFT, the operator algebra associated with a bounded region is typically a type~III von Neumann algebra, and an exact tensor factorization $\mathcal{H}\simeq \mathcal{H}_R \otimes \mathcal{H}_{R^c}$ is not available; this is closely tied to the ultraviolet structure and divergent entanglement at sharp boundaries (see, e.g., \cite{Haag1996}). Omega Theory takes Axiom~O2 as indicating a fundamentally regulated description with finite information per causal region, for which local algebras are type~I and tensor factorization is meaningful at the microscopic level.

The continuum QFT description is interpreted as an effective scaling limit in which type~III behavior is recovered. In that limit, an approximate tensor-product structure can be formulated using the \textbf{split property}: for nested regions $R \subset R_{\epsilon}$ there exists an intermediate type~I factor that enables a controlled notion of localization and effective subsystem Hilbert spaces \cite{Buchholz1987}. Throughout this manuscript, statements such as $\mathcal{H}=\bigotimes_{v\in V}\mathcal{H}_v$ should therefore be read as microscopic/regulated constructions, not as a claim about exact factorization in continuum QFT.

\subsection{Axiom O3: Causal Locality}
To define "region" and "area" without a pre-existing manifold, we need a graph structure.
\begin{axiom}[Causal Locality Axiom]
There exists a discrete-time unitary update $\hat{U}$ compatible with the quasi-local structure such that information propagates only within a finite neighborhood per time step. Equivalently, for any local observable $\hat{A}_x$ supported on a site $x$ (or on a finite region), the Heisenberg-evolved operator is supported only on a bounded neighborhood $\mathcal{N}(x)$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{supp}\!\left(\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{A}_x \hat{U}\right)\ \subseteq\ \mathcal{N}(x).
\end{equation}
Compatibility with the static-universe postulate (Axiom~O1) is implemented by taking the Omega state to be stationary under this update, i.e.\ for all quasi-local $\hat{A}\in\mathfrak{A}$,
\begin{equation}
    \omega_\Omega(\hat{U}^\dagger \hat{A}\hat{U})=\omega_\Omega(\hat{A}).
\end{equation}
Thus the state is fixed while $\hat{U}$ acts as a locality-preserving automorphism on observables; effective change is encoded in relational correlations and in conditional descriptions on appropriate subalgebras (cf.\ Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}).
This finite-range causality induces a strict discrete "light cone" structure and permits recovery of an effective relativistic causal structure in an appropriate continuum limit.
\end{axiom}

\subsection{Axiom O4: The Holographic Mapping}
\begin{axiom}[Holographic Mapping Axiom]
Let $\mathcal{H}_{\text{bulk}}$ denote the bulk degrees of freedom (e.g.\ a code subspace of the QCA on the graph $G$) and let $\mathcal{H}_{\partial G}$ denote the boundary degrees of freedom associated with the boundary $\partial G$. We postulate the existence of a holographic encoding map
\begin{equation}
    \Phi:\ \mathcal{H}_{\text{bulk}}\ \to\ \mathcal{H}_{\partial G},
\end{equation}
which is an \emph{approximate isometry} on the relevant code subspace, with reconstruction error bounded and vanishing in an appropriate large-scale (or large-bond) limit. Moreover, $\Phi$ supports approximate operator-algebra quantum error correction: for any boundary region $R$, bulk operators in the entanglement wedge $\mathcal{W}_E(R)$ admit reconstruction on $R$ up to a controlled error (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}; cf.\ \cite{AlmheiriDongHarlow2015,Pastawski2015,Harlow2017}).
\end{axiom}
\noindent\textit{Concrete realization.} In this manuscript we study a specific tensor-network implementation of $\Phi$ (the ``Golden MERA''), treated as part of the model assumptions stated below \cite{Vidal2007MERA,Swingle2012MERA}.

\subsection{The Factorization Problem and Baby Universes}
A central puzzle in modern holography is the "Factorization Problem" \cite{Harlow2021}: in a theory with multiple boundaries, the gravitational path integral (which includes connected topologies like Euclidean wormholes) fails to factorize, suggesting an ensemble average over dual theories rather than a single unitary description. This is formalized by the inclusion of "Baby Universes" or $\alpha$-parameters in the Hilbert space.

Omega Theory approaches this issue in an operator-algebraic language rather than via the gravitational Euclidean path integral. In many discussions, ``baby-universe'' operators are modeled as global/topological degrees of freedom that commute with the quasi-local algebra of observables accessible to finite observers. In our framework this corresponds to operators in the commutant/center of the physical representation of $\mathfrak{A}$, hence to \emph{superselection data}. Under standard factoriality/irreducibility conditions, such commuting operators reduce to fixed $\alpha$-parameters (a choice of sector) rather than dynamical variables within that sector (Appendix~\ref{app:baby_universes}, Theorem~\ref{thm:alpha_superselection}). The Omega Axiom (O1) then postulates a unique global state $\omega_\Omega$, which may be read as selecting a single sector of such superselection data. This provides a concrete microscopic notion of ``fixing $\alpha$'' compatible with unitarity in the regulated Hilbert-space description; it should not be interpreted as a general theorem about the full gravitational path-integral measure.

The geometric area law is implemented kinematically by tensor-network constructions: for a state $|\psi\rangle$ defined by a network with bond dimension $\chi$, the boundary entropy satisfies the rigorous min-cut \emph{upper bound}
\begin{equation}
    S(\rho_R)\ \le\ |\gamma_{\min}(R)|\,\ln\chi,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma_{\min}(R)$ is a minimal cut separating $R$ from $R^c$ (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}, Theorem~\ref{thm:mincut_entropy}). Interpreting $|\gamma_{\min}(R)|$ as a discrete area functional and identifying $\ln\chi$ with an area-density scale yields the familiar area-law scaling; approximate saturation (turning the bound into an approximate equality) is an additional property of special tensor classes (e.g.\ perfect or random tensors) and is treated here as a model target rather than an automatic consequence.

\subsection{Model Assumptions and Auxiliary Assumptions}

The axioms above specify a minimal framework. To prevent model-specific structure from being misread as axiomatic, we collect here the concrete assumptions used in the constructions and phenomenological estimates throughout this manuscript.

\begin{assumption}[M1 (Statistical Isotropy via Icosahedral Quasicrystal)]
\label{assump:M1}
The underlying interaction graph $G$ is realized as an icosahedral quasicrystal (cut-and-project set) in $\mathbb{R}^3$ \cite{LevineSteinhardt1984,Senechal1995,BaakeGrimm2013AperiodicOrder}. Moreover, the neighbor-direction empirical statistics are assumed to become isotropic at long wavelengths: for mesoscopic scales $L\gg \varepsilon$ (with $\varepsilon$ the microscopic spacing), the low spherical multipoles of the normalized edge-direction distribution vanish in the continuum limit $\varepsilon/L\to 0$ (equivalently, the discrete averages of $Y_{\ell m}$ over edge directions are $o(1)$ for $1\le \ell\le 5$). In the idealized icosahedral $5$-design case these low-$\ell$ multipoles vanish exactly (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:design_suppresses_lowell}); equivalently, direction-averaged plane-wave factors match the isotropic spherical average $\sin|q|/|q|$ through fifth order in $|q|$ (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:design_plane_wave_isotropy}).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[M2 (On-site Internal Fiber and $G_2$ Structure)]
\label{assump:M2}
Each site Hilbert space admits a factorization $\mathcal{H}_x = \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{orbital}}\otimes \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{internal}}$ with $\dim \mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{internal}}=8$, and the internal sector is organized by octonionic/$G_2$ structure as an ansatz for Standard-Model-like quantum numbers (Part~IV).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[M3 (Golden MERA and Golden-Spectrum Update Rule)]
\label{assump:M3}
We adopt a specific realization in which the holographic encoding map $\Phi$ of Axiom~O4 is implemented by a scale-invariant tensor network adapted to the quasicrystal inflation hierarchy (the ``Golden MERA'') \cite{Vidal2007MERA,Swingle2012MERA}. The microscopic update is taken to be a PQCA built from local unitaries with a Golden-Ratio spectrum, providing the quasi-periodic intrinsic phase structure used in Part~VI and the tensor-network constructions formalized in Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[S1 (Readout-kernel symmetry / sharp projection limit)]
\label{assump:S1}
When probabilities are inferred from holographic micro-realizations, we assume the readout kernel of Axiom~O5 is permutation-symmetric on the admissible fine-grained micro-realizations compatible with a given coarse-grained macro-description, and that in the sharp-readout limit it reduces to a uniform distribution on the corresponding degeneracy class. This symmetry hypothesis underlies the counting normalization recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[C1 (Cosmological Near-Saturation)]
\label{assump:C1}
On cosmological scales (e.g.\ causal patches bounded by an apparent horizon), the holographic bound of Axiom~O2 is assumed to be approximately saturated up to order-unity factors so that the effective information capacity tracks the horizon area. This near-saturation is used when relating an infrared length scale $L$ to a horizon scale (such as $R_H$) and deriving $\Lambda(t)$ scaling relations (Part~V).
\end{assumption}
