\section{The Omega Metric: Rewriting Schwarzschild}

\subsection{Classical Schwarzschild Metric}
The standard Schwarzschild metric in Schwarzschild coordinates $(t, r, \theta, \phi)$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
    ds^2 = -\left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)dt^2 + \left(1 - \frac{2M}{r}\right)^{-1}dr^2 + r^2 d\Omega^2
\end{equation}
This metric, derived by Schwarzschild in 1916 \cite{Schwarzschild1916}, exhibits a coordinate singularity at the event horizon $r_s = 2M$ and an essential singularity at $r=0$. While the horizon singularity can be removed by coordinate transformations such as Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates \cite{Kruskal1960}, the curvature singularity at the center remains a non-removable feature in classical General Relativity \cite{Penrose1965}.

\subsection{Holographic Polar Coordinates}
We transition to the Holographic Polar Coordinates defined in Paper I. Let the radial coordinate be $\rho$ and the scanning index be $n$ (replacing continuous time $t$).
The transformation is governed by the holographic map $\Phi$:
\begin{equation}
    r \to \rho, \quad t \to n
\end{equation}
However, unlike the linear radius $r$, the holographic radius $\rho$ is subject to the inversion symmetry of the Riemann sphere.

\subsection{The Topological Inversion Factor}
We introduce the \textbf{Holographic Inversion Factor} $\mathcal{J}(\rho)$, which regulates the metric near the origin. This factor is not arbitrary but arises from the requirement that the scanning density must remain invariant under the mapping from bulk to boundary.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{J}(\rho) = \frac{\rho - \rho_{crit}}{\rho + \rho_{crit}}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{crit}$ corresponds to the classical Schwarzschild radius $2M$.

This structure mirrors the UV/IR connection in the AdS/CFT correspondence \cite{Maldacena1998, Witten1998}, where positions deep in the bulk (small $\rho$) map to high-energy scales on the boundary. Here, the factor $\mathcal{J}(\rho)$ acts similarly to a redshift factor, ensuring that the phase scanning rate $\omega_{scan}$ measured at infinity remains finite even as the local coordinate nears the critical point.

Substituting this into the metric structure, the divergent term $(1 - 2M/r)$ is replaced by the smooth phase factor derived from $\Theta$-scanning:
\begin{equation}
    g_{00} \sim \mathcal{J}(\rho)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Resolution of the Singularity}
As $\rho \to 0$ (approaching the center), the metric does not diverge. Instead, the geometry undergoes a conformal inversion. The region $\rho < \rho_{crit}$ maps to a dual space where radial progression is inverted.
The ``center'' of the black hole is effectively a topological throat that connects the interior to the holographic boundary at infinity. Thus, $r=0$ is topologically equivalent to $r=\infty$ on the dual manifold, removing the singularity point entirely.
