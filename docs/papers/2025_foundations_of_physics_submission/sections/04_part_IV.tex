\subsection{Internal Hilbert Space Structure}
The local Hilbert space at each site factors into $\mathcal{H}_x = \mathcal{H}_{\text{orbital}} \otimes \mathcal{H}_{\text{internal}}$. The "orbital" part connects to neighbors (spacetime index), while the "internal" part contains the quantum numbers of the Standard Model (spin, color, flavor) \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995}.
Omega Theory postulates that $\mathcal{H}_{\text{internal}}$ is related to the octonion algebra $\mathbb{O}$. The automorphism group of the octonions is $G_2$, which contains $SU(3)$ as a maximal subgroup. Octonionic/$G_2$ constructions can motivate $SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$-like gauge organization through standard bundle/ideal structures in $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}$ \cite{Baez2002, Furey2017}.
\paragraph{Scope and limitations.}
This part specifies a concrete algebraic \emph{organization} of Standard-Model-like quantum numbers using octonionic/$G_2$ structure and states the minimal consistency conditions (chirality, anomaly cancellation, and Yukawa structure) that any dynamical completion must satisfy \cite{Baez2002,Furey2017,PeskinSchroeder1995}. We do not yet derive a complete low-energy effective field theory (EFT) from the QCA dynamics, nor do we provide a controlled RG matching; the corresponding closure is isolated as a checkable matching problem in Conjecture~\ref{conj:eft_matching}. Where appropriate we therefore formulate the content as an explicit model ansatz with stated assumptions.

\subsection{Relation to prior octonion-based SM reconstructions}
Octonion-based routes to Standard-Model-like gauge content have a substantial prior literature; in particular, the $G_2$ automorphism viewpoint and minimal-ideal constructions in $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}$ have been developed in detail \cite{Baez2002, Furey2017}. Omega Theory does not claim to reinvent these algebraic facts. The new ingredient proposed here is how such internal-algebra data are \emph{packaged into a local finite-information substrate} (Part~III) and coupled to a holographic/tensor-network architecture (Axiom~O4): the same locality constraints that define the QCA also define the allowed internal couplings and hence constrain which octonionic substructures can be dynamically realized.
\par
\noindent
Operationally, we treat $\mathcal{H}_{\text{internal}}$ as a finite-dimensional on-site space (dimension $d_{\text{int}}$) that carries a representation of the internal symmetry algebra. The octonionic proposal is a minimality statement: the smallest non-associative division algebra has just enough structure to host $SU(3)$ and to motivate an $SU(2)\times U(1)$ complement, while remaining compatible with a finite on-site Hilbert-space postulate.
\paragraph{Remark (Hurwitz lattice and discrete rigidity).}
A standard rigid discretization of $\mathbb{O}$ is given by the Hurwitz/Octavian integers (a maximal order), which form a Euclidean lattice with respect to the octonion norm and are isometric to a scaled $E_8$ root lattice; this provides a concrete finite-information lattice candidate for organizing internal coordinates \cite{Baez2002,ConwaySmith2003,Ma2025HPA}.

\subsection{Chirality and representation content (minimal requirements)}
Any SM-like embedding must reproduce \emph{chiral} fermions. In a QCA setting, chirality has two layers:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spacetime chirality}: the orbital QCA sector supplies a Dirac/Weyl structure in the long-wavelength limit (Part~III). On the continuum side one may use the usual projectors $P_{L/R}=\frac{1}{2}(1\mp\gamma^5)$.
    \item \textbf{Internal chirality}: the internal algebra must distinguish left-handed $SU(2)$ doublets from right-handed singlets and assign hypercharge consistently.
\end{enumerate}
In practice, the internal-chirality requirement can be formulated as the existence of a decomposition of the on-site internal space into chiral subspaces,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{H}_{\text{internal}} \ \cong\  \mathcal{H}_{L}\oplus \mathcal{H}_{R},
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal{H}_{L}$ transforming as $SU(2)$ doublets and $\mathcal{H}_{R}$ as singlets (with appropriate $U(1)_Y$ charges). In octonion-based constructions, this is typically implemented by idempotent/ideal projectors in a complexified algebra (e.g.\ minimal left ideals in $\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathbb{O}$) \cite{Furey2017}. In Omega Theory we treat such a decomposition as fixed internal data at each site; the dynamical question is whether QCA-local couplings and the holographic code constraints can select a unique such structure.

\subsection{Anomaly cancellation as a consistency condition}
To be viable as an EFT limit, the induced gauge theory must be anomaly-free. This does not require knowing all UV details: it is a purely representation-theoretic constraint on one generation of chiral fermions. Denote by $Y$ the hypercharge and by $T^a$ the generators of $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$ in the relevant representations. The standard anomaly-cancellation conditions include, for one generation \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995},
\begin{align}
    &\sum_{\text{LH Weyl}} Y = 0, \\
    &\sum_{\text{LH Weyl}} Y^3 = 0, \\
    &\sum_{\text{LH Weyl}} Y\,\mathrm{Tr}(T^aT^b)\big|_{SU(2)} = 0,\qquad
      \sum_{\text{LH Weyl}} Y\,\mathrm{Tr}(T^aT^b)\big|_{SU(3)} = 0.
\end{align}
Accordingly, any octonionic assignment of internal quantum numbers in Omega Theory is acceptable only if it reproduces the SM hypercharge pattern (up to overall normalization conventions), since that pattern is known to satisfy the anomaly constraints \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995}. This provides a sharp falsification criterion for the internal-algebra ansatz independent of the details of the QCA dynamics.

\begin{lemma}[One-generation SM hypercharges satisfy the anomaly constraints]
\label{lemma:sm_anomaly_cancellation}
Consider one Standard Model generation written as left-handed Weyl fields \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995}
$Q_L\sim(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2})_{Y=1/6}$, $u_R^c\sim(\overline{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1})_{Y=-2/3}$,
$d_R^c\sim(\overline{\mathbf{3}},\mathbf{1})_{Y=+1/3}$,
$L_L\sim(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{2})_{Y=-1/2}$, and $e_R^c\sim(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1})_{Y=+1}$.
Then the mixed gauge, cubic, and mixed gravitational $U(1)_Y$ anomalies vanish:
\begin{equation}
    \sum Y = 0,\qquad \sum Y^3=0,\qquad
    \sum Y\,T_2(R)=0,\qquad \sum Y\,T_3(R)=0,
\end{equation}
where $T_2$ and $T_3$ are the Dynkin indices for the $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$ representations (normalized by $T(\mathbf{2})=T(\mathbf{3})=\tfrac12$).
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is the standard one-generation Standard Model anomaly-cancellation check; see, e.g., \cite{PeskinSchroeder1995}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Yukawa sector as overlap data (model constraint)}
The SM Yukawa couplings require gauge-invariant bilinears connecting left- and right-handed fermions via a Higgs doublet. In the present framework, the Higgs-like field $\Phi$ is identified with a geometric breathing mode of the internal fiber, so the Yukawa matrices are modeled as overlap data between internal wavefunctions in $\mathcal{H}_L$ and $\mathcal{H}_R$ weighted by the local geometric configuration.\par
\noindent
A minimal effective parameterization is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{Y} = - \sum_{i,j}\left( y^{(u)}_{ij}\,\overline{Q}_{Li}\,\tilde{H}\,u_{Rj} + y^{(d)}_{ij}\,\overline{Q}_{Li}\,H\,d_{Rj} + y^{(e)}_{ij}\,\overline{L}_{Li}\,H\,e_{Rj} \right) + \mathrm{h.c.},
\end{equation}
with $y_{ij}$ modeled as internal overlap data. In Omega Theory, the claim is not that these matrices are derived here, but that the \emph{space of allowed Yukawas} is constrained by finite-information locality and by the chosen octonionic decomposition; the geometric/topological origin of hierarchies is treated as an explicit model target for a future dynamical completion.

\subsection{Fermion Generations as Topological Wrapping}
Why are there three generations of fermions? In our geometric framework, we model generations as corresponding to a "wrapping number" or topological index of the wavepacket around the compact internal dimensions of the Omega Network.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Generation 1 (e, u, d)}: Trivial wrapping (Index 1). Stable.
    \item \textbf{Generation 2 ($\mu$, c, s)}: Double wrapping (Index 2). Metastable in the effective description; decays to Gen~1 via transitions that reduce the internal topological index (template).
    \item \textbf{Generation 3 ($\tau$, t, b)}: Triple wrapping (Index 3). More weakly protected/shorter-lived in the effective description (template).
\end{itemize}
Within this organizing ansatz, the mass hierarchy is modeled as increasing with wrapping index: higher wrapping numbers require longer effective path lengths in the internal manifold and thus accumulate larger geometric phases, corresponding to larger effective masses.
\par
\noindent\textit{Remark (status).} The wrapping picture is a phenomenological organizing ansatz: it postulates the existence of discrete internal topological sectors labeled by an integer $w$ and proposes that the observed three-generation structure corresponds to the first few such sectors. Establishing this identification requires an explicit internal spectral analysis (mode content, selection rules, and couplings) in a concrete microscopic completion.
\paragraph{Remark (Jordan kinematics and a structural ``three'').}
Independently of the wrapping template, in Jordan-algebraic formulations of quantum kinematics, if one insists that observables form a finite-dimensional formally real Jordan algebra and admits octonionic coordinates, the exceptional algebra $J_3(\mathbb{O})$ is singled out (the octonionic Hermitian construction yields a Jordan algebra only in the $3\times 3$ case), making the number $3$ structural rather than a free choice \cite{JordanVonNeumannWigner1934,Baez2002,Ma2025HPA}.

\paragraph{Mass from projective speed and internal budget.}
By the Anandan--Aharonov relation (Part~II), the intrinsic speed of a state in projective Hilbert space is fixed by the Hamiltonian uncertainty, $v_{\tau}=\frac{2}{\hbar}\Delta H$. For a localized excitation constrained to a topological sector labeled by wrapping index $w$, let $\Delta H_w$ denote the characteristic (or minimal) uncertainty compatible with that sector. We define the corresponding effective rest-energy scale by
\begin{equation}
    \Delta H_w \equiv m_w c^2.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $m_w$ is the proportionality constant between projective speed and intrinsic-time flow for that sector,
\begin{equation}
    v_{\tau,w} = \frac{2}{\hbar} m_w c^2.
\end{equation}
In the information-budget parametrization (Part~V), the same mass/inertia content is encoded operationally by the internal allocation $v_{\text{int}}$: tighter (higher-index) wrappings demand a larger persistent internal processing overhead and therefore correspond to larger effective $m_w$.

\subsection{Emergence of the Higgs Sector as Geometric Rigidity}
In Omega Theory, we reinterpret the Higgs mechanism as the crystallization of the internal geometry. The "Higgs field" $\Phi$ corresponds to the breathing mode (volume deformation scalar) of the compactified $G_2$ manifold.
Motivated by the observed Higgs mass ($\sim 125\,\mathrm{GeV}$), we suggest that a Higgs-like scalar mass scale could correspond to the natural frequency of radial vibrations of this fiber, controlled by an effective spectral gap of the internal geometry.
We define the geometric effective potential $V_{eff}(\Phi)$ via the scalar curvature of the internal fiber $\tilde{R}$:
\begin{equation}
    V_{eff}(\Phi) = - \frac{M_{Pl}^2}{2} \tilde{R}(\Phi) + \frac{\lambda}{4} \Phi^4 + \dots
\end{equation}
\noindent
To make the hierarchy claim quantitative one must specify the stabilization mechanism for the breathing mode. A standard route in compactification models is that non-perturbative contributions generate exponentially small scales \cite{KKLT2003}. We therefore record the following elementary implication.
\begin{proposition}[Exponential hierarchy from a volume-suppressed potential term]
\label{prop:higgs_hierarchy}
Let $\Phi$ be a canonically normalized scalar with an effective potential containing a non-perturbative contribution of the form
\begin{equation}
    V_{\mathrm{np}}(\Phi)=M_{Pl}^4\,e^{-S(\Phi)},\qquad S(\Phi)\equiv \frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{G_2}(\Phi)}{l_P^7},
\end{equation}
and suppose $S(\Phi_\ast)\gg 1$ at a local minimum $\Phi_\ast$ and that $S'(\Phi_\ast)$ is not parametrically small. Then the mass of small fluctuations of $\Phi$ about $\Phi_\ast$ is exponentially suppressed relative to $M_{Pl}$:
\begin{equation}
    m_\Phi\ \sim\ M_{Pl}\,e^{-S(\Phi_\ast)/2},
\end{equation}
up to model-dependent polynomial prefactors coming from $S'(\Phi_\ast)$ and $S''(\Phi_\ast)$.
\end{proposition}
\noindent\textit{Justification.} This follows by differentiating $V_{\mathrm{np}}(\Phi)=M_{Pl}^4 e^{-S(\Phi)}$ twice and evaluating at the minimum $\Phi_\ast$.
\noindent
Interpreting the Higgs-like mode as the breathing fluctuation of the internal fiber suggests the benchmark identification $m_H\sim m_\Phi$ in such a stabilization regime. For illustration, taking $S(\Phi_\ast)=\mathcal{O}(10^2)$ yields an electroweak-scale hierarchy without fine-tuning of dimensionless couplings.
\begin{equation}
    m_H \approx M_{Pl} \cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2} Vol(G_2)/l_P^7} \sim \mathcal{O}(10^2\,\mathrm{GeV}).
\end{equation}
This provides a heuristic geometric mechanism for generating a large hierarchy, pending a complete effective-field-theory and renormalization analysis.

\subsection{Neutrino Mass and the Geometric Seesaw}
As a phenomenological ansatz, neutrino modes can be modeled as internal states with parametrically small overlap with the Higgs-like breathing sector. Motivated by the standard seesaw scaling $m_\nu\sim v^2/M$ \cite{Minkowski1977Seesaw}, we propose a \textbf{geometric seesaw template} in which the small masses are further organized by golden scaling:
\begin{equation}
    m_{\nu_k} \sim \frac{v^2}{M_{Pl}} \cdot \phi^{-k}, \quad k=1,2,3.
\end{equation}
Here, $\phi \approx 1.618$. As a phenomenological ansatz, this yields a hierarchical neutrino scale (e.g.\ $m_{\nu}\lesssim 0.1\,\mathrm{eV}$ for modest $k$), but a gauge-invariant Yukawa/seesaw construction and controlled RG matching are not developed here.


\subsection{Baryogenesis via Topological Chirality}
The observed universe is dominated by matter over antimatter ($\eta_B \approx 6 \times 10^{-10}$). The standard Sakharov conditions require baryon-number violation, C/CP violation, and departure from thermal equilibrium \cite{Sakharov1967Baryogenesis}.
In Omega Theory, baryon number is modeled as a topological charge carried by internal sectors. A concrete and fully standard template is the winding number of an $SU(2)$-valued order parameter field $U(x)$ (as in Skyrmion constructions) \cite{Nakahara2003}: after compactifying space at infinity, one has a map $U:S^3\to SU(2)\cong S^3$, whose homotopy class lies in $\pi_3(SU(2))\cong\mathbb{Z}$. The corresponding integer can be written as the Chern--Simons/winding functional
\begin{equation}
    B[U]\ \equiv\ \frac{1}{24\pi^2}\int_{S^3}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl((U^{-1}dU)^3\bigr)\ \in\ \mathbb{Z},
\end{equation}
\begin{lemma}[Integer winding number]
\label{lemma:winding_integer}
For any smooth map $U:S^3\to SU(2)$, the functional $B[U]$ defined above is an integer and equals the topological degree of $U$ under the identification $SU(2)\cong S^3$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is standard (degree/class in $H^3(SU(2),\mathbb{Z})$ and normalization of the Cartan 3-form); see, e.g., \cite{Nakahara2003}.
\end{proof}
with antiparticles corresponding to $B\mapsto -B$. In the present manuscript, the role of the internal $G_2$ sector is to supply the requisite low-energy order parameter structure (e.g.\ via an $SU(2)$ subgroup and its associated modes); establishing the detailed EFT reduction is left as part of the dynamical completion program.
As a parity-odd diagnostic available in $G_2$-structure geometry, one may consider the following topological functional, which we use as an internal handedness index within the ansatz:
\begin{equation}
    \chi_{\Omega}\ \equiv\ \int_{\mathcal{M}_7} \varphi \wedge p_1(T\mathcal{M}_7)
    \ \propto\ \int_{\mathcal{M}_7} \varphi \wedge \mathrm{Tr}(R\wedge R),
\end{equation}
where $\varphi$ is the $G_2$-structure 3-form and $p_1(T\mathcal{M}_7)$ is the first Pontryagin form of the Levi-Civita connection (so $p_1\propto \mathrm{Tr}(R\wedge R)$). This integral changes sign under orientation reversal and can therefore serve as a parity-odd ``handedness'' index within the ansatz; see, e.g., \cite{Joyce2000SpecialHolonomy}.
\begin{lemma}[Orientation reversal flips the sign of $\chi_\Omega$]
\label{lemma:chiOmega_orientation}
Let $\mathcal{M}_7$ be an oriented 7-manifold and let $\alpha$ be any smooth $7$-form on $\mathcal{M}_7$. Denote by $-\mathcal{M}_7$ the same manifold with the opposite orientation. Then
\begin{equation}
    \int_{-\mathcal{M}_7}\alpha = -\int_{\mathcal{M}_7}\alpha.
\end{equation}
In particular, with $\alpha=\varphi\wedge p_1(T\mathcal{M}_7)$ one has $\chi_\Omega\mapsto -\chi_\Omega$ under orientation reversal.
\end{lemma}
\noindent\textit{Justification.} This is immediate from the definition of orientation on integration of top-degree forms (see, e.g., \cite{Nakahara2003}).
This suggests a possible source of effective $CP$ violation during an early ``crystallization'' epoch: if the internal geometry admits a chiral invariant (modeled here by $\chi_\Omega\neq 0$), defect formation could bias winding production. In that spirit one may parametrize a bias by factors such as $e^{-\chi_\Omega}$, yielding a model-dependent relation between $\eta_B$ and microscopic chiral data rather than a derived prediction at this stage.

\subsection{Proton Stability and the Hierarchy}
Grand Unified Theories (GUTs) typically predict proton decay ($p \to e^+ \pi^0$) mediated by $X,Y$ bosons, with lifetimes $\tau_p \sim 10^{34}$ years. Current searches set lower limits on $\tau_p/B$ at the level of $10^{34}$ years \cite{SuperK2017ProtonDecay}.
In Omega Theory, proton stability is attributed to a conserved topological charge in the internal sector (e.g.\ the integer winding $B$ above in an effective $SU(2)$ order-parameter description). If the low-energy dynamics preserves this charge to very high accuracy, then baryon-number-violating processes require either explicit charge-violating operators in the effective action or a global transition that changes the relevant topological sector. In that sense, proton decay can be topologically suppressed at low energies (model-dependent). A quantitative lifetime estimate would require an explicit EFT embedding and a computation of the leading baryon-number-violating operators and their suppression scales.

\subsection{The Strong CP Problem and a Geometric Mechanism}
QCD allows for a CP-violating term $\frac{\theta}{32\pi^2} G_{\mu\nu} \tilde{G}^{\mu\nu}$, while the neutron electric dipole moment bound implies an effective $\theta$ parameter constrained at the level $\theta \lesssim 10^{-10}$ (order of magnitude) \cite{Abel2020NeutronEDM}. The Peccei-Quinn mechanism postulates a new particle (Axion) to cancel this.
Omega Theory suggests a \textbf{geometric mechanism} that could favor a small effective $\theta$ by relating it to holonomy/topological data of the internal fiber. In special constructions (e.g., unimodular-lattice projections) certain integrated topological indices may vanish, which within the ansatz would correspond to $\theta_{\mathrm{eff}}\approx 0$. A full account requires an explicit QCD embedding and a treatment of nonperturbative dynamics.

\section{Geometric Derivation and Renormalization}

\subsection{Topological Ansatz}
Omega Theory treats low-energy dimensionless constants as \emph{matching data} determined by rigid discrete/topological inputs of the regulated construction. Concretely, in the scan--projection viewpoint (Axioms~O5--O6) the relevant quantities are modeled as \emph{normalized phase-volume / process-cost invariants} of compact internal phase spaces, with canonical normalization fixed by projective (ray-space) identifications. Appendix~\ref{app:constants_proof} records the minimal closed-form realization used in this manuscript.

\subsection{The Proton-Electron Mass Ratio \texorpdfstring{($\mu$)}{(mu)}}
The ratio $\mu=m_p/m_e$ compares the hadronic scale to the leptonic scale. In the phase-volume (cost) realization adopted here, $\mu$ is modeled by canonical volumes of compact internal phase spaces, normalized so that the electron reference corresponds to unit cost. Using the three-color structure of QCD and a per-color phase space modeled by a product of projective rotation and phase factors (Appendix~\ref{app:constants_proof}, Theorem~\ref{thm:mu_geo}), one obtains the closed-form value
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:mu_geo}
    \mu_{\text{geo}} \equiv 6\pi^5 \approx 1836.1181087.
\end{equation}
Comparing with the CODATA value $\mu_{\mathrm{exp}}$ \cite{CODATA2022RMP}, the relative offset is at the level $\sim 10^{-5}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:constants_proof}, Eq.~(\ref{eq:mu_mismatch_def})), and we treat the residual as a multiplicative matching factor encoding QCD binding and radiative structure.

\subsection{The Fine Structure Constant \texorpdfstring{($\alpha$)}{(alpha)}}
In the same phase-volume (geometric impedance) viewpoint, the inverse fine structure constant is modeled as a serial (additive) cost across three projective strata (bulk/boundary/line) represented by compact phase spaces with canonical volumes. With the ray-space (projective) identification implemented by $\mathbb{Z}_2$ quotients, the minimal closed-form model yields (Appendix~\ref{app:constants_proof}, Theorem~\ref{thm:alpha_three_channel})
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:alpha_geo}
    \alpha^{-1}_{\text{geo}} \equiv 4\pi^3 + \pi^2 + \pi \approx 137.0363038.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Interpretation (geometric impedance).}
The key structural point is \emph{serial composition}: when readout constraints are enforced sequentially (no parallel bypass), the corresponding costs add. In the present normalization, the three terms are canonical phase volumes of $U(1)\times SU(2)$, $SO(3)$, and $\mathbb{R}P^1$ (Appendix~\ref{app:constants_proof}, Lemma~\ref{lem:group_volumes}). Comparing with CODATA $\alpha(0)$ \cite{CODATA2022RMP} yields a small offset $\Delta\alpha^{-1}$ at the $10^{-4}$ level, which we treat as a controlled matching input at the readout/renormalization interface (Appendix~\ref{app:constants_proof}, Eq.~(\ref{eq:alpha_mismatch_def})).

\subsection{Implications}
If constants are geometric measures, they are susceptible to geometric deformations. This suggests that in regimes where the relevant geometric moduli are displaced or develop gradients (e.g.\ high-curvature/early-universe environments), $\alpha$ and $\mu$ may exhibit spatial and/or temporal variation. The phenomenological parameterizations and observational bounds used in this manuscript are recorded in Part~VI.
