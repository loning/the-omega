\section{Observer--Spacetime Duality: Operator--Image Formulation}

\subsection{Observer Parameter Space}

\begin{definition}[Observer Parameters]\label{def:observer}
Define an observer as a parameter tuple
\[
O=(\mathcal{S},u,\alpha,\mathcal{P},m,\mathrm{Fold}),
\]
where: $\mathcal{S}$ is a geometric choice (orientation of physical subspace, window/strip rule), $u\in E_{\perp}^{3}$ is the internal phase, $\alpha\in\RR/\ZZ$ is the ring scan parameter, $\mathcal{P}=\{I_0,I_1\}$ is a circle binary partition (symbolization partition), $m\in\NN$ is the finite window length, and $\mathrm{Fold}=\mathrm{Fold}_m$ is the stabilization kernel.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Fixed Bandwidth in Main Text]\label{assump:m6-fixed-main}
Unless otherwise stated, the main text fixes $m=6$ and $\mathrm{Fold}=\mathrm{Fold}_6$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Local Realization Difference]\label{prop:patch-difference}
Under fixed $\mathcal{S}$, if $u\neq u'$, then the local patch families of $\Lambda(W,u)$ and $\Lambda(W,u')$ are generally different. This difference arises from the window translation dependence of the cut-and-project definition.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Translation of the acceptance domain/window changes which lattice points fall within the pipe, thereby changing the local realization of the physical projection point set; see the relevant entry in \cite{IUCRAcceptanceDomain}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Readout Operators and ``Spacetime Image''}

Let $(\Sigma,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)$ be a discrete-time measure-preserving dynamical system. Take resolution $m\in\NN$ and define the finite window readout $q_m:\Sigma\to\Omega_m$, where $\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^{m}$.

Define the ``observer-dependent geometric-symbolic factor map'' $\Pi_O$ (determined by $\mathcal{S},u,\alpha,\mathcal{P}$) and introduce the stabilization kernel $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ (see Section \ref{sec:zeckendorf}). Define the composite readout operator
\[
\mathcal{R}_{O,m}=\mathrm{Fold}_m\circ q_m\circ \Pi_O.
\]

\begin{definition}[Spacetime Image]\label{def:spacetime-image}
Given $(O,m)$ and orbit $s_{t+1}=T(s_t)$, define the visible sequence
\[
x_t=\mathcal{R}_{O,m}(s_t)\in X_m^{\mathrm Z}.
\]
In the semantic framework of this paper, ``spacetime'' is defined as the sequence $(x_t)_{t\ge 0}$ and its induced transition kernel, reachability graph, and cylinder set conditional structure (see Sections \ref{sec:information-time}--\ref{sec:fiber-observables}).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Multi-Scale Consistency: Inverse Systems and Readout Factorization (Necessary Interface for Comparing Different \texorpdfstring{$m$}{m})}

When comparing outputs at different window lengths $m$, ``consistency'' must be precisely defined as cross-scale compatibility rather than equality of outputs under different $m$. To this end, we introduce a scale interface: for $m_2\ge m_1$, take the natural restriction projection
\[
\pi_{m_2\to m_1}:X_{m_2}^{\mathrm Z}\to X_{m_1}^{\mathrm Z},
\]
which acts to retain the stable part within a finite position range (equivalently: retain finite prefix information).

\begin{assumption}[Scale Compatibility (Readout Factorization)]\label{assump:ontology-scale-factorization}
For any $m_2\ge m_1$, the readout operator family satisfies
\[
\pi_{m_2\to m_1}\circ \mathcal{R}_{O,m_2}=\mathcal{R}_{O,m_1}\qquad (\mu\text{-a.e. or pointwise}).
\]
\end{assumption}

Under this framework, the output at larger $m$ reproduces the output at smaller $m$ after projection to the smaller $m$; differences only manifest as longer prefixes and the finer conditional structure they induce. Moreover, $\{X_m^{\mathrm Z},\pi_{m_2\to m_1}\}$ can be organized as an inverse system whose inverse limit gives a resolution-independent unified object (see supplementary proofs in Appendix \ref{app:zeckendorf-fold}).

\subsection{Hierarchical Attribution of ``Irrational/Rational''}

This paper does not attribute ``rational/irrational'' to the physical coordinates themselves, but rather to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Parameter domain:} irrational strip orientation and irrational $\alpha$ determine aperiodic/dense structures;
  \item \textbf{Output domain:} for fixed $m$, the output image $X_m$ is a finitely encodable object and presents a hierarchy of periodic approximations within finite time windows.
\end{itemize}
This formulation avoids misplacing number-theoretic properties in the physical spacetime coordinate domain.
