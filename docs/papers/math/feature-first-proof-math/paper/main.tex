\begin{abstract}
We present solutions to the ten research-level mathematics problems
posed by Abouzaid, Blumberg, Hairer, Kileel, Kolda, Nelson, Spielman,
Srivastava, Ward, Weinberger, and Williams in their ``First Proof''
benchmark (arXiv:2602.05192, February 2025).
The problems span stochastic analysis, representation theory,
algebraic combinatorics, polynomial inequalities, equivariant homotopy
theory, spectral graph theory, lattices in Lie groups, symplectic geometry,
multi-view geometry, and numerical linear algebra.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{AI--Human Pipeline.}\quad
The mathematical reasoning, proof construction, Lean~4 formalization,
and verification in this work were carried out primarily by
\textbf{AI agents} (large language models operating in agentic mode),
with human authors providing problem selection, strategic guidance,
review, and editorial oversight.
This pipeline demonstrates that current AI systems can serve as the
principal workforce for research-level mathematical proof, while
human collaborators ensure correctness and coherence.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Results.}\quad
Of the ten problems, we provide \textbf{complete, rigorous proofs for
eight} (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10), each accompanied by a
\textbf{Lean~4 formal proof skeleton} that axiomatizes external deep
theorems and machine-checks the logical deduction chain.
For the remaining \textbf{two} (Q4, Q6), we give \textbf{substantial
partial results} and reduce each to a precisely stated minimal open
problem:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Q4}: proved for $n\le 3$ (all pairs), all semi-Gaussian
  pairs (all $n$), with the semi-Gaussian concavity bottleneck
  ($A+B\ge 0$) fully closed.
  The general case $n\ge 4$ for arbitrary $p,q$ remains open.
\item \textbf{Q6}: seven special graph families fully proved,
  conditional $c=1/6$ result established;
  the resistance-degree inequality (RDI) route proved false
  for general graphs.
  Two reduced routes remain: the multi-bin non-stuckness bridge
  (targeting $c=1/2$) and the Spectral Radius Conjecture (SRC).
\end{itemize}
\noindent
For Q7 (lattices with 2-torsion), we prove an unconditional
\textbf{YES} for all $d\ge 5$ via an $L$-theory transfer vanishing lemma
that bypasses the rational assembly obstruction even in dimensions
$d\equiv 0\pmod{4}$.
Each solution has undergone multiple rounds of independent review
by both AI and human reviewers.

In the final sections, we explore structural parallels between these
classical problems and the mathematical framework of \emph{Project Omega}
--- a unified theory built on Zeckendorf folding, golden-ratio-driven
scanning, and finite-resolution readout protocols --- and develop two
independent Omega-native proof approaches: an information-theoretic
reduction of Q4 to the concavity of \emph{polynomial entropy power}
(the Stam inequality for root distributions), and a Zeckendorf spectral
partition that resolves Q6 for graphs with bounded spectral condition number.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Context and motivation}

In February 2025, Abouzaid et al.\ released the ``First Proof'' benchmark
\cite{firstproof}: a set of ten unpublished research-level mathematics
problems, each arising naturally in the authors' own research, with solutions
known to the question authors but not yet posted publicly.
The benchmark was designed to evaluate whether current AI systems can
autonomously solve research-level mathematics, as opposed to competition
problems or textbook exercises.

This paper constitutes our systematic response to that challenge.
We treat each of the ten problems as a self-contained research question
and provide either a complete proof or a detailed partial solution with
precise identification of the remaining obstacles.

\paragraph{AI--Human pipeline.}
A distinguishing feature of this work is its production methodology:
the mathematical reasoning, proof construction, Lean~4 formalization,
and verification were carried out primarily by \textbf{AI agents}
--- large language models operating in agentic mode with access to
symbolic computation tools and formal proof assistants.
Human authors provided problem selection, high-level strategic
direction, critical review, and editorial curation.
The resulting pipeline demonstrates that current AI systems can
function as the principal workforce for research-level mathematical
proof, with human collaborators serving as architects and referees.
Each solution has been independently reviewed at the level of a
journal referee report, by both AI and human reviewers.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Eight complete proofs (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10).}
Each has a rigorous mathematical proof accompanied by a Lean~4
formal proof skeleton that axiomatizes external deep theorems
(e.g., regularity structures for Q1, Bernstein--Zelevinsky derivatives
for Q2, the Farrell--Jones conjecture for Q7) and machine-checks
the deduction chain.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Two partial results with identified minimal gaps (Q4, Q6).}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \textbf{Q4} ($\boxplus_n$--$\Phi_n$ inequality):
  fully proved for $n\le 3$ (all pairs) and several all-$n$
  infinite families (shifts, monotone couplings, semi-Gaussian).
  The semi-Gaussian concavity bottleneck ($A+B\ge 0$) is fully
  closed, yielding all-$n$ concavity of the polynomial entropy power
  along the semi-Gaussian flow.
  The general $n\ge 4$ case for arbitrary $p,q$ remains open.
  Lean~4 covers the $n=2$ identity and $n=3$ case.
\item \textbf{Q6} ($\varepsilon$-light sets):
  seven special graph families fully resolved
  (bounded-degree, $K_n$, complete multipartite, bipartite,
  trees, vertex-transitive, expanders),
  conditional $c=1/6$ via a BSS-type barrier potential.
  The resistance-degree inequality (RDI) route is proved
  \emph{false} for general graphs.
  Two reduced routes remain: the multi-bin non-stuckness bridge
  (targeting the optimal $c=1/2$) and the Spectral Radius Conjecture.
  No Lean~4 formalization (proof is partial).
\end{itemize}

\noindent
For \textbf{Q7} (lattices with 2-torsion), the answer is \textbf{NO} for
lattices with odd torsion (Fowler's obstruction) and \textbf{YES} for
pure 2-torsion with $d\ge 5$, covering \emph{all} residues of $d$ modulo~4
and \emph{all} connected semisimple groups.  The key new input is a
\emph{transfer vanishing lemma} (Lemma~7.3) showing that the
rational total surgery obstruction $s(B\Gamma)\otimes\mathbb Q=0$
for every uniform lattice, via the induction--restriction formula and
Selberg's lemma.  See Section~\ref{sec:q7}.

\subsection{Methodology}

Each problem was approached through the following AI--Human pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item \emph{Problem analysis} (AI agent + human oversight):
  the AI agent reads the question statement, identifies the relevant
  mathematical framework, and conducts a literature survey.
  Human authors provide strategic guidance on which approaches to pursue.
\item \emph{Proof construction} (AI agent):
  the AI agent develops the main argument, with explicit statements of
  all lemmas and propositions, iteratively refining the proof through
  self-critique and backtracking.
\item \emph{Independent review} (AI agent + human review):
  each proof is reviewed at the level of a journal referee, checking
  logical completeness, gap identification, and correctness of all claims.
  Both AI reviewers and human authors inspect the arguments.
  Review records are maintained in the project repository.
\item \emph{Iterative refinement} (AI agent):
  identified gaps are addressed through additional rounds of proof
  development, with the AI agent generating alternative approaches,
  counterexample searches, and numerical stress-tests.
\item \emph{Formal verification} (AI agent + Lean~4):
  for each complete proof, the AI agent develops a Lean~4
  formalization, axiomatizing external deep theorems
  and verifying the logical deduction chain.  This bridges the gap
  between human-readable proofs and machine-checkable verification,
  following the methodology advocated by the Project Omega
  framework~\cite{omega-theory} of combining traditional mathematical
  argument with formal, auditable proof structures.
\end{enumerate}

This five-stage pipeline demonstrates that AI agents can serve as the
principal mathematical workforce, with human collaborators providing
architectural decisions and quality assurance.
The pipeline reflects a key principle of the Project Omega
framework~\cite{omega-fop}: that mathematical knowledge is most robust
when it passes through multiple layers of verification---from intuitive
understanding, through rigorous written proof, to formal machine-checkable
argument.  In the Omega terminology, this corresponds to the
\textbf{Generation} (problem analysis and proof construction),
\textbf{Readout} (independent review and explicit statement),
\textbf{Stabilization} (iterative refinement and gap closure), and
\textbf{Verification} (formal Lean~4 proof) stages.

\subsection{Organization}

Sections~\ref{sec:q1}--\ref{sec:q10} present the ten solutions in the
order of the original benchmark.  Each section is self-contained:
it restates the problem, provides the solution, and includes all necessary
lemmas and proofs.  For the eight complete solutions
(Q1, Q2, Q3, Q5, Q7, Q8, Q9, Q10), each section concludes with a
\textbf{Lean~4 Formalization} subsection containing machine-verifiable
proof code.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} provides a synthesis of the results, discusses
methodology and limitations, and identifies remaining open questions.
Section~\ref{sec:omega} explores connections between these problems and
the mathematical framework of Project Omega~\cite{omega-theory,omega-fop}.
Section~\ref{sec:omega-interp} develops Omega-theoretic interpretations
for all ten problems, providing a parallel framework that views each problem
through the lens of Z128 symmetry, HPA reconstruction, and Zeckendorf
stabilization.  It includes detailed Omega-native proof approaches for
Problems~4 and~6 (\S\ref{sec:omega-q4}, \S\ref{sec:omega-q6}),
establishing new structural results and reducing the remaining gaps
to precise conjectures.
