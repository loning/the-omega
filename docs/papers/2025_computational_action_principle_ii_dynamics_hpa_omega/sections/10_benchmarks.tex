\section{Benchmark limits}
\label{sec:benchmarks}

This section summarizes the consistency targets CAP-II is required to reproduce in appropriate limits.
The emphasis is not on claiming uniqueness, but on recording the regimes in which the routing-overhead lapse should match known weak-field and cosmological templates under the closure assumptions.

\subsection{Newtonian / weak-field limit}
\label{subsec:newtonian_limit}

In the static weak-field regime, write the metric in scalar-potential form
\begin{equation}
  \dd s^2 = -(1+2\phi)\,\dd t^2 + (1-2\phi)\,\dd\mathbf{x}^2,
  \qquad |\phi|\ll 1.
\end{equation}
Then $g_{00}\approx -(1+2\phi)$ and the lapse satisfies $\lapse\approx 1+\phi$.
We denote the ADM lapse by $\lapse$ (often written $N$ in the GR literature).
With the dictionary $\Phi=-\log\lapse$, one has $\Phi\approx -\phi$ to leading order.
The $00$ Einstein equation reduces to the Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\phi = 4\pi G\,\rho_{\mathrm{tot}},
\end{equation}
so in vacuum $\Delta\phi=0$ and asymptotic flatness forces $\phi(r)=-GM/r$ for an isolated source.
See, e.g., \cite{Wald1984,Carroll2004}.
\par
\noindent In terms of the auditable routing overhead, the computational-lapse dictionary gives
$u:=\log(\kappa/\kappa_0)=-\log\lapse=:\Phi$.
Therefore, in the same weak-field gauge one has $\Phi\approx -\phi$ and hence
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\Phi\ =\ -4\pi G\,\rho_{\mathrm{tot}}
  \qquad (|\phi|\ll 1).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Direct fit target: Schwarzschild lapse from operational \texorpdfstring{$\kappa$}{kappa}}
\label{subsec:schwarzschild_fit}

For a static, spherically symmetric exterior region in GR one has the Schwarzschild lapse
\begin{equation}
  \lapse_{\mathrm{Schw}}(r)=\sqrt{1-\frac{2GM}{r}},
  \qquad r>2GM.
\end{equation}
In the weak-field regime $r\gg 2GM$ this becomes
\begin{equation}
  \lapse_{\mathrm{Schw}}(r)=1-\frac{GM}{r}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{r^2}\right),
  \qquad
  \frac{\kappa(r)}{\kappa_0}=\frac{1}{\lapse(r)}=1+\frac{GM}{r}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{r^2}\right),
  \label{eq:kappa_schw_weak}
\end{equation}
where the last identity uses the operational lapse definition $\lapse=\kappa_0/\kappa$.
Therefore a minimal quantitative check is a linear fit of $\kappa(r)/\kappa_0-1$ against $1/r$ in an exterior window where weak-field and finite-resolution conditions hold.
\par
\noindent\textbf{Exact inversion (Schwarzschild benchmark).}
If one assumes the exact Schwarzschild form $\lapse^2(r)=1-2GM/r$ in an exterior window, then the benchmark admits a pointwise inversion:
\begin{equation}
  GM
  = \frac{r}{2}\left(1-\lapse(r)^2\right)
  = \frac{r}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\kappa(r)/\kappa_0)^2}\right)
  = \frac{r}{2}\left(1-\left(\frac{\gamma(r)}{\gamma_0}\right)^2\right),
\end{equation}
where the last identity uses the linewidth proxy $\gamma/\gamma_0\approx\lapse$.
The weak-field regressions in Section~\ref{subsec:fit_objective} are the leading-order linearizations of this inversion.
\begin{remark}[Scalar hair and why Schwarzschild is a benchmark, not an identity]
\label{rem:schwarzschild_as_benchmark}
CAP-II couples gravity to an additional scalar sector determined by the cost-to-density identification.
In Einstein gravity with a \emph{massless} scalar, the generic static spherically symmetric solution is not Schwarzschild but the Fisher/JNW family \cite{Fisher1948,JanisNewmanWinicour1968}.
Accordingly, the Schwarzschild lapse is used here as a benchmark fit target in a controlled regime: either the scalar sector is effectively short-ranged (pinned by the potential) or its backreaction is negligible in the chosen exterior window.
Departures from the benchmark provide quantitative constraints on the scalar potential family and coupling scale.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Using the scattering proxy.}
If $\kappa(r)$ is inferred from Wigner--Smith delay via \eqref{eq:ws_kappa_identification}, then in the same regime one predicts
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\gamma(r)}{\gamma_0}
  = \frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(r)}
  = \lapse(r)
  = 1-\frac{GM}{r}+O\!\left(\frac{1}{r^2}\right),
  \label{eq:gamma_schw_weak}
\end{equation}
so $GM$ can be estimated by a weighted least-squares fit of $1-\gamma(r)/\gamma_0$ against $1/r$.
The same fit yields a falsifiable consistency check: the inferred $GM$ must be stable under changing the coarse-graining scale $\varepsilon$ within the regime $h\ll\varepsilon\ll r$.

\subsection{Quantitative regression target and uncertainty (minimal)}
\label{subsec:fit_objective}

Let $(r_i,\kappa_i)_{i=1}^n$ be measurements (or compiled estimates) of routing overhead at radii $r_i$ in an exterior window, and define
\begin{equation}
  x_i := \frac{1}{r_i},
  \qquad
  y_i := \frac{\kappa_i}{\kappa_0}-1.
\end{equation}
In the weak-field model \eqref{eq:kappa_schw_weak} one has the linear regression target
\begin{equation}
  y_i = (GM)\,x_i + \epsilon_i,
  \label{eq:wls_linear_model}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_i$ collects finite-resolution bias (controlled by $\varepsilon$), finite-horizon tick-count rounding error, and measurement noise.
\begin{remark}[Calibration offset and intercept fit]
\label{rem:intercept_fit}
Model \eqref{eq:wls_linear_model} fits through the origin and is appropriate when $\kappa_0$ (or $\gamma_0$) is calibrated in a region where the benchmark normalization $\lapse\approx 1$ applies.
If instead the reference is taken in a finite region, the weak-field fit should include an intercept nuisance parameter $b$:
\begin{equation}
  y_i = (GM)\,x_i + b + \epsilon_i.
\end{equation}
\end{remark}
Given nonnegative weights $w_i$ (e.g.\ inverse variances), the weighted least-squares estimator is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{GM}
  = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i y_i}{\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^2}.
  \label{eq:gm_wls_estimator}
\end{equation}
If the errors are independent with $\mathrm{Var}(\epsilon_i)=\sigma_i^2$ and $w_i=\sigma_i^{-2}$, then the standard estimated variance is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Var}(\widehat{GM}) \approx \left(\sum_{i=1}^n w_i x_i^2\right)^{-1},
  \label{eq:gm_wls_var}
\end{equation}
up to model-misspecification bias.

\paragraph{Window conditions (what makes the fit meaningful).}
The fit window should satisfy simultaneously:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Weak field:} $r_i\gg 2GM$ so that truncation at $O(1/r^2)$ is controlled.
  \item \emph{Readout separation:} $\varepsilon \ll r_i$ so that coarse-graining does not wash out the radial profile.
  \item \emph{Microscopic separation:} $h\ll \varepsilon$ so that continuum derivatives on coarse-grained fields are meaningful.
\end{itemize}
The stability condition across $\varepsilon$ is therefore not cosmetic but a key falsifiable test: if $\widehat{GM}$ drifts systematically as $\varepsilon$ varies within the separation regime, the closure is inconsistent with a Schwarzschild-like exterior interpretation.

\paragraph{Equivalent regression from linewidth ratios.}
When $\kappa$ is inferred through the Wigner--Smith linewidth proxy \eqref{eq:ws_kappa_identification}, one may instead set
\begin{equation}
  y_i := 1-\frac{\gamma_i}{\gamma_0},
\end{equation}
and use the same estimator \eqref{eq:gm_wls_estimator} under the weak-field prediction \eqref{eq:gamma_schw_weak}.

\subsection{Homogeneous cosmology with lapse}
\label{subsec:frw_lapse}

For an FLRW ansatz with homogeneous lapse,
\begin{equation}
  \dd s^2 = -\lapse^2(t)\,\dd t^2 + a^2(t)\,\dd\Sigma_k^2,
\end{equation}
proper time is $\dd\tau=\lapse(t)\,\dd t$ and the Friedmann equations take their standard form in $\tau$.
This reduction provides a controlled setting for testing how the cost-to-density map (Assumption~\ref{ass:cost_to_density}) and the potential $V(\chi^2)$ impact effective expansion histories.

\subsection{Linearized sector (optional target)}
\label{subsec:linearized_target}

If the effective theory is to capture dynamical gravitational degrees of freedom beyond the Newtonian sector, the linearized equations around a background should reproduce standard wave-like propagation at long wavelengths.
In strictly local microscopic substrates (QCA/PQCA), causality bounds (Lieb--Robinson-type) constrain signal/front velocities; the effective description must remain consistent with such bounds in its domain of validity.
