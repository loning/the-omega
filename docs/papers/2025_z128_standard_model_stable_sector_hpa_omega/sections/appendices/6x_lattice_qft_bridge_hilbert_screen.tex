\section{Hilbert-screen lattice QFT bridge (audit-facing)}
\label{app:lattice_qft_bridge_hilbert_screen}

\paragraph{Purpose.}
\AuditTag This appendix records a minimal, fully finite bridge from the Hilbert-screen protocol objects to the standard perturbative QFT combinatorics (Wick contractions and Feynman diagrams).
It is a \emph{dictionary and scope contract}: it introduces no new axioms beyond tick and CAP, and it does not modify theorem-level folding statements.
All claims below are to be read at the interface layer as statements about explicitly defined finite-dimensional objects.

\subsection{Screen locality, site/edge carriers, and gauge redundancy}

\paragraph{Finite lattice carrier.}
\MathTag Fix a Hilbert order $n$ and let $G_n=(V_n,E_n)$ be the display graph induced by the addressing map (Definition~\ref{def:addressing_map_graph}).
We treat $V_n$ as lattice sites and $E_n$ as nearest-neighbor edges.

\paragraph{Protocol-side origin of link redundancy.}
\MathTag At fixed window length $m$, each site carries a stable label $w_x\in X_m$ and its microstate fiber $P(w_x)=\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w_x)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:omega_quotient_equals_Xm}).
Cross-site comparison requires a compensating transport rule represented (after padding to a common slot size) by a finite permutation, and local slot relabelings act by conjugation (Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge_compensation}; (E3) in Appendix~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}).

\paragraph{Lattice gauge dictionary.}
\InterfaceTag In a continuum dictionary where the CAP-minimal compact factors are $U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}), we represent compensating transport on each edge $(x,y)\in E_n$ by link variables
\[
U_{xy}^{(1)}\in U(1),\qquad U_{xy}^{(2)}\in SU(2),\qquad U_{xy}^{(3)}\in SU(3).
\]
Gauge transformations are site-local group elements $g_x^{(i)}$ acting by the standard lattice rule
$U_{xy}^{(i)}\mapsto g_y^{(i)}\,U_{xy}^{(i)}\,(g_x^{(i)})^{-1}$.
Gauge-invariant curvature proxies are Wilson loops/plaquette holonomies, consistent with the protocol holonomy diagnostics (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}).

\subsection{Fields from the closed \texorpdfstring{$18\oplus 3$}{18+3} interface}

\paragraph{Matter fields (site variables).}
\InterfaceTag Let $\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{SM}}$ be the set of $18$ left-handed Weyl multiplets closed by the labeling map (Theorem~\ref{thm:labeling_unique}; Table~\ref{tab:sm_labeling_table}).
On each site $x\in V_n$ we place Grassmann fields $\psi_f(x)$ with the corresponding $(SU(3),SU(2))_Y$ representation labels.

\paragraph{Scalar uplift field (site variable).}
\InterfaceTag When the scalar uplift interface is activated, we place a complex Higgs-doublet field $H(x)\in\CC^2$ on sites.
At the protocol level, a concrete deterministic representative is available at $m=10$ via coarse-grained suffix-bit channels and the canonical bundling
$H_1=\overline b_0+\iu\,\overline b_3$, $H_2=\overline b_1+\iu\,\overline b_2$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:scalar_sector_closed}; Appendix~\ref{app:scalar_interface_audits}).
Its SM quantum numbers are closed by bounded-family CAP selection as $(1,2)_{1/2}$ (Table~\ref{tab:higgs_quantum_numbers_cap_closure}).

\subsection{A finite Gaussian anchor, propagators, and resolvent-trace observables}

\paragraph{Quadratic kernel.}
\AuditTag Fix a finite set of lattice degrees of freedom (for example the $16$ block-quotient variables used at the anchor in the protocol RG operator audits; Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).
Let $K$ be a real symmetric positive-definite matrix (``free kernel'') acting on these variables.
In this paper, a protocol-native choice is to derive $K$ from an explicitly audited operator family (e.g.\ from the $16\times 16$ RG operator $F_n$ by a declared symmetrization/normalization convention recorded in the generating script).

\paragraph{Gaussian measure and propagator.}
\MathTag The corresponding (finite-dimensional) Gaussian partition function and expectations are
\[
Z_0:=\int \exp\!\left(-\tfrac12 \phi^\top K\phi\right)\,\dd\phi,
\qquad
\langle \mathcal{O}\rangle_0:=\frac{1}{Z_0}\int \mathcal{O}(\phi)\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12 \phi^\top K\phi\right)\,\dd\phi,
\]
with propagator (two-point function) $G:=K^{-1}$.
The resolvent family $R(z):=(K-zI)^{-1}$ and its traces are likewise finite matrix observables.

\paragraph{Protocol alignment.}
\AuditTag The operator-resolvent trace audits in the main pipeline already compute a family of trace functionals on finite operators (Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility}).
The present bridge treats these as canonical QFT-adjacent observables: $\Tr(G)$ and $\log\det K$ are the simplest one-point spectral summaries of the Gaussian anchor and appear ubiquitously in effective-action/free-energy calculations.

\subsection{Local interactions and Wick expansion}

\paragraph{Local interaction templates.}
\InterfaceTag On the lattice, gauge-covariant kinetic terms are obtained by replacing differences by link-parallel-transported differences (standard lattice gauge theory).
At the renormalizable EFT level, the minimal local interactions include:
(i) a Higgs potential $V(H)=-\mu^2 H^\dagger H+\lambda(H^\dagger H)^2$ (closed on a bounded family by CAP; Table~\ref{tab:higgs_ewsb_potential_closure}),
and (ii) Yukawa couplings between the closed fermion multiplets and $H$/$\widetilde H$ (feasibility and minimal failure points audited in Appendix~\ref{app:yukawa_beta_protocol_closure}).

\paragraph{Perturbative expansion and diagrammatics (finite).}
\MathTag Let $S(\phi)=\tfrac12\phi^\top K\phi + g\,V(\phi)$ where $V$ is a polynomial local functional (built from site/edge fields) and $g$ is a formal coupling.
Then for any polynomial observable $\mathcal{O}$,
\[
\langle \mathcal{O}\rangle
=\frac{\langle \mathcal{O}\,\exp(-gV)\rangle_0}{\langle \exp(-gV)\rangle_0}
=\sum_{k\ge 0}\frac{(-g)^k}{k!}\,\langle \mathcal{O}\,V^k\rangle_{0,\mathrm{c}},
\]
where $\langle\cdot\rangle_{0,\mathrm{c}}$ denotes Gaussian cumulants.
By Wick's theorem, each Gaussian moment is a finite sum over pairings, with each pairing contributing a product of propagator entries $G_{ij}$.
Collecting terms by contraction pattern yields the usual representation by Feynman graphs: vertices come from $V$, edges from propagators, and symmetry factors are finite combinatorial weights.

\subsection{Internal-dimension unfolding as a visual carrier (2D/3D screens)}
\label{subsec:internal_dimension_unfolding_visual}

\paragraph{Narrative.}
\InterfaceTag In the protocol language, a fixed screen site is not a featureless point: it carries a stable prefix label (the $X_6$ interface, hence the closed $18\oplus 3$ identification at the anchor) and, under resolution uplift, it carries additional suffix-bit microtexture.
These suffix channels form an ``internal fiber'' that grows with $m$ while the spatial axes remain unchanged; coarse graining makes such internal structure visible as multi-channel fields on the same screen.
This is the protocol-native version of the standard field-theory fact that fields take values in internal representation spaces (spin/isospin/color), except here the internal degrees are realized as uplift microtexture with a functorial prefix semantics.
