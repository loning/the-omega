\section{Time and mass as delay: scattering and relativistic lapse dictionaries (interface)}
\label{app:time_mass_delay}

This section records the matching-layer dictionaries underlying the mass-as-delay discussion in Section~\ref{subsubsec:mass_as_delay_interface}.
It connects protocol-level cost/overhead coordinates to standard measurable proxies (scattering delay, redshift/lapse) and to standard kinematic relations (special and general relativity), while keeping these statements at the interface layer rather than as theorem-level consequences of the folding core.

\subsection{Scattering delay as an operational proxy: Wigner--Smith}
\label{app:wigner_smith_delay_z128}

In platforms where a scattering description is available, delays are directly measurable from complex $S$-parameters as functions of frequency.
Let $S(\omega)$ be a unitary scattering matrix (lossless elastic scattering) at angular frequency $\omega$.
The Wigner--Smith time-delay matrix is defined by \cite{Wigner1955,Smith1960}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wigner_smith_omega}
Q(\omega):=-\iu\,S(\omega)^\dagger\frac{\dd S}{\dd \omega},
\end{equation}
and a common scalar summary is the total delay
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tau_ws_trace_omega}
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega):=\Tr Q(\omega).
\end{equation}
In a one-channel setting $S(\omega)=\e^{\iu\delta(\omega)}$, \eqref{eq:wigner_smith_omega} reduces to the group/phase derivative
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:wigner_smith_one_channel}
\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)=\frac{\dd\delta}{\dd\omega}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{From delay to overhead and lapse (clock-rate dictionary)}
\label{app:delay_to_lapse_dictionary}

Fix a reference tick duration $\tau_0>0$ that defines one unit of baseline protocol time in physical units (seconds).
We compress delay into a dimensionless overhead proxy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:kappa_ws_def}
\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega):=\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)}{\tau_0},
\end{equation}
and define an associated lapse proxy by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:lapse_ws_def}
N_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega):=\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_0$ is a chosen reference overhead (a normalization convention).
Operationally, larger delay corresponds to larger overhead and hence to a smaller lapse proxy.

This matches the general ``computational lapse'' dictionary used elsewhere in the HPA--$\Omega$ series: a local overhead field $\kappa$ induces a clock-rate factor $N=\kappa_0/\kappa$ so that local proper time satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dtau_lapse_dictionary}
\dd\tau_{\mathrm{loc}}(x)=N(x)\,\dd t.
\end{equation}
In this paper, we use the dictionary only as a matching layer: it is not used as a premise for any folding or labeling theorem.

\subsection{Mass as a time scale: Compton clocks and the depth coordinate}
\label{app:compton_clock_dictionary}

Independently of any specific dynamics, a mass scale defines a frequency scale by combining the standard relations $E=mc^2$ and $E=\hbar\omega$ \cite{Einstein1905,NielsenChuang2010}.
Define the Compton angular frequency and time scale
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:compton_clock}
\omega_C(\mu):=\frac{\mu c^2}{\hbar},
\qquad
\tau_C(\mu):=\frac{1}{\omega_C(\mu)}=\frac{\hbar}{\mu c^2}.
\end{equation}
Because the present paper works primarily with ratios relative to $m_e$, the constants $c$ and $\hbar$ cancel:
\[
\frac{\omega_C(\mu)}{\omega_C(m_e)}=\frac{\mu}{m_e},
\qquad
\frac{\tau_C(\mu)}{\tau_C(m_e)}=\frac{m_e}{\mu}.
\]
Consequently the resolution coordinate used in the mass-spectrum closure (Section~\ref{sec:mass_spectrum_closure}) can be read equally as a log-frequency or log-time coordinate:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:r_as_log_compton}
r(\mu)=\log_\varphi\!\left(\frac{\mu}{m_e}\right)
=\log_\varphi\!\left(\frac{\omega_C(\mu)}{\omega_C(m_e)}\right)
=-\log_\varphi\!\left(\frac{\tau_C(\mu)}{\tau_C(m_e)}\right).
\end{equation}
This gives a strong interface meaning to ``mass as depth'': it is also ``mass as a clock-rate coordinate'' in the standard Compton sense.
