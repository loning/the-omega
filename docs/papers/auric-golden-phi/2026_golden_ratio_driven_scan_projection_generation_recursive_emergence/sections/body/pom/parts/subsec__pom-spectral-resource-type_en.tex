\subsubsection{Spectral Resource Types: \texorpdfstring{$\Sigma_{\mathrm{space}}$}{Sigma space} and the Convex Closure Monad \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{Conv}$}{Conv}}\label{subsec:pom-spectral-resource-type}

The preceding discussion organized the Perron roots \(r_q(u)\) of the weighted collision moment-kernel into a two-dimensional pressure surface
\(
P(q,u)=\log r_q(u)-q\log|A_{\mathrm{out}}|
\)
(\S\ref{subsec:pom-pressure-surface-qu}), which will be used in \S\ref{subsec:pom-quantile-budget-law} to control tail quantiles and prime-register quotas.
To upgrade ``the spectrum is a resource'' into a derivable type system (thereby directing strategy selection in rewriting/compilation), we tighten \(q\mapsto P(q,u)\) into a discrete convex budget object and regard its convex closure as the normal form.

\paragraph{Budget domain and discrete convex closure}
Let
\[
\mathbf{Bud}:=\{\,b:\ZZ_{\ge 2}\to\overline{\RR}\,\},
\qquad
b\le c\ \Longleftrightarrow\ (\forall q\ge 2)\ b(q)\le c(q),
\qquad
(b+c)(q):=b(q)+c(q),
\]
viewed as a symmetric monoidal preorder category.
Let \(\mathbf{Conv}\subseteq \mathbf{Bud}\) be the full subcategory satisfying the discrete convexity condition
\[
b(q+1)-b(q)\ \ge\ b(q)-b(q-1)\qquad(q\ge 3)
\]
and denote the inclusion functor by \(J:\mathbf{Conv}\hookrightarrow \mathbf{Bud}\).
Define the \textbf{discrete lower convex closure} \(\mathbb{T}:\mathbf{Bud}\to\mathbf{Conv}\) as follows: for any \(b\in\mathbf{Bud}\), \(\mathbb{T}(b)\) is the greatest discrete convex function \(c\) satisfying \(c\le b\) (equivalently, the lower convex hull of its epigraph; a reproducible implementation is described in Remark \ref{rem:pom-pressure-multifractal-q60}).
Then \(\mathbb{T}\dashv J\), which induces an idempotent \emph{closure monad} \(J\circ\mathbb{T}:\mathbf{Bud}\to\mathbf{Bud}\):
\[
\mathbb{T}(b)\le b,\qquad
\mathbb{T}(\mathbb{T}(b))=\mathbb{T}(b),\qquad
b\le b'\ \Longrightarrow\ \mathbb{T}(b)\le \mathbb{T}(b').
\]
Under the convex duality framework, this closure can also be obtained via the bi-dual of the same Legendre--Fenchel conjugate (Remark \ref{rem:pom-legendre-chernoff-same-dual}).

\paragraph{Spectral resource and type normal form for projection words}
Following the spectral semantics functor \(\mathcal{S}:\mathbf{W}\to \mathbf{Kern}\) of Remark \ref{rem:pom-word-category}.
For any projection word \(w\), temperature parameter \(u\in(0,1]\) and character channel \(\chi\), let \(r^{(w)}_{q,\chi}(u)\) denote the Perron root of the weighted collision moment-kernel of the kernel \(\mathcal{S}(w)\) under \(\chi\)-twist and temperature (the construction is analogous to \S\ref{subsec:pom-pressure-surface-qu}, with the underlying kernel replaced by the \(\chi\)-channel of \(\mathcal{S}(w)\)).
Define the \textbf{spatial spectral resource curve} as
\[
\Sigma_{\mathrm{space}}(w)(q;\chi,u)
:=\log r^{(w)}_{q,\chi}(u)-q\log|A_{\mathrm{out}}|.
\]
and define the \textbf{spectral resource type normal form} as
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathrm{Ty}_{\mathrm{space}}(w;\chi,u)
:=\mathbb{T}\bigl(q\mapsto \Sigma_{\mathrm{space}}(w)(q;\chi,u)\bigr)\in\mathbf{Conv}.}
\]
In the base case where \(w\) is the collision moment readout gate of this paper and \(\chi=\chi_0\), \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{space}}(w)\) reduces to the two-dimensional pressure \(P(q,u)\).

Hence, after fixing the channel parameters \((\chi,u)\), the above construction can be viewed as a combinatorial valuation from the projection word $2$-category to the convex budget domain:
\[
\mathbf{PW}\xrightarrow{\ \Sigma_{\mathrm{space}}(-;\chi,u)\ }\mathbf{Bud}\xrightarrow{\ \mathbb{T}\ }\mathbf{Conv},
\]
where the idempotent closure monad given by \(\mathbb{T}\dashv J\) provides a canonical ``type normalizer.''

\begin{remark}[Constant-level vs.\ exponential-level anomalies: complementary verification via \texorpdfstring{$\mathsf{Anom}_G$}{AnomG} and \texorpdfstring{$r^{(w)}_{q,\chi}$}{rqchi}]\label{rem:pom-constant-vs-exponential-anom}
The anomaly signature \(\mathsf{Anom}_G(K;\theta)\) (Definition \ref{def:pom-anom-signature}) records the \emph{constant-level drift} of twisted channels in the Abel/Hadamard finite part of \(\zeta\);
meanwhile \(\Sigma_{\mathrm{space}}(w)(q;\chi,u)=\log r^{(w)}_{q,\chi}(u)-q\log|A_{\mathrm{out}}|\) records the \emph{exponential-level principal scale} of the \(q\)-collision spectrum.
One may therefore define an exponential-level ``spectral anomaly'' associated with the character channel (comparing nontrivial twist against the trivial channel at the same temperature \(u\)):
\[
\Delta^{\mathrm{exp}}_{\chi}(w;q,u)
:=\log r^{(w)}_{q,\chi}(u)-\log r^{(w)}_{q,\chi_0}(u).
\]
When \(\Delta^{\mathrm{exp}}_{\chi}\equiv 0\) (over the audited \(q\)-window/grid), the twisted channel does not affect the exponential growth of the collision spectrum; whether \(\mathsf{Anom}_G\) vanishes further characterizes whether the constant-level residual can be eliminated by rewriting.
This provides a two-dimensional cross-verification for diagnosing ``commutativity failure'': the same character family can be checked at the constant level via \(\mathsf{Anom}\) and at the exponential level via \(\Delta^{\mathrm{exp}}_{\chi}\).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Type-directed rewriting: upgrading ``optimization'' to normal form selection]\label{rem:pom-type-directed-normalization}
When the same readout task admits multiple auditable compilation paths (e.g., two hardening routes for sequential gates: temperature hardening vs.\ moment-quantile-CRT spatialization, Remark \ref{rem:pom-hardening-u-vs-q}), one often obtains a family of candidate projection words \(\{w_i\}\) that are equivalent (or \(\varepsilon\)-sound equivalent, Definition \ref{def:pom-eps-sound-rewrite}) under the visible output specification.
In this case, one may use \(\mathrm{Ty}_{\mathrm{space}}(w_i;\chi,u)\) as a static budget type: select the pointwise-minimal element under the order \( \le \) as the lexicographic normal form representative, and record the deficit as a certificate (e.g., the failure probability ledger of \((\mathrm{RCRT}_\varepsilon)\) and the \(\gamma\)-certificate).
Since \(\mathbb{T}\) normalizes the budget into a discrete convex object, the optimization (e.g., searching for the dual-optimal moment order \(q^\star\)) has a discrete quasi-convex structure over the integers; see the unimodality discussion in \S\ref{subsec:pom-quantile-budget-law}, which unifies ``choosing \(q\)'' and ``choosing a compilation strategy'' into a single auditable type minimization problem.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[Kink and kink set (discrete convex budget)]\label{def:pom-kink-set}
Let \(b\in\mathbf{Conv}\), and consider its discrete slopes on a finite window \(q\in\{2,3,\dots,Q_{\max}\}\):
\[
\Delta b(q):=b(q)-b(q-1)\qquad(q=3,\dots,Q_{\max}).
\]
Discrete convexity is equivalent to \(\Delta b(q)\) being nondecreasing in \(q\).
Define the \textbf{kink set} of \(b\) on this window as
\[
\mathrm{Kink}(b)
:=\{2,Q_{\max}\}\ \cup\
\Bigl\{\,q\in\{3,\dots,Q_{\max}-1\}:\ \Delta b(q+1)>\Delta b(q)\Bigr\}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Kink principle for \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-selection (discrete optimization reduction via type closure)]\label{prop:pom-kink-principle-q-selection}
Fix the channel parameters \((\chi,u)\) and a projection word \(w\), and let
\(
b:=\mathrm{Ty}_{\mathrm{space}}(w;\chi,u)\in\mathbf{Conv}
\)
be its spectral resource type normal form (as defined in \S\ref{subsec:pom-spectral-resource-type}).
Then on any finite window \(q\in\{2,\dots,Q_{\max}\}\):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Only kinks need to be checked)} For any affine objective \(L(q)=aq+c\), the optimal solution set of the discrete convex optimization
  \[
  \arg\min_{q\in\{2,\dots,Q_{\max}\}}\bigl(b(q)+L(q)\bigr)
  \]
  is a (possibly degenerate) contiguous integer interval; hence there exists an optimal point lying in \(\mathrm{Kink}(b)\).
  \item \textbf{(Equivalent dual search formulation)} Equivalently, for any \(\gamma\in\RR\), the maximization of
  \(
  q\mapsto (q-1)\gamma-b(q)
  \)
  (discrete Legendre--Fenchel dual) can be localized by comparing the discrete slopes \(\Delta b(q)\) with \(\gamma\) at the elements of \(\mathrm{Kink}(b)\): the optimal \(q^\star\) can be taken as some kink such that \(\gamma\) falls within its left-right slope interval.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore, any \(q\)-optimization ``based on the type closure \(\mathbb{T}\)'' need not perform a full scan: it suffices to conduct a finite check on the kink set of the type normal form (the number of kinks is far smaller than the scan window length and can be output as a byproduct when computing \(\mathbb{T}\)).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(1) Since \(b\in\mathbf{Conv}\), \(\Delta b(q)\) is nondecreasing, and \(b(q)+aq\) remains a discrete convex function.
The sublevel sets of a discrete convex function are intervals, so the optimal solution set is also an interval; the interval endpoints correspond to locations where the discrete slope undergoes a jump (or lie at the window boundary), i.e., they belong to the kink set of Definition \ref{def:pom-kink-set}.
(2) This is the dual formulation of (1): maximizing \((q-1)\gamma-b(q)\) is equivalent to minimizing \(b(q)-(q-1)\gamma\); the criterion for the optimal \(q^\star\) is given by the monotonicity of the discrete slopes.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection{Quantile Budget Law: From Fiber Multiplicity Tails to Prime-Register Budgets}\label{subsec:pom-quantile-budget-law}

This paragraph rewrites the ``space-for-time'' prime-register design from a ``worst-case fiber'' specification to a ``tail quantile'' specification, so that the resource budget can be directly determined by the moment-kernel pressure curve.

\begin{definition}[Tail quantile of the fiber index]\label{def:pom-gamma-quantile}
Following Definition \ref{def:pom-fiber-spectrum} for \(\gamma_m(x)=\frac{1}{m}\log d_m(x)\), sample the random variable \(\gamma_m(X)\) under the output distribution \(w_m(x)=d_m(x)/2^m\).
For \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\), define the tail quantile
$$
\gamma_{m}(\varepsilon):=\inf\Bigl\{\gamma\ge 0:\ \mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[\gamma_m(X)>\gamma\big]\le \varepsilon\Bigr\}.
$$
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Chernoff upper bound: bounding the tail via \texorpdfstring{$S_q$}{Sq}]\label{prop:pom-quantile-chernoff}
For any integer \(q\ge 2\) and any \(\gamma\ge 0\),
$$
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[\gamma_m(X)\ge \gamma\big]
\ \le\
\exp\!\bigl(-(q-1)m\gamma\bigr)\cdot \frac{S_q(m)}{2^m}.
$$
In particular, if \(\tau(q)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log S_q(m)\) (Definition \ref{def:pom-moment-pressure}), then at the exponential scale
$$
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log \mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[\gamma_m(X)\ge \gamma\big]
\ \le\
\tau(q)-\log 2-(q-1)\gamma.
$$
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By Proposition \ref{prop:pom-fiber-index-cgf},
\(
\mathbb{E}_{w_m}\big[\exp((q-1)m\gamma_m(X))\big]=S_q(m)/2^m
\).
Applying the Markov inequality to the event \(\{\gamma_m(X)\ge\gamma\}\) yields the first inequality; taking logarithms, dividing by \(m\), and taking \(\limsup\) yields the second. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Dual optimization: turning \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q} into a solvable budget controller]\label{cor:pom-quantile-dual-optimizer}
Let \(\tau(q)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log S_q(m)\) (Definition \ref{def:pom-moment-pressure}), and define
\[
\Phi(q):=\tau(q)-\log 2,
\qquad
I(\gamma):=\sup_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}\bigl((q-1)\gamma-\Phi(q)\bigr).
\]
Then for any \(\gamma\ge 0\) the exponential-scale upper bound holds:
\[
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log \mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[\gamma_m(X)\ge \gamma\big]
\ \le\ -\,I(\gamma).
\]
In particular, when \(S_q(m)=\Theta(r_q^m)\) (so that \(\tau(q)=\log r_q\)),
\(
\Phi(q)=\log r_q-\log 2
\)
organizes tail control entirely as the convex dual of the computable moment principal scale family \(\{r_q\}_{q\ge 2}\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Strongest computable tail bound from the integer spectrum (finite-order truncation)]\label{cor:pom-integer-moment-strongest-tail-bound}\label{cor:pom-best-computable-tail-bound-integer-moments}
In practice one often has access to only finitely many integer moment orders: given \(K\ge 2\), suppose only \(\{\Phi(q)\}_{2\le q\le K}\) (equivalently \(\{\tau(q)\}_{2\le q\le K}\) or \(\{r_q\}_{2\le q\le K}\)) are known.
Define the truncated dual
\[
I_{\le K}(\gamma):=\sup_{2\le q\le K}\bigl((q-1)\gamma-\Phi(q)\bigr).
\]
Then the exponential-scale upper bound still holds:
\[
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log \mathbb{P}_{w_m}\big[\gamma_m(X)\ge \gamma\big]
\ \le\ -\,I_{\le K}(\gamma).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Information-theoretic optimality (under the condition of ``knowing only these integer moments'')]\label{rem:pom-integer-moment-tail-optimality}
The \(I_{\le K}\) given by Corollary \ref{cor:pom-integer-moment-strongest-tail-bound} is not merely ``a computable upper bound,'' but rather the \textbf{strongest uniform exponential tail bound} under the information set \(\{\Phi(q)\}_{2\le q\le K}\):
if one attempts to strictly improve the tail exponent bound at some \(\gamma\) (i.e., claim \(J(\gamma)>I_{\le K}(\gamma)\) for all candidate distributions consistent with the moment information), then in general it is not possible to simultaneously maintain consistency with all moment constraints for \(q\le K\).
This can be understood as a convex duality/dual linear programming phenomenon for ``moment information \(\leftrightarrow\) tail bounds'': the integer moment spectrum provides a family of linear (affine) certificates, and taking their upper envelope yields the strongest exponential certificate that cannot be uniformly improved within this information set.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Quantile budget for prime-registers (replacing worst-case values with tail bounds)]\label{cor:pom-prime-register-quantile-budget}
Take \(\varepsilon\in(0,1)\) and let \(B_m(\varepsilon):=\exp(m\,\gamma_m(\varepsilon))\).
If a set of pairwise coprime moduli is chosen so that their product \(P_m\) satisfies
$$
P_m\ >\ 2\,B_m(\varepsilon),
$$
then under the interval readout/reconstruction specification of Definition \ref{def:pom-order-elimination-rewrite}, when using a prime-register (CRT) for external storage of fiber labels, the probability that ``label overflow prevents unique reconstruction'' is at most \(\varepsilon\) (under the \(w_m\)-sampled output stable type specification).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For an output sample \(X\sim w_m\), its fiber size is \(d_m(X)=\exp(m\gamma_m(X))\).
When \(\gamma_m(X)\le\gamma_m(\varepsilon)\), we have \(d_m(X)\le B_m(\varepsilon)\).
Taking the interval bound \(B=B_m(\varepsilon)\), the side condition \(P>2B\) of Definition \ref{def:pom-order-elimination-rewrite} ensures unique CRT reconstruction.
The probability of \(\gamma_m(X)>\gamma_m(\varepsilon)\) does not exceed \(\varepsilon\) by Definition \ref{def:pom-gamma-quantile}. \qed
\end{proof}

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Quasi-necessity of the quantile budget threshold (interval readout version)}
Under the ``interval readout/reconstruction'' protocol of Definition \ref{def:pom-order-elimination-rewrite}, the product budget \(P_m\) of the prime-register not only provides the sufficient threshold of Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-quantile-budget}, but is also \emph{sharp} under this protocol:
if an external prime-register scheme aims to push the ``failure probability due to label overflow preventing unique reconstruction'' to \(\le \varepsilon\), then its total modular product must satisfy
\[
\boxed{\ P_m\ >\ 2\,B_m(\varepsilon)\ }.
\]
Equivalently, if \(P_m\le 2B_m(\varepsilon)\), then for any interval bound \(B\) satisfying the side condition \(P_m>2B\) (necessarily \(B<P_m/2\)), the minimality of the tail quantile (Definition \ref{def:pom-gamma-quantile}) implies
\(\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\!\big[d_m(X)>B\big]>\varepsilon\), so the failure probability cannot be driven below \(\le\varepsilon\) under the same protocol
(up to strict inequality/integer rounding details).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Information conservation---quantile law (scaling of external information required for \(\varepsilon\)-tolerant reversibility)}
Under the specification ``microstates sampled uniformly, output \(X\sim w_m\), given \(X=x\) the fiber is uniformly distributed,'' the information erased by the projection is precisely characterized by
\[
L_m(X):=\log d_m(X)
\]
(in nats); its \((1-\varepsilon)\)-tail quantile is
\(\log B_m(\varepsilon)=m\,\gamma_m(\varepsilon)\).
The quasi-necessity of the quantile budget threshold shows that under this protocol, achieving failure probability \(\le\varepsilon\) for external storage requires providing at least
\[
\log P_m\ \ge\ \log 2+\log B_m(\varepsilon)
\qquad(\text{equivalently }P_m>2B_m(\varepsilon)),
\]
so \(B_m(\varepsilon)=\exp(m\gamma_m(\varepsilon))\) gives, at the exponential scale, the \emph{exact scaling} of the external information needed to ``recover the information lost by projection,'' and this scale cannot be further reduced.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Pressure--dual design equation (recovering the minimal prime-register growth rate from \(\tau(q)\))}
Let \(\beta_m(\varepsilon):=\frac{1}{m}\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\).
By Proposition \ref{prop:pom-quantile-chernoff} and the definition of the tail quantile (Definition \ref{def:pom-gamma-quantile}), the quantile strength can be upper-bounded at the exponential scale as a closed-form lower envelope over \(q\):
\[
\boxed{\;
\gamma_m(\varepsilon)\ \le\
\inf_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}
\frac{\tau(q)-\log 2+\beta_m(\varepsilon)}{q-1}
\qquad(\text{exponential scale}).\;}
\]
Therefore, under the interval readout/reconstruction specification of Definition \ref{def:pom-order-elimination-rewrite}, a sufficient auditable budget can be written as
\[
\boxed{\;
\log P_m\ \gtrsim\ \log 2\ +\ m\cdot
\inf_{q\in\ZZ_{\ge 2}}
\frac{\tau(q)-\log 2+\beta_m(\varepsilon)}{q-1}\; }.
\]
Under the verified specification \(S_q(m)=\Theta(r_q^m)\), the \(\tau(q)\) above can be replaced by \(\log r_q\), and Remark \ref{rem:pom-quantile-budget-computable} provides a fully computable certificate-based design equation.

\begin{corollary}[\texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-parameterized quota upper bound: failure probability--register exponential budget family]\label{cor:pom-prime-register-q-quota-family}
For any integer \(q\ge 2\) and any threshold \(B>0\), the following strict tail bound holds:
\[
\boxed{\;
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\!\bigl(d_m(X)\ge B\bigr)
\ \le\
\frac{S_q(m)}{2^m}\cdot B^{-(q-1)}\; }.
\]
In particular, writing \(B=e^{m\gamma}\),
\[
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\!\bigl(d_m(X)\ge e^{m\gamma}\bigr)
\ \le\
\exp\!\bigl(-(q-1)m\gamma\bigr)\cdot \frac{S_q(m)}{2^m},
\]
and under the specification \(S_q(m)=\Theta(r_q^m)\) one reads off the exponential-scale ``phase diagram''
\[
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\!\bigl(d_m(X)\ge e^{m\gamma}\bigr)
\ \lesssim\
\exp\!\Bigl(m\bigl(\log r_q-\log 2-(q-1)\gamma\bigr)\Bigr).
\]
Therefore, if one wishes to achieve \(\mathbb{P}_{w_m}(d_m(X)\ge B)\le \varepsilon\), a fully explicit sufficient condition is to take
\[
\boxed{\;
B\ \ge\ B_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\varepsilon)
::=\varepsilon^{-1/(q-1)}\left(\frac{S_q(m)}{2^m}\right)^{\!1/(q-1)}\; }.
\]
Under the verified exponential specification \(S_q(m)=\Theta(r_q^m)\), this is equivalent to the exponential-scale closed-form budget family
\[
\boxed{\;
B\ \gtrsim\ B_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)
::=\varepsilon^{-1/(q-1)}\left(\frac{r_q}{2}\right)^{\!m/(q-1)}\; }.
\]
Substituting into the CRT side condition (Definition \ref{def:pom-order-elimination-rewrite}, \(P>2B\)) yields an auditable prime-register quota family:
\[
\boxed{\;
P_m\ >\ 2\,B_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\varepsilon)
\ \Longrightarrow\
\mathbb{P}(\text{CRT label overflow prevents unique reconstruction})\ \le\ \varepsilon\; }.
\]
Writing
\[
\beta_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}
:=\frac{1}{q-1}\left(\frac{1}{m}\log S_q(m)-\log 2\right),
\qquad
\beta_q:=\frac{\log r_q-\log 2}{q-1},
\]
then
\(
\log B_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\varepsilon)
=m\,\beta_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}+\frac{1}{q-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)
\)
and under the \(r_q\) specification,
\(
\log B_m^{(q)}(\varepsilon)=m\,\beta_q+\frac{1}{q-1}\log(1/\varepsilon)
\).
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Set \(\gamma=(1/m)\log B\). Then the event \(\{d_m(X)\ge B\}\) is equivalent to \(\{\gamma_m(X)\ge \gamma\}\).
Substituting \(\gamma=(1/m)\log B\) into Proposition \ref{prop:pom-quantile-chernoff} gives (where \(\pi_m\) denotes the old notation for \(w_m\)):
\[
\mathbb{P}_{w_m}\!\bigl(d_m(X)\ge B\bigr)
=\mathbb{P}_{\pi_m}\!\bigl(\gamma_m(X)\ge \tfrac{1}{m}\log B\bigr)
\le
\exp\!\bigl(-(q-1)\log B\bigr)\cdot \frac{S_q(m)}{2^m}
=\frac{S_q(m)}{2^m}\,B^{-(q-1)}.
\]
Setting the right-hand side \(\le \varepsilon\) and solving for \(B\) gives \(B_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\varepsilon)\).
The final statement ``\(P_m>2B_{m,q}^{\mathrm{fin}}(\varepsilon)\Rightarrow\) failure probability \(\le\varepsilon\)'' follows immediately from the monotonicity of Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-quantile-budget} for any \(B\): when \(P_m>2B\), overflow failure can only occur on the event \(\{d_m(X)>B\}\).
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[ISA-level constants: \texorpdfstring{$\beta_q$}{betaq} and endpoint closure]\label{rem:pom-betaq-isa}
Corollary \ref{cor:pom-prime-register-q-quota-family} compresses the prime-register ``quota'' into an exponential law that can be directly substituted into cost models:
\[
\log P_m\ \gtrsim\ \log 2\ +\ m\,\beta_q\ +\ \frac{1}{q-1}\log(1/\varepsilon),
\qquad
\beta_q=\frac{\log r_q-\log 2}{q-1}.
\]
Therefore each integer \(q\ge 2\) induces an auditable ``register exponential budget slope'' \(\beta_q\) (Table \ref{tab:fold_collision_renyi_spectrum} provides exact \(r_q\) for \(q\le 17\)).
For example, the lowest-order certificate \(q=2\) gives
\[
B_m^{(2)}(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{-1}\left(\frac{r_2}{2}\right)^m,
\qquad
\beta_2=\log(r_2/2),
\qquad
r_2=2.481194304092,
\]
yielding an ``extremely rigid'' closed-form spatial budget.
\par
More importantly, the endpoint constant locks the ``moment/time axis'' R\'{e}nyi fingerprint and the ``space/worst-case fiber'' to the same fact.
By Proposition \ref{prop:pom-rq-qinfty-endpoint} (equivalently, by the closed-form maximum fiber theorem \ref{thm:pom-max-fiber} and the endpoint corollary \ref{cor:pom-max-fiber-rate-endpoint}),
\[
\lim_{q\to\infty}\frac{1}{q}\log r_q=\log\sqrt{\varphi},
\qquad\text{hence}\qquad
\lim_{q\to\infty}\beta_q=\log\sqrt{\varphi}.
\]
Moreover, \(D_m=\max_x d_m(x)\), with the closed-form/enumeration table (Table \ref{tab:fold_max_fiber_achievers_phase}), shows that
\(
m^{-1}\log D_m
\)
numerically approaches the same constant \(\log\sqrt{\varphi}\) rapidly, resulting in a closed-loop alignment of the ``high-order R\'{e}nyi endpoint'' and the ``worst-case fiber exponent'' within the framework of this paper.
\end{remark}
