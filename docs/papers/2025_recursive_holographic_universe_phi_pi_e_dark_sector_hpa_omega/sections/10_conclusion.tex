
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a discrete-readout cosmology anchored in a fully explicit minimal folding model.
The mathematical core is a finite local readout space $\Omega_6=\{0,1\}^6$ with $|\Omega_6|=64$ and a $\varphi$-stable admissible sector $X_6$ of size $21$, together with a canonical $\pi$-refinement $21=18\oplus 3$ and an $\e$-channel analytic pole-barrier viewpoint.
The folding map $\mathrm{Fold}_6:\{0,\dots,63\}\twoheadrightarrow X_6$ is computable with explicit preimages and a rigid degeneracy histogram, yielding a structured notion of hidden degrees of freedom.

On the cosmological side, we proposed identifications that are testable rather than purely narrative:
ghost microstates and folding redundancy induce routing/compilation overhead, which couples to the metric via an effective lapse and produces gravitational effects without requiring new luminous particles.
Resolution refinement induces grid tension and an information potential that can behave as a dark-energy component.
Horizons are interpreted as computational boundaries generated by channel saturation, while singularities correspond to recursive resolution resets.

Finally, we stated falsifiable observational signatures and an executable reconstruction protocol for an overhead proxy field $\chi$.
The minimal $64\to 21$ model is not claimed to be the full universe; it is a concrete, computable anchor intended to constrain higher-resolution generalizations by data.
