\section{Gravity as computation: refractive index, routing cost, and Fermat principle}
\label{sec:gravity_as_computation}

\subsection{Computational refractive index and propagation delay}
\label{subsec:comp_index}

The computational lapse $\lapse(x)=\kappa_0/\kappa(x)$ can be interpreted as an effective slowdown factor: under a fixed background depth $\dd t$, the number of realizable local logical updates is reduced by routing overhead. This is equivalent to viewing spacetime as a \emph{computational refractive medium} with index
\begin{equation}
  n_{\mathrm{comp}}(x) \sim \frac{1}{\lapse(x)} = \frac{\kappa(x)}{\kappa_0}.
\end{equation}
Regions of larger $n_{\mathrm{comp}}$ incur larger effective delays, matching the operational content of gravitational time delay (e.g.\ Shapiro delay) when the dictionary $\lapse\leftrightarrow\sqrt{-g_{00}}$ is adopted.

In the weak-field regime, one may write $g_{00}\approx -(1+2\phi)$ with Newtonian potential $\phi$, so $\lapse\approx 1+\phi$ \cite{Carroll2004,Wald1984}. Then $n_{\mathrm{comp}}\approx 1-\phi$, making the optical-length language directly compatible with standard lensing and delay calculations.

\subsection{A computational form of Fermat's principle}
\label{subsec:fermat}

In this readout--implementation language, ``geodesics'' are not introduced as primitive geometric minimizers; rather, propagation follows trajectories minimizing \emph{total implementation cost}. In a slowly varying background, this reduces to Fermat's principle in the effective index $n_{\mathrm{comp}}(x)$: light and information follow paths of stationary optical length, which in turn reproduces the familiar macroscopic bending and delay phenomena.

Concretely, for spatial paths $\gamma$ connecting two endpoints, CAP suggests an effective optical functional
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{T}[\gamma] \propto \int_{\gamma} n_{\mathrm{comp}}(\mathbf{x})\, \dd \ell ,
\end{equation}
so that stationary $\delta \mathcal{T}=0$ yields the Euler--Lagrange equations equivalent to null geodesics of the associated optical metric. The novelty is semantic rather than algebraic: $n_{\mathrm{comp}}$ is not a material refractive index but an operational proxy for routing overhead.

CAP thus supplies a second closed chain:
\begin{equation}
  \text{routing overhead } \kappa
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \text{computational lapse } \lapse
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \text{effective metric component } g_{00}
  \;\Rightarrow\;
  \text{refraction/delay and emergent curvature}.
\end{equation}
