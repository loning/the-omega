\section{Minimal ontological commitments: arithmetic as modal geometry}
\label{sec:skeleton}

\begin{definition}[Counting as modality (interpretive)]
\label{def:counting_as_modality}
HPA treats ``counting'' as the selection of a discrete \emph{mode} rather than as a linear successor process.
Concretely, an integer label $n$ is interpreted as an $n$-fold symmetry class of a fundamental unit state (the monolith) under a scan protocol.
In this view, arithmetic is not the accumulation of units along a line, but the tuning and composition of discrete resonant patterns.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Integers as resonant modes (not quantities)}
Instead of viewing $\NN_{>0}$ as a collection of linearly accumulated quantities (where $n+1$ is obtained by adding a unit), we treat each $n\in\NN_{>0}$ as a distinct \emph{resonant mode} or standing-wave topology on a fundamental cycle.
An ``$n$'' labels an $n$-fold symmetry: the same underlying monolith is partitioned into $n$ indistinguishable geometric sectors.
Accordingly, the transition $n\to n+1$ is not privileged as a primitive axiom of the ontology; it is a discrete reconfiguration of mode type that becomes visible only after a chosen readout grammar is imposed (Section~\ref{sec:interference}).

\subsection{Multiplication as mode mixing}
The multiplicative operation $m\cdot n$ is interpreted as \emph{mode mixing}: composing an $m$-fold and an $n$-fold symmetry yields an $mn$-fold composite symmetry.
This preserves the input structure in a rigid way (a product of orders) and motivates why multiplication is taken as the intrinsic first-order operation of the object domain, while additive bookkeeping appears only after projection.

\subsection{Primes as irreducible geometric knots}
Within the modal viewpoint, primes acquire an ``irreducibility'' meaning that is geometric rather than procedural.
A prime mode $p\in\PP$ is one that cannot be synthesized as a nontrivial mixing of lower-order modes: composites are precisely those modes that admit a nontrivial decomposition.
The familiar prime factorization theorem then becomes a statement that every composite mode decomposes uniquely into irreducible prime constituents, so that prime data is the atomic spectrum of the multiplicative ontology.

\paragraph{Formal \texorpdfstring{$\mathbb{F}_1$}{F1} skeleton.}
While the preceding language is interpretive, the formal skeleton used throughout the paper is minimal.
Let
$$
M := (\NN_{>0},\cdot)
$$
be the multiplicative monoid of positive integers.
By ``$\mathbb{F}_1$ skeleton'' we only use the following core.
For background on $\mathbb{F}_1$ viewpoints (well beyond what is used here), see e.g.~\cite{Deitmar2005}.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Prime generation.} Every $n\in M$ admits a unique prime factorization
    $$
    n=\prod_{p\in\PP} p^{a_p(n)},\qquad a_p(n)\in\NN,\quad a_p(n)=0\text{ for all but finitely many }p.
    $$
    \item \textbf{Multiplication as first-order structure.} All ``geometric'' quantities introduced below (radial and phase) are required to be homomorphic or covariant with respect to multiplication on $M$.
\end{itemize}
