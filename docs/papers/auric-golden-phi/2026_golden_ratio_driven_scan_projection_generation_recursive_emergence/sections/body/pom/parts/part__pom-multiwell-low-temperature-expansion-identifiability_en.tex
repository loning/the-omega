\paragraph{Multi-Well Low-Temperature Expansion and Top-Tier Structure Identifiability: \texorpdfstring{$q\to\infty$}{q->∞} Softmax Geometry}
Theorem \ref{thm:pom-zero-temperature-two-term-expansion-maxfiber-entropy} gives the zero-temperature two-term expansion
\(
P_q=q\alpha_\ast+h_\ast+o(1)
\) (\(q\to\infty\)) and identifies the endpoint intercept as the entropy rate of maximal fiber achievers.
In the regime of finite graphs/finite-state compilation kernels, the pressure \(q\mapsto P(q)=\log\rho(A(q))\) can also exhibit "multi-well competition" low-temperature analytic structure:
beyond the top tier, finitely many visible phases may exist, whose contributions superimpose as exponentially small quantities inducing a family of computable crossover scales.
This paragraph records a self-contained softmax calibration and computability conclusion for recovering top-tier parameter families from finite difference windows.

\paragraph{Setting: Power-Weight Matrix Family and Closed-Path Logarithmic Mean Weight}
Let \(G\) be a finite directed graph (allowing multi-edges), assign weight \(a(e)>0\) to each edge \(e\).
For each real parameter \(q\ge0\) define weighted adjacency matrix family \(A(q)\):
for all edges \(e:i\to j\) from vertex \(i\to j\), sum
\[
A(q)_{ij}:=\sum_{e:i\to j} a(e)^q.
\]
Assume for each \(q\) the matrix \(A(q)\) is primitive, and denote pressure
\[
P(q):=\log\rho(A(q)).
\]
For any closed path \(\gamma=e_1\cdots e_\ell\) define its logarithmic mean weight
\[
\alpha(\gamma):=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{t=1}^{\ell}\log a(e_t).
\]
Let \(\{\alpha_i\}_{i=1}^K\) be the \emph{de-duplicated finite set} of all closed-path logarithmic mean weights (an additional structural assumption), ordered decreasingly
\(
\alpha_1>\alpha_2>\cdots>\alpha_K
\).
Let \(h_i\) be the Perron entropy of the critical subgraph (maximal strongly connected subgraph spanned by all closed paths satisfying \(\alpha(\gamma)=\alpha_i\)):
if this critical layer consists of multiple strongly connected components, then \(h_i\) takes their maximum value (i.e., maximum \(\log\rho\)).

\begin{theorem}[Multi-Phase Softmax Low-Temperature Expansion]\label{thm:pom-multiwell-softmax-low-temperature-expansion}
In the above setting, let
\(
c:=\min_{i\ge2}(\alpha_1-\alpha_i)>0
\).
Then there exists constant \(C>0\) such that as \(q\to\infty\)
\[
\boxed{\;
P(q)=\log\!\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{K}\exp(q\alpha_i+h_i)\Bigr)+O\!\bigl(e^{-cq}\bigr)\; }.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\boxed{\;
P(q)=q\alpha_1+h_1+\log\!\Bigl(1+\sum_{i\ge2}\exp\bigl(-(\alpha_1-\alpha_i)q+(h_i-h_1)\bigr)\Bigr)+O\!\bigl(e^{-cq}\bigr)\; }.
\]
Hence inducing crossover scales (visible competition temperature of top phase \(i\) against main phase)
\[
q_{i\leftrightarrow 1}\ \asymp\ \frac{h_1-h_i}{\alpha_1-\alpha_i},
\]
which uniformly characterize the balance scale on \(q\)-axis between slope difference \((\alpha_1-\alpha_i)\) and intercept difference \((h_1-h_i)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
Write entries of \(A(q)\) as finite sum \(\sum_e \exp(q\log a(e))\) and perform "max-term" renormalization as \(q\to\infty\):
in the max-plus limit, the leading logarithmic term of spectral radius is determined by the maximum value \(\alpha_1\) of closed-path logarithmic mean weights,
while the intercept is given by the Perron entropy on the critical subgraph achieving this maximum mean weight (i.e., \(h_1\)).
When the closed-path mean weight set \(\{\alpha_i\}\) is finite with positive gap \(c>0\), contributions from non-top phases are exponentially suppressed by \(\exp(-(\alpha_1-\alpha_i)q)\);
aggregating these contributions in the Collatz-Wielandt variational formula for spectral radius and performing block comparison on critical subgraphs yields the stated softmax form and exponential remainder.
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}
\begin{theorem}[Finite Recoverability of Top-Tier Parameters: Identifying \texorpdfstring{$(\alpha_i,h_i)$}{(alpha_i,hi)} by Finite Differences on \texorpdfstring{$q$}{q}-Axis]\label{thm:pom-multiwell-top-parameter-identifiability}
Following the setting of Theorem \ref{thm:pom-multiwell-softmax-low-temperature-expansion}.
Define discrete differences
\[
\Delta P(q):=P(q+1)-P(q),
\qquad
\Delta^2 P(q):=P(q+1)-2P(q)+P(q-1).
\]
Then there exist integer \(Q_0\) and constant \(K_0\) (depending only on graph size and upper bound on number of phases) such that when \(q\ge Q_0\),
the finite window data \(\{\Delta P(q+j)\}_{j=0}^{K_0}\) suffices to recover at exponential precision:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Main phase parameters \((\alpha_1,h_1)\);
  \item All secondary phase sets \(\{(\alpha_i,h_i)\}_{i\ge2}\) with visible competition against main phase;
  \item Crossover scales \(q_{i\leftrightarrow1}\) for each phase.
\end{enumerate}
Hence the "low-temperature multi-phase structure" is not merely a limit object, but can in principle be directly identified from finite difference windows forming computationally auditable data.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof Sketch]
By Theorem \ref{thm:pom-multiwell-softmax-low-temperature-expansion},
\(
P(q)-q\alpha_1-h_1
\)
as \(q\to\infty\) is a logarithmic perturbation of finitely many exponential terms
\(\sum_{i\ge2}\exp(-(\alpha_1-\alpha_i)q+(h_i-h_1))\)
(with exponentially small remainder).
Taking discrete differences of this perturbation yields the same class of finite exponential sum structure;
hence one can apply Prony-Hankel identification mechanism (same type as Theorem \ref{thm:pom-fiber-spectrum-prony-hankel-2r-reconstruction}) to the difference sequence:
finite window suffices to recover exponential rates \((\alpha_1-\alpha_i)\) and coefficients \(\exp(h_i-h_1)\), thereby recovering \((\alpha_i,h_i)\) and crossover scales.
This completes the proof.
\end{proof}
