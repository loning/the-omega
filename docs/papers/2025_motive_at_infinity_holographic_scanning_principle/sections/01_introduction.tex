
\section{Introduction: from the stairway to the motive}
\label{sec:introduction}

Foundational mathematical physics faces a persistent mismatch between what is \emph{modeled} and what is \emph{recorded}. Microscopic descriptions privilege unitary evolution and continuous symmetries; observational records are discrete, finite-resolution, and statistical. The standard narrative resolves this by placing continuity in ``ontology'' and discreteness in an externally imposed measurement postulate. In the audit program pursued across the HPA--$\Omega$ manuscripts, the organizational stance is the opposite: \emph{readout is part of the structure}. Time, probability, and discreteness must arise within an explicit scan--readout protocol under finite information constraints, in a way that admits a concrete dependency chain (Layer~0/1) and a strict separation from semantic narratives (Layer~2).

In a companion work, \cite{Ma2025STI} (``The Stairway to Infinity'') formalized the climb from noncommutative scanning to arithmetic rigidity and further to automorphic (Langlands) semantics. The paper closed an auditable chain
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:sti_chain}
\text{Weyl pair}\;\Longrightarrow\;A_\alpha\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{(modular) geodesic/Gauss flow}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{cusp $q$-coefficients}\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{Hecke prime skeleton},
\end{equation}
and left two explicit tasks open: a functorial upgrade of protocol-level constructions and a principled equivalence criterion for protocols.

The present paper addresses a different, more ``source-level'' question that remains after \eqref{eq:sti_chain} is closed:
\begin{itemize}
\item Why do stable constants persistently reappear as $\pi$, logarithms, and zeta-values?
\item Why does the modular/Hecke route look canonical rather than contingent?
\item What is the structural origin of the arithmetic rigidity observed at cusps, beyond the level of coefficient extraction procedures?
\end{itemize}

Our answer is not a new physical narrative; it is a further audit-compatible \emph{mathematical} move. We introduce a period--motive interface as a minimal structure ``above the cusp'' and show that, in a controlled subcategory, scanning itself is an algorithmic realization of Kontsevich--Zagier periods.

\subsection{Core claim: scanning as a period realization}
\label{sec:core_claim}

The key thesis is closed in the controlled protocol class $\ScanAlg$ (Section~\ref{sec:scan_alg_and_hsp}) and can be stated as a single theorem by combining the realization results of Section~\ref{sec:period_realization} with the finite-resource bounds of Section~\ref{sec:error_budgets}.

\begin{theorem}[Scanning realizes periods with an auditable finite-$N$ certificate]
\label{thm:scan_realizes_period_intro}
Let $\mathcal{P}=(\TT^d,\alpha,x_0,f,\mathcal{R})\in\ScanAlg$ and let $P_N=\{x_0,\dots,x_{N-1}\}\subset[0,1)^d$ be its orbit-prefix point set. Assume the rational-independence condition \eqref{eq:rational_independence} (or $\alpha\notin\QQ$ if $d=1$) and that the regularized kernel $f_{\mathcal{R}}$ is Riemann integrable on $[0,1)^d$. Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro_main_limit}
\lim_{N\to\infty}\avgN{\mathcal{P}}
=
\mathrm{per}(\HSP(\mathcal{P}))
=
\int_{[0,1)^d} f_{\mathcal{R}}(x)\,\dd x.
\end{equation}
If, moreover, $\HKVar(f_{\mathcal{R}})<\infty$, then for every $N\ge 1$ one has the explicit finite-resource bound
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:intro_sampling_certificate}
\left|\avgN{\mathcal{P}}-\mathrm{per}(\HSP(\mathcal{P}))\right|
\le
\HKVar(f_{\mathcal{R}})\,\DNstar(P_N).
\end{equation}
Finally, if $f$ is an ideal kernel and $\mathcal{R}$ is a regularization rule, then the total error to the ideal period admits an auditable sampling--regularization decomposition (Proposition~\ref{prop:error_decomposition}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The limit statement \eqref{eq:intro_main_limit} is Corollary~\ref{cor:scan_period_md} (or Corollary~\ref{cor:scan_period_1d} in $d=1$). The finite-$N$ certificate \eqref{eq:intro_sampling_certificate} is Corollary~\ref{cor:sampling_error}. The final sentence is Proposition~\ref{prop:error_decomposition}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:scan_realizes_period_intro} has two consequences.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Numerical layer.} The repeated appearance of $\pi$, $\log$, and zeta-values is explained by the fact that these constants are periods, and the protocol computes periods.
\item \textbf{Structural layer.} ``Protocol equivalence'' can be anchored to period data: morphisms in $\ScanAlg$ induce period-datum equivalences in $\PerDatum$, hence $\ScanAlg$-isomorphic protocols yield identical long-time limits and identical period values (Section~\ref{sec:scan_alg_and_hsp}). A full classification of protocols by period data is left as a separate problem.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What is new in this paper}
\label{sec:contributions}

Compared to \cite{Ma2025STI}, this paper contributes the following closed components.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{A controlled protocol category $\ScanAlg$.} We define a subcategory of protocols whose scan dynamics are Kronecker rotations on tori and whose readout kernels are rational functions on $[0,1)^d$, equipped with explicit regularization rules (Section~\ref{sec:scan_alg_and_hsp}).
\item \textbf{A period-data category $\PerDatum$ and a functor $\HSP$.} We define $\PerDatum$ as a category of period data and construct a holographic scanning functor $\HSP:\ScanAlg\to\PerDatum$ mapping protocols to integrable rational kernels on $[0,1)^d$ (Section~\ref{sec:scan_alg_and_hsp}).
\item \textbf{A closed realization theorem.} We prove that, under the standard rational-independence condition on the scan slope, the Birkhoff readout average converges to the period associated with $\HSP(\mathcal{P})$ (Section~\ref{sec:period_realization}).
\item \textbf{Auditable error budgets.} We provide explicit finite-$N$ error decompositions into discrepancy-controlled sampling error and provable truncation/regularization error, and we emphasize how these bounds propagate across protocol compositions (Section~\ref{sec:error_budgets}).
\item \textbf{Reproducible experiments and a toy selection signal.} We provide pure-Python scripts reproducing period realizations of $\log 2$, $\pi$, $\zeta(2)$ and $\zeta(3)$ and a low-complexity constant search illustrating a sharp ``uniqueness gap'' under a description budget (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

Beyond the closed components, we formulate a falsifiable \emph{selection principle} as a programmatic statement: under finite resources, period data that are stably realizable with low description complexity are preferentially selected. This principle is presented as a conjectural interface (Section~\ref{sec:selection_principle}) and does not enter the closed proof chain.

\subsection{Relation to motives and to the Langlands chain}
\label{sec:relation_to_motives_and_langlands}

Motives organize the relations among periods by comparing different cohomological realizations (Betti, de~Rham, $\ell$-adic). We do not assume any conjectural statements from the general theory of motives. The role of motives in this paper is \emph{organizational} and appears only through a minimal ``period datum'' interface that stays within Layer~0/1 audit constraints.

Nevertheless, the motive viewpoint offers a natural compatibility target for the stairway chain: Hecke eigenvalues can be interpreted as Frobenius traces on suitable realizations of motives, while periods are the numerical shadows of comparison isomorphisms. Section~\ref{sec:langlands_compatibility} formulates a commutative-diagram objective that factors the functorial Langlands upgrade of \cite{Ma2025STI} through period data and motives.
