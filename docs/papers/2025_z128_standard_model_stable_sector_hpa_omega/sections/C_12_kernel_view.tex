\section{Kernel view: a protocol renormalization core for cross-scale computation}
\label{sec:kernel_view}

This section repackages the construction in an operator/renormalization language.
No new assumptions are introduced.
The purpose is to make the \emph{iterable} object explicit: a finite-resolution kernel that can be applied across window uplift and coarse graining, and that interfaces cleanly with the $r$-coordinate RG dictionary.

\subsection{Three coupled kernels: language, folding, and protocol flow}
\label{subsec:three_kernels}

\paragraph{(K1) Language kernel: the admissible shift and its analytic package.}
The $\varphi$-channel defines the admissible block language
$X_m\subset\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m$ by the forbidden substring ``11'' (Definition~\eqref{eq:Xm_def}).
Equivalently, $X_m$ is the length-$m$ block language of the golden-mean shift (Appendix~\ref{app:sturmian_vs_golden_mean}).
The resulting finite combinatorics is rigid:
$|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:xm_fib}), and the $\pi$-channel induces the cyclic/boundary split with $|X_m^{\mathrm{bdry}}|=F_{m-2}$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:cyc_bdry_size}).

The $\e$-channel packages the same symbolic constraint into an analytic object.
For the golden-mean shift, the transition matrix
$A=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\[2pt]1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ yields the Artin--Mazur determinant identity
$\zeta(z)=1/\det(I-zA)=1/(1-z-z^2)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:golden_zeta_matrix}),
and the Abel normalization $z=r/\varphi$ places the principal pole at the unit-circle boundary $r=1$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:abel_normalized_zeta}).
In this paper, the $\e$-channel is used as an analytic stability template and as a rigidity discipline (Remark~\ref{rem:abel_first_pole_barrier_discipline}), not as an additional finite-word filter at $m=6$ (Remark~\ref{rem:e_channel_distinct}).

\paragraph{(K2) Folding kernel: the dyadic$\to$Zeckendorf bridge and fiber statistics.}
At each window length $m$, the paper fixes a deterministic folding map
\[
\mathrm{Fold}_m:\{0,\dots,2^m-1\}\twoheadrightarrow X_m
\]
by taking Zeckendorf digits and truncating to the first $m$ digits (Definition~\eqref{eq:foldm_def}).
Surjectivity onto $X_m$ is theorem-level (Proposition~\ref{prop:foldm_surjective}).
The induced fiber statistics
\[
g_m(w):=\left|\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)\right|,
\qquad
r_m:=\max_{w\in X_m} g_m(w)
\]
are the canonical finite-resolution measure of dyadic redundancy under the $\varphi$-stable readout coordinate system (Definition~\ref{def:rm_max_degeneracy}).
They appear downstream as protocol-native inputs for compensation/connection constructions and for uplift diagnostics.

Two points make this bridge principled rather than a discretionary encoding choice.
First, a bounded counterfactual family at $m=6$ is audited in Appendix~\ref{app:fold_family_sensitivity}.
Second, once one commits to a \emph{protocol-local} folding \emph{family} across window lengths---value consistency together with no-backreaction refinement under dyadic uplift---the Zeckendorf-truncation family is uniquely forced (Appendix~\ref{app:fold_family_uniqueness}, Theorem~\ref{thm:fold_family_uniqueness}).

\paragraph{(K3) Protocol RG kernel: uplift and coarse graining as a deterministic step.}
Finite resolution is primary in this framework: both window length $m$ and addressing scale $n$ are protocol parameters.
The protocol flow step is fixed as a deterministic uplift and coarse-graining update on finite observables (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_flow_step}).
The functorial refinement structure needed to move between resolutions is the prefix projection
$\pi_{m\to k}:X_m\to X_k$ (Appendix~\ref{app:functorial_refinement}), together with the corresponding pushforward/pullback of derived observables.

For kernel-view bookkeeping, it is convenient to package this into a single symbol:
given a chosen coarse-graining map $\mathsf{C}$, define the \emph{protocol renormalization step}
\[
\mathcal{R}_{(m,n)\to(m',n')}:=\mathsf{C}\circ \pi_{m'\to m}^{\ast},
\]
where $\pi_{m'\to m}^{\ast}$ denotes the protocol-defined refinement transport of the chosen observable family from resolution $(m,n)$ to $(m',n')$ via recomputation at $(m',n')$ and functorial consistency under prefix projection (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_flow_step}).
This notation does not add content; it only makes explicit that the paper already fixes an \emph{iterable} step operator on finite objects.

\subsection{Two scale rates and what they control}
\label{subsec:kernel_scale_rates}

The kernel view separates two distinct exponential rates that otherwise tend to get conflated:

\paragraph{Capacity of the admissible stable-type language.}
The block language size $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ grows with rate $\log\varphi$ (Appendix~\ref{app:sturmian_vs_golden_mean}, Lemma~\ref{lem:golden_mean_language_equals_Xn}).
This is the origin of the $\log\varphi$ normalization used throughout the $r$-coordinate resolution dictionaries, and it should not be confused with the entropy \emph{rate} of the Sturmian time-series readout, which is zero (Appendix~\ref{app:sturmian_entropy}).

\paragraph{Redundancy exponent of the folding kernel.}
The folding fibers quantify how much dyadic microstate information is suppressed when one reports only the stable Zeckendorf prefix.
The maximal degeneracy $r_m$ has an exponential rate
\[
\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{1}{m}\log r_m=\log(2/\varphi)=\log 2-\log\varphi
\]
in the theorem-level sense of Proposition~\ref{prop:rm_entropy_gap_rate}.
In the kernel view, $\log(2/\varphi)$ is the intrinsic redundancy rate of the dyadic$\to$stable bridge at fixed window length, and it is a natural finite-resolution proxy for ``compression overhead'' along the resolution axis.

\subsection{Cross-scale computation and the RG dictionary}
\label{subsec:kernel_cross_scale_computation}

The reason to make the kernel explicit is operational: it provides a reusable interface for cross-scale computation.
At the protocol level, one chooses a family of observables at finite resolution---stable-type counts, degeneracy histograms, coarse-grained scalar summaries, holonomy distributions, and derived effective parameters (Definition~\ref{def:protocol_flow_step}).
Cross-scale propagation is then the deterministic application of the protocol renormalization step $\mathcal{R}$ across uplifts, with the $\e$-channel providing analytic rigidity templates when generating functions are used (Section~\ref{subsec:e_channel} and Appendix~\ref{app:thermodynamic_formalism_pressure}).
Appendix~\ref{app:generated_tables} records compact finite checks of this kernel picture: the $m$-sweep summary is in Table~\ref{tab:fractal_kernel_sweep}, the folding entropy decomposition identity is certified numerically in Table~\ref{tab:folding_entropy_decomposition}, an end-to-end staircase demo in $\mu/r$ coordinates (threshold selection plus folding-degeneracy summaries) is in Table~\ref{tab:kernel_mu_r_bridge}, and a balanced-chain coarse-grained flow summary along $m=2n$ is in Table~\ref{tab:kernel_rg_flow_balanced}.

When one embeds the finite kernel into a standard EFT dictionary, running and threshold dependence are treated as a matching-layer translation.
The Fibonacci resolution map $\mu(r)=\mu_0\varphi^r$ and the chain rule
$\dd g/\dd r=(\log\varphi)\beta(g)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:rg_dictionary_r}) convert standard RG flow into the $r$ coordinate (Appendix~\ref{app:running_couplings_resolution_flow}).
Threshold matching is then interpreted as a discrete uplift along the resolution axis (Appendix~\ref{subsec:threshold_matching}).
This is the intended division of labor: the theorem-level kernel fixes the discrete objects and their uplift semantics, while RG is used as an auditable translation dictionary for scheme/scale dependence in EFT embeddings.

\subsection{A finite-dimensional operator closure of the protocol RG kernel (mother-space realization)}
\label{subsec:kernel_operator_closure}

The kernel view is designed to make an \emph{iterable} object explicit.
This can be made fully concrete in a finite-dimensional operator language that aligns directly with the resolvent/determinant bookkeeping of the operator mother space (Section~\ref{app:operator_mother_space_dictionary} and Appendix~\ref{app:operator_mother_space}).
The construction below introduces no new empirical inputs: it only packages already fixed protocol choices (Hilbert addressing, block coarse graining, and dyadic uplift) into an explicit linear operator.

\begin{definition}[Balanced-chain microstate index spaces and a fixed $4\times 4$ block quotient]
\label{def:balanced_chain_block_quotient}
\InterfaceTag Fix a Hilbert order $n\ge 3$ and set the balanced coupling $m:=2n$.
Define the microstate index set
\[
\Omega_n:=\{0,1,\dots,4^n-1\}=\{0,1,\dots,2^m-1\}.
\]
Let $H_n:\Omega_n\to\{0,\dots,2^n-1\}^2$ be the protocol-fixed Hilbert addressing map (Section~\ref{sec:hilbert_addressing}).
Partition the $2^n\times 2^n$ grid into a fixed axis-aligned $4\times 4$ block grid (block side length $2^{n-2}$), yielding $16$ blocks.
Let $\mathcal{B}_n\cong\RR^{16}$ denote the corresponding block-constant quotient space.

Define the deterministic projection (block averaging)
\[
P_n:\RR^{\Omega_n}\to\RR^{16},
\qquad
(P_nf)_b:=\frac{1}{|B_{n,b}|}\sum_{k\in B_{n,b}} f(k),
\]
and the deterministic lift (block-constant pullback)
\[
P_n^\ast:\RR^{16}\to\RR^{\Omega_n},
\qquad
(P_n^\ast v)(k):=v_{b(k)},
\]
where $B_{n,b}\subset\Omega_n$ is the $b$-th block and $b(k)\in\{1,\dots,16\}$ is the block index of the site $H_n(k)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Dyadic uplift and Koopman pullback]\label{def:dyadic_uplift_koopman}
\InterfaceTag Define the Hilbert-recursive dyadic uplift map
\[
u_n:\Omega_{n+1}\to\Omega_n,
\qquad
u_n(k'):=H_n^{-1}\!\left(\left\lfloor \frac{H_{n+1}(k')}{2}\right\rfloor\right),
\]
where $H_n:\Omega_n\to \{0,\dots,2^n-1\}^2$ is the Hilbert addressing map and
$\lfloor(x,y)/2\rfloor:=(\lfloor x/2\rfloor,\lfloor y/2\rfloor)$,
and the corresponding Koopman pullback operator
\[
U_n:\RR^{\Omega_n}\to\RR^{\Omega_{n+1}},
\qquad
(U_nf)(k'):=f(u_n(k')).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Protocol RG kernel as an explicit $16\times 16$ operator]\label{def:protocol_rg_operator_Fn}
\InterfaceTag With $P_n,U_n$ as above, define a protocol-local transport operator $T_{n+1}:\RR^{\Omega_{n+1}}\to\RR^{\Omega_{n+1}}$ as a single lazy nearest-neighbor random-walk step on the $2^{n+1}\times 2^{n+1}$ Hilbert screen graph (reflecting boundary), and define the finite-dimensional protocol RG operator
\[
F_n:\RR^{16}\to\RR^{16},
\qquad
F_n:=P_{n+1}\circ T_{n+1}\circ U_n\circ P_n^\ast.
\]
This operator is protocol-deterministic and genuinely dynamical on the block quotient: it depends only on the fixed Hilbert addressing, the fixed $4\times 4$ block coarse graining, and the protocol-local transport rule, and it is iterable along the balanced chain $m=2n$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[A folding-weighted operator family]\label{def:weighted_protocol_rg_operator}
\InterfaceTag Let $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ be the deterministic folding map (Definition~\eqref{eq:foldm_def}) and let $g_m(w)=|\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)|$ be its degeneracy function (Definition~\ref{def:rm_max_degeneracy}).
At balanced coupling $m=2n$, define the microstate potential
\[
\varphi_n(k):=\log g_{2n}\!\bigl(\mathrm{Fold}_{2n}(k)\bigr),
\qquad k\in\Omega_n,
\]
and the corresponding diagonal multiplication operator $M_{\varphi_n}$ on $\RR^{\Omega_n}$.
For $t\in\RR$, define the weighted protocol RG operator
\[
\widehat F_n(t):=P_{n+1}\circ T_{n+1}\circ U_n\circ \e^{tM_{\varphi_n}}\circ P_n^\ast:\RR^{16}\to\RR^{16}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[One-point and two-point readout for block-averaged observables]\label{thm:block_readout_one_two_point}
\MathTag Fix $n\ge 3$ and let $q:\Omega_n\to\RR$ be any microstate observable (e.g.\ $q(k)=|w(k)|_1$, $q(k)=V_m(w(k))$, $q(k)=D_\pi(w(k))$, or $q(k)=\log g_m(w(k))$ as in Table~\ref{tab:kernel_rg_flow_balanced}).
Let $a:=P_n q\in\RR^{16}$ be the $4\times 4$ block-average vector.
Define the block mean and (population) variance by
\[
\mu(a):=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{b=1}^{16} a_b,
\qquad
\Var(a):=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{b=1}^{16}(a_b-\mu(a))^2.
\]
Then $\mu(a)$ is a one-point linear readout and $\Var(a)$ is a two-point (tensor) readout in the operator-mother-space sense.
Indeed, identifying $a$ with the diagonal operator $Q_a$ on $\RR^{16}$ given by $Q_a e_b=a_b e_b$, one may take
\[
K^{(1)}:=\frac{1}{16}I_{16}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu(a)=\Tr(K^{(1)}Q_a),
\]
and, on $\RR^{16}\otimes\RR^{16}$, one may take the diagonal projector
\[
K^{(2)}:=\frac{1}{16}\sum_{b=1}^{16}\ket{e_b\otimes e_b}\bra{e_b\otimes e_b}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\frac{1}{16}\sum_{b=1}^{16} a_b^2=\Tr\!\bigl(K^{(2)}(Q_a\otimes Q_a)\bigr).
\]
In particular, $\Var(a)=\frac{1}{16}\sum_b a_b^2-\mu(a)^2$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Resolvent-trace closure for iterated one-point and two-point statistics]\label{thm:resolvent_trace_closure_protocol_rg}
\MathTag Fix $n\ge 3$ and let $F$ denote either $F_n$ or $\widehat F_n(t)$ acting on $\RR^{16}$.
Let $\ell:\RR^{16}\to\RR$ be any fixed one-point linear readout (e.g.\ the block mean functional $\ell(a)=\mu(a)$) and let $Q:\RR^{16}\to\RR$ be any quadratic two-point readout that can be represented by a linear functional on $\RR^{16}\otimes\RR^{16}$ (e.g.\ the block second moment $a\mapsto \frac{1}{16}\sum_b a_b^2$).
Then for $|z|<\rho(F)^{-1}$ one has the Neumann-series identities
\[
\sum_{t\ge 0} z^t\,\ell(F^t v)=\ell\!\bigl((I-zF)^{-1}v\bigr),
\]
and, writing $F^{\otimes 2}:=F\otimes F$,
\[
\sum_{t\ge 0} z^t\,Q(F^t v)=Q\!\bigl((I-zF^{\otimes 2})^{-1}(v\otimes v)\bigr),
\]
where the right-hand sides are resolvent-trace readouts in the operator-mother-space sense.
Full details (including the explicit tensor-kernel reduction used by the variance readout) are recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol_rg_operator_closure}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proposition}[RG pole barrier as a spectral radius]\label{prop:rg_pole_barrier_radius}
\MathTag Let $F$ be any finite-dimensional protocol RG operator (e.g.\ $F_n$ or $\widehat F_n(t)$).
Define
\[
|z|_\star(F):=\rho(F)^{-1}.
\]
Then $|z|_\star(F)$ is the natural pole-barrier radius for the corresponding resolvent and determinant objects:
the resolvent $(I-zF)^{-1}$ is analytic for $|z|<|z|_\star(F)$ and encounters a singular obstruction at $|z|=|z|_\star(F)$.
Normalizing $z=r/\rho(F)$ places the leading obstruction at $r=1$, directly paralleling the $\e$-channel Abel normalization $z=r/\varphi$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:abel_normalized_zeta}).
\end{proposition}
