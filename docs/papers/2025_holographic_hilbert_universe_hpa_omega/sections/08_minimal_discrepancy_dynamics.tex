
\section{Minimal dynamical closure: least-discrepancy flow and an auditable arrow of time}
\label{sec:dynamics}

\subsection{Accumulated mismatch as a Lyapunov candidate}
\label{sec:mismatch_lyapunov}

For a scan prefix $P_N$, define the accumulated mismatch
\[
E_N := N\,\DNstar(P_N).
\]
For bounded-type irrational scans (notably the golden branch), discrepancy certificates imply $E_N=O(\log N)$, so mismatch grows slowly and remains auditable at large horizons.
In contrast, protocols that effectively introduce exponential-gain modes (Appendix~\ref{app:trace_rigidity}) destroy Abel admissibility and drive $E_N$ beyond sustainable bounds.

\begin{remark}[Sensitivity away from the golden branch]
\label{rem:slope_sensitivity}
The mismatch growth rate is quantitatively controlled by the continued-fraction data of the scan slope $\alpha$.
For Kronecker scans, standard certificates bound $\DNstar(P_N)$ in terms of partial quotients (Appendix~\ref{app:discrepancy_notes}); bounded-type slopes yield the logarithmic $O((\log N)/N)$ rate, while large partial quotients produce bursts of irregularity at intermediate horizons.
When the defect density $\sigma$ is constructed from occupation bias (Example~\ref{ex:occupation_bias_sigma}), such discrepancy bursts translate into source perturbations at the readout scale.
Propagation to effective geometry is then controlled by the Poisson stability estimate (Proposition~\ref{prop:poisson_stability}): coarse-grained perturbations of $\sigma$ induce proportionate perturbations of $\nabla\Phi$ (hence of lapse ratios) in Sobolev norms.
\end{remark}

This motivates a minimal closed-layer arrow-of-time statement:
in the space of admissible protocols, sustainable evolution must flow toward configurations that keep mismatch growth controlled under fixed resource budgets.

\subsection{A least-discrepancy variational template}
\label{sec:least_discrepancy_template}

Let $\mathcal{P}$ denote protocol parameters (scan slope, readout resolution, address map selection within the admissible family, routing scheme, truncation budget, etc.).
A minimal auditable cost functional at horizon $T$ takes the schematic form
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:least_discrepancy_functional}
\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P})
=
\mathrm{CertDisc}_T(\mathcal{P})
\;+\;
\lambda\,\mathrm{Cost}(\mathcal{P})
\;+\;
\mu\,\mathrm{RegTail}_T(\mathcal{P}),
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
\item $\mathrm{CertDisc}_T$ is a deterministic discrepancy-based certificate (e.g.\ $\Var(f)\,\DNstar$);
\item $\mathrm{Cost}$ is an implementation overhead cost (routing depth, memory, circuit size), including the lapse profile $\lapse$;
\item $\mathrm{RegTail}_T$ controls truncation/regularization tail errors under the Abel-first convention.
\end{itemize}

One may then define dynamics as a (possibly damped) gradient flow on protocol space decreasing $\mathcal{E}_T$.
In the broader HPA--$\Omega$ program, this template is elevated to an $\Omega$ action principle with explicit Fisher-information and gravitational terms \cite{Ma2025CAP}.
For the present paper, \eqref{eq:least_discrepancy_functional} serves only as the minimal closed-layer bridge between discrepancy certificates, routing overhead, and effective gravitational templates.

\subsection{An explicit least-discrepancy evolution and an \texorpdfstring{$H$}{H}-theorem}
\label{sec:least_discrepancy_h_theorem}

To answer the ``minimal deviation'' question quantitatively, we record an explicit evolution equation that is sufficient for a closed-layer monotonicity statement.

\begin{definition}[Least-discrepancy gradient flow (continuous time)]
\label{def:least_discrepancy_flow}
Assume the protocol space is modeled as an open subset $\mathcal{U}\subset\RR^m$ with coordinate vector $\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{U}$, and assume $\mathcal{E}_T:\mathcal{U}\to\RR$ is differentiable.
Fix a \emph{mobility} (preconditioner) field $M(\mathcal{P})\in\RR^{m\times m}$ such that $M(\mathcal{P})$ is symmetric and positive semidefinite for all $\mathcal{P}$.
The least-discrepancy evolution is the gradient flow
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:least_discrepancy_flow}
\frac{\dd \mathcal{P}}{\dd \tau}=-\,M(\mathcal{P})\,\nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}),
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is an auxiliary (protocol) time parameter indexing updates in protocol space.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[\texorpdfstring{$H$}{H}-theorem (continuous flow)]
\label{prop:h_theorem_continuous}
Let $\mathcal{P}(\tau)$ satisfy \eqref{eq:least_discrepancy_flow} and suppose $\mathcal{E}_T$ is continuously differentiable along the trajectory.
Then the ``entropy'' functional $H(\tau):=\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}(\tau))$ is nonincreasing:
\[
\frac{\dd H}{\dd \tau}=-\left\langle \nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}),\,M(\mathcal{P})\,\nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P})\right\rangle\ \le\ 0.
\]
Moreover, $\dd H/\dd\tau=0$ holds if and only if $M(\mathcal{P})\,\nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P})=0$ (a stationary protocol under the chosen mobility).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate $H(\tau)=\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}(\tau))$ and use \eqref{eq:least_discrepancy_flow}:
\[
\frac{\dd H}{\dd \tau}
=\left\langle \nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}),\,\frac{\dd \mathcal{P}}{\dd\tau}\right\rangle
=-\left\langle \nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}),\,M(\mathcal{P})\,\nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P})\right\rangle\le 0,
\]
since $M(\mathcal{P})$ is positive semidefinite.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Auditable discrete-time update (proximal/gradient step)]
\label{def:least_discrepancy_discrete_update}
In practice the protocol is updated in discrete steps $k\in\ZZ_{\ge 0}$.
A minimal auditable update rule compatible with \eqref{eq:least_discrepancy_flow} is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:least_discrepancy_discrete}
\mathcal{P}_{k+1}=\mathcal{P}_k-\eta_k\,M_k\,\nabla_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_k),
\end{equation}
where $\eta_k>0$ is a step size and $M_k\succeq 0$ is a chosen preconditioner (for example, a diagonal scaling reflecting heterogeneous audit costs of changing different protocol knobs).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Sufficient condition for monotonic decrease (discrete time)]
\label{prop:h_theorem_discrete}
Assume $\mathcal{E}_T$ has an $L$-Lipschitz gradient on a convex subset containing the iterates (i.e.\ $\mathcal{E}_T$ is $L$-smooth).
If $M_k=I$ and $0<\eta_k\le 1/L$, then the discrete update \eqref{eq:least_discrepancy_discrete} satisfies
\[
\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_{k+1})\le \mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_k)
\]
for every step $k$.
More generally, for symmetric $M_k\succeq 0$, the same conclusion holds whenever $\eta_k\|M_k\|_2\le 1/L$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For an $L$-smooth function, the standard descent lemma gives
\[
\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_{k+1})\le \mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_k)
\;+\;
\left\langle \nabla\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_k),\,\mathcal{P}_{k+1}-\mathcal{P}_k\right\rangle
\;+\;\frac{L}{2}\,\|\mathcal{P}_{k+1}-\mathcal{P}_k\|_2^2.
\]
Substituting $\mathcal{P}_{k+1}-\mathcal{P}_k=-\eta_k M_k\nabla\mathcal{E}_T(\mathcal{P}_k)$ yields a negative quadratic form whenever $\eta_k\|M_k\|_2\le 1/L$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[When the \texorpdfstring{$H$}{H}-theorem can fail]
\label{rem:h_theorem_failure_modes}
Monotonicity is guaranteed only under regularity and step-size control (Propositions~\ref{prop:h_theorem_continuous}--\ref{prop:h_theorem_discrete}).
In particular, $\mathcal{E}_T$ is generally nonconvex in realistic protocol spaces, and large steps or poorly conditioned preconditioners can increase $\mathcal{E}_T$ transiently.
This is not a contradiction: it simply means the protocol update rule is itself part of the auditable specification of dynamics.
\end{remark}

\subsection{From auditable defect budgets to a Poisson closure}
\label{sec:poisson_from_action}

Section~\ref{sec:gravity} uses a phase potential $\Phi=-\log\lapse$ and a Poisson closure as the Newtonian-limit template.
Here we record a closed-layer derivation of this closure from a minimal energy principle.

\paragraph{Scope of the closure.}
The purpose of this subsection is to provide a minimal and auditable route from protocol-level defect budgets to an effective \emph{scalar} potential.
It should be read as a weak-field/static (Hamiltonian-constraint) template: once a source convention and boundary condition are fixed, Poisson closure follows from the Dirichlet principle.
A full time-dependent recovery of the ADM system requires additional constitutive input coupling conserved defect currents to an effective shift/drift sector (Section~\ref{sec:beyond_static_shift}).

\subsubsection{Defect charge measures and continuity constraints}
\label{sec:defect_charge}

To connect discrepancy/regularization budgets to a field source, it is convenient to treat mismatch as a signed ``defect charge'' distributed over the screen.

\begin{definition}[Defect charge and source density]
\label{def:defect_charge_sigma}
Let $\Omega\subset\RR^3$ denote a coarse-grained region of the screen continuum limit.
A protocol at horizon $T$ induces an \emph{auditable defect charge measure} $\Sigma_T$ on $\Omega$ such that $\Sigma_T(B)$ equals the net mismatch budget assigned to a measurable region $B\subseteq\Omega$.
Assume $\Sigma_T$ is finite and absolutely continuous with respect to volume measure, so that there exists a density $\sigma_T\in L^1(\Omega)$ with
\[
\Sigma_T(B)=\int_B \sigma_T(x)\,\dd^3x.
\]
In the static closure, we write $\sigma:=\sigma_T$ at a fixed horizon and suppress $T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{example}[A canonical auditable choice: coarse-grained occupation bias]
\label{ex:occupation_bias_sigma}
At resolution $n$, let $x_t^{(n)}\in\Sigma_n$ denote the addressed site at tick $t$ and let
\[
\nu_T^{(n)}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\delta_{x_t^{(n)}}
\]
be the empirical occupation measure on $\Sigma_n$ up to horizon $T$.
Let $u_n:=|\Sigma_n|^{-1}\sum_{x\in\Sigma_n}\delta_x$ be the uniform reference measure and let $w^{(\varepsilon)}$ be a nonnegative readout kernel of width $\varepsilon$ used to coarse-grain lattice data into a continuum density.
Then a concrete auditable defect measure is obtained by smoothing the signed occupation bias $\nu_T^{(n)}-u_n$ and scaling by a calibration constant:
\[
\Sigma_T := \gamma_\sigma \,(w^{(\varepsilon)}\ast (\nu_T^{(n)}-u_n)),
\qquad
\sigma_T=\frac{\dd\Sigma_T}{\dd^3x}.
\]
On a periodic box, this construction automatically yields $\int_\Omega \sigma_T\,\dd^3x=0$ (zero mean), matching the solvability condition of Remark~\ref{rem:sigma_constraints}.
\end{example}

\begin{remark}[A discrete implementable source at resolution \texorpdfstring{$n$}{n}]
\label{rem:discrete_sigma_implementable}
Let $N_T^{(n)}(x):=\#\{0\le t<T:\ x_t^{(n)}=x\}$ be the occupation count at site $x\in\Sigma_n$.
The signed occupation bias
\[
b_T^{(n)}(x):=\frac{N_T^{(n)}(x)}{T}-\frac{1}{|\Sigma_n|}
\]
satisfies $\sum_{x\in\Sigma_n} b_T^{(n)}(x)=0$.
A concrete discrete source for the graph Poisson equation \eqref{eq:poisson_graph} is then
\[
\sigma_n(x):=\gamma_\sigma\,b_T^{(n)}(x),
\]
and the continuum source density $\sigma$ may be obtained by smoothing $\sigma_n$ at readout scale $\varepsilon$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Conservation/solvability constraints]
\label{rem:sigma_constraints}
If $\Omega$ is treated as periodic (as in the FFT Poisson experiment), solvability of $-\Delta\Phi=4\pi G\sigma$ requires the zero-mean constraint $\int_\Omega \sigma\,\dd^3x=0$, corresponding to fixing the Laplacian zero mode.
On non-compact domains with decay at infinity, or on bounded domains with Dirichlet boundary conditions, no such global neutrality is required.
\end{remark}

For time-dependent protocol flows, one may encode conservation of auditable defect charge by a continuity equation.

\begin{definition}[Continuity equation (dynamic defect conservation)]
\label{def:continuity_sigma}
Let $\sigma(t,x)$ be a time-dependent source density and let $J(t,x)$ be a defect current.
We say that defect charge is conserved if, in the distributional sense on $(0,T)\times\Omega$,
\[
\partial_t\sigma+\nabla\cdot J=0.
\]
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{Dirichlet principle and the Poisson equation}
\label{sec:dirichlet_principle}

We now isolate the minimal variational structure that yields Poisson closure.

\paragraph{Discrete screen formulation (graph Dirichlet principle).}
At finite resolution $n$, the screen lattice $(\Sigma_n,\sim)$ is a finite graph with nearest-neighbor edge set $E_{\mathrm{screen}}$ (Definition~\ref{def:dilation_congestion}).
A discrete source $\sigma_n:\Sigma_n\to\RR$ may be taken as the coarse-grained defect charge assigned to each site (Definition~\ref{def:defect_charge_sigma}), with the neutrality constraint $\sum_{x\in\Sigma_n}\sigma_n(x)=0$ in the periodic convention (Remark~\ref{rem:sigma_constraints}).

\begin{definition}[Screen graph Laplacian]
\label{def:screen_graph_laplacian}
For a function $\Phi:\Sigma_n\to\RR$, define the (unscaled) graph Laplacian
\[
(\Delta_{\Sigma_n}\Phi)(x):=\sum_{y\in\Sigma_n:\,y\sim x}\bigl(\Phi(y)-\Phi(x)\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Discrete Dirichlet principle and graph Poisson equation]
\label{prop:dirichlet_poisson_discrete}
Fix $G>0$ and a discrete source $\sigma_n:\Sigma_n\to\RR$.
Consider the discrete Dirichlet functional on potentials $\Phi:\Sigma_n\to\RR$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dirichlet_functional_Phi_discrete}
\mathcal{J}_n[\Phi]
:=
\sum_{\{x,y\}\in E_{\mathrm{screen}}}\frac{1}{8\pi G}\,\bigl(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\bigr)^2
\;-\;
\sum_{x\in\Sigma_n}\sigma_n(x)\,\Phi(x),
\end{equation}
with boundary convention (periodic/Dirichlet) fixed.
If $\Phi$ is a stationary point of $\mathcal{J}_n$ under arbitrary variations $\eta:\Sigma_n\to\RR$ compatible with the boundary convention, then $\Phi$ satisfies the graph Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_graph}
-\Delta_{\Sigma_n}\Phi=4\pi G\,\sigma_n.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\Phi_\epsilon=\Phi+\epsilon\eta$.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:dirichlet_functional_Phi_discrete} at $\epsilon=0$ gives
\[
0=\left.\frac{\dd}{\dd\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\mathcal{J}_n[\Phi_\epsilon]
=\sum_{x\in\Sigma_n}\eta(x)\left(\frac{1}{4\pi G}\sum_{y\sim x}\bigl(\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\bigr)-\sigma_n(x)\right),
\]
since each undirected edge contributes to exactly its two endpoints.
Because $\eta$ is arbitrary, the bracket must vanish for each $x$, which is equivalent to \eqref{eq:poisson_graph} using Definition~\ref{def:screen_graph_laplacian}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Continuum limit and calibration]
\label{rem:poisson_discrete_to_continuum}
On a regular grid of spacing $h=2^{-n}$, the standard scaled discrete Laplacian is $h^{-2}\Delta_{\Sigma_n}$, which converges to the continuum Laplacian under smoothness/coarse-graining assumptions.
In the present framework, the overall coefficient $G$ is fixed by calibration at finite resolution (Remark~\ref{rem:units_calibration}), so the discrete and continuum conventions may be treated uniformly once a boundary convention and scale map are specified.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Dirichlet functional and Poisson Euler--Lagrange equation]
\label{prop:dirichlet_poisson}
Fix a coupling constant $G>0$.
Let $\Omega\subset\RR^3$ be a domain and let $\sigma\in L^1(\Omega)$ be a source density (Definition~\ref{def:defect_charge_sigma}).
Consider the functional on admissible potentials $\Phi$ with suitable boundary conditions,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dirichlet_functional_Phi}
\mathcal{J}[\Phi]
:=
\int_\Omega \left(\frac{1}{8\pi G}\,\abs{\nabla\Phi(x)}^2-\sigma(x)\,\Phi(x)\right)\dd^3x.
\end{equation}
If $\Phi$ is a stationary point of $\mathcal{J}$ under compactly supported variations (or variations compatible with the boundary condition), then $\Phi$ satisfies the Poisson equation
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:poisson_from_action}
-\Delta\Phi=4\pi G\,\sigma
\end{equation}
in the weak sense; if $\Phi$ is sufficiently smooth, then \eqref{eq:poisson_from_action} holds pointwise.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $\eta$ be a smooth test function compatible with the boundary condition and consider $\Phi_\epsilon=\Phi+\epsilon\eta$.
Differentiating \eqref{eq:dirichlet_functional_Phi} at $\epsilon=0$ and integrating by parts yields
\[
0=\left.\frac{\dd}{\dd\epsilon}\right|_{\epsilon=0}\mathcal{J}[\Phi_\epsilon]
=\int_\Omega \left(\frac{1}{4\pi G}\,\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\eta-\sigma\,\eta\right)\dd^3x
=\int_\Omega \left(-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\,\Delta\Phi-\sigma\right)\eta\,\dd^3x,
\]
which is exactly the weak formulation of \eqref{eq:poisson_from_action}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Minimal closed-layer input for Poisson closure]
\label{rem:minimal_poisson_input}
Propositions~\ref{prop:dirichlet_poisson_discrete}--\ref{prop:dirichlet_poisson} show that the Poisson closure for the phase potential is not an additional axiom once the defect-density interface is fixed.
Beyond Axioms~\ref{ax:O5}--\ref{ax:O6} and Hilbert folding (Axiom~\ref{ax:H1}), the only additional closed-layer inputs are:
(i) an auditable defect charge density $\sigma$ (or $\sigma_n$) at a chosen horizon and readout scale (Definition~\ref{def:defect_charge_sigma});
(ii) a boundary/gauge convention (periodic/Dirichlet/decay), including neutrality when required for solvability (Remark~\ref{rem:sigma_constraints});
and (iii) a macroscopic coupling constant $G$ fixed by calibration (Remark~\ref{rem:units_calibration}).
Given these, the Dirichlet principle uniquely determines $\Phi$ (up to the standard Laplacian zero mode in the periodic convention), and the force template follows by $\mathbf{P}_\Phi=-\nabla\Phi$ (Section~\ref{sec:poisson_closure}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Dimensional analysis and calibration]
\label{rem:units_calibration}
In the GR matching dictionary of Section~\ref{sec:gravity}, $\Phi=-\log\lapse$ is dimensionless.
Defining the Newtonian potential $\phi_N:=-c^2\Phi$ gives $\phi_N$ the standard physical units.
Then \eqref{eq:poisson_from_action} becomes the classical Poisson equation $\Delta\phi_N=4\pi G\rho$ upon setting $\rho:=c^2\sigma$.
Operationally, the coefficient $G$ is fixed by calibration against a reference redshift profile (e.g.\ Earth-to-GPS) once $\sigma$ is defined from auditable protocol budgets.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Weak-field limit from general covariance]
\label{rem:weak_field_gr_poisson}
In general relativity, Einstein's equation is $G_{\mu\nu}=(8\pi G/c^4)\,T_{\mu\nu}$.
In the weak, static, slow-motion limit with $T_{00}\approx \rho c^2$ and negligible stresses, one may write
$g_{00}=-(1+2\phi_N/c^2)+O(c^{-4})$ and $g_{0i}=0$.
Linearizing the field equations yields the Newtonian Poisson equation $\Delta\phi_N=4\pi G\rho$ (see, e.g., \cite{MisnerThorneWheeler1973,Wald1984,Carroll2004,Poisson2004}).
In a static slicing with vanishing shift, the lapse is $N=\sqrt{-g_{00}}=1+\phi_N/c^2+O(c^{-4})$, hence
\[
\Phi=-\log N=-\frac{\phi_N}{c^2}+O(c^{-4}).
\]
Therefore $-\Delta\Phi=4\pi G\,\rho/c^2+O(c^{-4})=4\pi G\,\sigma+O(c^{-4})$, matching \eqref{eq:poisson_from_action} at leading order.
\end{remark}
