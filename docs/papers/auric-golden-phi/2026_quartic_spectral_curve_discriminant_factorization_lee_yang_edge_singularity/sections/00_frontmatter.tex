\begin{abstract}
Given a family of partition functions \(\{Z_m(y)\}_{m\ge 0}\) governed by a quartic spectral curve, we characterize a Lee--Yang-type critical boundary in a strictly auditable, finite-dimensional setting.
We first show that the ordinary generating function \(Z(t,y)=\sum_{m\ge 0} Z_m(y)t^m\) is a reduced rational function, which implies a minimal linear recurrence of order \(4\) and a fixed Hankel rank \(4\) for all parameters \(y\) outside a finite degeneracy set.
We then compute and completely factor the discriminant of the quartic spectral polynomial \(\Pi(\lambda,y)\) (as a polynomial in \(\lambda\)), obtaining that branching occurs only at \(y\in\{0,1\}\) and at the roots of \(256y^3+411y^2+165y+32\).
The latter cubic has exactly one real root \(\yLY\in(-1.13446,-1.13445)\), which provides an algebraic anchor for a nontrivial real-axis Lee--Yang-type edge singularity.
Next, introducing an exponential family via the normalization \(Z_m(e^s)/Z_m(1)\), we prove that the limiting log-moment generating function is controlled by the dominant spectral branch \(\lambda_+(y)\), and we obtain closed-form constants for a law of large numbers and a central limit theorem: mean \(5/18\) and variance \(67/972\).
Finally, we outline a certificate-oriented computational workflow based on the order-\(4\) recurrence, enabling polynomial-time evaluation of \(Z_m(y)\), localization of zeros, and verification of the critical boundary structure.
\end{abstract}
