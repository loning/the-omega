\subsection{Natural Weight and Integral Transforms: Finitization of Unbounded Integrals}\label{subsec:unit-circle-phase-integrals}
From \eqref{eq:unit-circle-phase-upsilon} one obtains the derivative
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-circle-phase-derivative}
\boxed{\ \frac{\dd \upsilon}{\dd x}=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^2)}\ }.
\end{equation}
Hence the change-of-variable relation reads
\[
\dd \upsilon=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^2)}\,\dd x.
\]
Define the natural weight measure on \(\RR\):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-circle-phase-cauchy-measure}
\boxed{\ \dd\mu(x):=\frac{1}{\pi(1+x^2)}\,\dd x\ }.
\end{equation}
Then \(\upsilon\) pushes forward \((\RR,\mu)\) to \((\mathbb{U},\mathrm{Leb})\) (Lebesgue measure).
\begin{remark}[Intrinsic information weight induced by the Haar pullback]\label{rem:unit-circle-phase-haar-pullback-weight}
Viewing \(\mathbb{U}\) as the normalized phase box of the angular coordinate \(\vartheta/(2\pi)\), the Lebesgue measure \(\mathrm{Leb}\) corresponds precisely to the normalized Haar measure on the unit circle \(\TT\).
Therefore, the measure \(\mu\) given by \eqref{eq:unit-circle-phase-cauchy-measure} may be interpreted as the pullback of this Haar measure via \(x=\tan(\vartheta/2)\), and it is \textbf{uniquely determined} in the normalized sense.
Its tail decays as \(x^{-2}\), thereby structurally excluding the non-compact pathology of ``infinity carrying infinite information'' under this coordinate gate: infinity corresponds only to the single point \(\iota(\infty)=-1\) on the phase boundary.
\end{remark}
Consequently, for any integrable function \(f\) one has two basic formulae:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Finitization via the natural weight:}
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-circle-phase-weighted-integral}
  \boxed{\ \int_{\RR} f(x)\,\dd\mu(x)=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} f\bigl(\tan(\pi u)\bigr)\,\dd u\ }.
  \end{equation}
  \item \textbf{Change of variable with respect to Lebesgue measure:} From \(x=\tan(\pi u)\) one obtains \(\dd x=\pi\sec^2(\pi u)\,\dd u\), hence
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:unit-circle-phase-lebesgue-integral}
  \boxed{\ \int_{\RR} f(x)\,\dd x=\int_{-1/2}^{1/2} f\bigl(\tan(\pi u)\bigr)\,\pi\sec^2(\pi u)\,\dd u\ }.
  \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
Equation~\eqref{eq:unit-circle-phase-weighted-integral} rewrites an unbounded integral as a strict finite integral over the phase box; equation~\eqref{eq:unit-circle-phase-lebesgue-integral} provides the standard change-of-variable caliber compatible with this compactification.
