\section{Relation to Existing Theories}
\label{chap:relation}

Omega Theory aims to synthesize key elements of several competing programs in quantum gravity.

\subsection{String Theory}
We agree with String Theory on:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Holography}: Bulk effective degrees of freedom can admit a boundary encoding description in appropriate regimes \cite{thooft1993dimensional,susskind1995world}.
    \item \textbf{Higher Dimensions}: The internal organization (octonions/$G_2$ sector) motivates an effective higher-dimensional internal geometry; in particular a $7$-dimensional internal fiber is a natural bookkeeping target, so that a $4{+}7$ split suggests an 11D-like structure at the level of model organization (no unique dimensionality theorem is derived here).
\end{enumerate}
We disagree on:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Continuum Background}: Rather than relying on a continuum landscape of distinct vacua, we adopt a regulated discrete substrate and encode model-dependent ``vacuum data'' in the choice of the Omega state and its spectral/modular data.
    \item \textbf{Supersymmetry}: We do not assume spacetime SUSY at the fundamental level; instead we propose that additional partner-like excitations arise as different topological wrapping modes of the internal sector.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Loop Quantum Gravity (LQG)}
We agree on:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Discreteness}: In canonical LQG, area/volume operators have discrete spectra in the kinematical theory \cite{RovelliSmolin1995,AshtekarLewandowski1997Area}.
    \item \textbf{Spin Networks}: Both approaches employ graph-based kinematics; modern spin-foam formulations provide a covariant path-integral realization of this idea \cite{Perez2013SpinFoam}.
\end{enumerate}
We disagree on:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Dynamics}: LQG faces technical challenges in defining and controlling the Hamiltonian/dynamics so as to recover an effective $3{+}1$ spacetime description in a fully predictive way. Our ``static vector'' ansatz sidesteps this by defining intrinsic time via spectral/modular readouts rather than by evolving a fundamental spacetime metric.
\end{enumerate}



\section{Consistency with Quantum Gravity Constraints}
\label{chap:swampland}

Any candidate theory of Quantum Gravity should be compatible with ``swampland'' considerations---i.e.\ avoid effective field theories that cannot be UV-completed. Within the geometric/topological ans\"atze adopted in this manuscript, one can formulate concrete analogs of several swampland-motivated scaling statements. We emphasize that this is not a derived UV-completion theorem; rather, it is a consistency check that the proposed scaling data are compatible with the qualitative form of the conjectures.

\subsection{The Weak Gravity Conjecture (WGC)}
The Weak Gravity Conjecture (WGC) posits that gravity is the weakest force, implying the existence of a particle with mass $m$ and charge $q$ such that $m < q M_{Pl}$ (in appropriate units) \cite{ArkaniHamedMotlNicolisVafa2007}.
In Omega Theory, charge is modeled by an integer winding number $q$ (a topological index) and mass by an internal cost/length scale associated with sustaining a wrapped sector. In this language, the WGC becomes a constraint on the internal geometric normalization: one must exhibit at least one charged excitation for which the ratio $m/q$ is bounded above by a Planck-scale quantity.
At the present stage of the manuscript, this is treated as a \emph{model-building consistency check} rather than as a derived theorem: a quantitative verification requires a concrete internal metric/measure and an explicit computation of $m(q)$ and the corresponding effective gauge coupling. The qualitative compatibility is that in geometric models of charged cycles, gauge contributions can scale more favorably than gravitational ones for sufficiently small charged cycles, making the WGC inequality natural to satisfy once the microscopic normalization is fixed.

\subsection{The Swampland Distance Conjecture (SDC)}
The Swampland Distance Conjecture (SDC) posits that at infinite distance in moduli space $\Delta \phi \to \infty$, an infinite tower of states becomes exponentially light: $m \sim M_{Pl} e^{-\alpha \Delta \phi}$ \cite{OoguriVafa2007,Palti2019}.
In our "Golden Spiral" phase space, large distances correspond to moving down the spiral into the infrared ($n \to \infty$). The mass of the "breathing modes" (geometric scalars) scales self-similarly:
\begin{equation}
    m_n \sim M_{Pl} \phi^{-n}.
\end{equation}
If one parametrizes the effective distance by a logarithmic scale variable $\Delta_{geo}\equiv n\ln\phi$, then this tower matches the SDC-type exponential form:
\begin{equation}
    m(\Delta_{geo}) \sim M_{Pl} e^{-\Delta_{geo}}.
\end{equation}
In this reading, the ``tower of states'' can be interpreted as a KK-like spectrum associated with the self-similar internal structure.
\begin{proposition}[SDC-type exponential tower from golden scaling]
\label{prop:sdc_golden}
Assume a tower of masses obeys $m_n=M_{Pl}\phi^{-n}$ with $\phi>1$ and define a geometric distance parameter by $\Delta_{geo}\equiv n\ln\phi$. Then
\begin{equation}
    m(\Delta_{geo})=M_{Pl}\,e^{-\Delta_{geo}}.
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is an immediate change of variables.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Monogamy of Entanglement and Firewalls}
The "Firewall Paradox" (AMPS) argues that a black hole horizon cannot simultaneously satisfy smoothness (near-vacuum entanglement across the horizon) and unitarity of evaporation (Page-curve purification of late radiation by early radiation) if one assumes a naive tensor-product factorization of the relevant degrees of freedom \cite{AlmheiriMarolfPolchinskiSully2013}. In the standard tripartite labeling, let $B$ denote a late outgoing Hawking mode, $A$ its interior partner, and $R$ the early radiation collected at infinity.
\begin{proposition}[AMPS monogamy tension (exact factorization form)]
\label{prop:amps_monogamy}
Let $|\Psi\rangle_{ABR}$ be a pure state. If the horizon is smooth in the strong sense that the reduced state on $AB$ is pure (equivalently $S(AB)=0$), then $|\Psi\rangle_{ABR}$ factorizes as $|\Psi\rangle_{ABR}=|\psi\rangle_{AB}\otimes|\phi\rangle_{R}$. In particular, $B$ is uncorrelated with $R$ and cannot be purified by $R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is standard (pure reduced state implies factorization across the complementary bipartition); see, e.g., \cite{NielsenChuang2000}.
\end{proof}
\noindent
In realistic black hole evaporation, the smooth-horizon requirement is approximate (the $AB$ state is only approximately pure), while unitarity after the Page time requires the outgoing mode $B$ to be significantly correlated with $R$ (entropy of the radiation decreases upon adding $B$). The AMPS tension is then sharpened by entropy inequalities (strong subadditivity/monogamy) under the assumption that $A$, $B$, and $R$ are independent subsystems.

Omega Theory adopts the modern holographic/error-correction viewpoint: the ``interior'' is treated as a protected code subspace rather than as a naive tensor factor, and interior operators can in principle be reconstructed from different boundary regions (Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}). In holographic QEC and in Page-curve/island developments, the resolution mechanism is precisely that the naive factorization is not the correct algebraic description of the effective degrees of freedom after sufficient scrambling/Page time \cite{AlmheiriDongHarlow2015,Pastawski2015,Harlow2017,Penington2020,Almheiri2019,Harlow2021}. In this manuscript, the firewall discussion therefore reduces to a concrete modeling task: verify that the microscopic encoding map of Axiom~O4 realizes the required recovery/reconstruction properties in the relevant regimes.
\par\noindent
\textit{Formal bridge.} Appendix~\ref{app:isometry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:no_naive_factorization_code} records the corresponding kinematic point: in an isometric code with entanglement-wedge reconstruction, interior degrees of freedom are defined algebraically on a code subspace rather than by a fixed Hilbert-space tensor factorization.

\section{Cosmological Discriminators vs. Fine-Tuning}
\label{chap:discriminators}

High-precision cosmology (CMB-S4, LiteBIRD) provides the ultimate testing ground for Omega Theory versus the standard Inflationary paradigm.

\subsection{Primordial Gravitational Waves}
Standard Inflationary models typically predict a red-tilted spectrum of primordial gravitational waves; in simple large-field models the tensor-to-scalar ratio can be as large as $r\sim 10^{-2}$.
In the non-singular bounce templates considered in this manuscript (Section~\ref{chap:cosmology}; cf.\ LQC-inspired effective dynamics \cite{AshtekarPawlowskiSingh2006}), near-saturation of the holographic bound at the bounce motivates a suppression of long-wavelength tensor modes. Accordingly the tensor spectrum is expected to be blue-tilted ($n_T>0$) and the CMB-scale amplitude can be parametrically small:
\begin{equation}
    r_{\mathrm{CMB}}\ \ll\ 10^{-2}.
\end{equation}
A non-detection of $B$-mode polarization by LiteBIRD would falsify simple chaotic inflation but would be consistent with such a bounce template. Conversely, a detection of $r \gtrsim 10^{-2}$ would strongly constrain the bounce/near-saturation sector and the assumed tensor transfer through the high-curvature regime.

\subsection{Anthropic principle and fixed matching data}
A major appeal of String Theory Landscape models is the "Anthropic Principle"---the idea that fundamental constants like $\alpha$ are random environmental variables selected by the requirement of observers.
Omega Theory suggests a different viewpoint: within the geometric ans\"atze adopted here, $\alpha$ and $\mu$ are tied to internal $G_2$-structured data (Eqs.~(\ref{eq:alpha_geo}) and (\ref{eq:mu_geo}); see Appendix \ref{app:constants_proof} for the definitions used here). They are not treated as random variables drawn from an ensemble distribution $P(\alpha)$, but as fixed matching data of the microscopic construction.
Within this ansatz, the effective distribution over $\alpha$ is sharply peaked around a geometric value; one may idealize this as a Dirac delta distribution:
\begin{equation}
    P(\alpha) \approx \delta(\alpha - \alpha_{\text{geo}}).
\end{equation}
There is no "Multiverse" of varying constants in the sense of an \emph{ensemble} of theories: under the Omega Axiom (O1) the theory is specified by a single fixed global state $\omega_\Omega$. In that sense, the constants are fixed matching data of the microscopic construction rather than random variables drawn from a distribution; the remaining task is to explain them from the internal geometric measure data.

\subsection{Hubble tension (inhomogeneous template)}
The ``Hubble tension'' refers to a persistent mismatch between late-time distance-ladder inferences of the expansion rate and early-time CMB-based inferences within minimal $\Lambda$CDM \cite{VerdeTreuRiess2019H0,Planck2018CosmoParams}. In the Omega Action framework (Section~\ref{chap:omega_action}), the informational field $\varrho$ behaves as an additional scalar sector whose stress tensor includes gradient contributions. Away from exact homogeneity, these contributions can vary spatially, so that different observational pipelines (sampling different environments and averaging schemes) can in principle infer different \emph{effective} expansion rates.
\par\noindent
As a minimal phenomenological parameterization of such an environment-dependent shift, we write
\begin{equation}
    H_{\mathrm{eff}}(z)\ \equiv\ H_{\mathrm{global}}(z)\left(1+\xi\,\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{info}}(z)}{\rho_{\mathrm{crit}}(z)}\right),
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{\mathrm{info}}\equiv \langle T_{00}^{info}\rangle$ is the informational-sector energy density implied by the Omega Action and $\xi$ is an effective dimensionless response parameter encoding the observational averaging/selection. A quantitative confrontation requires a fully specified inhomogeneous solution and a joint fit (including BAO, SNe, and growth constraints), so we treat this as an illustrative template rather than as a derived resolution claim.

\subsection{Confrontation with Experimental Constraints}
While the predictions in Table \ref{tab:constraints} are distinct, they must be reconciled with existing high-precision data.

\subsubsection{Photon Dispersion and Fermi-LAT}
Quantum Cellular Automata typically induce Lorentz-violating dispersion relations. We quantify this using the standard Effective Quantum Gravity (EQG) parameterization for broken Lorentz symmetry:
\begin{equation}
    E^2 = p^2 c^2 \left[ 1 + s_n\left( \frac{p}{E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}}} \right)^n + \mathcal{O}\!\left(\frac{p}{E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}}}\right)^{n+1} \right],
\end{equation}
where $s_n=\pm 1$ encodes the sign (superluminal/subluminal in the usual conventions) and $E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}}$ is the effective quantum-gravity scale for the leading operator of order $n$.\par
\noindent
Time-of-flight analyses of Gamma-Ray Bursts constrain the low-order terms very strongly. For example, the Fermi-LAT analysis of GRB~090510 implies $E^{(1)}_{\mathrm{QG}}$ at or above the Planck scale and $E^{(2)}_{\mathrm{QG}}$ in the $\sim 10^{11}$\,GeV range \cite{Abdo2009,Vasileiou2013}. More recently, LHAASO-based analyses of the BOAT GRB~221009A report (95\% confidence) bounds at the level
\begin{equation}
    \frac{E^{(1)}_{\mathrm{QG}}}{10^{19}\,\mathrm{GeV}} > 21.1,\qquad
    \frac{E^{(2)}_{\mathrm{QG}}}{10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV}} > 14.9,
\end{equation}
corresponding to $E^{(1)}_{\mathrm{QG}} \gtrsim 2.1\times 10^{20}$\,GeV and $E^{(2)}_{\mathrm{QG}} \gtrsim 1.5\times 10^{12}$\,GeV in that analysis \cite{Hua2025DisCan}.\par
\noindent
In the Omega construction, parity-symmetric analytic dispersion implies the absence of a linear ($n=1$) time-of-flight term (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}, Proposition~\ref{prop:no_linear_mdr}). The \textbf{icosahedral symmetry} of the quasicrystal is intended to suppress low-rank \emph{anisotropies}, yielding an approximately isotropic leading correction. For illustration, if the leading isotropic MDR term is $n=2$, then the group-velocity deviation scales as $v(E)=c\,[1+\mathcal{O}((E/E^{(2)}_{\mathrm{QG}})^2)]$ in the subluminal convention. Quantitatively, the time delay for order-$n$ dispersion scales as
\begin{equation}
    \Delta t \sim \frac{n+1}{2}\,\frac{L}{c}\left(\frac{E}{E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}}}\right)^n,
\end{equation}
For cosmological sources, a more accurate expression replaces $L/c$ by an integral over redshift, $\int_0^z \frac{(1+z')^{n}}{H(z')}dz'$, but the scaling with $(E/E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}})^n$ remains. For $E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}}\sim M_{Pl}$, even at $E\sim 10^5\text{--}10^6$ GeV and $z\sim 1$ the implied $\Delta t$ is many orders of magnitude below practical timing resolution for $n\ge 3$. Thus, time-of-flight measurements primarily constrain the existence of lower-order terms ($n=1,2$), while $n\ge 3$ terms become relevant only if the effective scale is parametrically below $M_{Pl}$.
\par\noindent
\textit{Remark (unitarity-forced even-order structure).} Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry} also records a simple unitary scan/difference example in which an arc--chord identity on $U(1)$ fixes an exact nonlinear dispersion and therefore rigidly determines the leading even-order correction coefficient in the long-wavelength expansion \cite{Ma2025HPA}.

\subsubsection{Mapping to Standard-Model Extension (SME)}
Modified dispersion can be organized systematically in the photon sector of the Standard-Model Extension (SME) \cite{Colladay1998,KosteleckyMewes2009}. In that framework, higher-dimension operators of mass dimension $d$ typically induce energy-dependent corrections scaling as $E^{d-4}$, i.e.\ $n=d-4$ in the MDR notation above. The phenomenological role of the MDR scale $E^{(n)}_{\mathrm{QG}}$ can therefore be compared to bounds on the corresponding SME operator coefficients in the appropriate isotropic/nonbirefringent subsector.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{CPT-odd term ($n=1$; $d=5$)}: Linear-in-energy time-of-flight terms correspond to dimension-$5$ photon-sector operators and are very strongly constrained by GRB analyses. In parity-symmetric coined-walk settings, such $n=1$ terms are absent at the level of the analytic dispersion expansion (Appendix~\ref{app:mdr_symmetry}).
    \item \textbf{Leading isotropic term ($n=2$; $d=6$)}: A quadratic-in-energy time-of-flight term corresponds to a dimension-$6$ operator in SME language. For Planck-scale suppression, its effect is far below current timing sensitivity at present GRB energies, while still providing a well-defined target for mapping specific Omega QCA dispersions to SME coefficient bounds.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Holographic Noise and the Holometer}
The Fermilab Holometer experiment rules out ``random walk'' holographic noise ($\delta x \sim \sqrt{L l_P}$) \cite{Chou2016Holometer}. In deterministic-update benchmarks, any effective ``noise'' refers to coarse-grained fluctuations of relational observables and is generically colored rather than Markovian; in particular, in golden-scaling templates one expects a structured spectrum with model-dependent high-frequency cutoffs.
We model the signal in terms of the \textbf{one-sided strain power spectral density} $S_h(f)$ (units $1/\mathrm{Hz}$), with a leading ``pink'' scaling
\begin{equation}
    S_h(f) \approx A_h\,\frac{l_P}{L}\,\frac{1}{f},
\end{equation}
where $A_h$ is a dimensionless amplitude coefficient. A $1/f$ envelope with log-periodic modulation is the canonical signature of (approximate) discrete scale invariance (Appendix~\ref{app:one_over_f}; see, e.g., \cite{Sornette1998DSI}); in our phenomenological template the peaks are concentrated near characteristic geometric frequencies $f_k = (c/L)\,\phi^k$ (see Part VI). The corresponding one-sided displacement power spectral density is $S_x(f)=L^2 S_h(f)$, so the displacement amplitude spectral density scales as
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{S_x(f)} \approx \sqrt{A_h\,\frac{L\,l_P}{f}}.
\end{equation}
For Holometer-like baselines ($L\sim 40\,\mathrm{m}$) in the MHz band, this scaling is directly testable. A convenient translation of a bound on the displacement ASD at frequency $f$ is
\begin{equation}
    A_h \lesssim \frac{f\,S_x(f)}{L\,l_P},
\end{equation}
up to the usual caveats about template mismatch and the fact that published analyses may be optimized for specific spectral shapes. In our framework, the log-periodic peaks motivate a matched-filter (template) search rather than a broadband null test.\par
\noindent
Furthermore, if the effective holographic displacement field acts as a \emph{common-mode} signal for a pair of colocated interferometers, then the cross-spectrum inherits the same $1/f$ envelope (and any log-periodic structure) as the autospectrum (Appendix~\ref{app:one_over_f}, Proposition~\ref{prop:common_mode_crosscorr}). This motivates cross-correlation searches targeted at the log-periodic template frequencies rather than broadband white-noise null tests.

\subsubsection{Variability of Constants and Scalar Fields}
Atomic clock experiments limit $\dot{\alpha}/\alpha < 10^{-17} \text{yr}^{-1}$ \cite{Rosenband2008}. The benchmark amplitude $\Delta \alpha/\alpha \sim 10^{-6}$ discussed in Part~VI is a \textbf{spatial dipole template} (across a Hubble-scale volume), not a local temporal drift at the present epoch.
To model this, we identify the variation with the "Higgs-like" geometric breathing mode $\Phi(x)$ defined in Section \ref{chap:gauge_fields}. The scalar field creates a position-dependent modulation of the fiber volume $V_{fib}$:
\begin{equation}
    \alpha(x) = \alpha_0 e^{\beta \Phi(x)}.
\end{equation}
In a late-time quasi-static regime where $\Phi(x)$ is close to a constant background value, local temporal drift is expected to be suppressed relative to horizon-scale spatial gradients (schematically by Hubble-scale factors), so that clock bounds and a horizon-scale dipole template can be simultaneously accommodated at the level of an effective scalar-field parameterization.

\subsection{Theoretical Open Questions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Computational Constraints}: Our ansatz for $\mu$ relies on small-scale lattice simulations ($N < 100$). Verifying the scaling behavior to the cosmological limit ($N \sim 10^{122}$) is currently computationally intractable and relies on the assumption of exact self-similarity.
    \item \textbf{Effective Field Theory}: We have not yet rigorously derived the full continuum path integral of the Standard Model from the discrete cellular automaton, relying instead on the correspondence of symmetry groups and conservation laws.
    \item \textbf{Experimental Discrimination}: Direct laboratory discrimination of the golden-spectrum ansatz from standard Quantum Mechanics requires isolating extremely small corrections in the appropriate effective sector. In practice, the most promising probes are indirect, through dedicated searches for the predicted template features in high-precision data (e.g.\ absence of a linear MDR term together with bounds on $n=2$ dispersion; and targeted analyses for holographic-noise templates and spatial dipoles in constants), rather than through any direct observation at the Planck-time scale.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion and Roadmap}
\label{chap:conclusion}

The ``problem of time'' can be viewed as a tension between descriptions in which time is a dynamical coordinate (GR) and descriptions in which it is an external parameter (QM). Omega Theory proposes to reframe this tension by treating the universe as a single static global state on a quasi-local algebra together with intrinsic/time-readout constructions on regulated code subspaces.
Omega Theory proposes a radical shift:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Ontology}: A single static global state $\omega_\Omega$ on a quasi-local operator algebra (represented by $|\Omega\rangle$ in a suitable Hilbert-space representation), together with a regulated/local finite-dimensional description on effective code subspaces.
    \item \textbf{Dynamics}: Using an aperiodic torus-rotation ansatz (golden spectrum): equidistribution on orbit closures provides canonical long-time orbit averages (Convention~R1), while the non-mixing/quasiperiodic nature of the motion explains why generic thermalization arguments do not automatically apply in projective Hilbert space.
    \item \textbf{Matter}: Proposing a geometric organization of internal symmetries (octonions/$G_2$) and geometric ans\"atze for certain dimensionless constants ($\alpha, \mu$).
    \item \textbf{Meaning}: Modeling an observer/selection map ($\hat{\mathcal{O}}$) as an interpretation layer for handling computational incompleteness/undecidability in sufficiently expressive dynamics.
\end{itemize}
The proximity of the proposed geometric ans\"atze for $\alpha$ and $\mu$ to the measured values motivates further work toward a fully specified microscopic measure and an explicit renormalization procedure. The universe is framed here as a ``Golden Quasicrystal'' constructed from finite information.
