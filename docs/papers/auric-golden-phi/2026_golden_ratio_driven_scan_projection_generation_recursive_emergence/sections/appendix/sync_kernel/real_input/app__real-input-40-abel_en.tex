\subsubsection{Abel Normalization: Locking the Dominant Pole to the $r\to 1$ Interface Coordinate}\label{app:real-input-40-abel}
In Section~\ref{sec:zeta-finite-part} of the main text, ``discretization--regularization--finite part'' serves as the stable interface: for SFT/sofic systems, discretization compresses the dynamics to a finite matrix;
regularization turns divergent quantities into controllable analytic objects; the finite part extracts ``model-invariant constants'' as comparable scalars (Definition \ref{def:abel-finite-part}).
For the real input 40-state kernel, this closed loop is completely closed in the present appendix: $M$ is an auditable finite matrix, $\zeta_M$ is an explicit rational function,
and $\log\mathfrak{M}$ is the Abel/finite part output.

\paragraph{Abel Coordinate}
Denote the dominant eigenvalue $\lambda=\rho(M)=\varphi^2$ and the dominant pole $z_\star=\lambda^{-1}$. Introduce the Abel normalized coordinate
\[
r:=\lambda z\quad(\Leftrightarrow z=r/\lambda),\qquad
\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r):=\zeta_M(r/\lambda).
\]
Then the dominant pole is moved from $z=z_\star$ to $r=1$, and from $\zeta_M(z)=1/\det(I-zM)$ we obtain an equally explicit rational expression:
\[
\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r)
=\frac{1}{\det\!\bigl(I-(r/\lambda)M\bigr)}
=\frac{1}{(1-r/\lambda)^2(1+r/\lambda)^3\bigl(1-3r/\lambda+r^2/\lambda^2\bigr)\bigl(1+r/\lambda-r^2/\lambda^2\bigr)}.
\]
Using $\lambda^2-3\lambda+1=0$ and $\lambda=\varphi^2$, the two quadratic factors can be further factored into linear factors in the $r$-variable:
\[
1-3r/\lambda+r^2/\lambda^2=(1-r)\bigl(1-r/\lambda^2\bigr),\qquad
1+r/\lambda-r^2/\lambda^2=\bigl(1+r/\varphi\bigr)\bigl(1-r/\varphi^3\bigr).
\]
Therefore the normalized determinant can be written in a completely factored form from which pole positions are directly readable:
\[
\det\!\bigl(I-(r/\lambda)M\bigr)
=-\frac{(r-\lambda)^2(r+\lambda)^3(r-1)(r-\lambda^2)(r-\varphi^3)(r+\varphi)}{\lambda^9}.
\]
From this one can directly read off all singular positions of $\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r)$:
\[
r\in\{1,\ \pm\lambda,\ \lambda^2,\ \varphi^3,\ -\varphi\},
\]
all of which except $r=1$ satisfy $|r|>1$. Hence in $|r|<1$, $\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r)$ has no poles whatsoever; the sole critical obstruction lies at $r=1$.
Moreover, the dominant pole residue constant in Abel coordinates can likewise be read as
\[
\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r)=\frac{C}{1-r}+O(1),
\qquad
C=\lim_{r\to 1}(1-r)\,\widetilde{\zeta}_M(r)=\frac{47+21\sqrt5}{100}.
\]
