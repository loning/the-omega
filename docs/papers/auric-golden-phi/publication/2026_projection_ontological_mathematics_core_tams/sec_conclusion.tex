\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

The paper isolates a finite-window object that is closed already at the
definition level: Zeckendorf stabilization modulo \(F_{m+2}\).  The residue
description and affine transfer of Sections~\ref{sec:preliminaries} and
\ref{sec:residue-affine} show that the resulting fiber spectrum is not an
auxiliary rewriting artefact.  The main theorem is
\Cref{thm:partition-difference}: after an explicit affine permutation, each
fiber multiplicity is exactly the Fibonacci-lag discrete derivative of the
classical Fibonacci partition function.

This reidentification changes the status of the whole theory.  The collision
moments become difference-power sums of a classical arithmetic function, and
the quadratic recurrence of \Cref{thm:cubic-recurrence} becomes a new exact
identity for the energy of that discrete derivative.  The reflector formulas of
Section~\ref{sec:ledger} then appear naturally as applications of a fiber
formula that is already rigid at the single-value level.

The present paper stops at this finite-window core.  It does not attempt to
develop the full arithmetic theory of the minimal recurrences attached to the
difference-power sums \(M_{q,m}\), nor the dynamical refinements suggested by
Kempton's cocycle model.  Those directions are substantial enough to warrant
separate treatment.  What is established here is the structural bridge itself:
finite-window Zeckendorf fiber multiplicities are exactly Fibonacci-lag
differences of the Fibonacci partition function.
