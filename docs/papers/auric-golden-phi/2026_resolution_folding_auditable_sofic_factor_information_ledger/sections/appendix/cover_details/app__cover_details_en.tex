\section{Right-resolving presentations and Fischer cover interface}\label{app:cover-details}

This appendix summarizes standard facts used in Section~\ref{sec:sofic-cover} and fixes the precise ``computable interface'' viewpoint.

\subsection{Determinization yields right-resolving}\label{app:det-right-resolving}
Given a labeled graph $G=(V,E,\ell)$, the subset construction produces a deterministic automaton whose states are subsets $S\subseteq V$ and whose transitions are partial maps $S\mapsto \delta(S,a)$ for labels $a$.
By construction, each state has at most one successor for each label, hence the resulting presentation is right-resolving \cite{LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998SymbolicDynamics}.

\subsection{Follower separation and minimality}\label{app:follower-separation}
Two states are \emph{follower equivalent} if they admit the same set of right-infinite label continuations.
Quotienting a right-resolving presentation by follower equivalence yields a follower-separated right-resolving presentation.
The right Fischer cover is the minimal such presentation in the sense of universal mapping properties among right-resolving covers \cite{LindMarcus1995,Kitchens1998SymbolicDynamics}.

\subsection{Why we isolate cover-level invariants}\label{app:cover-audit}
For auditing, cover-level invariants are preferable because they reduce to finite matrices:
entropy and PF data are computed from $\rho(A)$ and its eigenvectors, and the cover zeta function is a determinant.
Intrinsic sofic invariants can also be computed, but they require additional automaton-level bookkeeping (e.g. transition semigroup constructions \cite{Nasu1995TextileSystems}).
