\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Formal semantics is usually presented as truth at a fully specified point of evaluation. That picture is exact when the relevant assignment or world has already been fixed. It is too coarse, however, when information is only partial. One may have ruled out some realizations without having selected a unique one; one may have an admissible reference before a global object has formed; or one may have compatible local data that fail to glue. In such situations the natural semantic notion is not pointwise truth but forcing at an information state.

Several familiar traditions isolate parts of this picture. Kripke and Beth semantics replace absolute truth by forcing at stages of information and impose persistence under refinement \cite{Beth1956,Kripke1965,TroelstraVanDalen1988}. Sheaf-theoretic semantics separates local from global existence and treats gluing failure as a structural defect rather than as a mere lack of value \cite{MacLaneMoerdijk1994}. Related local-to-global obstruction viewpoints also appear in the sheaf-theoretic analysis of contextuality and its cohomological refinements \cite{AbramskyBrandenburger2011,Caru2017}. Dynamic semantics explains later decision by elimination of possibilities rather than by revision of truth conditions. The present paper combines these themes in order to address a more specific question: once an admitted reference carries a finite abelian gluing obstruction, what part of that obstruction remains semantically visible without enlarging the state space, and how much additional information is required to recover the hidden part?

The scope of the paper is deliberately narrower than that of a full logic-architectural reconstruction. The broader research program from which the present article draws employs a longer conservative-extension chain and additional coupling and observer-symmetry structure. Here we isolate only the fragment needed for the cohomological visibility problem: pointwise semantics, information-state forcing, local-object semantics, structural absence with gluing obstructions, and the refinement dynamics required to state the monotonicity results. The paper should therefore be read as a focused application of state forcing to a precise descent-theoretic question, not as an attempt to recover the entire surrounding framework.

Only a small amount of abstract machinery is needed for that purpose. \Cref{sec:preliminaries} isolates the notion of conservative extension that lets one pass from ordinary pointwise semantics to state forcing without altering the meaning of lower-level formulas. \Cref{sec:information-states} then introduces information states $p=(\Gamma_p,R_p)$, proves persistence under refinement and singleton conservativity, and shows that ordinary pointwise soundness lifts to the state setting (\Cref{prop:lifted-pointwise-soundness}). Atomized states moreover admit a canonical Kripke reduct, so the new semantics remains close to familiar forcing-based models.

The main results appear in \Cref{sec:null-decomposition}. After separating admissible reference, local sectionability, compatible local sectionability, and global sectionability, we show that compatible local existence is characterized by sheafification (\Cref{thm:sheafification-characterization}) and that typed readouts obey an address-before-value constraint (\Cref{prop:address-before-value}). The missing categorical bridge is then made explicit: we introduce realization prestacks whose presheaf of isomorphism classes is exactly the local object functor, prove that stackification recovers the sheafification of those classes (\Cref{prop:stackification-components}), and identify visible value classes with the global connected components of the realization stack (\Cref{thm:visible-value-components}). This corrects the naive single-gerbe picture. In general, an admitted reference determines a stack that decomposes into component gerbes
\[
\mathfrak L_r\simeq \coprod_{v\in \Vis_{p,s}(r)} \mathfrak G_v,
\]
one for each visible branch $v$ (\Cref{thm:component-gerbe-decomposition}). On gerbe-adapted covers each branch gerbe yields a Cech obstruction class $\omega_v$, and gluing-level absence occurs exactly when visible branches exist but all of their component gerbes are non-neutral (\Cref{thm:cech-bridge-compatible-realizations,thm:gerbe-null-semantics}). Only when $|\Vis_{p,s}(r)|=1$ does one recover a single obstruction class attached to the reference.

Fixing one visible branch $v$, a finite abelian obstruction presentation yields the strict visible quotient $A/H_\alpha$, initial among coefficient reductions trivializing that chosen branch presentation (\Cref{thm:initial-visible-quotient}) and detected exactly by the presentation-admissible character channels (\Cref{thm:character-definability-barrier,cor:visible-quotient-character-separation}). The class-level analysis is sharper. Once a finite Cech nerve for $\omega_v$ has been fixed, the universal coefficient theorem yields a homological evaluation map
\[
\mathrm{ev}_{\omega_v}:H_2(N(\mathcal U),\mathbb Z)\to A.
\]
Its image is the intrinsic hidden subgroup and its cokernel is the intrinsic visible quotient; \Cref{thm:intrinsic-visible-quotient} proves that this same quotient is exactly the one detected by the class-admissible character channels. Thus intrinsic visibility detects precisely the $H_2$-visible part of a branch obstruction. A second theorem shows that this quotient is initial among coefficient quotients leaving only a pure $\operatorname{Ext}$-type remnant (\Cref{thm:intrinsic-pure-ext-initiality}), so every strict visible quotient is a quotient of the intrinsic one (\Cref{cor:strict-to-intrinsic-visible}). More precisely, the gap between strict and intrinsic visibility is the gap between the image of a cocycle on all $2$-chains and its image on genuine $2$-cycles (\Cref{prop:chain-vs-homological-visibility,cor:strict-intrinsic-coincidence-criterion}).

The blind spot of the theory is equally sharp. Nontrivial branch obstructions may persist even when every character channel is admissible, precisely because the surviving class lies entirely in the $\operatorname{Ext}$-summand of the universal coefficient sequence (\Cref{thm:character-blind-obstructions,ex:rp2-character-blind}). The novelty is not the universal coefficient theorem or finite abelian duality by themselves; it is that, inside the state-forcing obstruction theory, these tools identify the exact boundary between the semantically visible part of gluing failure and the genuinely blind $\operatorname{Ext}$-residual. The complementary extreme is equally informative: if $H_1$ of the nerve is free, then no nontrivial branch obstruction can be character-blind (\Cref{cor:h1-free-no-blindness}), while if $H_2$ vanishes, every branch obstruction is character-blind (\Cref{cor:h2-vanishing-blindness}). Finally, branchwise intrinsic visibility aggregates in two different ways. If the branch label is known, visibility is controlled by $\bigcap_v K_v$; if the same channel must remain admissible on every branch, it is controlled by $\sum_v K_v$; and the exact branch-preserving strictification budget is $\max_v |K_v|$ (\Cref{thm:branch-sensitive-visible-quotient,thm:branch-uniform-visible-quotient,thm:multi-branch-strictification-budget}). The two worked examples, \Cref{ex:one-bit-hidden-obstruction} and \Cref{ex:rp2-character-blind}, exhibit the visible and blind extremes of the theory.

\Cref{sec:multiaxis-refinement} records a secondary application of the same semantics to refinement dynamics. Delayed decision is explained by elimination of admissible realizations rather than by revision of semantic clauses, and the associated support problems admit finite-state upper bounds. More importantly for the present paper, branchwise visibility is monotone under refinement: admissible character channels can only increase, while hidden branch budgets can only decrease (\Cref{thm:branch-visibility-monotonicity,cor:branch-budget-monotonicity}).
