\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Zeckendorf's theorem identifies every positive integer with a unique Fibonacci
expansion having no adjacent \(1\)'s \cite{Lekkerkerker1952,Zeckendorf1972}.
The normalization of arbitrary Fibonacci representations, and its
finite-automaton implementation, are likewise classical
\cite{Frougny1992NumbersAutomata,Frougny1999OnlineAddition,
ShallitShan2023,AhlbachUsatineFrougnyPippenger2013}.  We therefore regard the
existence and computability of Zeckendorf normalization as input data.  The
object of this paper is different: the exact fiber spectrum of finite-window
Zeckendorf stabilization.

Fix \(m\ge 1\).  Let
\[
  \Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m,
  \qquad
  X_m=\{x\in\Omega_m:\ x_ix_{i+1}=0\text{ for }1\le i<m\},
\]
and define
\[
  V_m(x)=\sum_{j=1}^m x_jF_{j+1},
  \qquad
  N_m(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^m \omega_jF_{j+1}.
\]
Since \(V_m\) is a bijection from \(X_m\) onto \(\{0,\dots,F_{m+2}-1\}\), the
correct finite-window fold map is
\[
  \Fold_m(\omega):=V_m^{-1}\bigl(N_m(\omega)\bmod F_{m+2}\bigr).
\]
Equivalently, \(\Fold_m(\omega)\) is obtained by taking the full Zeckendorf
expansion of \(N_m(\omega)\) and retaining the first \(m\) visible digits.
Write
\[
  d_m(x):=\card{\Fold_m^{-1}(x)},
  \qquad
  d_m^\#(r):=d_m(V_m^{-1}(r)).
\]

Two structural facts, proved in Sections~\ref{sec:preliminaries} and
\ref{sec:residue-affine}, drive the whole paper.  First, the fibers of
\(\Fold_m\) are exact residue classes modulo \(F_{m+2}\).  Second, there is an
explicit affine permutation
\[
  \pi_m(r)\equiv F_{m+1}r+F_{m+1}-1 \pmod{F_{m+2}}
\]
such that \(d_m^\#(\pi_m(r))\) is the coefficient of \(z^r\) in
\(\prod_{j=1}^m(1+z^{F_j})\).  The central point of the present paper is that
this coefficient spectrum is not merely analogous to the classical Fibonacci
partition function: it is exactly a Fibonacci-lag discrete derivative of it.

\subsection{Main results}

Following Chow and Slattery \cite{ChowSlattery2021}, let \(R(n)\) denote the
number of partitions of \(n\) into distinct Fibonacci numbers, where the two
unit weights \(F_1=F_2=1\) are not distinguished.  Define
\[
  R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n):=R(n)+R(n-1),
  \qquad
  R(t):=0\quad (t<0).
\]
Then \(R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n)\) is the indexed Fibonacci partition function in
which the two unit weights are distinguished.

\begin{theoremA}
For every integer \(n\) with \(0\le n<F_{m+2}\),
\[
  d_m^\#(\pi_m(n))
  =
  R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n)-R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n-F_{m+1}).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  d_m^\#(\pi_m(n))
  =
  R(n)+R(n-1)-R(n-F_{m+1})-R(n-F_{m+1}-1).
\]
\end{theoremA}

\noindent
Thus the finite-window fiber spectrum is exactly the Fibonacci-lag discrete
derivative of the classical Fibonacci partition function.

\begin{theoremB}
For every integer \(q\ge 1\), the \(q\)-th collision moment
\[
  M_{q,m}:=\sum_{x\in X_m} d_m(x)^q
\]
satisfies
\[
  M_{q,m}
  =
  \sum_{n=0}^{F_{m+2}-1}
  \bigl(R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n)-R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n-F_{m+1})\bigr)^q.
\]
Hence all collision moments are difference-power sums of the Fibonacci
partition function.
\end{theoremB}

\begin{theoremC}
Let
\[
  T_2(m):=
  \sum_{n=0}^{F_{m+2}-1}
  \bigl(R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n)-R^{\mathrm{dbl}}(n-F_{m+1})\bigr)^2.
\]
Then \(T_2(m)=M_{2,m}\), and
\[
  T_2(1)=2,\qquad T_2(2)=6,\qquad T_2(3)=14.
\]
For every \(m\ge 4\),
\[
  T_2(m)=2T_2(m-1)+2T_2(m-2)-2T_2(m-3).
\]
Its characteristic polynomial is \(\lambda^3-2\lambda^2-2\lambda+2\), so the
second-order term is eventually alternating.
\end{theoremC}

\begin{theoremD}
Let \(K_m:=Q_mP_m\).  Then \(K_m\) is the fiber-uniform reflector onto the
visible model, and for every pair of probability laws \(\mu,\nu\) on
\(\Omega_m\),
\[
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\|K_m\nu)
  =
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\|K_m\mu)
  +
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_m\mu\|P_m\nu).
\]
In particular,
\[
  H(\mu)=H(P_m\mu)+\EE_{P_m\mu}[\log d_m(X)]-D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\mu\|K_m\mu),
\]
so \(K_m\mu\) is the unique information projection of \(\mu\) onto the
fiber-uniform visible model.
\end{theoremD}

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

Theorem~A gives the paper its decisive connection to the existing literature.
Chow and Slattery proved exact formulas for the Fibonacci partition function in
terms of the Zeckendorf expansion of \(n\) \cite{ChowSlattery2021}.  Our
Theorem~A shows that finite-window Zeckendorf fibers are not a merely related
combinatorial object: they are precisely the Fibonacci-lag discrete derivative
of the indexed partition function \(R^{\mathrm{dbl}}\).  Consequently their
individual values inherit explicit closed formulas from Chow and Slattery
without any new partition-theoretic reproof.

Theorem~B then reinterprets every collision moment as a difference-power sum of
the Fibonacci partition function.  This places our moments alongside the
ordinary power sums studied by Sanna \cite{Sanna2025}.  In particular, our
quadratic theorem is no longer an isolated fold-specific recurrence, but a new
identity for the squared energy of a Fibonacci-lag partition derivative.

This perspective also clarifies the relation with Chow and Jones
\cite{ChowJones2024}.  They showed that the ordinary variance of the Fibonacci
partition function is governed by the dominant root of
\(\lambda^3-2\lambda^2-2\lambda+2\).  Our Theorem~C produces the same cubic in a
different, exact finite-window setting and explains it structurally: the
quadratic fold moment is the quadratic energy of a discrete derivative of the
partition function.  Finally, work of Kempton \cite{Kempton2023} interprets the
partition function through a cocycle over irrational rotation; combined with
Theorem~A, this gives a natural dynamical reading of finite-window fiber
fluctuations as return-time differences of the same cocycle.

\subsection{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} fixes the finite-window fold map and its basic
overflow structure.  Section~\ref{sec:residue-affine} proves the residue
description, the affine transfer principle, and the partition-difference
formula for individual fibers.  Section~\ref{sec:second-collision} rewrites all
collision moments as difference-power sums and proves the cubic law in the
quadratic case.  Section~\ref{sec:ledger} records the reflector geometry and
entropy consequences as applications of the fiber formulas.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} explains the scope of the present paper and the
arithmetic questions left for later work.
