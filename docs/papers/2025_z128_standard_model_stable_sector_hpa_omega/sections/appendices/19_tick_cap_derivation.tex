\section{Tick\,+\,CAP derivation spine: from the sole input to all interface outputs}
\label{app:tick_cap_derivation}

This appendix records the full ``tick\,+\,CAP'' derivation spine in one place.
It introduces \emph{no additional axioms or independent physical inputs} beyond:
(i) tick as the sequential update index (Axiom~\ref{ax:readout_sequentiality}), and
(ii) CAP as the universal closure/selection rule on explicit finite candidate families (Axiom~\ref{ax:cap} and Appendix~\ref{app:cap_audit_template}).
All other ingredients used throughout the paper are either theorem-level definitions/proofs in the finite model, or CAP-closed interface components whose candidate families and tie-break rules are explicit and audited by deterministic scripts.
External reference conventions (PDG/CODATA targets, renormalization schemes, threshold choices) enter only at the matching layer as comparison inputs and are never used as premises for theorem-level folding statements.

\paragraph{Two-axiom spine (reader contract).}
Within this paper, ``physics'' means the executed protocol outputs of a run.
The only primitive \emph{input} to that execution is the tick stream, and the only primitive \emph{rule} for closing otherwise underdetermined interface components is CAP.
Accordingly, every nontrivial interface component below is recorded in one of two forms: a theorem-level finite construction, or a CAP-closure over an explicitly declared bounded candidate family with deterministic tie-break rules.

\label{subsec:tick_cap_derivation_reading_guide}

We use the following status tags consistently:

\end{itemize}
Appendix~\ref{app:inference_ledger} provides a compact ledger of these dependencies; the present appendix expands each nontrivial CAP-closure step in full audit form (candidate family, objective, tie-break, and where it is reproduced).

\label{subsec:tick_cap_golden_branch}

\[
C_m(\alpha):=\sum_{k=0}^{m} a_{k+1}\qquad\text{for }\alpha=[0;a_1,a_2,\dots],

\paragraph{[CAP] Objective and tie-break.}

\paragraph{[CAP] Result (rigidity).}

This rigidly closes the symbolic/arithmetic bridge to the Zeckendorf/Fibonacci digit system used downstream (Section~\ref{sec:hpa_readout}).

\subsection{Gauge fields as compensating connections (from fiber mismatch)}
\label{subsec:tick_cap_gauge}

\paragraph{[Tick,\;Math] Cross-site comparison forces extra transport data.}
At any fixed window length $m$, a stable label $w\in X_m$ stands for an entire microstate fiber $\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)$.
Therefore, if the protocol requires comparing or transporting stable labels across neighboring sites in a display graph, then stable labels alone are insufficient: one must choose how the endpoint fibers are matched.
This necessity is finite and intrinsic to window projection and stability folding.
Proposition~\ref{prop:gauge_compensation} formalizes the point and shows that, after a deterministic padding to a uniform slot size $r=\max_{w\in X_m}|\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)|$, each edge transport is represented by a permutation in $S_r$, with local relabelings acting by conjugation (a finite gauge redundancy).

\paragraph{[Math] Deterministic discrete holonomy at the anchor.}
At the CAP-minimal anchor $m=6$ one has $r=4$, and the paper provides a deterministic $S_4$ edge-connection construction together with plaquette holonomy diagnostics (Section~\ref{sec:protocol_connections_holonomy}).
This is the finite, protocol-internal origin of the connection/holonomy language used throughout.

\paragraph{[CAP] Three commuting channels close a three-factor compensation structure.}
The folding template isolates three commuting defect channels $(\varphi,\pi,\e)$.
If compensation is defined only up to independent local basis changes associated with each channel, then the minimal redundancy factorizes into three independent components (Proposition~\ref{prop:three_factor_compensation}).

\paragraph{[CAP] Gauge-factor closure (bounded family, objective, tie-break).}
To pass from the finite graph connection to a continuum dictionary, we treat gauge redundancy as internal and unitary (hence compact at the group level), and we restrict to three-factor products with one abelian phase sector and two inequivalent compact simple non-abelian factors.
Within the explicit bounded family
\[
U(1)\times G_2\times G_3,
\]
with $G_2$ and $G_3$ compact, simple, non-abelian and non-isomorphic, we use $\dim(\mathfrak{g})$ as the intrinsic complexity label and apply CAP as lexicographic minimization of $(\dim\mathfrak{g}_2,\dim\mathfrak{g}_3)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:channel_to_gauge}).
The unique minimizer is $U(1)\times SU(2)\times SU(3)$ (up to finite quotients), by the compact Lie classification and Lemma~\ref{lem:su2_su3_unique_by_dim}.
