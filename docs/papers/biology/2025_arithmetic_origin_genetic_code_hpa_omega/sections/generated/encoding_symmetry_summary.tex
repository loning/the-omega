Among the $24$ bijective two-bit encodings, 8 satisfy Watson--Crick complement as bitwise negation ($A\leftrightarrow U$ and $C\leftrightarrow G$ correspond to $\texttt{00}\leftrightarrow\texttt{11}$ and $\texttt{01}\leftrightarrow\texttt{10}$). For every encoding, the three classical nucleotide dichotomies (amino/keto, purine/pyrimidine, weak/strong) correspond to the three nontrivial linear observables on $\{0,1\}^2$ (the two bits and their XOR), but the assignment to $(b1,b0,\mathrm{xor})$ depends on the encoding. Under $\mu^\ast$, the assignment is b1=amino/keto, b0=purine/pyrimidine, xor=weak/strong; this exact assignment occurs for 4/24 encodings, and for 4/8 among the complement-as-bitwise-negation encodings.
