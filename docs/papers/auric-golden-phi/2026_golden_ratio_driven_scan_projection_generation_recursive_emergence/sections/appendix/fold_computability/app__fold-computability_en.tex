\section{Computability and Basic Properties of the Fold Operator \texorpdfstring{$\Fold_m$}{Fold\_m}}
\label{app:fold-computability}

The Zeckendorf normal form can be computed by a greedy algorithm: given $N\in\NN$, one repeatedly subtracts the largest Fibonacci weight not exceeding the current remainder and skips adjacent weights to enforce the no-consecutive-$1$'s constraint. The algorithm terminates and outputs the unique normal form $Z(N)=(c_1,c_2,\dots)$ (\cite{Zeckendorf1972}).

For any $\omega=\omega_1\cdots\omega_m\in\Omega_m$, one first computes the Fibonacci-weighted value according to Definition \ref{def:fold-word}:
\[
N(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^m \omega_k F_{k+1},
\]
then extracts the first $m$ digits of $Z(N(\omega))$ to obtain $\Fold_m(\omega)=\pi_m(Z(N(\omega)))\in X_m$. The entire procedure consists of finitely many integer arithmetic operations together with Zeckendorf greedy normalization, thereby furnishing an explicit algorithm for $\Fold_m$.

The three properties of Proposition \ref{prop:fold-basic} follow directly from Zeckendorf uniqueness:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Normality:} The first $m$ digits output by $\pi_m(Z(\cdot))$ satisfy the no-consecutive-$1$'s constraint and hence lie in $X_m$.
  \item \textbf{Idempotence:} If $\omega\in X_m$, then its weighted value $N(\omega)$ already constitutes a valid Zeckendorf representation; uniqueness forces the first $m$ digits of $Z(N(\omega))$ to equal $\omega$.
  \item \textbf{Surjectivity:} For any $x\in X_m$, setting $\omega=x$ yields $\Fold_m(\omega)=x$.
\end{itemize}

\begin{lemma}[Bijection on the Zeckendorf range]\label{lem:zeckendorf-range-bijection}
For any $m\ge 1$, if $0\le N\le F_{m+2}-1$, then the nonzero digit indices of $Z(N)$ do not exceed $m$, so that
\[
\pi_m(Z(N))=Z(N)\ \text{(as a zero-padded truncation of length }m\text{)}.
\]
Consequently, the map $N\mapsto \pi_m(Z(N))$ defines a bijection from $\{0,1,\dots,F_{m+2}-1\}$ onto $X_m$, whose inverse is $w\mapsto V_m(w)$.
\end{lemma}

\textbf{Proof:}
If $Z(N)$ had a $1$ in position $m+1$, then $N\ge F_{m+2}$ (since the weight of that position is $F_{m+2}$ and the remaining terms are nonneg\-ative), contradicting $N\le F_{m+2}-1$. Hence $c_{m+1}=c_{m+2}=\cdots=0$, so the truncation loses no information and yields the stated bijection.
