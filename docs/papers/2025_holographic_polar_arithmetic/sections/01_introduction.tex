\section{Introduction: upgrading the coordinate system of arithmetic}
\label{sec:intro}

\noindent At the ontological layer, HPA is guided by a simple geometric stance: conservative rotation on a unit sphere is primary, while observable coordinates arise only through readout, i.e.\ a rigid cut that produces \emph{distinctions}.
In this view, ``dimension'' is not a pre-existing container but a measurement grammar: an orthogonal decomposition that maximizes distinguishability while necessarily discarding components that do not align with the chosen channels (Section~\ref{sec:interference} and Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_holographic_slice}).
The present paper develops this stance as a coordinate upgrade for arithmetic.

\subsection{Linear minimality and its structural side effects}
In the classical one-dimensional presentation, integers live on a line and the primitive generator is the successor map $n\mapsto n+1$. Additive structure is represented as translation, and multiplicative structure is typically introduced as iterated addition. This ``Cartesian'' minimality is effective for local linearization, but it exhibits systematic side effects in three directions.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Open endpoints.} The ``point at infinity'' is not part of the object domain. Limit procedures therefore develop endpoint pathologies (singularity and divergence) that are extrinsic to the algebraic objects being studied.
    \item \textbf{Flattening multiplicativity.} Prime factorization encodes multiplicative topology and spectral information. In a purely additive coordinate these become highly nonlocal, motivating auxiliary prescriptions (cutoffs and regularization) when one attempts analytic or spectral constructions.
    \item \textbf{Conflation of readout and ontology.} Using a single linear index to encode both ``time/scan'' (iteration count) and intrinsic multiplicative structure forces conflicts between phase conventions and multiplicative homomorphisms. For instance, a naive phase $\theta(n)=2\pi\alpha n$ is generally incompatible with multiplicative additivity $\theta(mn)\equiv\theta(m)+\theta(n)$.
\end{enumerate}

The goal of this paper is not to patch these issues within the linear coordinate, but to upgrade the coordinate system so that multiplication and phase become primitive, and ``time/scan'' is represented by an explicit unitary dynamics.

\subsection{Polar minimality: a generator and a canonical readout}
We make ``minimal complexity'' explicit as the following design criteria.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(C1) Compactification.} Replace open linear endpoints by a compact phase space (circle or torus), so that ``infinity'' becomes an internal boundary point of an extended object domain.
    \item \textbf{(C2) Anti-resonance.} Avoid short periodic lock-in; prefer irrational rotations that are difficult to approximate by rationals.
    \item \textbf{(C3) Binary readout.} The observational alphabet is minimized to $\{0,1\}$.
    \item \textbf{(C4) Canonical coordinates.} Discrete readouts should correspond to a unique, computable, locally constrained integer coding.
    \item \textbf{(C5) Single generator.} Use a single unitary scan generator rather than multi-parameter assembly.
\end{itemize}

Under these constraints, we adopt the skeleton ``irrational rotation + binary window readout''. For irrational $\alpha$ and interval windows, the resulting mechanical word is Sturmian and hence has minimal factor complexity $p(n)=n+1$ among aperiodic binary sequences; see~\cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}. The golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ is then distinguished because its continued fraction coefficients are all $1$. This choice is extremal for anti-resonance (it attains the optimal Hurwitz constant among irrational rotations; see e.g.~\cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}) and it yields the Fibonacci word together with the binary Zeckendorf/Ostrowski canonical encoding (Sections~\ref{sec:readout}--\ref{sec:zeckendorf}).

\subsection{Contributions and organization}
This paper constructs and organizes a closed-loop toolkit.

\begin{itemize}
    \item Section~\ref{sec:ontological_premise} states an ontological premise: a conservative monolith is accessed through orthogonal readout cuts, so observable coordinates are a low-dimensional bookkeeping of a higher-dimensional rotation.
    \item Sections~\ref{sec:skeleton}--\ref{sec:embedding} define a radial character $\rho$ and a multiplicative phase $\theta_\times$ from the multiplicative skeleton $M=(\NN_{>0},\cdot)$, and introduce the holographic polar embedding $\mathcal{Z}$.
    \item Section~\ref{sec:interference} reframes addition as a lossy geometric readout: we prove a no-go theorem for additive completeness, formalize lattice mismatch, and define a projection-induced notion of additive readout together with the associated quantum gap.
    \item Section~\ref{sec:theta} introduces the unitary scan operator $\Theta$ and formalizes time as iteration count; Appendix~\ref{sec:cyclic_calculus} develops the orbit-wise operators (difference, orbit Newton/Taylor expansion, trace, finite part) used for regularization statements.
    \item Sections~\ref{sec:readout}--\ref{sec:zeckendorf} define window readouts and derive Sturmian/Fibonacci structure, then connect to Ostrowski numeration and its golden degeneration to Zeckendorf decompositions. Appendix~\ref{sec:cut_project} and Appendix~\ref{sec:zeckendorf_shift} collect the cut-and-project geometry and the Zeckendorf-shift trace/zeta formulas.
    \item Appendix~\ref{sec:modules} packages reusable modules (singularity compactification, finite-part extraction, lattice path sums), and Sections~\ref{sec:discussion}--\ref{sec:conclusion} summarize limitations and testable intermediate claims.
\end{itemize}

\section{Ontological premise: geometric dimension reduction}
\label{sec:ontological_premise}
The technical construction in this paper can be read through a single ontological premise: the fundamental object is conservative and high-dimensional, while what we call ``coordinates'' are the result of a low-dimensional readout that carves distinctions by orthogonal cuts.

\paragraph{The monolith and norm as conservation bookkeeping.}
Let $\hilbert$ be a Hilbert space and let $|\Psi\rangle\in\hilbert$ be a unit-norm state, $\|\Psi\|=1$.
Unitarity preserves this norm, so the primitive kinematics is rotation on a unit sphere in $\hilbert$.
In this sense, the monolith is not a vector of observed numbers; it is the invariant container from which observed numbers are extracted.

\paragraph{Orthogonal readout and a Pythagorean principle.}
A readout chooses a closed readout subspace $\hilbert_{\mathrm{R}}\subset\hilbert$ together with the orthogonal projector $P_{\mathrm{R}}:\hilbert\to\hilbert_{\mathrm{R}}$.
Choosing an orthonormal basis $\{|e_i\rangle\}$ of $\hilbert_{\mathrm{R}}$, the coefficients $c_i:=\langle e_i|\Psi\rangle$ satisfy
$$
\|P_{\mathrm{R}}\Psi\|^2=\sum_i |c_i|^2\le \|\Psi\|^2.
$$
Equivalently, every orthogonal cut induces the Hilbert-space Pythagorean law
$$
\|\Psi\|^2=\|P_{\mathrm{R}}\Psi\|^2+\|(I-P_{\mathrm{R}})\Psi\|^2,
$$
so conservation of the whole is expressed as a sum of an observed orthogonal component and an orthogonal remainder.
This identity is a geometric fact rather than an additional dynamical hypothesis, and it makes precise why distinguishability is naturally tied to orthogonality (Subsection~\ref{subsec:orthogonality_price_of_readout}).
In the minimal scan model, the window operator $\Pi_W$ is an orthogonal projector on $L^2(S^1)$ and binary readout arises as a sharp localization limit of a projection measurement (Remark~\ref{rem:omega_choice}).

\paragraph{Dimension as a readout grammar.}
From this viewpoint, a ``dimension'' is an orthogonal channel introduced to separate distinctions.
Non-orthogonal channels mix information; orthogonal channels maximize separation.
The price is that every concrete coordinate system is a cut: it discards structure that does not align with the chosen orthogonal grammar (Section~\ref{sec:interference} and Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_holographic_slice}).

\paragraph{Arithmetic as readout: from multiplicative ontology to additive bookkeeping.}
HPA implements this premise in an arithmetic setting.
The multiplicative skeleton (Section~\ref{sec:skeleton}) is embedded into a rotational phase geometry (Section~\ref{sec:embedding}), but Theorem~\ref{thm:no_go_additive} shows that classical linear addition cannot be preserved simultaneously with multiplicativity.
Therefore, addition must appear as a readout/projection (Section~\ref{sec:interference}), and the quantum gap $\delta$ is the residual left behind by forcing smooth synthesis into discrete coordinates (Figure~\ref{fig:hpa_holographic_slice} and Remark~\ref{rem:observation_cut}).

\paragraph{Time as access and incompatible cuts.}
The unitary scan operator $\Theta$ (Section~\ref{sec:theta}) supplies a minimal access order: time is iteration count.
Together with the phase operator $V$ it forms a Weyl pair, so incompatible readouts are structural (Theorem~\ref{thm:weyl_uncertainty}).
In this sense, uncertainty is the geometric price of insisting on maximal distinction within a conservative rotation.
