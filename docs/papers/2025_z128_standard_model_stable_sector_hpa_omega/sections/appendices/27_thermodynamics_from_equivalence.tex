\subsection{Thermodynamics from equivalence: entropy, temperature, and entropic force (frequency-first)}
\label{app:thermodynamics_from_equivalence}

This appendix closes a thermodynamic dictionary compatible with the tick\,+\,CAP spine and the equivalence semantics of Section~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}.
The goal is not to import thermodynamics as an independent axiom set, but to record how the standard thermodynamic notions can be treated as \emph{derived} from:
(i) finite observability (many-to-one coarse graining) and
(ii) CAP selection of minimal-cost macroscopic representatives.

\subsubsection{Entropy as coarse-grained state counting}
\label{subsec:entropy_as_counting}

\paragraph{Macrostate as an equivalence class.}
\InterfaceTag Fix a coarse-graining map $\mathsf{C}$ from microscopic protocol data to macroscopic descriptors.
Two microdescriptions are macroscopically equivalent if they map to the same coarse descriptor.
This is the semantic content of the coarse-graining equivalence in Section~\ref{subsec:equivalence_relations_minimal}.

\paragraph{Counting entropy (finite).}
\InterfaceTag For a macrostate $M$ with microstate set $\Gamma(M)$, define the counting entropy
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:counting_entropy}
S(M):=\log|\Gamma(M)|,
\end{equation}
optionally scaled to physical units by $k_B$.
At fixed window length $m$, the simplest instance is the fiber-count entropy $S_{\mathrm{fib}}(w)=\log|P(w)|$ for a stable type $w\in X_m$ (Appendix~\ref{subsec:entropy_state_counting}).

\begin{proposition}[Mean fiber entropy and mean degeneracy]\label{prop:mean_fiber_entropy_vs_dm}
\MathTag Fix a window length $m$ and let $P(w)=\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(w)$ be the microstate fibers of the folding quotient (Section~\ref{app:equivalence_semantics}).
Define the mean degeneracy $d_m:=2^m/|X_m|$ as in Appendix~\ref{app:cosmology_resolution_flow}.
Then the uniform mean fiber-count entropy satisfies
\[
\frac{1}{|X_m|}\sum_{w\in X_m} S_{\mathrm{fib}}(w)\ \le\ \log d_m\ =\ -\log f_{\mathrm{stab}}(m),
\]
where $f_{\mathrm{stab}}(m)=|X_m|/2^m$ is the stable fraction.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is Lemma~\ref{lem:mean_degeneracy_fiber_entropy_bounds} together with $S_{\mathrm{fib}}(w)=\log|P(w)|$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Boundary/channel entropy.}
\InterfaceTag In boundary-screen settings (black-hole or holographic cuts), the same idea is channel capacity:
if $\mathcal{N}_\partial$ is the maximum number of distinguishable boundary outcomes at a given resolution, then $S=k_B\log\mathcal{N}_\partial$ (Appendix~\ref{app:bh_wormholes_pointer}).

\subsubsection{Energy and temperature as frequency dictionaries}
\label{subsec:energy_temperature_frequency}

\paragraph{Energy as frequency (ratio-first).}
\InterfaceTag In the tick-first stance, a primary dimensionless quantity is a frequency ratio.
Mass/energy scales are therefore organized by the log-frequency coordinate $r(\mu)=\log_\varphi(\mu/m_e)$ (Section~\ref{subsec:mass_resolution_coordinate} and Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}).
In matching form one may use $E=\hbar\omega$ and $E=mc^2$ (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}), but the present program prefers ratios where constants cancel.

\paragraph{Temperature as a frequency scale.}
\InterfaceTag Temperature is the natural ``frequency scale'' of coarse excitations:
in thermal field theory one has Matsubara frequencies $\omega_n=2\pi n k_BT/\hbar$, and in classical statistical mechanics $k_BT$ sets typical energy increments.
Accordingly, in the frequency-first dictionary we treat $T$ as the scale conjugate to entropy, with the $k_B$ and $\hbar$ conversion treated as matching-layer calibration.

\begin{definition}[Thermodynamic temperature (conjugate definition)]
\label{def:temperature_conjugate}
Let $E(M)$ be an effective energy functional of macrostates and $S(M)$ an entropy functional.
In regimes where $E$ can be treated as a function of $S$ along a one-parameter family, define temperature by
\[
\frac{1}{T}:=\frac{\partial S}{\partial E}.
\]
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{Coarse-graining channels and entropy functionals (auditable operator form)}
\label{subsec:thermo_channels_entropy_functionals}

\paragraph{Coarse graining as a many-to-one channel.}
\InterfaceTag In the present paper, the words ``coarse graining'', ``forgetting'', and ``finite observability'' are operationally represented by a map that reduces distinguishability.
At the finite level this is a many-to-one map on microdescriptions, but for probabilistic or quantum descriptions it is more convenient to record it as a channel.

\begin{definition}[Classical coarse-graining channel (finite)]\label{def:z128_classical_channel}
\MathTag Let $\Omega$ and $\mathcal{Y}$ be finite sets.
A \emph{(classical) channel} is a Markov kernel $K:\Omega\to\Delta(\mathcal{Y})$, written $K(y|x)$ with $K(y|x)\ge 0$ and $\sum_{y\in\mathcal{Y}}K(y|x)=1$ for all $x\in\Omega$.
It maps an input distribution $p\in\Delta(\Omega)$ to an output distribution $q\in\Delta(\mathcal{Y})$ by
\[
q(y)=(Kp)(y):=\sum_{x\in\Omega}K(y|x)\,p(x).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quantum coarse-graining channel (CPTP)]\label{def:z128_quantum_channel_thermo}
\MathTag A \emph{quantum channel} is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map $\Phi:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:quantum_channels_cptp_stinespring}, Definition~\ref{def:z128_cptp_channel}).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Entropy functionals: counting vs information-theoretic.}
\InterfaceTag The counting entropy \eqref{eq:counting_entropy} is a coarse state-counting dictionary on equivalence classes (macrostates).
For monotonicity/irreversibility statements under channels, a more robust object is \emph{relative entropy} (classical or quantum), which is defined on probability states and contracts under coarse graining.

\begin{definition}[Classical relative entropy (finite)]\label{def:z128_classical_relative_entropy}
\MathTag For distributions $p,q\in\Delta(\Omega)$ with $\mathrm{supp}(p)\subseteq \mathrm{supp}(q)$, define
\[
D(p\Vert q):=\sum_{x\in\Omega} p(x)\log\frac{p(x)}{q(x)}.
\]
If $\mathrm{supp}(p)\not\subseteq \mathrm{supp}(q)$ set $D(p\Vert q)=+\infty$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Quantum relative entropy (Umegaki)]\label{def:z128_quantum_relative_entropy}
\MathTag For density matrices $\rho,\sigma$ on a finite-dimensional $\mathcal{H}$ with $\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\subseteq \mathrm{supp}(\sigma)$, define
\[
D(\rho\Vert\sigma):=\Tr\!\bigl(\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\bigr).
\]
If $\mathrm{supp}(\rho)\not\subseteq \mathrm{supp}(\sigma)$ set $D(\rho\Vert\sigma)=+\infty$.
\end{definition}

\subsubsection{CAP as a free-energy principle}
\label{subsec:cap_free_energy}

\paragraph{Free energy as CAP objective.}
\InterfaceTag A standard thermodynamic closure defines a free energy functional
\[
\mathcal{F}(M):=E(M)-T\,S(M),
\]
and asserts that equilibria minimize $\mathcal{F}$ subject to constraints.
In the present program, this is read as a CAP closure:
among all coarse representatives compatible with the protocol constraints, the realized representative minimizes a total cost that includes (i) mismatch/overhead and (ii) residual uncertainty.
For the standard transfer-operator/pressure viewpoint that turns such variational selection into spectral/analytic stability statements, see Appendix~\ref{app:thermodynamic_formalism_pressure}.

\begin{proposition}[CAP free-energy closure (audit form)]
\label{prop:cap_free_energy_closure}
Fix a finite coarse-graining family $\{\mathsf{C}_\theta:\theta\in\Theta(B)\}$ and a finite parameter family of macroscopic representatives $M(\theta)$.
Define an objective of the form
\[
J(\theta)=\kappa\,E(M(\theta))+\kappa_S\,(-S(M(\theta)))+\kappa_c\,\mathrm{Comp}(\theta),
\]
with a deterministic tie-break key as in Appendix~\ref{app:cap_audit_template}.
Then CAP selects a unique minimizer $\theta_B$ and therefore a unique macroscopic representative $M(\theta_B)$ within the declared finite family.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
\InterfaceTag The minus sign in $-S$ reflects the conventional role of entropy as a stabilizing/typicality factor in equilibrium selection.
Whether one writes the objective as $E-TS$ or as a weighted sum depends on which quantities are treated as fixed constraints and which as adjustable parameters; the audit requirement is only that the candidate family is finite and the tie-break is explicit.

\paragraph{CAP weights and the inverse-temperature scale.}
\InterfaceTag When $\kappa_S>0$, the CAP objective in Proposition~\ref{prop:cap_free_energy_closure} can be rewritten in the dimensionless free-energy form
\[
\frac{1}{\kappa_S}J(\theta)=\beta_{\mathrm{CAP}}\,E(M(\theta)) - S(M(\theta)) + \frac{\kappa_c}{\kappa_S}\,\mathrm{Comp}(\theta),
\qquad
\beta_{\mathrm{CAP}}:=\frac{\kappa}{\kappa_S}.
\]
Thus $\beta_{\mathrm{CAP}}$ plays the role of an \emph{inverse-temperature scale} in the internal dimensionless dictionary.
To interpret this scale in conventional thermodynamic units one must additionally fix the energy and entropy unit conventions (Appendix~\ref{app:physics_consensus_inputs}, Assumption~\ref{ass:pdr_entropy_energy_temperature_units}), i.e.\ whether $\beta_{\mathrm{CAP}}$ corresponds to $1/T$ or to $1/(k_B T)$, and whether $E$ is recorded in frequency units ($E=\omega$) or in energy units ($E_{\mathrm{phys}}=\hbar\omega$).

\subsubsection{The three laws (protocol reading)}
\label{subsec:three_laws_protocol}

\paragraph{First law (bookkeeping identity).}
\InterfaceTag Once an energy functional $E$ is fixed at the coarse level, the first law is a bookkeeping identity for how $E$ changes under (i) changes of macrostate multiplicity (heat) and (ii) changes of external constraints (work).
In a frequency-first description, the ``internal energy'' is a frequency/clock-rate functional, and work corresponds to controlled changes in constraints that shift that functional.

\paragraph{Second law (monotonicity under coarse graining).}
\InterfaceTag Irreversibility does not require a new axiom here: it is the generic signature of many-to-one coarse graining.
At the theorem level, the paper uses \emph{contractive distinguishability/relative-entropy certificates} for channels (Definitions~\ref{def:z128_classical_channel}--\ref{def:z128_quantum_relative_entropy}), which are monotone under coarse graining and therefore provide an auditable ``second-law'' backbone.
Counting entropy \eqref{eq:counting_entropy} is still the canonical state-counting dictionary on macrostates, but its monotonicity depends on the declared coarse-graining preorder.
CAP additionally selects representatives of minimal cost, giving a Lyapunov-style monotonicity for a declared finite-family objective (Proposition~\ref{prop:cap_free_energy_closure}).

\subsubsection{Second law as data processing: auditable monotonicity certificates}
\label{subsec:second_law_data_processing}

\begin{theorem}[Classical data processing inequality (finite)]
\label{thm:z128_classical_dpi}
\MathTag Let $K$ be a classical channel as in Definition~\ref{def:z128_classical_channel}.
Then for any distributions $p,q\in\Delta(\Omega)$ one has
\[
D(Kp\Vert Kq)\le D(p\Vert q).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is standard; see, e.g., \cite{CoverThomas2006}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Quantum data processing inequality (finite-dimensional)]
\label{thm:z128_quantum_dpi}
\MathTag Let $\Phi$ be a CPTP map as in Definition~\ref{def:z128_quantum_channel_thermo}.
Then for any density matrices $\rho,\sigma$ one has
\[
D(\Phi(\rho)\Vert \Phi(\sigma))\le D(\rho\Vert \sigma).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Reference]
This is standard (monotonicity of Umegaki relative entropy under CPTP maps); see, e.g., \cite{NielsenChuang2000}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[From data processing to ``thermodynamic'' monotonicity statements]
\label{rem:z128_second_law_reading_dpi}
\InterfaceTag The DPI theorems above express the minimal irreversibility content: \emph{coarse graining cannot increase distinguishability}.
To specialize this to a familiar thermodynamic statement (e.g.\ monotonic decay of a free-energy functional), one must (i) declare which entropy functional is used (counting/Shannon/von Neumann) and (ii) declare the comparison class or reference state (e.g.\ a Gibbs family) that defines the free-energy target.
Those additional identifications are interface/matching choices in this paper and must be explicitly scoped when used.
\end{remark}


\paragraph{Third law (stability floor and unattainability).}
\InterfaceTag In protocol terms, the third law is a statement about the existence of a minimal stable background (a reference macrostate) and about the cost of reaching lower-entropy configurations:
as $T\to 0$ (frequency scale of fluctuations collapses), the reachable macrostates shrink toward a stable sector whose residual entropy is determined by the remaining coarse-grained degeneracy (e.g.\ fiber multiplicities at the anchor).
In the CAP language, reaching strictly zero entropy would require eliminating all residual equivalence-class multiplicity, which generally requires unbounded resources/resolution.

\subsubsection{Entropic force and the gravity/delay dictionary}
\label{subsec:entropic_force_gravity}

\paragraph{Entropic force as response.}
\InterfaceTag Given a free energy (or any CAP objective) depending on a position-like parameter $x$,
force is defined as the response
\[
F(x)=-\nabla_x \mathcal{F}(x).
\]
If $E$ is approximately constant while $S$ varies with $x$, this yields the entropic-force form
\[
F(x)\approx T\,\nabla_x S(x).
\]

\paragraph{Relation to lapse and delay.}
\InterfaceTag The delay/lapse dictionary (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}) identifies overhead with clock-rate slowdown,
so spatial variation of overhead induces spatial variation of local clock frequency.
In the weak-field limit of Section~\ref{subsec:weak_field_poisson}, the same variation is encoded by $g_{00}\approx -(1+2\phi)$ and therefore by a potential $\phi$.
Thus, in a frequency-first language, ``gravitational force'' is equivalently the response of frequency/clock-rate structure to spatial displacement, consistent with the response definition above.
