\section{Notation and Preliminaries}\label{sec:preliminaries}

This section merely fixes the globally used notation, object hierarchy, and minimal input caliber for frequency statements; no new mathematical claims are introduced unless explicitly indicated.

\subsection{Writing Conventions (Separation of Proof Chains and Explanatory Text)}\label{subsec:writing-conventions}
The auditable proof chain of this paper consists solely of numbered definitions/lemmas/theorems/propositions/corollaries and their proofs; explanatory paragraphs serve only to locate semantic interfaces and motivations, and must not serve as premises in any subsequent reasoning. Whenever external results are invoked, they are cited with references, and their applicable conditions and the form used are stated at the point of use.

\subsection{Basic Notation and Object Hierarchy}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Number fields and sets:} \(\NN,\ZZ,\QQ,\RR,\CC\) denote the natural numbers, integers, rationals, reals, and complex numbers, respectively.
  \item \textbf{Time and iteration:} Time is the iteration count \(t\in\ZZ\) (or \(t\in\NN\)), and \(T^t\) denotes the \(t\)-th iterate of the singly generated action.
  \item \textbf{Readout and window:} Given a phase space \(X\) and a measurable window \(W\subseteq X\), the binary readout is \(s_t(x)=\ind_W(T^t x)\in\{0,1\}\).
  \item \textbf{Window microstates:} The window microstate space of length \(m\) is \(\Omega_m=\{0,1\}^m\).
  \item \textbf{Stable grammar and folding:} The stable word space is denoted \(X_m\subseteq\Omega_m\), and the folding \(\Fold_m:\Omega_m\to X_m\) is constructed in Section \ref{sec:folding} with an auditable implementation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Terminological Conventions: Stable Concepts and Stable Types}
Stable concepts and stable types are used at the event level and type level respectively: a stable concept is a measurable event in the finite-window event \(\sigma\)-algebra (Definition \ref{def:stable-concept}); a stable type is an element of the folded symbol space \(X_m\) (its construction with \(\Fold_m\) is given in Section \ref{sec:folding}).

\subsection{Setting for Frequency Statements}\label{subsec:freq-setting}
When ``limiting frequency/invariant states'' are involved, we fix the existence of a probability space \((X,\mathcal{B},\mu)\) and a measure-preserving transformation \(T\), with a measurable window \(W\in\mathcal{B}\) generating the readout \(s_t(x)=\ind_W(T^t x)\). If conclusions are to be independent of the initial value, we adopt unique ergodicity and impose the boundary zero-measure condition \(\mu(\partial W)=0\) on the indicator window (see Remark \ref{rem:unique-ergodic-indicator}).

\subsection{Ergodicity, Frequency, and Stable Concepts}\label{subsec:prelim-ergodic-frequency}
For any event \(A\in\mathcal{B}\) and initial value \(x\in X\), the orbit frequency is defined as
\[
\mathrm{freq}_N(A;x)=\frac1N\sum_{t=0}^{N-1}\ind_A(T^t x).
\]
When \(T\) is ergodic, the Birkhoff ergodic theorem gives \(\mathrm{freq}_N(A;x)\to\mu(A)\) for \(\mu\)-almost every \(x\) (\cite{WaltersErgodicTheory}).

\begin{remark}[Indicator function under unique ergodicity]\label{rem:unique-ergodic-indicator}
Under unique ergodicity, if \(\mu(\partial W)=0\), then for the indicator readout \(f=\ind_W\) one also obtains a frequency conclusion independent of the initial value; see \cite{WaltersErgodicTheory}.
\end{remark}

Let \(\mathcal{F}_m\) be the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by window events of length at most \(m\) (equivalently, generated by the cylinder sets of \(\Omega_m\)).
\begin{definition}[Statistically stable concept]\label{def:stable-concept}
An event \(A\in\mathcal{F}_m\) is called a stable concept at resolution \(m\) if there exists \(p(A)\in[0,1]\) such that for almost every \(x\),
\[
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathrm{freq}_N(A;x)=p(A).
\]
Under unique ergodicity, \(p(A)=\mu(A)\) and is independent of \(x\).
\end{definition}
