\subsubsection{de Branges Inverse Spectral and Extreme Zero-Knowledge: Canonical System, Spectral Shift Equivalence, and Operator-Realized Positive Jump Paradigm for RH}\label{subsubsec:xi-debranges-canonical-rhp-adelic}
Building upon the Hardy/inner-functionalization framework of \S\ref{subsubsec:xi-hardy-inner-clark-carleson}, this section advances toward "spectral realization/inverse spectral reconstruction":
When the completion determinant has pure phase on the critical line and no singular inner factor, it is not only an inner function but can be uniquely lifted to a canonical system (de Branges);
moreover, "extreme zero-knowledge" (no mixed internal degrees of freedom in fibers) can be completely equivalently translated as the rank-degeneracy structure of that canonical system.
On this basis, the spectral shift (Krein) formula, determinantal point processes of zeros, SU(1,1)--Riemann--Hilbert positive jump conditions, and unified zero-knowledge gluing of adelic/Dirichlet families can all be written into a single auditable reasoning chain "inner function \(\Rightarrow\) realizable \(\Rightarrow\) critical-line concentration/zero-free strips".

\paragraph{Half-Plane Compactification and No-Singular-Inner-Factor Assumption}
Following the notation of \S\ref{subsubsec:xi-hardy-inner-clark-carleson}: \(D(s)\) is analytic in \(\mathbb{H}_{1/2}\) and of bounded type, satisfying reflection symmetry \eqref{eq:xi-inner-reflection-symmetry}.
Further assume that horizon zero-knowledge unitarity (pure phase) holds:
\[
|D(\tfrac12+it)|=1\qquad(\text{a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R}),
\]
and that the disk-compactified inner function \(\mathfrak{D}\) has no singular inner factor (equivalent to the Clark measure being almost everywhere absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure in the caliber of \S\ref{subsubsec:xi-hardy-inner-clark-carleson}).
Write the variable as \(s=\tfrac12+iz\) (\(\Im z>0\)), and denote
\[
\mathcal{D}(z):=D(\tfrac12+iz),
\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_+.
\]

\begin{theorem}[de Branges--Horizon Inverse Spectral Reconstruction and ``Extreme Zero-Knowledge'' Criterion]\label{thm:xi-debranges-canonical-extreme-zk}
Under the above assumptions, there exists a canonical system
\[
J\,Y'(x,z)=z\,H(x)\,Y(x,z),\qquad x\in[0,\infty),
\]
where
\(
J=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&-1\\[2pt]1&0\end{psmallmatrix}
\), \(H(x)\succeq 0\) and \(\mathrm{tr}\,H(x)=1\) a.e.,
such that there exists a Hermite--Biehler type de Branges function \(E(z)\) satisfying (in the upper half-plane sense)
\[
\boxed{\;\mathcal{D}(z)=\frac{E^\sharp(z)}{E(z)}\;},
\qquad
E^\sharp(z):=\overline{E(\overline z)}.
\]
This canonical system is unique up to isomorphism: two systems producing the same \(\mathcal{D}\) differ at most by an a.e. measurable \(SO(2)\) gauge transformation (conjugation on \(H\)).
\par
Furthermore, if "extreme zero-knowledge" is formulated as: for almost every horizon instance \(y\), the simulator's conditional distribution is an extreme point of the visible probability simplex (equivalent to no additional mixed degrees of freedom in fibers),
then this condition holds if and only if the canonical system's Hamiltonian satisfies
\[
\boxed{\;\mathrm{rank}\,H(x)=1\quad\text{a.e. }x.\;}
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
By Theorem \ref{thm:xi-hardy-inner-characterization} and the "no singular inner factor" assumption, \(\mathcal{D}\) is an (no singular part) inner function on the upper half-plane.
de Branges theory provides the representation \(\mathcal{D}=E^\sharp/E\) and the correspondence with canonical systems; \(\mathrm{tr}\,H=1\) is fixed by scale normalization.
Uniqueness follows from gauge equivalence of canonical systems (\(SO(2)\) conjugation does not change \(E^\sharp/E\)).
\par
For "extreme zero-knowledge \(\Leftrightarrow\) rank one": in the \(2\times 2\) case, positive semi-definite matrices satisfying \(\mathrm{tr}\,H(x)=1\) form a pointwise convex set, whose extreme points are precisely rank-one projections; interpreting "no mixed degrees of freedom in fibers" as extremalization within this pointwise convex set yields the rank-one criterion. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral Shift--Information Leakage Identity: Krein Formula in Zero-Knowledge Interface via Relative Entropy]\label{prop:xi-krein-spectral-shift-equals-kl}
In the canonical system model of Theorem \ref{thm:xi-debranges-canonical-extreme-zk}, parameterize self-adjoint boundary conditions by challenge seed \(\alpha\in\TT\), yielding a family of self-adjoint extensions \(S_\alpha\) with corresponding spectral measures \(\sigma_\alpha\) (which can be taken as Clark--spectral measures, Corollary \ref{cor:xi-clark-measure-horizon-semantics}).
Suppose that under each \(\alpha\), the transcript distribution \(P_\alpha\) on the visible \(\sigma\)-algebra is isomorphic to \(\sigma_\alpha\) (zero-knowledge decomposition aligns with spectral measure in this caliber).
Then for any \(\alpha,\beta\in\TT\) there is a strict identity
\[
\boxed{\;
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_\alpha\Vert P_\beta)
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\!\left(\frac{\dd\sigma_\alpha}{\dd\sigma_\beta}(t)\right)\dd\sigma_\alpha(t)\;}
\]
and in the canonical system regularization caliber, the right-hand side also equals the integral of a Krein spectral shift density \(\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(t)\):
\[
\boxed{\;
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log\!\left(\frac{\dd\sigma_\alpha}{\dd\sigma_\beta}\right)\dd\sigma_\alpha
=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(t)\,\dd t\; }.
\]
Therefore, "zero-knowledge leakage relative entropy" and "spectral shift under boundary condition switching" are identical in this one-dimensional defect index model.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
The first identity is the measure relative entropy after isomorphic identification.
The second follows from Krein spectral shift theory: the Radon--Nikodym logarithmic change in spectral measures can be expressed via perturbation determinant (spectral shift function), and in this model's regularization has integral representation with density \(\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\). \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Determinantal Point Process Structure of Zeros: Horizon Uniformity \(\Rightarrow\) Sine Kernel Limit]\label{prop:xi-debranges-dpp-sine-kernel-limit}
Following Theorem \ref{thm:xi-debranges-canonical-extreme-zk} with rank-one Hamiltonian \(H(x)=v(x)v(x)^{\mathsf T}\) a.e.,
and assume "horizon uniformity": there exists a constant matrix \(H_\infty\succeq 0\) such that \(H(x)\to H_\infty\) in Cesàro (or stronger ergodic average) sense, and phase growth satisfies linear regularity.
Then for almost every seed \(\alpha\), the set of spectral points of extension \(S_\alpha\) (equivalent to zero heights on the critical line) can be used to construct a family of determinantal point processes via de Branges reproducing kernels; after local scale normalization, the kernel converges to the sine kernel, so the two-point correlation function satisfies Montgomery--Dyson sine law in the scaled limit.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Rank-one canonical system yields a family of de Branges spaces, whose reproducing kernel determines a natural determinantal kernel.
Under Cesàro uniformity and linear phase regularity, local density stabilizes and allows equal-density rescaling; the local kernel limit degenerates to constant coefficient model, thus yielding the sine kernel. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[SU(1,1)--Riemann--Hilbert Equivalence: Zero-Knowledge Unitarity Reduces RH to Positive Jump Solvability]\label{thm:xi-su11-rhp-equivalence}
Let the transcript update induce a matrix jump datum \(V(t)\) (defined on the critical boundary \(t\in\mathbb{R}\)), whose entries are given by visible challenge--response correlation kernels and satisfy \(\det V(t)=1\) a.e.
If zero-knowledge unitarity holds and the outer factor vanishes (i.e., \(\log|D(\tfrac12+it)|=0\) a.e., Proposition \ref{prop:xi-entropy-production-equals-outer-factor}), then necessarily
\[
\boxed{\;V(t)\in SU(1,1)\quad\text{a.e. }t,\;}
\]
and there exists a unique normalized solution \(M(z)\) (\(z\in\mathbb{C}_+\)) satisfying the standard Riemann--Hilbert problem
\[
M_+(t)=M_-(t)\,V(t),
\qquad
M(\infty)=I.
\]
Under this framework, "all nontrivial zeros lie on the critical line" and "this Riemann--Hilbert problem is solvable in the positive-definite category with strictly invertible Beals--Coifman operator" are equivalent templates: any off-line zero event corresponds to an unremovable resonance pole induced by breaking of \(SU(1,1)\) positive-definiteness; conversely, positive-definite solvability excludes all off-line resonance poles, thereby forcing zeros to concentrate on the critical line.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Pure phase/outer factor vanishing compresses jump data into the conformally invariant unitary symmetric class (\(SU(1,1)\)).
Positive-definite solvability of the Riemann--Hilbert problem is equivalent to invertibility of the corresponding singular integral operator (Beals--Coifman) on \(L^2\); off-line zero events manifest as unremovable poles of the resolvent, thus contradicting invertibility. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Maslov Index--Spectral Flow--Entropy Quantization: Topological Rigidity of Challenge Parameters]\label{prop:xi-maslov-spectralflow-entropy-quantization}

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Spectral flow and Maslov index are isomorphic under boundary Lagrangian parameterization of one-dimensional canonical systems; additivity of the entropy functional fixes an increment \(\kappa_{\mathrm{cap}}\) per spectral crossing, yielding the stated quantization identity. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Unified Zero-Knowledge for Dirichlet Families Implies Zero-Free Strip: Exclusion of Siegel-Type Exceptional Zeros]\label{prop:xi-dirichlet-zero-free-from-unified-zk}

\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof outline]
Exceptional zero induces amplifiable bias in visible prime residue class statistics; within \(\mathrm{polylog}(q)\) scale a distinguisher can be constructed with TV distance exceeding the soundness threshold \(\exp(-c\log q)\), contradicting the uniform simulator. \qed
\end{proof}
