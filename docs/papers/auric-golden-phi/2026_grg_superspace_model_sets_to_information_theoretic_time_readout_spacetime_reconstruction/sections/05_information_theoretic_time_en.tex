\section{Information-theoretic time from refinement}\label{sec:info-time}

The protocol layer provides a discrete scan index $t\in\NN$, but that index should not be conflated with physical time. In geometry-first reconstruction programs, it is natural to seek a notion of time that arises from \emph{information acquisition}: as an observer conditions on longer observation prefixes, the set of internal states compatible with the observations shrinks. This refinement process can be quantified by entropy rates and, under ergodic hypotheses, yields an intrinsic ``arrow''.

\subsection{Cylinder sets and posterior refinement}\label{sec:info-time:cylinders}

Let $(H,\mathcal{B},\mu)$ be a probability space supporting the scan dynamics (e.g.\ a torus rotation factor), and let $R_\Theta:H\to\mathcal{A}$ be a measurable readout (Definition~\ref{def:readout}). For a length-$t$ observation prefix $a_{0:t-1}=(a_0,\dots,a_{t-1})$, define the corresponding cylinder set in $H$:
\[
C(a_{0:t-1}) := \{h\in H:\ R_\Theta(h_k)=a_k\ \text{for }k=0,\dots,t-1\},
\]
where $h_k$ denotes the internal state at scan step $k$.
The posterior probability of this cylinder is $\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$, and refinement corresponds to decreasing $\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ as $t$ grows for typical sequences.

\subsubsection{Filtrations and posterior measures}
Let $A_t:=R_\Theta(T^t h)$ be the symbol process on $(H,\mu,T)$ and define the information filtration
\[
\mathcal{F}_t:=\sigma(A_0,\dots,A_{t-1}).
\]
For a realized prefix $a_{0:t-1}$, the posterior is the conditional measure $\mu(\cdot\,|\,\mathcal{F}_t)(a_{0:t-1})$, supported on the cylinder $C(a_{0:t-1})$. Thus the quantity $\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ is the protocol-auditable posterior mass of the compatibility set at refinement depth $t$.

\subsection{Entropy rate and the SMB theorem}\label{sec:info-time:smb}

Assume the scan dynamics is measure-preserving and ergodic, and the readout corresponds to a finite measurable partition $\mathcal{P}$ of $H$ (induced by $R_\Theta$). Let $\mathcal{P}^{(t)} := \bigvee_{k=0}^{t-1} T^{-k}\mathcal{P}$ be the join partition. The Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem states that for $\mu$-almost every $h\in H$,
\[
-\frac{1}{t}\log \mu\bigl(\mathcal{P}^{(t)}(h)\bigr)\ \longrightarrow\ h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P}),
\]
where $h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})$ is the entropy rate of the partition, and the supremum over generating partitions yields the Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy $h_\mu(T)$ \cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983,KatokHasselblatt1995}.

In the present context, $\mathcal{P}^{(t)}(h)$ is precisely the cylinder set determined by the first $t$ readout symbols produced from $h$. Thus the SMB theorem identifies an almost-sure exponential contraction rate of posterior cylinders.

\subsection{Generating partitions and KS entropy}\label{sec:info-time:ks}

The above statements depend on the choice of partition $\mathcal{P}$. The Kolmogorov--Sinai entropy is defined as
\[
h_\mu(T):=\sup_{\mathcal{P}} h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P}),
\]
where the supremum ranges over finite measurable partitions. If $\mathcal{P}$ is \emph{generating}, then $h_\mu(T)=h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})$ \cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983,KatokHasselblatt1995}. In readout language, ``generating'' means that the symbolic stream separates almost all internal states up to null sets when observed for infinite time; for finite-resolution protocols this is a strong assumption, and in practice one often works with \emph{approximately generating} partitions.

\subsubsection{Ergodicity, generating readouts, and what ``entropy-rate time'' actually depends on}\label{sec:info-time:generating-meaning}

\begin{definition}[Generating partition (measure-theoretic)]\label{def:generating-partition}
Let $(H,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)$ be a measure-preserving system and let $\mathcal{P}$ be a finite measurable partition of $H$.
Write
\[
\mathcal{P}^{(\infty)}:=\bigvee_{t\ge 0} T^{-t}\mathcal{P}
\]
for the \(\sigma\)-algebra generated by all time-shifts of \(\mathcal{P}\).
We say that \(\mathcal{P}\) is \emph{generating (mod \(\mu\))} if
\[
\mathcal{P}^{(\infty)}=\mathcal{B}\qquad (\mathrm{mod}\ \mu),
\]
i.e.\ every \(B\in\mathcal{B}\) agrees up to a \(\mu\)-null set with some \(B'\in\mathcal{P}^{(\infty)}\).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Protocol dependence vs.\ KS invariance]\label{rem:generating-consequence}
The SMB limit in Section~\ref{sec:info-time:smb} is a pointwise law for the \emph{chosen} protocol partition \(\mathcal{P}\): it identifies the almost-sure exponential decay rate of posterior cylinders \(\mu(\mathcal{P}^{(t)}(h))\).
The corresponding slope is \(h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})\), which depends on the readout parameters \(\Theta\) through \(\mathcal{P}\).
Only when \(\mathcal{P}\) is generating (Definition~\ref{def:generating-partition}) does one recover the system invariant \(h_\mu(T)\).
This is the precise sense in which ``entropy-rate time'' depends on ergodicity (to make the limit almost sure) and on the (approximately) generating character of the readout partition (to compare to KS).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Readout partitions define factors; they do not modify the base dynamics]\label{rem:partition-does-not-change-dynamics}
Changing the readout \(R_\Theta\) (hence changing \(\mathcal{P}\)) does not change the underlying scan dynamics \((H,\mu,T)\): the transformation \(T\) and invariant measure \(\mu\) are part of the object layer.
What changes is the \emph{observable} \(\sigma\)-algebra and thus the induced symbolic factor process \(A_t=R_\Theta(T^t h)\).
Equivalently, choosing \(\mathcal{P}\) chooses a measurable factor of \((H,\mu,T)\); different readouts correspond to different factors, and SMB/entropy-rate time quantifies refinement \emph{within that chosen factor}.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Generating readout (explicit hypothesis)]\label{assump:generating-readout}
When we compare protocols at the level of KS entropy (rather than at the level of a fixed readout partition), we assume the readout partition \(\mathcal{P}\) induced by \(R_\Theta\) is generating in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:generating-partition}.
Equivalently, the full readout \(\sigma\)-algebra \(\bigvee_{t\ge 0}\sigma(A_t)\) separates \(\mu\)-almost all internal states.
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[A concrete criterion: orbit separation]\label{prop:generator-orbit-separation}
Assume $(H,\mathcal{B},\mu,T)$ is an invertible measure-preserving system and $\mathcal{P}$ is a finite measurable partition with induced readout $A_t=R_\Theta(T^t h)$.
Then $\mathcal{P}$ is generating (mod $\mu$) if and only if for $\mu\times\mu$-almost every pair $(h,h')\in H\times H$ with $h\neq h'$ there exists $t\ge 0$ such that
\[
R_\Theta(T^t h)\neq R_\Theta(T^t h').
\]
Equivalently, for $\mu$-almost every $h$, the atom
\[
\bigcap_{t\ge 0} T^{-t}\mathcal{P}(h)
\]
is a singleton, i.e.\ infinite forward readout names identify $h$ up to a null set.
\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Torus scans: ergodicity and existence of finite generators]\label{prop:torus-scan-generators}
Let $H=\TT^d$ with Haar measure $\lambda$ and let $T_\beta(x)=x+\beta\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)$ be a torus translation.
Then:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Ergodicity (scan typicality)}: $T_\beta$ is ergodic (indeed uniquely ergodic) if and only if $\beta$ is totally irrational, i.e.\ $k\cdot \beta\notin\ZZ$ for every $k\in\ZZ^d\setminus\{0\}$.
  \item \textbf{Zero KS entropy}: $h_\lambda(T_\beta)=0$ for every $\beta$.
  \item \textbf{Generators exist despite $h=0$}: by the Krieger generator theorem, every ergodic system with finite KS entropy admits a finite generating partition; in particular, for torus translations there exist $k$-atom generating partitions for any $k\ge 2$.
\end{itemize}
This separates two logically distinct questions: (i) whether the scan is typical/ergodic (a property of $(H,\lambda,T_\beta)$), and (ii) whether the chosen protocol readout partition $\mathcal{P}$ is generating for that scan (a property of $R_\Theta$).\cite{Walters1982,Krieger1970}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[A concrete generator for the ring-factor readout]\label{prop:ring-factor-generator}
Let \((\TT,\lambda,T_\alpha)\) be an irrational rotation \(T_\alpha(x)=x+\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)\) with Lebesgue measure \(\lambda\).
Let \(\mathcal{P}=\{I,\TT\setminus I\}\) be a two-interval partition where \(I\subset\TT\) is a nondegenerate interval and endpoint degeneracies are avoided (so \(\partial I\) has \(\lambda\)-measure \(0\)).
Then \(\mathcal{P}\) is generating (mod \(\lambda\)).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The join partition \(\mathcal{P}^{(t)}=\bigvee_{k=0}^{t-1}T_\alpha^{-k}\mathcal{P}\) is the partition of \(\TT\) into intervals whose endpoints belong to the finite set of translated cut points \(\partial I - k\alpha\) for \(k=0,\dots,t-1\).
For irrational \(\alpha\), the orbit of every point is dense, hence the set of all translated cut points is dense in \(\TT\).
Therefore the atoms of \(\mathcal{P}^{(\infty)}\) refine intervals arbitrarily and generate the Borel \(\sigma\)-algebra up to boundary null sets.\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}
\end{proof}

An equivalent and often more operational viewpoint uses conditional entropies of the symbol process $(A_t)$:
\[
h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})=\lim_{t\to\infty} H(A_t\,|\,A_0,\dots,A_{t-1}),
\]
whenever the limit exists. This emphasizes that entropy-rate time can be estimated from data streams without reconstructing the underlying internal dynamics.

\subsection{Protocol definition of time}\label{sec:info-time:definition}

Motivated by this contraction behavior, we define an intrinsic protocol-time parameter as a monotone function of posterior refinement. One minimal choice is:

\begin{definition}[Entropy-rate time (protocol time)]\label{def:entropy-time}
Assume the SMB limit exists and is positive for the chosen protocol. Define the \emph{entropy-rate time} associated to an observation prefix of length $t$ by
\[
\tau(t) := -\log \mu(C(a_{0:t-1})).
\]
Under SMB hypotheses, $\tau(t)$ grows asymptotically linearly with slope $h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})$ for $\mu$-typical internal states.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of $\tau(t)$ under refinement]\label{prop:tau-monotone}
For any realized record prefix, the cylinders are nested
\[
C(a_{0:t})\subseteq C(a_{0:t-1}) \qquad (t\ge 1),
\]
and hence $\mu(C(a_{0:t}))\le \mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$. Consequently, the refinement functional is monotone:
\[
\tau(t+1)\ge \tau(t)\qquad\text{for all }t\ge 1.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Step-like growth and ``oscillations'' at zero entropy]\label{rem:tau-step-like}
The monotonicity in Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-monotone} does not imply smooth (or approximately linear) growth. In deterministic zero-KS regimes (e.g.\ Sturmian codings of an irrational rotation), cylinders can take only a small number of geometric shapes at each length (intervals with a small set of possible lengths). As a result, $\tau(t)$ typically exhibits a step-like profile with plateaus and occasional jumps; the relevant asymptotic statement is not a linear law but the sublinear regime $\tau(t)=o(t)$ (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:zero-ks}), together with an appropriate sublinear scale (e.g.\ $\log p(t)$).
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exponential law for posterior contraction (partition form)]\label{prop:exp-law}
Under the assumptions of the SMB theorem for a finite partition $\mathcal{P}$, for $\mu$-almost every internal state,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\,\tau(t)=h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P}).
\]
Equivalently, typical posterior cylinder measures satisfy $\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))\approx e^{-t h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})}$ up to subexponential corrections.
\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[KS form under a generating readout]\label{cor:exp-law-ks}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:exp-law}, if \(\mathcal{P}\) is generating (Assumption~\ref{assump:generating-readout}), then \(h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})=h_\mu(T)\) and hence
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\,\tau(t)=h_\mu(T).
\]
\end{corollary}

This definition has two conceptual advantages for geometry-first reconstruction:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item It does not presuppose a continuous time parameter at the object layer; it is built from the \emph{conditioning structure} induced by readout.
  \item It is quantitatively auditable: the slope is an entropy rate, and deviations from linear growth are measurable finite-sample effects.
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}[Protocol-level time arrow]\label{cor:time-arrow}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:exp-law}, ``time increases'' is equivalent (within the protocol) to exponential shrinkage of posterior compatibility sets. The entropy rate controls the arrow quantitatively.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Coarse-graining, factors, and monotonicity}\label{sec:info-time:factors}

Since all quantities above depend on a protocol-defined partition (or on a protocol-defined symbolic factor), it is essential to record how they behave under coarse-graining. The core principle is the standard entropy monotonicity under factor maps.

\begin{proposition}[Entropy monotonicity under factors]\label{prop:entropy-factor-monotone}
Let $(X,\mu,T)$ be a measure-preserving system and let $\pi:(X,\mu,T)\to (Y,\nu,S)$ be a measurable factor map, i.e.\ $\nu=\mu\circ\pi^{-1}$ and $\pi\circ T=S\circ\pi$. Then
\[
h_\nu(S)\le h_\mu(T).
\]
More generally, for any finite partition $\mathcal{Q}$ of $Y$ one has
\[
h_\nu(S,\mathcal{Q}) = h_\mu(T,\pi^{-1}\mathcal{Q}) \le h_\mu(T),
\]
and if $\mathcal{P}$ is a finite partition of $X$ that refines $\pi^{-1}\mathcal{Q}$ then $h_\mu(T,\pi^{-1}\mathcal{Q})\le h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})$.
\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity of refinement depth under factor cylinders]\label{prop:tau-factor-monotone}
Let $(A_t)$ be a stationary process induced by a finite partition $\mathcal{P}$ of $(X,\mu,T)$, and let $(B_t)$ be a factor process obtained from $(A_t)$ by a measurable factor map. For a realized length-$t$ prefix $b_{0:t-1}$ of the factor, let $C_B(b_{0:t-1})$ denote the corresponding cylinder set in $X$ (compatibility set of internal states producing that factor prefix). Then
\[
C_A(a_{0:t-1})\subseteq C_B(b_{0:t-1})\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad
\mu(C_B(b_{0:t-1}))\ge \mu(C_A(a_{0:t-1})),
\]
and hence the associated refinement functional satisfies
\[
\tau_B(t):=-\log\mu(C_B(b_{0:t-1}))\ \le\ \tau_A(t):=-\log\mu(C_A(a_{0:t-1})).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Sliding-block stabilization: an $O(m)$ alignment bound]\label{cor:tau-blockcode}
Let $\Pi_m$ be the sliding-block stabilization factor from Definition~\ref{def:Pi_m} and let $X_t=(\Pi_m(A))_t$ be the stabilized process at depth $m$. Then the length-$t$ stabilized prefix $x_{0:t-1}$ is determined by the length-$(t+m-1)$ raw prefix $a_{0:t+m-2}$, and the corresponding cylinders satisfy
\[
C_A(a_{0:t+m-2})\subseteq C_X(x_{0:t-1})\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\tau_X(t)\le \tau_A(t+m-1).
\]
Under SMB hypotheses for $(A_t)$ this implies that $\tau_X(t)$ cannot grow faster than linearly with slope $h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})$, and in particular the stabilized entropy rate satisfies $h(X)\le h(A)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[SMB/entropy-rate time after stabilization]\label{prop:smb-after-stabilization}
Let $(A_t)$ be a stationary ergodic process on a finite alphabet and let \(X:=\Pi(A)\) be any measurable factor process obtained by a shift-commuting map \(\Pi\) (in particular, \(\Pi\) may be the stabilization factor \(\Pi_m\) from Definition~\ref{def:Pi_m}).
Then \(X\) is stationary and ergodic.
Consequently, the Shannon--McMillan--Breiman theorem applies to \(X\), and for \(\nu\)-almost every stabilized realization one has
\[
-\frac{1}{t}\log \nu([X_0^{t-1}])\ \longrightarrow\ h(X),
\]
where \(\nu\) is the law of \(X\) and \([X_0^{t-1}]\) denotes the corresponding length-\(t\) cylinder in the stabilized shift.
Equivalently, the stabilized refinement functional (defined from stabilized cylinders) has an almost-sure linear slope \(h(X)\) in the positive-entropy regime.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[How stabilization interacts with SMB/KS]\label{rem:stabilization-smb-ks}
Proposition~\ref{prop:smb-after-stabilization} formalizes the conservative interaction principle used throughout this review:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Stabilization is a factor operation, hence it can only decrease entropy rate: \(h(X)\le h(A)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-factor-monotone}).
  \item If stabilization is invertible up to finite memory (i.e.\ it is part of a finite-memory equivalence class in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:finite-memory-equivalence}), then the entropy-rate time law is preserved, up to the bounded alignment constants in Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-finite-memory} and Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-class-invariance}.
  \item When one compares to the KS entropy of an underlying internal dynamics, the relevant issue is not stabilization but whether the readout partition is (approximately) generating (Assumption~\ref{assump:generating-readout}); stabilization then acts \emph{after} the readout and thus cannot create missing information, only compress it.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Loss of generatingness under stabilization (the generic case)]\label{rem:loss-of-generator}
Even if a readout partition \(\mathcal{P}\) is generating for the base \((H,\mu,T)\), a subsequent stabilization/folding \(\Pi\) applied to the observed record is typically \emph{many-to-one}. This means the stabilized observation \(\sigma\)-algebra is strictly smaller than the original readout \(\sigma\)-algebra, so it need not be generating for \((H,\mu,T)\) and generally cannot recover \(h_\mu(T)\).
What is always true is more modest and sufficient for this review:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item SMB still applies to the stabilized process \(X=\Pi(A)\) (Proposition~\ref{prop:smb-after-stabilization}), so an entropy-rate time law exists \emph{for the stabilized factor} whenever it exists for the corresponding stationary ergodic process.
  \item The stabilized entropy rate satisfies \(h(X)\le h(A)\), with strict inequality whenever \(\Pi\) discards information at positive rate.
  \item Recovering KS-level invariants after stabilization requires an explicit near-invertibility hypothesis (finite-memory equivalence / measurable isomorphism), which is not generic and must be stated when used.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Protocol-equivalence classes: finite-memory recodings}\label{sec:info-time:equivalence}

Factor monotonicity (Proposition~\ref{prop:entropy-factor-monotone}) captures what happens under irreversible coarse-graining. For protocol comparisons, it is also useful to identify when two protocols are \emph{equivalent up to finite memory}, meaning that they can simulate each other's symbolic record with bounded lookahead.

\begin{definition}[Finite-memory (sliding-block) protocol equivalence]\label{def:finite-memory-equivalence}
Let $(A_t)$ and $(B_t)$ be stationary processes over finite alphabets. We say that $B$ is a \emph{finite-memory recoding} of $A$ if there exists an integer $r\ge 0$ and a measurable map $\psi:\mathcal{A}^{r+1}\to\mathcal{B}$ such that $B_t=\psi(A_t,\dots,A_{t+r})$ for all $t$ (a one-sided sliding block code). Two protocols are \emph{finite-memory equivalent} if each process is a finite-memory recoding of the other (possibly with different radii).
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Cylinder alignment under finite-memory equivalence]\label{prop:tau-finite-memory}
Assume $B$ is a finite-memory recoding of $A$ with radius $r$ in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:finite-memory-equivalence}. Then for every realized length-$t$ prefix $b_{0:t-1}$ there exists a length-$(t+r)$ prefix $a_{0:t+r-1}$ consistent with the same underlying realization such that the corresponding cylinder sets satisfy
\[
C_A(a_{0:t+r-1})\subseteq C_B(b_{0:t-1}),
\]
and hence
\[
\tau_B(t)\le \tau_A(t+r).
\]
If, moreover, $A$ is a finite-memory recoding of $B$ with radius $s$, then one also has $\tau_A(t)\le \tau_B(t+s)$, so that the two refinement functionals control each other up to an $O(1)$ shift in scan depth.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Protocol invariance up to additive/multiplicative constants]\label{cor:tau-affine-class}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-finite-memory}, assume $A$ and $B$ are finite-memory equivalent with radii $(r,s)$. Then:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Additive ambiguity (finite alignment)}: for all $t$ one has
  \[
  \tau_B(t)\le \tau_A(t+r),\qquad \tau_A(t)\le \tau_B(t+s),
  \]
  so the two protocol times agree up to a bounded-depth alignment in $t$ (hence up to an $O(1)$ offset once an asymptotic growth law is fixed).
  \item \textbf{Multiplicative ambiguity (information units)}: changing the logarithm base rescales $\tau$ by a fixed constant factor. In particular, reporting in bits vs.\ nats multiplies $\tau$ by $\log 2$.
\end{itemize}
Consequently, within a finite-memory equivalence class, the asymptotic \emph{time law} (linear slope when $h>0$, or sublinear growth class when $h=0$) is invariant; only the conventional units and finite-alignment constants change.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[Invariance of entropy-class and growth-class under finite memory]\label{cor:tau-class-invariance}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-finite-memory}:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item If $(A_t)$ satisfies SMB with positive entropy rate (equivalently $\lim_{t\to\infty}\tau_A(t)/t=h(A)>0$ for typical realizations), then $(B_t)$ also satisfies SMB and has the same entropy rate $h(B)=h(A)$. In particular, the linear slope of $\tau(t)$ is invariant under finite-memory recodings.
  \item If $h(A)=0$, then $\tau_B(t)/t\to 0$ whenever $\tau_A(t)/t\to 0$, and more generally the \emph{growth class} of sublinear refinement (e.g.\ logarithmic vs.\ power-law) is stable under finite-memory equivalence, up to the constant-depth alignment in Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-finite-memory}.
\end{itemize}
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[Where protocol constants enter]\label{rem:tau-constants}
Two sources of additive/scale constants should be distinguished:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Recoding alignment}: finite-memory equivalence introduces an $O(1)$ offset in the prefix length ($r,s$), hence an $O(1)$ ambiguity in $\tau(t)$ even before taking limits.
  \item \textbf{Units of information}: changing the logarithm base rescales $\tau$ multiplicatively; finite-data estimators of $\mu(C)$ (e.g.\ plug-in prefix frequencies) introduce additional finite-size bias that is again controlled at the level of $O(1)$ corrections for fixed estimator choices.
\end{itemize}
The point of introducing finite-memory equivalence is that it defines a robust protocol class in which such constants do not alter the entropy class (positive vs.\ zero) and do not affect the asymptotic growth regime used to report ``time'' at the protocol layer.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Sensitivity to window/partition choice and to factor maps}\label{sec:info-time:sensitivity}

The refinement functional $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ depends on the protocol through the induced partition/coding. In practice, one wants both (i) an \emph{operational} metric for how far two window/partition choices are, and (ii) a statement of what aspects of $\tau$ are invariant under changes that are ``acceptable'' (finite-memory recodings, small boundary shifts, or passage to a canonical factor).

\begin{definition}[Partition mismatch rate]\label{def:partition-mismatch}
Let $R_\Theta,R_{\Theta'}:H\to\mathcal{A}$ be two deterministic readouts on the same probability space $(H,\mu)$ with the same finite alphabet $\mathcal{A}$. Define the one-step partition mismatch rate
\[
\varepsilon(\Theta,\Theta'):=\mu\bigl(\{h\in H:\ R_\Theta(h)\neq R_{\Theta'}(h)\}\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Window shifts induce small mismatch away from the boundary]\label{prop:window-mismatch-boundary}
Let $H$ be a compact metric Abelian group with Haar probability measure $\mu$, and consider two binary window readouts
\[
R_\Theta(h)=\mathbf{1}_{W}(h),\qquad R_{\Theta'}(h)=\mathbf{1}_{W+\delta}(h),
\]
where $W\subset H$ is measurable and $W+\delta$ is its translate by $\delta\in H$. Then the mismatch rate from Definition~\ref{def:partition-mismatch} satisfies
\[
\varepsilon(\Theta,\Theta')=\mu\bigl(W\,\triangle\,(W+\delta)\bigr),
\]
where $\triangle$ is symmetric difference. In particular, if $W$ is \emph{regular} (boundary of $\mu$-measure $0$), then $\varepsilon(\Theta,\Theta')\to 0$ as $\delta\to 0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Quantitative boundary-layer bounds]
If $H$ is a torus and $W$ has sufficiently regular boundary (e.g.\ piecewise $C^1$), then $\mu(W\,\triangle\,(W+\delta))$ is $O(\|\delta\|)$ as $\|\delta\|\to 0$, with a constant controlled by an appropriate surface-area notion of $\partial W$. This is the standard ``boundary-layer'' mechanism by which small window perturbations produce small symbol mismatch.\cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974,BaakeGrimm2013}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Exact invariance under lifting through a factor]\label{prop:tau-factor-lift-invariance}
Let $\varphi:(H,\mu,T)\to (H',\mu',T')$ be a measurable factor map, i.e.\ $\mu'=\varphi_\ast\mu$ and $\varphi\circ T=T'\circ\varphi$. Let $R':H'\to\mathcal{A}$ be a readout and define the lifted readout $R:=R'\circ\varphi$ on $H$. Then for every realized prefix $a_{0:t-1}$,
\[
\mu\bigl(C_R(a_{0:t-1})\bigr)=\mu'\bigl(C_{R'}(a_{0:t-1})\bigr),
\]
and hence the associated refinement functionals agree exactly:
\[
\tau_R(t)=\tau_{R'}(t)\qquad \text{for all }t.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation: $\varphi$ changes the object layer, not the protocol time]
Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-factor-lift-invariance} formalizes an important point: if two observers differ only by whether they describe internal states in $H$ or in a factor $H'$ (related by $\varphi$), and they use consistent readouts (one is the pullback of the other), then $\tau(t)$ is exactly the same number. In contrast, if the observer changes the readout in a way that is not a pullback along a factor (e.g.\ by moving the window boundary), then the change is quantitative and is captured by the mismatch-rate bounds below.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Maximal equicontinuous factor and a canonical protocol equivalence class}\label{sec:info-time:mef}

In the deterministic zero-entropy regimes emphasized by superspace model-set examples, a large class of internal dynamical systems admits a distinguished topological factor that captures the ``most rigid'' (purely rotational) component of the dynamics.

\begin{definition}[Maximal equicontinuous factor (MEF)]\label{def:mef}
Let $(X,T)$ be a compact metric dynamical system (topological dynamics). A factor $\pi_{\mathrm{eq}}:(X,T)\to (X_{\mathrm{eq}},S)$ is called the \emph{maximal equicontinuous factor} if $(X_{\mathrm{eq}},S)$ is equicontinuous and every continuous factor map from $(X,T)$ to an equicontinuous system factors uniquely through $\pi_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
\cite{Auslander1988,Ellis1969}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Universality of the MEF for equicontinuous information]\label{prop:mef-universal}
Let $(X,T)$ be minimal and uniquely ergodic and let $\pi_{\mathrm{eq}}$ be its MEF. If a protocol readout depends only on an equicontinuous coordinate of the system (i.e.\ it factors through some equicontinuous factor), then it necessarily factors through $\pi_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
Consequently, any two such protocols are comparable on the same canonical base $(X_{\mathrm{eq}},S)$, and their refinement functionals $\tau(t)$ differ only through their choice of partition/window on $X_{\mathrm{eq}}$ (hence are quantitatively controlled by boundary mismatch as in Proposition~\ref{prop:window-mismatch-boundary}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Model sets: torus parametrization as the MEF]
For regular model sets and many substitution/Meyer-type hulls with pure point spectrum, the MEF is a compact Abelian group rotation (often described as the torus parametrization in the cut-and-project literature).\cite{BaakeGrimm2013,Moody2000}
In such cases, restricting attention to readouts that are measurable with respect to the MEF (plus a controlled noise channel) defines a natural \emph{acceptable protocol class}: within it, the protocol-time ordering is stable up to the finite-memory and mismatch-rate equivalences already recorded in this section.
\end{remark}

\begin{definition}[MEF-adapted protocols]\label{def:mef-adapted-protocol}
Let $(X,T)$ be a compact metric system with invariant measure $\mu$ and let $\pi_{\mathrm{eq}}:(X,T)\to (X_{\mathrm{eq}},S)$ be its maximal equicontinuous factor (Definition~\ref{def:mef}) with invariant measure $\mu_{\mathrm{eq}}:=(\pi_{\mathrm{eq}})_\ast\mu$.
A readout protocol $\Theta$ is \emph{MEF-adapted} if its deterministic part factors through the MEF:
there exists a measurable $R_{\mathrm{eq}}:X_{\mathrm{eq}}\to\mathcal{A}$ such that
\[
R_\Theta = R_{\mathrm{eq}}\circ \pi_{\mathrm{eq}}
\qquad (\mu\text{-a.e.}).
\]
Equivalently, the induced partition $\mathcal{P}_\Theta$ on $X$ is the pullback of a partition $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{eq}}$ on $X_{\mathrm{eq}}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Protocol refinement preorder and mutual refinability]\label{def:protocol-refinement-preorder}
Restrict to MEF-adapted deterministic protocols on the same underlying system $(X,\mu,T)$ and write $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{eq}}(\Theta)$ for the corresponding finite partition on $(X_{\mathrm{eq}},\mu_{\mathrm{eq}},S)$.
Define a refinement preorder on such protocols by
\[
\Theta_1\ \preceq_{\mathrm{ref}}\ \Theta_2
\quad:\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\sigma\!\left(\bigvee_{t\ge 0} S^{-t}\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{eq}}(\Theta_1)\right)
\ \subseteq\
\sigma\!\left(\bigvee_{t\ge 0} S^{-t}\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{eq}}(\Theta_2)\right)
\qquad (\mathrm{mod}\ \mu_{\mathrm{eq}}),
\]
i.e.\ every event decidable from the infinite forward symbolic name of $\Theta_1$ is also decidable from that of $\Theta_2$ (up to null sets).
We say that $\Theta_1$ and $\Theta_2$ are \emph{mutually refinable} if both $\Theta_1\preceq_{\mathrm{ref}}\Theta_2$ and $\Theta_2\preceq_{\mathrm{ref}}\Theta_1$ hold, i.e.\ the two protocols generate the same observable $\sigma$-algebra on the MEF.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[How ``different $\phi$'' can be protocol-equivalent]\label{rem:phi-same-mef}
In the examples emphasized in this review, a ``ring factor'' $\phi$ is a continuous factor map from the MEF (a compact Abelian rotation) to a circle rotation.
Different choices of $\phi$ can therefore lead to protocols that are distinct at the level of coordinates (different derived 1D angles) while remaining MEF-adapted in the sense of Definition~\ref{def:mef-adapted-protocol}.
If two such choices induce the same quotient factor of $X_{\mathrm{eq}}$ (equivalently, they differ by a measurable isomorphism on the factor), then their induced symbolic descriptions are mutually refinable (Definition~\ref{def:protocol-refinement-preorder}).
Even when two ring factors are not isomorphic, their join partition on $X_{\mathrm{eq}}$ provides a canonical common refinement: one can always compare protocols inside the same MEF by pulling back both readouts and considering the joint readout $(R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(1)},R_{\mathrm{eq}}^{(2)})$ as a combined protocol.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Quantifying protocol dependence: mismatch-rate bounds}\label{sec:info-time:stability-bounds}

The previous invariance statements describe what is stable \emph{qualitatively} (monotonicity under factors; invariance within finite-memory equivalence classes). For practical protocols, one often wants a \emph{quantitative} dependence on window/partition choice, factor maps, and noise level.

\begin{definition}[Coupling mismatch rate]\label{def:mismatch-rate}
Let $(A_t)$ and $(B_t)$ be stationary processes on a common finite alphabet $\mathcal{A}$, defined on a common probability space (a coupling). Define the one-step mismatch rate by
\[
\varepsilon:=\mathbb{P}(A_0\neq B_0).
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Block-law variation controlled by mismatch]\label{prop:block-tv-by-mismatch}
In the setting of Definition~\ref{def:mismatch-rate}, for every $t\ge 1$ and every length-$t$ word $a_{0:t-1}\in\mathcal{A}^t$,
\[
\bigl|\mathbb{P}(A_{0:t-1}=a_{0:t-1})-\mathbb{P}(B_{0:t-1}=a_{0:t-1})\bigr|
\le \mathbb{P}(A_{0:t-1}\neq B_{0:t-1})
\le t\,\varepsilon.
\]
Equivalently, the total variation distance between the $t$-block laws is bounded by $\lVert \mathcal{L}(A_{0:t-1})-\mathcal{L}(B_{0:t-1})\rVert_{\mathrm{TV}}\le t\varepsilon$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[Finite-$t$ stability of $\tau$ under mismatch]\label{cor:tau-stability-finite-t}
Fix $t\ge 1$ and a word $a_{0:t-1}\in\mathcal{A}^t$ with block probabilities
\[
p_t:=\mathbb{P}(A_{0:t-1}=a_{0:t-1}),\qquad q_t:=\mathbb{P}(B_{0:t-1}=a_{0:t-1}).
\]
If $\min(p_t,q_t)>0$, then
\[
\bigl|-\log p_t + \log q_t\bigr|\ \le\ \log\!\left(1+\frac{t\varepsilon}{\min(p_t,q_t)}\right),
\]
and in particular if $\min(p_t,q_t)\gg t\varepsilon$ the difference is $O(t\varepsilon/\min(p_t,q_t))$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proposition}[Entropy continuity bound from mismatch]\label{prop:entropy-continuity-mismatch}
In the setting of Definition~\ref{def:mismatch-rate}, let $K:=|\mathcal{A}|$ and define the binary entropy function (using the same logarithm base as elsewhere)
\[
H_2(\varepsilon):=-\varepsilon\log\varepsilon-(1-\varepsilon)\log(1-\varepsilon).
\]
Then for every $t\ge 1$,
\[
\bigl|H(A_{0:t-1})-H(B_{0:t-1})\bigr|\ \le\ t\Bigl(H_2(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\log(K-1)\Bigr).
\]
Consequently, whenever the entropy rates exist,
\[
\bigl|h(A)-h(B)\bigr|\ \le\ H_2(\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\log(K-1).
\]
\cite{CoverThomas2006}
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[How this quantifies window/partition and noise dependence]\label{rem:mismatch-interpretation}
Definition~\ref{def:mismatch-rate} is intentionally protocol-facing: $\varepsilon$ can be estimated by running two protocols on matched phases (or matched underlying trajectories) and measuring the empirical Hamming mismatch rate. Changes of window/partition parameters and additive readout noise (e.g.\ symbol corruption at rate $\varepsilon$) both contribute to mismatch; Propositions~\ref{prop:block-tv-by-mismatch} and \ref{prop:entropy-continuity-mismatch} then turn that operationally measurable $\varepsilon$ into explicit error bars for block entropies, entropy rates, and (when block probabilities are not too small) for the refinement functional $\tau$ via Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-stability-finite-t}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What structure $\tau$ does (and does not) induce]\label{rem:tau-structure}
The functional $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ is not a metric on $H$; it is a protocol-defined refinement depth attached to an observation prefix. However, the nested cylinders induce a canonical information order on prefixes: $a_{0:t-1}$ refines $b_{0:s-1}$ iff $t\ge s$ and $a_{0:s-1}=b_{0:s-1}$, equivalently $C(a_{0:t-1})\subseteq C(b_{0:s-1})$. This order is a partial order on protocol information states (up to $\mu$-null distinctions).
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Zero-KS regimes: subexponential refinement}\label{sec:info-time:zero-ks}

Definition~\ref{def:entropy-time} explicitly assumes a \emph{positive} entropy rate in order to obtain a linear growth law. This assumption fails in many structurally important symbolic systems. A canonical example in this review is the coding of an irrational rotation by a two-interval partition, which yields a Sturmian process with minimal factor complexity $p(n)=n+1$ (Section~\ref{sec:readout:sturmian}) \cite{Queffelec2010,Lothaire2002}. Such systems are uniquely ergodic but have $h_\mu(T)=0$ \cite{Walters1982}.

\begin{proposition}[Probabilistic-window crossover at small $\epsilon$]\label{prop:epsilon-crossover-tau}
Consider a deterministic zero-KS base scan $(H,\mu,T)$ (e.g.\ a torus translation) and the probabilistic-window readout model of Section~\ref{sec:readout:probabilistic-template} with kernel $\kappa_\epsilon$.
Let $A^{(\epsilon)}$ be the induced observed process and let $\tau_\epsilon(t)$ denote the refinement functional computed from its length-$t$ cylinders (Definition~\ref{def:entropy-time}).
Assume $A^{(\epsilon)}$ is stationary and ergodic for each fixed $\epsilon>0$.
Then SMB implies that for $\nu_\epsilon$-almost every observed realization,
\[
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\tau_\epsilon(t)=h\!\left(A^{(\epsilon)}\right).
\]
Moreover, when the base has $h_\mu(T)=0$, the entropy rate satisfies the protocol-facing upper bound
\[
0\ \le\ h\!\left(A^{(\epsilon)}\right)\ \le\ \int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h),
\]
and hence $h(A^{(\epsilon)})\to 0$ whenever $\int H_b(\kappa_\epsilon)\,\mathrm{d}\mu\to 0$ as $\epsilon\to 0$ (e.g.\ under boundary-layer kernels; cf.\ Proposition~\ref{prop:h-vanishes-eps}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{corollary}[A scaling law: $\tau_\epsilon(t)=O(\epsilon t)$ under boundary layers]\label{cor:tau-eps-scaling}
In the setting of Proposition~\ref{prop:epsilon-crossover-tau}, assume additionally that $\kappa_\epsilon$ is a boundary-layer kernel satisfying the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:boundary-layer-scaling}, so that
\[
\int_H H_b(\kappa_\epsilon(h))\,\mathrm{d}\mu(h)\ =\ C\,\epsilon+o(\epsilon)
\quad\text{as }\epsilon\to 0
\]
for an explicit constant $C>0$ determined by the sigmoid profile and boundary geometry.
Then the linear posterior contraction rate vanishes at most linearly in $\epsilon$:
\[
\limsup_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\tau_\epsilon(t)\ \le\ C\,\epsilon+o(\epsilon).
\]
In particular, the natural joint scaling window is $t\asymp 1/\epsilon$: for $t_\epsilon:=\lfloor u/\epsilon\rfloor$ one has
\[
\limsup_{\epsilon\to 0}\ \tau_\epsilon(t_\epsilon)\ \le\ u\,C.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[What ``time'' looks like at $h_\mu(T)=0$]\label{rem:time-zero-ks}
When $h_\mu(T)=0$, the SMB limit still implies $\lim_{t\to\infty}\tau(t)/t=0$ for $\mu$-typical trajectories, so cylinders shrink \emph{subexponentially}. The same refinement functional $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ remains well-defined and monotone, but it no longer provides an approximately affine reparameterization of the scan index. In practice, one should treat $\tau$ as a \emph{refinement depth} and calibrate it against an external physical clock only after choosing the relevant scaling regime (e.g.\ logarithmic).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Dependence on the reference/initial measure]\label{rem:tau-measure-dependence}
Definition~\ref{def:entropy-time} uses an invariant reference measure $\mu$ on the internal state space to quantify posterior contraction. Two distinct (and easily conflated) ``measure dependences'' should be separated:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Choice of invariant measure}: if the object-layer system admits multiple invariant measures (non-unique ergodicity), then the typical refinement behavior can depend on which invariant $\mu$ is selected. In the uniquely ergodic settings emphasized in this review (e.g.\ irrational rotations and regular model-set hulls), this ambiguity is absent.
  \item \textbf{Initial distribution vs.\ invariant reference}: even in uniquely ergodic systems, one may start from an initial distribution $\nu$ different from $\mu$. If $\nu$ is absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$ with bounded density, then switching between $\mu$ and $\nu$ changes $\tau(t)$ by at most an $O(1)$ additive constant (Proposition~\ref{prop:tau-change-of-measure}) and does not affect the entropy class ($h>0$ vs.\ $h=0$) nor the growth class.
\end{itemize}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Change-of-measure bound for $\tau(t)$]\label{prop:tau-change-of-measure}
Let $\nu$ be another probability measure on the internal space such that $\nu\ll \mu$ and the Radon--Nikodym derivative is essentially bounded away from $0$ and $\infty$:
\[
0<c\ \le\ \frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mu}\ \le\ C<\infty
\quad\text{$\mu$-a.e.}
\]
Define the alternative refinement functional $\tau_\nu(t):=-\log \nu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$. Then for every $t$ and every realized prefix,
\[
\log c\ +\ \log \mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))\ \le\ \log \nu(C(a_{0:t-1}))\ \le\ \log C\ +\ \log \mu(C(a_{0:t-1})),
\]
and hence
\[
|\tau_\nu(t)-\tau(t)|\ \le\ \max\{-\log c,\ \log C\}.
\]
In particular, $\tau_\nu(t)/t$ and $\tau(t)/t$ have the same limit whenever either limit exists.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[A soft logarithmic bound from low complexity]\label{prop:log-bound}
Let $(A_t)$ be the symbolic process induced by a uniquely ergodic system and a finite partition, and let $p(t)$ be the number of distinct length-$t$ words with nonzero probability. Then for every $t$ there exists a length-$t$ cylinder $C$ with
\[
\mu(C)\ge \frac{1}{p(t)} \qquad\Rightarrow\qquad \tau(t)\le \log p(t).
\]
In particular, for Sturmian codings $p(t)=t+1$, so $\tau(t)$ is at most logarithmic for an infinite subsequence of prefixes. Numerically (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix:toy}), typical prefixes exhibit step-like growth consistent with $\tau(t)=O(\log t)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Sturmian cylinders have $\Theta(1/t)$ size (hence $\tau(t)=\log t+O(1)$)]\label{prop:sturmian-cylinder-theta}
Consider an irrational circle rotation $T_\alpha:x\mapsto x+\alpha\ (\mathrm{mod}\ 1)$ equipped with the Lebesgue invariant measure, and a nondegenerate two-interval coding $R$ that yields a Sturmian process (Section~\ref{sec:readout:sturmian}). Let $C(a_{0:t-1})$ be the length-$t$ cylinder in the circle for an observed prefix.
Then there exist constants $c_-,c_+>0$ (depending on $\alpha$ and on the partition boundary, but not on $t$) such that for every $t\ge 1$ and every admissible prefix,
\[
\frac{c_-}{t+1}\ \le\ \mu\bigl(C(a_{0:t-1})\bigr)\ \le\ \frac{c_+}{t+1}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
\log(t+1)-\log c_+\ \le\ \tau(t)\ \le\ \log(t+1)-\log c_-,
\]
so $\tau(t)=\log(t)+O(1)$ and its step-like fluctuations are bounded at $O(1)$ amplitude around the logarithmic scale.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
For a two-interval coding of a rotation, every length-$t$ cylinder is an interval in $\TT$ whose endpoints belong to the finite set of orbit cuts $\{-k\alpha\}_{k=0}^{t}$ translated by the partition boundary. Thus the set of all length-$t$ cylinders is exactly the collection of gaps between the ordered points $\{x_0, x_0-\alpha,\dots,x_0-t\alpha\}$ (up to a boundary translation). The three-gap theorem implies that these gaps take at most three values and are all comparable to $1/(t+1)$, uniformly in $t$ (with constants depending on $\alpha$ and on boundary nondegeneracy).\cite{KuipersNiederreiter1974}
Taking $\mu$ as Lebesgue measure identifies cylinder measures with these gap lengths, yielding the stated two-sided bound.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Complexity-calibrated protocol time in the zero-KS regime]\label{def:complexity-time}
In a protocol for which $h_\mu(T)=0$ (hence $\tau(t)=o(t)$ for typical realizations), we use the factor complexity $p(t)$ to set the \emph{canonical} sublinear reference scale
\[
g(t):=\log p(t).
\]
We call $g(t)$ the \emph{complexity-calibrated protocol time scale}. In this regime one reports refinement in the units of $g(t)$ (e.g.\ compares $\tau(t)$ to $g(t)$ along a chosen scaling window), rather than using the scan index $t$ as a proxy for elapsed time.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[A normalized zero-KS time coordinate]\label{def:zero-ks-normalized-time}
In the zero-KS regime, define the \emph{normalized refinement time} (dimensionless) by
\[
\widehat{\tau}(t):=\frac{\tau(t)}{\log p(t)}\qquad (t\ge 2),
\]
whenever $\log p(t)>0$.
For Sturmian codings, $p(t)=t+1$ and Proposition~\ref{prop:sturmian-cylinder-theta} implies $\widehat{\tau}(t)=1+O(1/\log t)$.
An equivalent ``effective time index'' is obtained by exponentiation,
\[
t_{\mathrm{eff}}(t):=\exp(\tau(t))=\frac{1}{\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))},
\]
which is directly interpretable as the inverse posterior mass of the compatibility set (a multiplicity scale).
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Alternative sublinear scales: algorithmic complexity and pointwise dimension]\label{rem:zero-ks-alternatives}
Definition~\ref{def:complexity-time} uses word (factor) complexity because it is purely symbolic and therefore protocol-auditable from data without introducing extra geometric structure. Two alternative ``sublinear clocks'' are often discussed in the ergodic/information literature:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Algorithmic (Kolmogorov/Brudno) complexity}: one may use the Kolmogorov complexity $K(a_{0:t-1})$ of the observed prefix as a refinement scale. In positive-entropy ergodic regimes, Brudno-type theorems relate $\lim_{t\to\infty}K(a_{0:t-1})/t$ to the KS entropy; in the zero-KS regime this ratio vanishes, and one can compare $\tau(t)$ against the sublinear growth of $K(a_{0:t-1})$ as an alternative protocol-defined ``information time''.
  \item \textbf{Pointwise dimension (requires a metric interface)}: if one equips the internal state space $H$ with a metric and relates cylinders $C(a_{0:t-1})$ to metric balls (via a regularity assumption on the partition geometry), then $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C)$ can be compared to a local dimension scale $\log(1/r)$ where $r$ is an effective cylinder diameter. This route is powerful but is not purely symbolic: it requires an explicit protocol-to-geometry interface that controls how symbolic refinement translates into geometric localization.
\end{itemize}
In this review we keep $\log p(t)$ as the canonical reference scale because it makes the minimal number of additional assumptions while still capturing the correct subexponential order in standard zero-KS examples (e.g.\ Sturmian codings).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[A sublinear ``clock'' in zero-entropy regimes]\label{rem:sublinear-clock}
In deterministic zero-KS regimes (e.g.\ Sturmian codings of rotations), the operational content of $\tau(t)$ is not ``elapsed time'' but \emph{how many internal states remain compatible} with the observed record at depth $t$. A protocol can therefore adopt a \emph{sublinear clock} by selecting a scaling map $g(t)$ such that $\tau(t)$ is comparable to $g(t)$ in the regime of interest. Two auditable choices are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Refinement-depth clock}: use $\tau(t)$ itself as a dimensionless time coordinate.
  \item \textbf{Complexity-calibrated clock (canonical in this review)}: use $\log p(t)$ (Definition~\ref{def:complexity-time}) as a coarse upper bound and reference scale for $\tau(t)$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:log-bound}). For Sturmian codings, $p(t)=t+1$ yields a logarithmic scale $\log(t+1)$ that captures the correct subexponential order.
\end{itemize}
The intended interpretation is purely protocol-level: $g(t)$ specifies the unit in which refinement depth is reported. Any identification with a physical clock requires an additional calibration map (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:definition}).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Avoiding over-dependence on the chosen coding]\label{rem:coding-dependence-zero}
In zero-entropy settings, numerical values of $\tau(t)$ can depend on the chosen partition/coding, because different codings induce different cylinder geometries. The robust content is instead:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Entropy class}: $h_\mu(T)=0$ (subexponential refinement) is invariant under finite-alphabet recodings and measurable factors.
  \item \textbf{Growth class of complexity}: for minimal rotations, any nondegenerate finite partition yields at most linear factor complexity, and Sturmian codings achieve the minimal $p(t)=t+1$; thus $\log p(t)\sim \log t$ is stable as a \emph{scaling class} even when constant factors vary.
  \item \textbf{Finite-memory recoding bounds}: if two codings are related by a sliding block code of fixed window size, their cylinder refinements are mutually controlled up to an $O(1)$ alignment in $t$ (cf.\ Corollary~\ref{cor:tau-blockcode}); consequently the choice of a logarithmic (or more generally sublinear) scaling regime is not an artifact of a single encoding.
\end{itemize}
This is the sense in which the ``sublinear clock'' proposal avoids being a free choice: it is constrained by protocol-equivalence classes (finite-memory recodings) rather than by a single preferred code.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Calibration and units}

Definition~\ref{def:entropy-time} produces a dimensionless quantity (a negative log-probability). To compare with physical time scales one needs an additional calibration map, for example by fixing a unit of refinement depth per unit macroscopic time, or by relating $\tau$ to a measurable rate (e.g.\ a characteristic decay time of conditional uncertainty in an experimental setup). This calibration is intentionally external to the object layer: it is part of the protocol-to-physics interface and can be constrained (but not derived) by empirical input.

\subsubsection{Estimating entropy rates from finite data}

In practice, the entropy rate $h_\mu(T,\mathcal{P})$ (and hence the slope of $\tau(t)$) must be estimated from a finite symbolic record. Several estimators are standard in information theory and symbolic dynamics:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Block-entropy estimators}: estimate $H(A_0^{n-1})$ for increasing block lengths $n$ and extrapolate $H(A_0^{n-1})/n$; this is conceptually closest to the KS/SMB definitions but suffers from combinatorial explosion for large alphabets.
  \item \textbf{Conditional-entropy estimators}: estimate $H(A_n\,|\,A_0^{n-1})$ for moderate $n$ and look for stabilization; this aligns with the interpretation of entropy rate as a refinement speed.
  \item \textbf{Universal compression / Lempel--Ziv-type estimators}: infer entropy rates from compression lengths; these can be robust for long sequences but require careful control of nonstationarity and finite-size bias.
\end{itemize}
For GRG-facing applications, the key methodological point is that entropy-rate claims should be accompanied by an explicit estimator choice, a finite-size error assessment, and a phase-averaging strategy (Section~\ref{sec:falsifiability:practice}). A concise introduction to these estimation ideas can be found in standard information theory references \cite{CoverThomas2006}.

\begin{remark}[Time fibers and ``future'' as unresolved uncertainty]\label{rem:future}
At fixed resolution (word length $m$), stabilization induces time fibers $\mathcal{F}_m(x)=\Fold_m^{-1}(x)$ (Section~\ref{sec:stabilization:fibers}). Conditioning on longer observation prefixes corresponds to refining the observer's posterior \emph{within} these fibers. In this sense, what remains ``in the future'' of a protocol is the scale of residual uncertainty in the conditional distribution on the fiber (e.g.\ conditional entropy), rather than an additional ontological object. This viewpoint is useful when interfacing with relativistic language: it cleanly separates epistemic refinement from causal structure.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Ergodic reconstruction from a single trajectory}\label{sec:info-time:holography}

Beyond entropy rates, ergodic theory supplies another important ingredient for ``auditable'' reconstruction: time averages along a typical orbit recover invariant statistics. Concretely, if $(H,\mu,T)$ is measure-preserving and ergodic, then Birkhoff's ergodic theorem implies that for any $f\in L^1(\mu)$,
\[
\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} f(T^t h)\ \longrightarrow\ \int_H f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
\quad\text{for }\mu\text{-almost every }h.
\]
\cite{Walters1982,Petersen1983}

Applied to protocol-defined observables (e.g.\ $f(h)=M(x^{(m)}(h))$ or to information densities), this means that a sufficiently long single readout sequence can approximate the global invariant law. This is sometimes described as a ``pointwise holography'' principle: global statistical structure is recoverable from typical local data streams. In this review we only use the rigorous content (ergodic reconstruction) and treat the holography terminology as an interpretive metaphor.

\subsection{What is (and is not) claimed}\label{sec:info-time:limits}

Entropy-rate time is a protocol-level notion. It is \emph{not} automatically the same as proper time in a Lorentzian manifold or as time in a Hamiltonian quantum theory. What it provides is a disciplined way to talk about an arrow induced by information acquisition under finite-resolution readout. Any physical identification (e.g.\ mapping $\tau$ to a macroscopic time coordinate) must be made via an explicit interface and is therefore contingent on additional assumptions. This viewpoint will guide the causality discussion in the next section.
