\section{Space complexity as readout resolution: from window cuts to canonical numeration}
\label{sec:space}

\subsection{Readout produces a mechanical word: space as the cost of distinction}
A window projection $\PiW$ turns a continuous orbit into a binary stream $\{s_k\}$ (Equation~\eqref{eq:mechanical_word}).
This is the minimal observational alphabet: two outcomes corresponding to whether the phase lies inside the window.
For irrational rotations with interval windows, the resulting sequence is Sturmian and has minimal factor complexity among aperiodic binary sequences:
\begin{equation}
 p(n)=n+1.
\end{equation}
In other words, a Sturmian readout is a ``least complex'' way to encode a nonperiodic structure in a binary grammar \cite{MorseHedlund1940,Lothaire2002}.

In the present language, \emph{space} is the amount of grammar required to make distinctions stable.
A longer prefix yields finer resolution because it refines the orbit partition and reduces discrepancy fluctuations (Equation~\eqref{eq:sturmian_prefix_sum}).
Thus readout space complexity is naturally measured by prefix length (Definition~\ref{def:readout_space_complexity}); see Appendix~\ref{app:readout_bounds} for explicit $O(1/N)$ discrepancy bounds.

\subsection{Ostrowski and Zeckendorf: canonical coding as spatial resolution}
The binary stream is not only a data record; in HPA it is also a coordinate system.
Ostrowski numeration provides a canonical way to represent integers based on the continued fraction expansion of the slope $\alpha$.
In the golden branch $\alpha=\varphi^{-1}$ (continued fraction coefficients all equal to $1$), Ostrowski representation degenerates to Zeckendorf representation: every $N\in\NN$ has a unique Fibonacci-sum decomposition with no adjacent Fibonacci terms \cite{Zeckendorf1972}.

This is precisely why coding length is an appropriate space measure (Definition~\ref{def:coding_space_complexity}): a readout prefix of length $N$ corresponds to a canonical digit string whose length is a discrete notion of spatial resolution.

\subsection{Golden anti-resonance and coding minimality}
Two extremal properties of the golden slope justify its role as a canonical ``least locking'' scan clock.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Anti-resonance (Diophantine hardness).} The golden ratio is extremal for rational approximation: it is ``worst approximable'' in the Hurwitz sense (Appendix~\ref{app:readout_bounds}), which minimizes phase lock-in and short periodic traps \cite{Cassels1957,KuipersNiederreiter1974}.
    \item \textbf{Binary canonicality.} Continued fraction coefficients all equal to $1$ yield the simplest Ostrowski constraints and hence the simplest canonical coding (Fibonacci/Zeckendorf).
\end{itemize}

Operationally: for fixed observational alphabet and fixed window grammar, the golden branch distributes orbit visits in a way that is simultaneously aperiodic and as non-resonant as possible, supporting stable readout without artificial periodicity.

\subsection{Space is not a container but an orthogonal-cut syntax}
The no-go theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:no_go_additive}) forces addition to be a readout, not an ontological operation. Hence every observable ``coordinate'' carries a memory of the cut that produced it.
From this perspective, space complexity is the price of orthogonalization: increasing distinguishability requires either more channels (larger effective Hilbert workspace) or longer stabilization prefixes. This directly aligns with $S_{\mathrm{QCA}}(R)=|R|\log d$ (Definition~\ref{def:qca_space_complexity}) and with the view that dimension is a measurement grammar rather than a primitive container.
