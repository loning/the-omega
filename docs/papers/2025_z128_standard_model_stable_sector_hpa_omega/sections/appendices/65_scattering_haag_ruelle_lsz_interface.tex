\subsection{Scattering interfaces (Haag--Ruelle / LSZ) and the delay dictionary}
\label{app:scattering_haag_ruelle_lsz_interface}

This appendix records an audit-facing scattering interface layer.
Its purpose is not to construct interacting QFTs from the finite folding core, but to make explicit how standard scattering objects (S-matrix, phase shifts, time delay) connect to the paper's operational delay dictionary (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}) under explicitly stated prerequisites.

\subsubsection{Minimal scattering objects}
\paragraph{S-matrix (interface object).}
\InterfaceTag In any framework with asymptotic in/out states, the scattering operator $S$ is the map between asymptotic Hilbert spaces.
In a frequency or energy representation, $S(\omega)$ encodes phase and mixing data and is the natural object behind time-delay observables.

\paragraph{Prerequisites (explicitly scoped).}
\AuditTag Standard scattering constructions (Haag--Ruelle and LSZ-type formulas) typically require additional assumptions such as:
\begin{itemize}
\item existence of a vacuum representation with a positive-energy spectrum condition,
\item a suitable one-particle subspace (mass gap and stability conditions),
\item locality/microcausality in a relativistic sense,
\item asymptotic completeness (optional; often not provable in 4D interacting models).
\end{itemize}
These assumptions are not closed from the tick-only finite folding core and are treated as explicit interface prerequisites whenever scattering statements are used.

\subsubsection{Time delay as an operational interface invariant}
\paragraph{Wigner--Smith delay.}
\InterfaceTag The paper adopts a unified operational time-delay dictionary in Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay}.
In particular, for a unitary scattering matrix $S(\omega)$, the Wigner--Smith operator
\[
Q(\omega)=-\iu\,S(\omega)^\dagger \frac{\dd S}{\dd \omega}
\]
and the scalar delay $\tau_{\mathrm{WS}}(\omega)=\Tr Q(\omega)$ provide an operational proxy for ``latency'' across channels.

\paragraph{Interface consequence.}
\InterfaceTag Within the declared dictionary, phase advance and time delay are not separate primitives: delay is the frequency derivative of scattering phase.
This aligns the scattering viewpoint with the paper's mass-as-latency closure language (Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay} and Appendix~\ref{app:time_mass_delay_reference}).
\AuditTag For an explicit computer-science realization of the delay dictionary as a discrete tick-time process (queueing and saturation diagnostics) and side-by-side toy comparisons to black-hole-like saturation and Page-surrogate behavior, see Appendix~\ref{app:scattering_bh_toy_equivalence_audits}.

\subsubsection{Boundary: what is and is not claimed here}
\AuditTag This appendix does not claim a derivation of an interacting 4D S-matrix from the protocol core.
It records the minimal interface map that allows audited comparisons across delay/phase/scattering channels once a scattering representation is declared.

\subsubsection{Minimal failure points and falsifiability hooks (audit)}
\label{subsec:scattering_minimal_failure_points}
\AuditTag The scattering interface is conditional on explicitly stated prerequisites.
When scattering statements are invoked, the minimal failure points are the canonical S1--S3 records in Appendix~\ref{app:minimal_failure_point_templates}.
In failure mode (S1) the paper reverts to the delay-as-proxy pipeline with explicit stability sweeps; in (S2)--(S3) it reverts to a net/state bookkeeping language (AQFT) and treats scattering as a declared external interface rather than as a reconstructed theorem-level object.
