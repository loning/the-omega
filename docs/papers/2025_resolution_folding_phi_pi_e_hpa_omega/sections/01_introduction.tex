
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Problem statement: from 64 microstates to a stable 21-type sector}
Fix a finite local readout space with $64$ microstates,
\[
\Omega_6:=\{0,1\}^6,\qquad |\Omega_6|=2^6=64,
\]
and its linearization $\mathcal{H}_6:=\ell^2(\Omega_6)\cong \CC^{64}$.
Our goal is to formalize, in a purely mathematical language, a notion of \emph{resolution folding}:
how a finite-resolution readout can admit a computable projection from the full microstate set to a smaller, stable effective set, and how this mechanism can iterate across scales.

\begin{definition}[Resolution folding scheme (finite window)]
\label{def:resolution_folding_scheme}
Fix a window length $m\ge 1$ and let $\Omega_m:=\{0,1\}^m$ be the microstate alphabet.
A \emph{resolution folding scheme} at window length $m$ consists of:
\begin{itemize}
\item a \emph{stable type set} $X_m\subseteq \Omega_m$ selected by constraint channels (equivalently, $X_m=\{w\in\Omega_m:\ \mathcal{D}(w)=0\}$ for some nonnegative defect function $\mathcal{D}$);
\item a surjective \emph{folding map} $\mathsf{Fold}_m:\Omega_m\twoheadrightarrow X_m$ (or, after indexing $\Omega_m\cong\{0,\dots,2^m-1\}$, a surjection from $\{0,\dots,2^m-1\}$ onto $X_m$);
\item the induced orthogonal projection $P_m:\ell^2(\Omega_m)\to \ell^2(\Omega_m)$ onto $\ell^2(X_m)$.
\end{itemize}
A \emph{scale interface} is a family of such schemes as $m$ varies, together with natural maps relating $(X_m,\mathsf{Fold}_m)$ across different $m$ (Section~\ref{sec:resolution_recursion}).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Related work and positioning.}
The golden mean constraint defines a shift of finite type and is among the standard model examples in symbolic dynamics; see \cite{LindMarcus1995}.
The determinant/zeta identities used here are classical for topological Markov shifts and sit within thermodynamic formalism and transfer-operator methods; see \cite{Bowen1975,Ruelle1978,ParryPollicott1990} for general frameworks and refinements (pressure, weighted zeta functions, and Ruelle operators).
Zeckendorf and Fibonacci numeration, and associated algorithms, are standard in the combinatorics on words and automatic sequences literature; see \cite{AlloucheShallit2003,Lothaire2002}.
The Hilbert curve and its use as a locality-preserving ordering (e.g.\ in indexing and embedding contexts) are classical; see \cite{Hilbert1891,Sagan1994,MoonJagadishFaloutsosSaltz2001}.

\subsection{Contributions (and what is classical)}
\label{subsec:intro_contrib}
At fixed word length $6$, the golden-mean forbidden grammar (\emph{no adjacent ones}) selects precisely $21$ admissible binary words.
This Fibonacci count and its asymptotic growth rate are classical.
The goal of this paper is not to re-prove standard symbolic-dynamics background, but to make the \emph{folding mechanism} explicit and quantitative at a fixed finite resolution, while keeping a clean interface to higher-resolution generalizations.

Concretely, beyond standard background, the paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An explicit folding map with closed preimages.}
We define a computable surjection $\mathrm{Fold}_6:\{0,\dots,63\}\twoheadrightarrow X_6$ via Zeckendorf normalization and window truncation, and we give closed-form preimage sets and an exact degeneracy histogram (Theorem~\ref{thm:fold6_preimages}; Appendix~\ref{app:fold6_table}).
\item \textbf{Quantitative control beyond the base case.}
We provide a general preimage characterization for $\mathrm{Fold}_m$ and derive explicit degeneracy bounds and scaling laws for the $2^m\to F_{m+2}$ compression (Section~\ref{sec:fold_map}).
\item \textbf{A symmetry-invariant spatial interface.}
We formalize the dihedral layout family $D_4$ for Hilbert addressing and prove exact invariance statements for adjacency-based statistics under layout changes (Section~\ref{sec:state_space_addressing}).
\item \textbf{An analytic stability channel with a separable extension.}
We formulate the $\e$-channel via zeta/Abel normalization and record a weighted finite-state extension in which analytic stability is not equivalent to a single forbidden word (Section~\ref{sec:e_constraint}).
\end{itemize}

With these pieces in place, we enrich the $\varphi$-stable sector with two additional, commuting channels:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{($\pi$) discrete monodromy / closure.}
Within the admissible set, a cyclic wrap-around condition splits the $21$ stable words into $18$ cyclically admissible states and $3$ boundary states.
\item \textbf{($\e$) analytic stability / Abel--zeta pole barrier.}
The golden-mean shift has zeta function $\zeta(z)=1/(1-z-z^2)$; after spectral normalization $z=r/\varphi$, the normalized zeta is holomorphic on $|r|<1$ with its principal pole on the boundary.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Main results (finite resolution)}
Let $X_6\subset \Omega_6$ be the set of length-$6$ words with no occurrence of the substring $11$.
Our main finite-resolution statements are:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Stable dimension ($64\to 21$).} The orthogonal projection $P_\varphi:\mathcal{H}_6\to\mathcal{H}_6$ onto $\ell^2(X_6)$ has rank $|X_6|=21$.
\item \textbf{Canonical split ($21=18\oplus 3$).} The cyclic admissibility condition (wrap-around allowed transition) defines a partition
\[
X_6=X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}}\ \sqcup\ X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}},
\qquad |X_6^{\mathrm{cyc}}|=18,\quad |X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}|=3,
\]
with $X_6^{\mathrm{bdry}}=\{100001,100101,101001\}$.
\item \textbf{A computable folding map.} We define a natural surjection
\[
\mathrm{Fold}_6:\{0,\dots,63\}\twoheadrightarrow X_6
\]
by taking the Zeckendorf representation of an integer and truncating to a length-$6$ window.
\item \textbf{Strong degeneracy law.} The full preimage structure of $\mathrm{Fold}_6$ on $\{0,\dots,63\}$ is explicit: every output has degeneracy $2$, $3$, or $4$, with histogram $(8,4,9)$ for $(2,3,4)$, and closed-form preimage sets (Theorem~\ref{thm:fold6_preimages}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Resolution as recursion: Hilbert addressing and Zeckendorf windowing}
Two recursive structures provide the scale interface.
On the spatial side, finite-resolution Hilbert addressing maps a one-dimensional tick index to a two-dimensional $2^n\times 2^n$ grid, with an $8$-element layout group $D_4$ acting by global symmetries.
On the syntactic side, increasing the Zeckendorf window length $m$ grows the admissible set size as $|X_m|=F_{m+2}$ (Fibonacci recursion), with asymptotic rate $|X_m|\asymp \varphi^m$.
We view resolution folding as a coupling of these two recursions: spatial refinement changes the embedding/locality model, while syntactic refinement changes the stable type space.
