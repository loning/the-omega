
\section{The dark sector from folding and routing overhead}
\label{sec:dark_sector}

This section proposes a concrete identification: dark matter and dark energy arise as effective readout consequences of hidden folding degrees of freedom and resolution refinement.
The mathematical anchor remains the minimal $64\to 21$ folding structure, while the dynamical bridge is a routing/compilation overhead field that couples to the metric via an effective lapse \cite{Ma2025HPT,Ma2025CAP,Ma2025FoP}.

\subsection{Dark matter as ghost microstates and routing cost}
\label{subsec:dm_ghost_states}

At window length $6$,
\[
|\Omega_6|=64,\qquad |X_6|=21,
\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad |\Omega_6\setminus X_6|=43.
\]
We call the elements of $\Omega_6\setminus X_6$ \emph{ghost microstates}: they violate the $\varphi$ grammar and do not survive as stable types under the $\varphi$-projection.
Equivalently, in the folding map picture, they correspond to the excess degrees of freedom that are collapsed into the many-to-one preimages of $\mathrm{Fold}_6$ (Section~\ref{sec:folding_64_21}).

To make ghost microstates gravitate without coupling to visible channels, we introduce a dynamical readout cost.
Let $\kappa(x)$ denote a local routing/compilation overhead, interpreted operationally as a readout-time delay or scheduling depth.
Define the lapse-like factor
\[
N(x):=\frac{\kappa_0}{\kappa(x)},
\]
where $\kappa_0$ is a reference overhead.
In the HPA--$\Omega$ framework this lapse factor controls redshift and time-delay effects \cite{Ma2025HPT}.

\begin{assumption}[Overhead correlates with folding degeneracy]
\label{assump:kappa_degeneracy}
The local overhead $\kappa(x)$ is positively correlated with local folding degeneracy statistics, e.g.\ with the frequency of $\varphi$-defects or with the expected preimage size
\[
\bar{g}(B):=\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\mu_B}\Bigl(\bigl|\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(\mathrm{Fold}_m(N))\bigr|\Bigr)
\]
computed on a local block $B$ under a local input distribution $\mu_B$.
\end{assumption}

This assumption yields an explicit micro-to-macro chain:
\[
\text{ghost microstates / degeneracy}\ \longrightarrow\ \kappa(x)\ \longrightarrow\ N(x)\ \longrightarrow\ g_{\mu\nu}\ \longrightarrow\ \text{effective gravity}.
\]
In particular, regions with larger hidden folding redundancy induce larger overhead (smaller $N$), producing gravitational redshift and lensing without introducing new luminous degrees of freedom.

\subsection{Dark energy as grid tension and information potential}
\label{subsec:de_grid_tension}

Dark energy is an approximately homogeneous component with negative pressure.
In an overhead-field interpretation, a natural candidate is a potential-dominated regime of an effective scalar field built from the overhead.
Define
\[
s(x):=\frac{\kappa(x)}{\kappa_0}=\frac{1}{N(x)},
\qquad
\chi(x):=\log s(x).
\]
Treating $\chi$ as an effective scalar degree of freedom, one obtains an energy density and pressure of the form
\[
\rho_\chi=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\chi}^2+V(\chi),
\qquad
p_\chi=\frac{1}{2}\dot{\chi}^2-V(\chi).
\]
When $V$ dominates, $w_\chi=p_\chi/\rho_\chi\to -1$, producing an accelerated-expansion component consistent with dark-energy phenomenology.

The readout interpretation suggests a geometric origin for $V(\chi)$: \emph{grid tension from refinement}.
As Hilbert order $n$ increases (spatial refinement), maintaining locality and causal connectivity requires higher scan scheduling and higher refresh rates.
The associated scheduling ``tension'' is a natural source of an approximately uniform background potential at large scales.

Finally, the $\e$-channel provides a rigorous interface between analytic stability and a pressure-like quantity:
for weighted transfer operators, the pressure is $\log\rho(A_\beta)$ \cite{Ruelle1978}.
This identifies a natural bridge between the analytic pole-barrier viewpoint and an effective potential for $\chi$ at higher resolution.
