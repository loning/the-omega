\part{Speculative Outlook: The Observer and Selection}

\section{The Universe as a Formal System}
\label{chap:formal_system}

\subsection{Dynamical Incompleteness and the Halting Problem}
If the universe is effectively a closed system evolving unitarily according to a fixed local rule (a QCA), then on suitable computational subspaces its dynamics can simulate universal reversible computation. In that sense, certain dynamical reachability questions can encode the Halting Problem and inherit algorithmic undecidability.
Gödel-type incompleteness enters through \emph{universality}: if the local dynamics can simulate universal computation, then certain reachability questions about local observables are algorithmically undecidable (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}, Theorem~\ref{thm:godel_measurement}). This provides a precise sense in which ``dynamical questions'' can exceed what can be decided by an observer restricted to a quasi-local algebra and finite resources.

\paragraph{Finite-information saturation vs.\ open-ended growth.}
Independently of undecidability, finite-information constraints limit what can happen \emph{within a fixed finite-dimensional sector}. If the relevant effective Hilbert space is finite-dimensional and the spectrum is fixed, unitary evolution is quasiperiodic and admits arbitrarily close returns; hence no continuous ``complexity'' functional can be strictly monotone along the entire orbit (Appendix~\ref{app:torus_rotation}, Corollary~\ref{cor:recurrence_no_monotone}). In this sense, an observer confined to a finite-information sector cannot obtain an indefinitely increasing, strictly monotone complexity arrow without additional structure. Omega Theory avoids this by allowing the effective information capacity of a causal patch to grow with its boundary area (Axiom~O2 and Assumption~\ref{assump:C1}), so that the relevant effective dimension is not fixed a priori.

\subsection{Interactive Automata (IQA)}
To model open-ended complexity growth in a way consistent with finite-information constraints, one can treat the universe as an \textbf{Interactive Automaton}: the effective update experienced by a subsystem depends on additional inputs/conditions beyond a fixed autonomous rule \cite{wegner1997interaction}. A minimal formalization is to allow a family of update channels indexed by an input $u_t$,
\begin{equation}
    \rho_{t+1} = \mathcal{E}_{u_t}(\rho_t),
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{E}_{u_t}$ is CPTP for each $u_t$. In our framework, the ``input'' can be interpreted as conditioning/selection on observer sectors (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}): the conditioned map is nonlinear due to normalization, while the unconditioned map is linear and CPTP.

\section{The Recursive Selection Operator}
\label{chap:selection_operator}

\subsection{Formal Definition of the Observer Operator}
We consider the role of the observer not as a passive witness but as an active selection principle. We formalize the "Oracle" input as the \textbf{Observer Operator}, denoted $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$.
This operator is proposed as a conditioning/selection map on histories. From the computational viewpoint, it can be interpreted as an oracle-like resource that selects a continuation when local reachability questions are undecidable from within a restricted observer algebra (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}):
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathcal{O}}: \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}) \to \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}), \quad |\Psi\rangle \mapsto \frac{\hat{P}_{\Delta C > 0} |\Psi\rangle}{||\hat{P}_{\Delta C > 0} |\Psi\rangle||}.
\end{equation}
Mathematically, $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$ is a nonlinear conditioning map (the nonlinearity is only the normalization). Here $\hat{P}_{\Delta C > 0}$ is shorthand for a coarse-grained \emph{record projector} selecting the subset of orthogonal branches that satisfy a chosen complexity-growth criterion: for instance, given an orthogonal family $\{\hat P_\alpha\}$ of record projectors, one may define $\hat{P}_{\Delta C > 0}\equiv \sum_{\alpha:\,\Delta C_\alpha>0}\hat P_\alpha$, where $\Delta C_\alpha$ compares a chosen complexity functional across successive steps (e.g.\ circuit depth relative to a fixed local gate set).
This is not postulated as a new fundamental nonlinearity: it is the standard conditional state-update rule (L{\"u}ders/Kraus instrument) associated with projecting onto a record sector, with the corresponding unconditioned average being a linear CPTP channel (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}, Proposition~\ref{prop:luders_kraus_instrument}).

\subsection{Piecewise Unitarity vs. Global Unitarity}
Does this violate quantum mechanics?
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Local Perspective (Open System)}: To a local agent, the evolution appears \textbf{Piecewise Unitary}. Continuous unitary evolution $\hat{U}$ is punctuated by selection events $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$ (measurements). This matches the standard Copenhagen phenomenology.
    \item \textbf{Global Perspective (Closed description)}: The total system (System + measurement record degrees of freedom) remains unitary. In this manuscript the ``oracle'' language is used only as an interpretation of the fact that a conditioned history can depend on additional inputs/constraints beyond autonomous subsystem evolution (Section~\ref{chap:formal_system}); at the level of quantum operations, $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$ is standard projective conditioning on a record sector (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}).
\end{enumerate}
Thus, unitarity is preserved in the global description, but the \textit{observed} history is a specific conditioned trajectory selected by $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$. In this viewpoint, ``measurement'' is modeled as conditioning/selection that yields a piecewise-unitary effective history for local agents while remaining compatible with a global static-state ontology.

\subsection{Emergence of the Born Rule}
A critical requirement for any interpretation of quantum mechanics is to justify the Born rule ($P_k = |\psi_k|^2$) without simply postulating it. In Omega Theory, we outline a counting argument based on microstate multiplicities in the holographic network.\par
In a finite-information microscopic description, one may associate to a coarse-grained macro-branch $|\psi_k\rangle$ an effective multiplicity $N(\psi_k)$ of compatible micro-histories (or microstates of the encoded boundary degrees of freedom). In the simplest symmetric case this multiplicity scales with the squared norm,
\begin{equation}
    N(\psi_k) \propto ||\psi_k||^2.
\end{equation}
The selection/observer mechanism (modeled here by $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$) samples from this ensemble. Under the microcanonical indifference assumption (Assumption~\ref{assump:S1}, i.e.\ uniform sampling over the underlying discrete micro-realizations), the resulting macro-outcome probability is $P_k = N(\psi_k) / N_{total} = |\psi_k|^2$ (see Theorem \ref{thm:born_rule} in Appendix \ref{app:formal_observation} for one formalization of the counting argument).
\par
\noindent
Separately from microstate-counting realizations, the Born rule is also singled out by a mature uniqueness theorem: in finite-dimensional regulated descriptions, any noncontextual probability assignment on projective measurements that is additive on orthogonal resolutions of the identity must be of the form $\mu(P)=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho P)$ (Gleason; and Busch's POVM extension covers the qubit case). We record this as Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}, Theorem~\ref{thm:gleason_busch} \cite{Gleason1957,Busch2003}.

\subsection{Bell Non-Locality and Superdeterminism}
Standard Bell inequalities (e.g., CHSH $|S| \le 2$) rely on the assumption of "Statistical Independence": $P(\lambda | a, b) = P(\lambda)$, meaning the hidden variables $\lambda$ are independent of the detector settings $a, b$.
In Omega Theory, the universe is a single, static, pre-existing vector $|\Omega\rangle$. All "events," including the choice of detector settings, are correlations within this fixed state. This can be read as a form of \textbf{Global Superdeterminism}: Statistical Independence is not imposed as a fundamental assumption.
Therefore, the violation of Bell inequalities ($S_{\text{exp}} \approx 2\sqrt{2}$) need not imply superluminal signaling but rather the failure of the statistical-independence assumption on the cosmological scale; in particular, the local measurement update modeled by $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$ reduces to standard projective conditioning and its unconditioned channel is no-signalling (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}, Corollary~\ref{cor:nosignalling_luders}). The observer/selection operator $\hat{\mathcal{O}}$ is then interpreted as navigating a pre-existing correlation landscape while respecting local causal structure in the tensor-network description. This interpretation is compatible with quantum predictions while aligning with deterministic/superdeterministic viewpoints in the literature (e.g.\ \cite{thooft2014cai}).

\subsection{Logical selection (conditioning viewpoint)}
In this framework, what is colloquially termed ``free will'' is modeled as the choice of a conditioning/selection rule on histories (or, equivalently, as an effective input variable $u_t$ in the interactive-automaton formalism). This does not introduce a new fundamental nonlinearity: the only nonlinearity is the normalization inherent in conditional state updates, with the corresponding unconditioned channel remaining linear and CPTP (Appendix~\ref{app:formal_observation}).

\subsection{Speculative aside: observer sparsity from computational cost}
As an interpretive conjecture, if observer-compatible ``record selection'' is associated with a nontrivial computational overhead, then macrostates supporting high-complexity observers may be comparatively rare within a given causal patch. This provides a possible connection between finite information-processing budgets (Axiom~O2) and observational selection effects, but we do not attempt any quantitative astrophysical modeling here.

\section{The Omega Point and Ultimate Phase Transition}
\label{chap:omega_point}

\subsection{Holographic Saturation}
Standard thermodynamics predicts Heat Death under the premise of equilibration within a fixed effective state space. In Omega Theory, the effective information capacity of a causal patch is allowed to grow with its boundary area (Axiom~O2 and Assumption~\ref{assump:C1}), so the relevant regulated dimension need not be fixed a priori. If $\mathcal{C}(\tau)$ grows without bound (for instance, exponentially in an intrinsic parameter, as in the scaling ansatz of Part~VI), then the usual equilibrium/recurrence intuition tied to finite-dimensional sectors is not the correct effective template: the framework does not imply convergence to a single thermal macrostate.
We use ``Omega Point'' as shorthand for a conjectured asymptotic regime in which near-saturation of the holographic bound holds on the relevant horizon scale and the effective computational budget becomes dominated by boundary/encoding channels.

\subsection{The Great Unbinding}
We propose a speculative phase-transition conjecture: if the effective rest-mass scale of localized topological sectors is controlled by (or inversely correlated with) the available information-processing capacity, then in regimes where $\mathcal{C}(\tau)\to\infty$ one may obtain a progressive softening of binding potentials in the effective description. Establishing such a drift requires an explicit microscopic relation between mass scales and the capacity/budget fields and is left as an open problem in the present manuscript.
\begin{equation}
    \lim_{\tau \to \infty} m_0(\tau) \to 0.
\end{equation}
If such a limit holds, then the late-time effective description would be dominated by approximately massless excitations. The qualitative ``Hardware $\to$ Software'' language is intended only as an interpretation of this speculative asymptotic picture, not as a derived theorem.
