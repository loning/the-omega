
\section{Dynamical closure: from overhead fields to effective gravity and cosmological equations}
\label{sec:dynamical_closure}

Sections~\ref{sec:folding_64_21}--\ref{sec:dark_sector} provide a finite-resolution classification and a micro-to-macro identification.
To make the framework predictive, we need a dynamical closure that maps folding and overhead statistics into metric dynamics.
We adopt the computational action principle in the HPA--$\Omega$ program \cite{Ma2025CAP,Ma2025FoP}.

\subsection{Computational action principle and the \texorpdfstring{$\Omega$}{Omega} action}
\label{subsec:cap_action}

The computational action principle treats readout consistency, overhead minimization, and invariance constraints as variational principles.
In a conservative formulation, one considers an action functional of the form
\[
S[g,\chi,\Psi]=S_{\mathrm{grav}}[g]+S_{\chi}[g,\chi]+S_{\mathrm{m}}[g,\Psi],
\]
where $g$ is the effective metric, $\Psi$ denotes visible matter fields, and $\chi$ is an overhead-derived scalar (Section~\ref{subsec:de_grid_tension}).
Variation yields an effective Einstein-type equation
\[
G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}=8\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}\left(T_{\mu\nu}^{(\mathrm{m})}+T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}\right).
\]
The exact form of $T_{\mu\nu}^{(\chi)}$ depends on the chosen kinetic term and potential $V(\chi)$; for a canonical scalar it is the standard stress tensor.

\subsection{Newtonian limit: Poisson equation and the identification of the potential}
\label{subsec:newton_limit}

In the weak-field, slow-motion regime, one writes \cite{Wald1984}
\[
g_{00}\approx -(1+2\Phi/c^2),
\]
where $\Phi$ is the Newtonian potential.
The computational action principle is designed to recover the Poisson equation
\[
\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}\rho_{\mathrm{m}}
\]
in the appropriate limit.
In the overhead interpretation, the lapse is determined by a dimensionless overhead field $\chi$ and a coupling strength $\gamma$ (Definition~\ref{def:degeneracy_overhead}; Appendix~\ref{app:overhead_lapse}):
\[
N(x)=\e^{-\gamma\chi(x)}.
\]
Thus
\[
g_{00}(x)\approx -N(x)^2=-\e^{-2\gamma\chi(x)}
\approx -(1-2\gamma\chi(x)),
\]
and comparing with $g_{00}\approx -(1+2\Phi/c^2)$ yields the leading-order identification
\[
\Phi(x)= -\gamma c^2\bigl(\chi(x)-\chi_0\bigr),
\]
where $\chi_0$ is an arbitrary reference (a constant shift of $\chi$ does not change forces).
Consequently, the effective source associated with an overhead field can be written as
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(x):=\frac{1}{4\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}}\nabla^2\Phi(x)
=-\frac{\gamma c^2}{4\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}}\nabla^2\chi(x).
\]
This is the closed bridge from folding statistics to an effective gravitational source once $\chi$ is specified by the folding degeneracy (Section~\ref{subsec:micro_to_macro}).

\subsection{A minimal halo fit: rotation curves as a constraint on \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}}
\label{subsec:rotation_fit}

For a static, spherically symmetric configuration, circular velocity satisfies
\[
v_c^2(r)=r\,\Phi'(r)= -\gamma c^2\, r\,\chi'(r).
\]
In particular, a flat rotation curve ($v_c(r)\approx v_{\mathrm{flat}}$ over a range of radii) implies
\[
\chi(r)= -\frac{v_{\mathrm{flat}}^2}{\gamma c^2}\log\frac{r}{r_0},
\]
and yields the standard isothermal effective density profile
\[
\rho_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=\frac{1}{4\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}}\nabla^2\Phi(r)
=\frac{v_{\mathrm{flat}}^2}{4\pi G_{\mathrm{eff}}\,r^2}
\]
\cite{BinneyTremaine2008}.
Therefore, once $\chi(r)$ is reconstructed from folding statistics (Appendix~\ref{app:data_protocol}), the observed $v_{\mathrm{flat}}$ constrains $\gamma$ by a one-parameter fit:
the product $\gamma\Delta\chi$ over the relevant radial interval must match $v_{\mathrm{flat}}^2/c^2$.
Flat rotation curves are empirically ubiquitous in disk galaxies \cite{SofueRubin2001}.
For example, a typical spiral-galaxy value $v_{\mathrm{flat}}\sim 200\,\mathrm{km/s}$ corresponds to $v_{\mathrm{flat}}^2/c^2\sim 4\times 10^{-7}$; if the reconstructed $\Delta\chi$ across the flat region is $O(1)$, then $\gamma$ is naturally at the $10^{-7}$ scale.

\paragraph{Least-squares estimator for $\gamma$.}
Given rotation-curve measurements $\{(r_i,v_i,\sigma_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ and a reconstructed profile $\chi(r)$, define
\[
v_{\mathrm{pred}}^2(r_i;\gamma):=-\gamma c^2\, r_i\,\chi'(r_i).
\]
Estimating $\chi'(r_i)$ by finite differences, one obtains a one-parameter weighted least-squares fit:
\[
\widehat{\gamma}=\arg\min_{\gamma>0}\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\bigl(v_{\mathrm{pred}}^2(r_i;\gamma)-v_i^2\bigr)^2}{\sigma_i^2}
=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^N \left(\frac{x_i v_i^2}{\sigma_i^2}\right)}{\sum_{i=1}^N \left(\frac{x_i^2}{\sigma_i^2}\right)},
\qquad
x_i:=-c^2 r_i\,\chi'(r_i).
\]

\subsection{Micro-to-macro interface: folding statistics as an overhead definition}
\label{subsec:micro_to_macro}

The closure requires a concrete mapping from local folding statistics to $\chi$.
We adopt the degeneracy-based definition (Definition~\ref{def:degeneracy_overhead}):
\[
\chi(B)=\log\frac{\bar{g}_m(B)}{\bar{g}_0},
\qquad
\bar{g}_m(B)=\mathbb{E}_{N\sim\mu_B}\bigl(g_m(N)\bigr),
\qquad
g_m(N)=\bigl|\mathrm{Fold}_m^{-1}(\mathrm{Fold}_m(N))\bigr|.
\]
In practice, if only coarse binary data are available, one may use the local grammar-defect density $\bar{D}_\varphi(B)$ as a proxy statistic, since $\varphi$-defects are necessary for leaving the admissible sector.

The minimal model provides a fully explicit calibration point: for $m=6$, the preimages of $\mathrm{Fold}_6$ are known exactly (Appendix~\ref{app:fold6_table}), so $\bar{g}_6$ can be computed without approximation once a local input distribution is specified.
This makes the micro-to-macro interface executable: it is not merely qualitative but a data pipeline (Appendix~\ref{app:data_protocol}).
