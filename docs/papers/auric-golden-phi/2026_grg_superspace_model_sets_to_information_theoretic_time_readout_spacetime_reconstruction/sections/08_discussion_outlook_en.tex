\section{Discussion and outlook}\label{sec:discussion}

We have organized a set of ideas around a simple but demanding principle: start from auditable geometric objects (superspace model sets and associated dynamical systems), introduce explicit finite-resolution readout protocols, and define time/casual notions at the protocol layer via refinement and entropy rates. This geometry-first stance is compatible with both classical and quantum space-time discussions provided one keeps interfaces explicit and avoids over-identification.

\subsection{Limitations of the present synthesis}\label{sec:discussion:limitations}

Several limitations should be kept in view:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{No claim of reproducing Einstein dynamics}: nothing in the review implies the Einstein field equations or a specific effective stress-energy content. Any such emergence would require additional assumptions and a concrete continuum limit.
  \item \textbf{No curvature reconstruction claim}: the protocol produces symbolic records and protocol-defined orders/entropies; it does not, by itself, reconstruct a Lorentzian metric, curvature tensor, geodesic structure, or energy conditions. Any such identification would require an explicit interface map and additional regularity/limit assumptions beyond those used for ergodic/SMB statements.
  \item \textbf{Protocol dependence}: readout-induced time and causality depend on protocol parameters $\Theta$. Identifying protocol-invariant structures (or universality classes) is essential before making strong physical claims.
  \item \textbf{Choice of entropy/time functional}: we focused on cylinder-measure contraction and SMB/KS entropy. Other choices (algorithmic complexity, coarse-grained free energy functionals) may be relevant, but should be compared carefully.
  \item \textbf{One-dimensional ring factors are not generic}: several motivating examples use a one-dimensional torus factor (circle rotation) and Sturmian/Fibonacci codings as a disciplined low-complexity layer. In higher-dimensional internal phase dynamics, codings are typically not Sturmian and can exhibit richer languages and higher (often polynomial) complexity even when the underlying translation has zero KS entropy. Any universality claim should therefore be formulated in terms of protocol-level invariants (factor monotonicity, finite-memory equivalence classes, mismatch-rate stability bounds) rather than in terms of Sturmian-specific structure unless the protocol explicitly enforces that reduction.
\end{itemize}

In addition, the source corpus motivating this review often highlights special constants (notably the golden ratio) through stability and renormalization considerations. For GRG-facing presentation, such constants should be treated as outputs of clearly stated optimization or invariance criteria (e.g.\ extremal Diophantine properties of slopes, fixed points of substitutions), not as universal physical constants by default. Whether any such constants survive as robust features of a spacetime reconstruction interface is itself an empirical and model-dependent question.

\subsection{Connections to established GR/QG themes}\label{sec:discussion:connections}

The topical collection is motivated by geometry of classical and quantum spacetimes, including approaches where geometry is reconstructed from discrete or algebraic data. The readout-based viewpoint has natural touch points:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Lorentzian causality}: protocol causal precedence provides a refinement/decidability order that can be compared with Lorentzian causal orders once an interface map $\Phi$ is specified.
  \item \textbf{Quantum geometry and operators}: treating readout as an operator/protocol resonates with the idea that geometric information is encoded in algebras of observables; in loop quantum gravity, for example, geometric operators and their spectra play a foundational role \cite{AshtekarLewandowski2004,Thiemann2007}.
  \item \textbf{Holographic and boundary perspectives}: the lightcone-boundary readout viewpoint parallels boundary-data paradigms, but here expressed in terms of protocol-accessible information rather than a fixed AdS/CFT setting.
  \item \textbf{Operational/relational time}: our protocol-level refinement time is conceptually adjacent to relational time proposals, including the thermal time hypothesis and Page--Wootters-type mechanisms, in the sense that ``time'' is defined from information/state updates rather than assumed as a primitive parameter \cite{ConnesRovelli1994,Rovelli2011,PageWootters1983}.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Thermal/modular time vs.\ protocol entropy-rate time}\label{sec:discussion:thermal-time}

The thermal time hypothesis (Connes--Rovelli) proposes that a physical notion of time flow can be extracted from a state on an algebra of observables via modular theory: given a faithful normal state on a von Neumann algebra, Tomita--Takesaki theory yields a canonical modular automorphism group (a modular flow) \cite{ConnesRovelli1994}. The key structural point is that time is tied to \emph{state + observable substructure}, not imposed as a background parameter.

Our protocol definition of time follows the same meta-pattern but in a measure-theoretic/symbolic setting: a protocol specifies a finite measurable partition (or readout map) and hence a symbolic process; entropy-rate time $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(a_{0:t-1}))$ measures posterior contraction under refinement and, under SMB hypotheses, grows linearly with slope given by an entropy rate (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:smb}).

Two comparisons are particularly clean:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Choice dependence as a feature}: modular time depends on the chosen state and observable algebra; protocol time depends on the chosen invariant measure and partition/readout parameters $\Theta$. In both cases, the correct discipline is to (i) make the choice explicit and (ii) study equivalence classes under admissible coarse-grainings/recodings.
  \item \textbf{Monotonicity under coarse-graining}: coarse-graining the observable structure is expected to reduce available information. In the protocol setting this is expressed by entropy monotonicity under factors and by the resulting monotonicity of refinement depth (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:factors}). This monotonicity is the shared invariance principle: while numerical time parametrizations change under re-encoding, the direction and rate-class of information acquisition are constrained by data-processing/entropy monotonicity.
\end{itemize}

It is equally important to state what does \emph{not} coincide:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Flow vs.\ scalar clock}: thermal time produces a one-parameter automorphism group (a flow) on an algebra, whereas SMB/KS time as used here is a scalar functional of a record prefix. In particular, $\tau(t)$ does not by itself define a dynamical flow on an observable algebra; it is a refinement-depth parameter along an already given scan/refinement process.
  \item \textbf{What is assumed vs.\ extracted}: modular time is designed to extract a canonical time flow from \emph{state + algebra} without presupposing an external time parameter. In contrast, SMB/KS time presupposes a scan dynamics (or at least a stationary process) and uses entropy theory to quantify information acquisition along that dynamics.
  \item \textbf{Commutative limit caution}: in purely classical (commutative) settings, modular theory does not automatically reproduce the object-layer scan dynamics. Thus, identifying $\tau(t)$ with modular time would require an additional, explicitly stated interface (e.g.\ a chosen noncommutative observable algebra representing the readout channel), which we do not assume in this review.
\end{itemize}

This alignment and separation suggest a practical bridge that is safe for GR/QG audiences: treat the protocol's readout $\sigma$-algebra (generated by the partition/filtration $\mathcal{F}_t$) as the commutative analog of a chosen observable subalgebra, treat factor/recoding operations as analogs of coarse-graining, and use entropy monotonicity (data processing) as the common control principle. Any stronger identification (e.g.\ interpreting $\tau$ as a modular parameter) should be framed as an additional hypothesis about the observable-algebra model of the readout channel, not as an automatic consequence of SMB theory.\cite{Takesaki2003}

\begin{remark}[Optional bridge via relative entropy: $\tau(t)$ as a state-update divergence]\label{rem:tau-relative-entropy-bridge}
There is, however, a precise overlap at the level of \emph{state update} and \emph{entropy monotonicity}. In the simplest deterministic readout setting, a realized prefix $a_{0:t-1}$ determines a cylinder $C=C(a_{0:t-1})$ and hence a posterior measure $\mu_C:=\mu(\,\cdot\,\cap C)/\mu(C)$.
Then the refinement functional is exactly the Kullback--Leibler divergence (classical relative entropy) of posterior vs.\ prior:
\[
\tau(t)= -\log\mu(C)\ =\ D(\mu_C\Vert \mu).
\]
This identity makes explicit why $\tau$ is monotone under refinement and why it is naturally controlled under coarse-graining: relative entropy is contractive under measurable maps (data processing), matching the factor monotonicity statements in Section~\ref{sec:info-time:factors}.\cite{CoverThomas2006}

In this sense, SMB/KS time overlaps with thermal/modular time not at the level of producing the same flow, but at the level of using a state (or posterior state) and a choice of accessible observables to define an intrinsic arrow via information divergence. Any attempt to identify $\tau$ with a modular parameter would require an explicit noncommutative algebraic model of the readout/conditioning channel (beyond the commutative $\sigma$-algebra picture), which we leave as a separate hypothesis layer.\cite{Takesaki2003,ConnesRovelli1994}
\end{remark}

\subsubsection{Relational/partial observables vs.\ protocol refinement}\label{sec:discussion:relational}

Relational-time and ``partial observables'' programs emphasize that time should be extracted from correlations between dynamical variables rather than assumed as an external parameter \cite{Rovelli2002PartialObservables,Dittrich2006PartialCompleteObservables}. The closest structural match in the present review is the following: a protocol defines a finite information state (a posterior cylinder, or a stabilized fiber state) and ``time'' is the monotone refinement of that information state as more correlations become operationally decidable.

Two concrete points of contact help locate our proposal:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{What plays the role of a clock}: in partial-observable language, a clock is a chosen partial observable against which other quantities are correlated. In our setting, the role of a clock is played by the \emph{protocol filtration} $\mathcal{F}_t=\sigma(A_0,\dots,A_{t-1})$ (Section~\ref{sec:info-time:cylinders}) and its refinement functional $\tau(t)=-\log\mu(C(A_0^{t-1}))$ (Definition~\ref{def:entropy-time}). The ``reading'' is not a value of a distinguished variable but a level of decidability under a chosen observation interface.
  \item \textbf{What is invariant}: partial/complete observables aim for gauge-invariant (Dirac) observables. Our review does not claim gauge-invariant observables at the object layer; instead it isolates protocol-level invariants and monotonicities (factor entropy monotonicity; finite-memory equivalence; mismatch-rate bounds) that are auditable from records. This is a weaker notion of invariance, but it is operational and directly testable.
\end{itemize}

This comparison suggests a useful positioning: entropy-rate time is best viewed as a disciplined \emph{relational refinement parameter} attached to a chosen observation algebra/partition, rather than as a substitute for a full Dirac-observable construction.

\subsubsection{Page--Wootters conditional time vs.\ protocol conditionalization}\label{sec:discussion:page-wootters}

The Page--Wootters mechanism extracts effective dynamics from a globally stationary state by conditioning on a clock subsystem \cite{PageWootters1983}. At the level of mathematical structure, the parallel with our protocol time is the centrality of conditionalization: Page--Wootters uses conditional states of ``system given clock reading'', while our refinement time uses conditional measures of ``internal states given observed record prefix''.

The key differences are equally important for interpretation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{No assumed global Hamiltonian evolution}: our object layer can be a deterministic translation on a compact group (zero KS entropy) or a more general measure-preserving system; the time arrow is induced by information acquisition and need not correspond to a unitary evolution parameter.
  \item \textbf{The role of noise/resolution}: positive entropy-rate regimes in our setting can arise from probabilistic windows/readout channels (Section~\ref{sec:readout:probabilistic-template}), with a quantitative lower bound on $h$ from the channel nontriviality (Proposition~\ref{prop:prob-window-positive-entropy}). This is closer to an operational ``measurement channel'' viewpoint than to a purely kinematical clock-system split.
\end{itemize}

In short, the Page--Wootters comparison clarifies what is shared (conditioning as the source of an effective time parameter) and what is not claimed here (a specific quantum-mechanical reconstruction of unitary dynamics).

\subsection{Open problems}\label{sec:discussion:open-problems}

We conclude with a short list of concrete problems that appear both mathematically well-posed and relevant to geometry-of-spacetime reconstruction:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{O\arabic*.},leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Universality under protocol changes}: characterize equivalence classes of protocols $\Theta$ that produce the same stabilized fiber statistics and the same entropy-rate time up to bi-Lipschitz reparameterization.
  \item \textbf{Causality compatibility theorems}: given a candidate interface $\Phi$ into a Lorentzian manifold, prove sufficient conditions under which protocol precedence implies Lorentzian causal precedence (or yields a causal set in an appropriate limit).
  \item \textbf{Geometric meaning of degeneracy spectra}: relate fiber degeneracy distributions induced by $\Fold_m$ to geometric invariants of windows (boundary regularity, curvature measures) and to spectral invariants of the underlying dynamical system.
  \item \textbf{Continuum limits}: develop controlled scaling limits in which superspace/readout structures converge to continuum spacetimes (or to effective geometries), and identify which quantities survive as macroscopic invariants.
  \item \textbf{Benchmark models}: establish a small set of canonical benchmark CPS/windows/protocols for cross-comparison across communities (mathematical quasicrystals, dynamical systems, and gravitational physics).
\item \textbf{Interface to continuum geometry}: identify minimal additional assumptions under which protocol-defined observables (Section~\ref{sec:stabilization:observables}) admit an interpretation as geometric quantities on an effective manifold (e.g.\ an effective lapse/redshift or an effective causal distance), and determine which such interpretations are robust under protocol changes.
\item \textbf{Renormalization across resolutions}: develop systematic relations between statistics at different word lengths $m$ (and window widths $\epsilon$), including conditions under which there exists a renormalization flow on protocols whose fixed points correspond to stable ``geometric phases'' of readout.
\end{enumerate}
